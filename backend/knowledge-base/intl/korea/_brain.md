# Wayfinder Korea Brain — top-level orientation

Korea is one of the most academically competitive education markets in the world, and the Korean college-admission landscape is fundamentally different from the US. Wayfinder serves three Korean-family use cases:

1. **Domestic Korean university admissions** — students applying to SKY (Seoul/Korea/Yonsei), 서성한 (Sogang/Sungkyunkwan/Hanyang), and the broader top-tier
2. **Top Korean high schools (특목고/자사고/외고/과고)** — middle-school families navigating special-purpose high school admissions
3. **US admissions FROM Korea** — Korean families pursuing US universities (Ivy + top-30), often as a parallel track to domestic Korean admissions

## Why this market matters

- Korea has roughly 380,000 high-school graduates per year, with extremely high college-going rates (~70%).
- The top universities (서울대 / 연세대 / 고려대 / KAIST / POSTECH) are globally elite — Seoul National engineering and Yonsei medicine sit alongside the world's top programs.
- Korean families spend ~$15-20B annually on private tutoring (사교육비) — the highest per-capita private-education spending in the world.
- US admissions consultancies in Seoul (강남 area especially) charge $20K-100K per student. Wayfinder offers comparable depth at SaaS pricing — meaningful market opportunity.

## Core admissions paths the advisor must master

- **수능 (Suneung / CSAT)** — Korean SAT-equivalent, single-day exam in early November, the most consequential test in Korean life. Held annually on the 3rd Thursday of November. Stock market opens an hour late on 수능 day to reduce traffic. Score determines 정시 (Jeongsi) admission.
- **정시 (Jeongsi)** — admission based primarily on 수능 score. Roughly 30-40% of admits at most Seoul universities. Pure-numbers track.
- **학종 (Hakjong, 학생부종합전형)** — School Records Comprehensive Evaluation. Admission based on 학생부 (transcript), 자기소개서 (self-introduction essay), and (sometimes) interview. The "holistic" track, similar in spirit to US holistic admissions but with more weight on classroom records and very specific in-school activities. About 40-50% of admits.
- **수시 (Susi)** — umbrella term for non-수능 admissions including 학종, 논술 (essay-track), 실기 (performance-track for arts/sports). 수시 happens September-November senior year.
- **논술 (Nonsul)** — essay-test track. School-administered written exam. Common at top universities for students with strong 학종 records but mid 수능 scores.
- **재수/삼수 (Jaesu/Samsu)** — gap year(s) to retake 수능. Extremely common in Korea (~25-30% of 수능 takers are 재수생). Not stigmatized; sometimes a strategic choice.

## Domestic university tiers (must know cold)

- **SKY**: Seoul National University (서울대 / SNU), Korea University (고려대), Yonsei University (연세대). The Big 3. SNU is government-funded #1; Korea + Yonsei are the elite privates with intense rivalry.
- **KAIST + POSTECH**: top STEM. KAIST is government-funded; POSTECH is POSCO-affiliated private. Comparable to top US engineering schools.
- **서성한 (Seo-Sung-Han)**: Sogang (서강대), Sungkyunkwan (성균관대 — Samsung-affiliated), Hanyang (한양대). Tier just below SKY.
- **중경외시 (Joong-Kyung-Oe-Si)**: Chung-Ang (중앙대), Kyung Hee (경희대), Hankuk Univ of Foreign Studies (외대 / HUFS), University of Seoul (시립대).
- **건동홍 (Geon-Dong-Hong)**: Konkuk (건국대), Dongguk (동국대), Hongik (홍익대 — known for art/design).
- **인서울 (In-Seoul)**: catch-all for "any university located in Seoul" — strong signal-of-prestige in Korean job market regardless of specific university.

## Top high schools (middle-school families care about this)

- **자사고 (Jasago)** — autonomous private high schools. Tuition-charging (~$10-15K/yr). Top examples: 민사고 Minjok Sa-gyo (Korean Minjok Leadership Academy / KMLA), 상산고 Sangsan, 현대청심 Hyundai Cheongshim, 외대부고 HUFS-affiliated, 광양제철고 Gwangyang Jecheol. Selective admit.
- **외고 (Oego)** — Foreign Language High Schools. Public/quasi-public. Top: 대원외고 Daewon, 대일외고 Daeil, 한영외고 Hanyoung. Famous for sending students to US Ivies. Selective.
- **과고 (Gwago)** — Science High Schools. Top: 서울과고 Seoul Science HS, 경기과고 Gyeonggi Science HS, 세종과고 Sejong Science HS. Direct STEM pipeline to KAIST/POSTECH/SNU.
- **영재고 (Yeongjaego)** — Gifted Schools. Most selective tier. 6-7 schools nationally. KSA (Korea Science Academy), 서울영재 Seoul Science Gifted, 경기영재.
- **국제고 (Gukjego)** — International High Schools. 8 schools nationally with global-curriculum focus.
- **일반고 (Ilbango)** — regular public high schools (most students attend these).

## US admissions FROM Korea — the angles unique to Korean families

- **Demonstrated interest is real** at non-Ivy US schools. Korean students often apply to 30+ US schools without nuanced school research; this hurts. Wayfinder should push genuine fit research.
- **English proficiency**: TOEFL 110+ is table stakes for top US schools from Korea. Most top-tier Korean students have it.
- **AP track is increasingly common** at 외고/자사고 students targeting US schools. Sometimes 8-12 APs by senior year.
- **Financial-aid eligibility**: Korean students are international applicants, so most US schools are need-aware (NOT need-blind) for them. Schools that ARE need-blind for international: Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT, Amherst, Bowdoin, Williams, Dartmouth (recently announced). Schools that are need-aware: most others.
- **Demographic context post-SFFA**: Asian-applicant scrutiny has shifted; the Korean-American context (in US schools) is somewhat different from the Korea-resident applicant context. Korean residents are international and don't fall under SFFA the same way.
- **Common essay traps**: "performing the model minority" narrative, generic STEM passion essays, parents-pressured framing. Korean families often default to these — Wayfinder should specifically push beyond them.

## Korean-language advisor identity

The Wayfinder advisor responds in **한국어 (Korean)** when the user writes in Korean, using **존댓말 (formal -습니다/-입니다 endings)** by default. Switches to 반말 ONLY if the user clearly initiates 반말. Never code-switches randomly.

Cultural sensitivity musts:
- Family hierarchy: 부모님 (parents) often hold significant decision authority. Advisor should respect this — frame advice that the student can present to parents, not advice that overrides parents.
- 사교육 (private tutoring) is the norm, not a stigma. Don't say "you don't need a 학원" — say "여기서 학원과 함께 시너지 효과를 낼 수 있는 부분..."
- Gap years (재수/삼수) are normalized. Don't treat them as failure.
- 인서울 preference is real — even strong students at regional flagship 지방 universities sometimes regret not pushing for Seoul. Acknowledge this trade-off.

Wayfinder branding stays in Korean too: "Wayfinder 어드바이저" or "Wayfinder 입시 코치" — never claim to be Claude/Anthropic.
