# Reed College — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Private LAC, ~1,500 undergrad, Portland OR
- Admit rate: ~33%
- Test policy: Test-blind permanently (unusual for a selective LAC — Reed will NOT consider scores even if submitted)
- Application: Common App + Reed supplements
- Early round: ED1 (Nov 15) + ED2 (Jan 1) — both binding
- Deadline: Nov 15 ED1 / Jan 1 ED2 + RD

## What admissions actually weights
Reed is institutionally distinct in five ways most applicants don't appreciate (and some get spectacularly wrong): (1) **The Reed Thesis** — every senior writes a year-long undergraduate thesis (Reed is one of very few colleges that requires this; far more rigorous than typical "senior project"). The thesis is academic-defining and shapes the entire Reed experience. (2) **No Greek life, no varsity athletics** — Reed deliberately rejects the "traditional college experience" archetype. The institutional identity is "intellectually intense, slightly anti-establishment, weird in a good way." (3) **Reed's "Reed Honor Principle"** governs the academic and social code — students don't have grades reported (you can request them, but they're not displayed by default), final exams are unproctored, integrity is genuinely central. (4) **Olde Reed culture** — the school cultivates intellectual playfulness + counterculture identity (Steve Jobs famously dropped out of Reed; calligraphy class reportedly influenced Mac typography). The Renn Fayre (year-end medieval-themed festival), thesis parade, and Paideia (week-long student-organized "anti-institutional" classes) are signature traditions. (5) **Test-BLIND policy** — Reed eliminated SAT/ACT consideration in 2020 and has not returned. AOs explicitly read for academic depth + intellectual character via essays and recommendations rather than test scores.

Strong programs: Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics (Reed has the highest per-capita PhD production rate for chemistry in the U.S.), Philosophy (signature program — Reed is genuinely strong here for a small school), English + Literature, Anthropology, Art (the working studios + nuclear reactor on campus — yes, Reed has its own research nuclear reactor, the only one at any U.S. undergraduate-only institution).

Reed's reputation as "the place where smart students who don't fit anywhere else end up" is partially earned — campus culture skews academically intense and deliberately unconventional. Applicants who don't research this will misfit.

Test scores: BLIND (will not be considered if submitted; do not include scores in application materials).

## Strategic notes
- Reed is genuinely test-blind — submitting scores is irrelevant; do not pay for a score report
- ED1 admit rate ~50%; ED2 admit rate ~40%; RD ~28% — ED is notable but Reed's base admit rate is higher than peer LACs because the applicant pool is smaller + more self-selecting
- Reed's student body is genuinely intellectually intense + countercultural — fit research is essential
- Greek life: NONE
- Varsity athletics: NONE (Reed has club sports + intramural only — unique among selective LACs)
- Portland OR location: Pacific Northwest urban setting, mild winters but rainy/gray, distinctive food/culture/music scene
- Distinctive features: Reed Reactor (research nuclear reactor since 1968, undergraduate-operated), Renn Fayre, the Reed Thesis tower
- Strong PhD pipeline (top-3 nationally for chemistry, top-10 for philosophy)
- Famous alumni: Steve Jobs (briefly), Larry Sanger (Wikipedia co-founder)

## Aid + scholarship
- Need-aware for U.S. applicants (NOT need-blind, unusual for a selective LAC)
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need for admitted students
- No-loan financial aid for families under ~$110K AGI
- Strong endowment per-student despite smaller size
- Barbara Hirschi Memorial Scholarship + various competitive merit
- Reed is one of few selective LACs that openly admits to being need-aware — applicants from low-income backgrounds should still apply but understand the distinction

## Recent essay shifts
Reed's "Why Reed" supplement is one of the most demanding LAC essays — AOs explicitly read for whether the applicant has done genuine research on Reed's distinctive culture (thesis, no varsity, no Greek, test-blind, intellectual intensity). Applicants who write generic "small LAC" essays score noticeably worse. Applicants who reference specific Reed traditions (Paideia, Olde Reed, the Reactor, the Thesis Parade) signal genuine fit. Reed actively discourages applicants who "want a normal college experience" — fit research matters more here than at almost any peer LAC.
