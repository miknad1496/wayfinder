# Ohio State University (Columbus) — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Public R1, ~47,000 undergrad (one of largest in U.S.), Columbus OH
- Admit rate: ~50% in-state, ~46% out-of-state (relatively similar — OSU is open to OOS applicants more than peer Big Ten flagships)
- Test policy: Test-optional through 2026-27
- Application: Common App + Ohio State supplements
- Early round: Early Action — non-binding (Nov 1)
- Deadline: Nov 1 EA / Feb 1 RD (priority deadline for scholarships is Nov 1 — apply early for merit aid)

## What admissions actually weights
Ohio State has three non-obvious mechanics that shape outcomes: (1) The Eminence Fellows + Morrill Scholars + Maximus Scholars programs are the elite merit pipelines — Eminence is particularly distinguished (full tuition + room/board + research/travel stipend, ~25 awards/year, separate competitive application). For top applicants, these merit awards make OSU compete with Ivy+ schools on financial fit. (2) Honors & Scholars Program is a single application choice that materially changes the OSU experience — Honors is for top-stats academic admits, Scholars is for thematic learning communities (e.g., International Affairs, Politics & Leadership, Health Sciences). Many applicants are competitive for both; choosing one over the other matters. (3) Direct admit to selective majors: Fisher College of Business + College of Engineering + College of Nursing all use direct freshman admit. Internal admit to those colleges later is much harder.

Strong programs: Fisher College of Business (top-15 public, particularly strong logistics + supply chain), College of Engineering (top-30 nationally, particularly Welding Engineering — only school in U.S. with an ABET-accredited Welding Engineering program), Nursing (top-20), Education + Human Ecology, Veterinary Medicine, Speech & Hearing Sciences (top-3 nationally — uniquely strong in audiology).

OSU "the" — the school's official name includes the article "The Ohio State University" (formally trademarked) — applicants who get this wrong in supplements stand out (negatively for legacies, neutrally for everyone else).

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1310-1450, ACT 28-32. Honors and Eminence admits cluster much higher (~1500+ SAT median for Eminence Fellows).

## Strategic notes
- APPLY BY NOV 1 for scholarship consideration — major scholarship programs cap at the EA deadline
- Eminence Fellows is the high-leverage path for top students (full tuition + room/board + stipend)
- Honors & Scholars: distinct programs, choose based on academic vs thematic interest
- Fisher Business + Engineering direct admit — competitive
- Buckeye athletic culture is genuinely central — Ohio Stadium ("The Horseshoe") seats 102,000+, football tickets are a meaningful part of student life
- Greek life ~12%, present but not dominant
- Columbus is a mid-large city (~900K) — distinctive from rural/college-town Big Ten peers
- Strong international student population, particularly Chinese + Indian
- Direct path to Ohio Wesleyan + Denison + Kenyon for transfer pathways (not common but established)

## Aid + scholarship
- OH residents: in-state tuition ~$13K (low for a state flagship)
- OOS: ~$36K tuition, but OSU is unusually willing to offer OOS merit
- Eminence Fellows: full tuition + room/board + summer research/travel stipend (~25/year)
- Morrill Scholars: full tuition awards for diversity/leadership applicants (separate application)
- Maximus Scholars: top-tier merit + research + study abroad funding
- Provost Scholarship: lower-tier automatic merit for top OOS admits
- Pell + state grant stacking for OH residents

## Recent essay shifts
OSU supplements are short. The "Why Ohio State" + "Why this major" + "How will you contribute" trio rewards specificity. Applicants who name the Eminence Fellows program, specific Fisher tracks, specific Engineering departments score noticeably better than applicants who write about football culture or "great Big Ten school" generically. AOs explicitly read for whether the applicant did genuine research vs. applied as a backup.
