# University of Pennsylvania (Penn) — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

> Wayfinder advisory file. Verify against current Penn admissions site and Common Data Set before relying on production answers.

## Snapshot
- Type: Private R1 Ivy League, ~10,500 undergrad enrollment across 4 undergraduate schools
- Most recent admit rate: ~5.7-6.5% overall; varies by school (Wharton most competitive)
- Test policy: Test-optional through 2026-27 cycle (announced policy continuation)
- Application: Common App or Coalition; Penn-specific supplements
- Early round: Early Decision (ED) — BINDING. Deadline Nov 1
- RD deadline: ~Jan 5

## What admissions actually weights
Penn evaluates by SCHOOL, not university-wide. The four undergraduate schools have distinct admissions:
- College of Arts and Sciences: ~7-8% admit rate, broad academic profile
- Wharton: ~3-4% admit rate, the most competitive Penn school, business + quantitative depth
- School of Engineering and Applied Science: ~6-7%, STEM rigor
- School of Nursing: ~10-12%, healthcare orientation

Penn explicitly looks for "purposeful engagement" — students who pursue specific interests with depth and articulate intentions. The "Why Penn?" essay is one of the highest-leverage in elite admissions: vague Penn essays get downgraded heavily. Specific program references (research centers, courses, faculty, clubs) signal real research.

Penn's admit profile is highly correlated with the major/program selected. A student applying to Wharton needs different positioning than one applying to the College — the supplements should reflect this.

Test scores (when submitted): median admit middle-50 ~1500-1560 SAT, 33-35 ACT.

## Recent essay shifts (2024-2026)
Penn's supplements are stable. The "thank you" essay added in 2023-24 (write a thank-you note to someone who has had an impact on you) has been generative — gives applicants room to show character and gratitude in a non-resume frame.

## Post-SFFA stance
Penn issued a measured statement after SFFA committing to diverse class within legal constraints. Penn has historically had strong representation across demographics; their post-SFFA approach has been to expand context-sensitive reading and explicit messaging about not using race proxies.

## Demonstrated interest
Penn DOES consider demonstrated interest, more than most Ivies. Visiting (where feasible), info sessions, email engagement, and ED commitment all factor in. ED commitment is a significant signal — Penn's ED admit rate is meaningfully higher than RD.

## Strategic notes for applicants
- ED at Penn is binding and signals maximum interest. The unhooked ED admit lift is real (~1.5-2x vs RD for COMPARABLE applicants, accounting for hook composition)
- The school-of-application choice matters strategically. Wharton transfers in are difficult — go directly if Wharton is the goal
- Penn's "purposeful engagement" framing rewards applicants with a clear narrative arc about what they want to study and why. Generic "well-rounded" applicants are at a slight disadvantage here vs schools like Yale that read more holistically
- Coordinated dual-degree programs (Huntsman, M&T, LSM, NETS) are separate ultra-competitive admissions tracks — apply directly if interested, can't transfer in

## Aid + scholarship policy
- Need-blind for U.S. citizens, permanent residents, and Mexican / Canadian citizens
- Need-aware for other international applicants
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need without loans for all admitted students
- Families under ~$75K pay nothing
- Limited merit aid (only Mayor's Scholarships, named scholarships through specific schools)
- QuestBridge partner
