# California Institute of Technology (Caltech) — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

> Wayfinder advisory file. Verify against current Caltech admissions site and Common Data Set before relying on production answers.

## Snapshot
- Type: Private R1, ~1,000 undergrad enrollment (smallest top STEM school)
- Most recent admit rate: ~3-4% (Class of 2027 cycle)
- Test policy: Test-blind through 2025; REINSTATING required for 2026-27 application cycle per their published policy
- Application: Caltech-specific application via Common App; STEM-focused supplements
- Early round: Restrictive Early Action (REA) — non-binding, restrictive
- RD deadline: ~Jan 3

## What admissions actually weights
Caltech is the most academically narrow of the top STEM schools. They publicly state they admit students who will thrive in a community of people who think deeply about hard problems. Quantitative/scientific depth is overwhelmingly the primary signal. Caltech doesn't try to build a "well-rounded class" — they build a class of focused researchers, mathematicians, and engineers.

Math depth is the differentiator. Successful applicants typically have:
- Multivariable calculus and beyond by senior year
- AIME / USAMO performance, or equivalent demonstrated math depth
- Research experience (often co-authored publications)
- Olympiad-level performance in physics, chemistry, biology, or computer science

Caltech's small size means class composition is very deliberate. They publicly say they're not chasing diversity of academic interest — students have to want what Caltech offers (intense quantitative immersion in a small community).

Test scores (when applicable): historically median middle-50 ~1530-1580 SAT, 35-36 ACT. Math section essentially needs to be perfect.

## Recent essay shifts (2024-2026)
Caltech's supplements emphasize STEM identity. The "what excites you intellectually" essay has been generative. Caltech AOs are explicit that they want evidence of self-directed engagement with hard problems — building things, doing research, pursuing math/science questions because of genuine fascination.

The 2026-27 reinstatement of required testing was framed by Caltech as restoring a key signal of math/science readiness. Their announcement specifically noted that test scores helped identify capable students from under-resourced schools where strong scores were diagnostic.

## Post-SFFA stance
Caltech's post-SFFA approach has been quieter than peers because their applicant pool is already self-selected for STEM depth. They committed to context-sensitive reading and have not made major demographic-targeted shifts in evaluation.

## Demonstrated interest
Caltech does NOT formally consider demonstrated interest. The applicant pool is small and self-selected — visits don't add diagnostic signal.

## Strategic notes for applicants
- Caltech is wrong for students who want a broad college experience. The community is intensely STEM-focused, the social structure is house-based, the workload is famously brutal. Self-knowledge matters
- REA is non-binding so financial commitment preserved. But restrictive — can't apply ED elsewhere
- Demonstrated quantitative depth is non-negotiable. AIME 12+ / USAMO qualifier / published research is closer to baseline than differentiator at Caltech
- Recruited athletes are very rare (Division III, no athletic scholarships); athletic profile is essentially irrelevant
- Caltech and MIT are siblings but with real cultural differences — Caltech smaller, more research-immersive, more theoretical; MIT larger, more applied/maker-focused

## Aid + scholarship policy
- Need-blind for all applicants (one of few schools, including international)
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need
- Loan-free for families under ~$110K income
- No merit-only scholarships (very rare exception: presidential scholarships)
- Total cost ~$84K; average aid recipient pays substantially less
