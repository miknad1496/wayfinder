# University of Wisconsin-Madison — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Public R1, ~36,000 undergrad, Madison WI
- Admit rate: ~50% in-state, ~38% out-of-state (gap is real but smaller than at peer Big Ten publics like Michigan/UVA)
- Test policy: Test-optional through 2026-27
- Application: Common App + UW-Madison supplements
- Early round: Early Action (non-binding, non-restrictive) — Nov 1 deadline; UW-Madison rolls admits starting late January
- Deadline: Nov 1 EA / Feb 1 RD (no Nov 1 advantage in admit odds — EA is purely an early-decision-notification convenience)

## What admissions actually weights
Wisconsin is institutionally distinct in two ways most applicants miss: (1) The "Wisconsin Idea" — the formal commitment that the university's boundaries are the state's boundaries, meaning AOs read for civic engagement and applied-research interest, not just academic credentials. Applicants who articulate how they'd use Wisconsin's research scale to address a real-world problem (Wisconsin's land-grant tradition, agricultural extension network, public-policy orientation) score better than pure-stats applicants. (2) Wisconsin School of Business uses a "Direct Admit Honors" program for top freshman applicants AND a sophomore-year admit pathway — sophomore-year admit GPA cut is brutal (~3.6+ in pre-reqs), so direct admit is the much safer route.

Top programs: Sociology (perennial #1 nationally), Business (Wisconsin School of Business — strong in finance and real estate), Engineering (Engineering Physics, Industrial), Agricultural & Life Sciences (CALS — separate admit), Communication Arts.

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1370-1490, ACT 28-32. Engineering and Business admits cluster at the top.

## Strategic notes
- Wisconsin School of Business direct admit is the right path — sophomore admit is much harder
- CALS (College of Agricultural and Life Sciences) is SEPARATE from L&S and has its own admit profile — easier than direct-CS or Business but still competitive
- Engineering direct admit competitive; engineering "exploring" pathway exists for undeclared
- Big Ten athletics: football, basketball, hockey (men's hockey is national-program tier) — recruited athletes get real edge
- Greek life ~14%, present but not central
- Madison weather is harsh (~30 below F windchills in January) — real fit factor for OOS applicants
- Madison itself is consistently ranked one of America's best college towns: walkable, food/music scene, Capitol/lakes

## Aid + scholarship
- WI residents: Bucky's Tuition Promise (full tuition + fees for families under ~$65K)
- WI Promise covers Pell-eligible students
- OOS: limited institutional need aid — UW-Madison is need-aware for OOS
- Chancellor's Scholarship + various school-specific merit awards (Engineering, Business)
- Posse Foundation partnership for Posse Scholars

## Recent essay shifts
The "Why UW-Madison" supplement weights heavily — AOs explicitly read for whether an applicant connects to the Wisconsin Idea (civic engagement, applied research) vs. just listing campus features. Applicants who write about Wisconsin Public Radio internships, the Morgridge Institute, Wisconsin Idea Project, or specific civic-action examples score noticeably higher than applicants who write about football games or lake views.
