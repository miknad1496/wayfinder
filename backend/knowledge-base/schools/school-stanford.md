## Snapshot
- Type: Private R1, ~7,500 undergrad enrollment
- Most recent admit rate: not publicly disclosed since Class of 2023 cycle (last published was ~3.95%); current rate widely estimated under 4% but Stanford has stopped releasing the figure
- Test policy: test-optional through 2026-27 cycle per public announcement; SAT/ACT scores accepted but not required
- Application: Common App or Coalition Application; Stanford-specific supplemental essays
- Early round: Restrictive Early Action (REA), non-binding, deadline ~Nov 1
- RD deadline: ~Jan 5

## What admissions actually weights
Stanford's stated philosophy emphasizes "intellectual vitality" and
authentic engagement over polished credentials. Per the admissions blog
and Dean Richard Shaw's public talks (now Dean Thiessen-era), they look
for evidence of how an applicant engages with ideas, communities, and
problems — not just achievement count. The supplemental essays carry
unusual weight here vs peers; the "What matters to you and why" prompt
has been a consistent diagnostic since the early 2010s.

Class rigor and curriculum context matter heavily — the AO reads the
school profile alongside the transcript. Strong candidates from
under-resourced schools who pursued every available rigor are read
favorably. Standardized test scores, when submitted, are interpreted
within the school context (median admit middle-50 SAT historically
~1500-1570, ACT ~34-35).

## Recent essay shifts (2024-2026)
The 2024-25 supplements retained the "What matters to you and why"
short-essay style but the AO team has publicly emphasized that overly
polished/manufactured-feeling responses register negatively. Several
admissions blog posts in 2023-24 explicitly called out essays that
"sound like they were rehearsed for years."

The roommate letter prompt remains. The shorter "five things you would
want your future roommate to know" continues to function as a voice
diagnostic — AOs say they can usually tell within 2 sentences whether
the writer sounds like a real teenager.

## Post-SFFA stance
Stanford issued a measured statement after the June 2023 SFFA decision
indicating commitment to building a diverse class within legal
constraints, with adversity essays now serving as the primary channel
for applicants to surface identity-shaping experiences. The 2023-24
cycle saw added supplemental room (in the existing essay set) for
applicants to discuss community/identity context if they chose.

## Demonstrated interest
Stanford does NOT consider demonstrated interest in admissions
decisions. Visiting campus, attending info sessions, and tracking
emails carry zero weight. Applicants from outside California are
not disadvantaged by inability to visit.

## Strategic notes for applicants
- The supplements are the make-or-break differentiator. Polish
  alone won't carry — the AO is looking for genuine voice + a clear
  sense of what the applicant cares about.
- "Spike vs well-rounded" debate: Stanford's class profiles show
  both, but the AOs publicly favor depth + commitment over breadth.
- Athletic recruiting is significant; ~12% of admitted class.
- The Knight-Hennessy Scholars program (graduate level) is a separate
  pathway and shouldn't be confused with undergrad strategy.

## Aid + scholarship policy
- Need-blind for U.S. citizens and permanent residents
- Need-aware for international applicants (recently increased
  international financial aid)
- 100% of demonstrated need met without loans for families under
  ~$75K income (no parental contribution); reduced contribution
  scaling up
- No merit-only scholarships at undergrad level
- QuestBridge partner since 2007
