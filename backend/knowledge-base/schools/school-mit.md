# MIT — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

> Wayfinder advisory file. Verify against current MIT admissions blog and Common Data Set before relying on production answers.

## Snapshot
- Type: Private R1, ~4,600 undergrad enrollment
- Most recent admit rate: ~4.5-5% (Class of 2027 cycle, MIT publishes)
- Test policy: SAT/ACT REQUIRED — never went test-optional permanently; reinstated requirement Spring 2022
- Application: MIT-specific application (not Common App), 5-6 short essays
- Early round: Early Action (EA) — non-binding, non-restrictive. Deadline ~Nov 1, results ~mid-Dec
- RD deadline: ~Jan 1

## What admissions actually weights
MIT explicitly publishes its evaluation framework. Five factors carry weight: (1) academic preparation, (2) match with MIT's mission/culture, (3) collaborative spirit, (4) initiative, (5) risk-taking with maker mindset. The phrasing "the world the MIT community will create" is a recurring theme in their admissions blog — they want builders who will improve the world, not just credentialed achievers.

Math/science rigor is non-negotiable but not sufficient. MIT publicly says they reject thousands of valedictorians each year. The differentiating signal is evidence of engagement with hard problems through hands-on work — research, projects, robotics, code published, things built. The "tinkered with X" or "broke and fixed Y" stories land disproportionately well.

Test scores: median admit middle-50 is roughly 1530-1570 SAT, 35-36 ACT. Below 1500 SAT is genuinely uphill at MIT.

## Recent essay shifts (2024-2026)
MIT's short essays have remained stable. Notable: the "describe an environment in which you've challenged your beliefs" prompt added in 2024-25 cycle. The "what you do for fun" essay continues to be the highest-leverage diagnostic — MIT explicitly says they want to see authentic personality, not curated achievements.

## Post-SFFA stance
MIT made a notable public statement after the SFFA decision committing to building a diverse class within legal constraints. They expanded their adversity-essay options. A specific shift: explicit messaging that students from under-resourced contexts who've worked with whatever tools were available should highlight that — they read profile in context.

## Demonstrated interest
MIT does NOT consider demonstrated interest. Visiting, info sessions, email frequency carry zero weight in admissions decisions.

## Strategic notes for applicants
- The "Maker portfolio" is real — for applicants with engineering/CS depth, an optional Maker portfolio submission gives concrete evidence and is read seriously
- MIT EA (non-restrictive) is genuinely strategic — doesn't lock you out of REA elsewhere except Harvard/Stanford/Yale/Princeton REA which forbid simultaneous EA at private schools (verify per school)
- Athletic recruiting is small but real (~5-7% of admits)
- MIT's "match" focus means generic prestige-chasing essays read as anti-fit. Concrete evidence of why MIT specifically is essential

## Aid + scholarship policy
- Need-blind for all applicants including international (one of few schools)
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need without loans for families under ~$100K
- Reduced parental contribution scaling up
- No merit-only scholarships at undergrad
- Tuition + fees + room/board total ~$87K, but average aid recipient pays ~$22K
