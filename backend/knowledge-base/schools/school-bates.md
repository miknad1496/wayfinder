# Bates College — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Private LAC, ~1,800 undergrad, Lewiston ME (130 miles north of Boston, 30 miles north of Portland ME)
- Admit rate: ~13-15%
- Test policy: Test-optional since 1984 (Bates was the FIRST selective U.S. college to make tests optional — predates the COVID-era wave by decades)
- Application: Common App + Bates supplements
- Early round: ED1 (Nov 15) + ED2 (Jan 1) — both binding
- Deadline: Nov 15 ED1 / Jan 1 ED2 / Jan 1 RD

## What admissions actually weights
Bates is institutionally distinct in four ways: (1) **First selective LAC to go test-optional** — Bates made the change in 1984 based on its own internal research showing test scores didn't predict student success. The institutional culture has spent 40+ years actually MEANING test-optional, not just performing it. AOs read essays + transcripts + recs as primary admit factors. (2) **Short Term**: A signature 5-week intensive between fall and spring semesters where students take ONE course, often experiential (research projects, off-campus study, internships, faculty-led travel). Short Term is academic-defining — the equivalent of Colby's Jan Plan, but distinct in pacing. (3) **NESCAC athletic conference identity** — D3, peer with Williams/Amherst/Bowdoin/Colby/Middlebury. Recruited athletes get real edge. (4) **Founded 1855 as a free college for descendants of veterans + abolitionists** — institutional identity is socially-engaged. The Bates motto "Amore ac studio" (with love and devotion) reflects this.

Strong programs: Politics (signature program), Economics, English (strong creative writing), Environmental Studies, Biology, Chemistry, Geology + Earth Science (Maine geological context is rich), Mathematics, Religious Studies (signature small-LAC program), Anthropology, French (Bates has historic French + francophone strengths).

Bates' "Maine LAC" identity — outdoorsy, NESCAC-traditional, intellectually serious — shapes culture. The Bates Outing Club is highly active.

## Strategic notes
- ED1 admit rate ~35-40%; ED2 admit rate ~25%; RD ~12% — ED is meaningful leverage
- Test-optional was Bates' invention — they take it seriously; do not feel compelled to submit weak scores
- Greek life: NONE (Bates abolished Greek system in 1880s)
- Lewiston ME: small post-industrial town with a strong Somali-American community (immigration story is part of Bates context)
- Notable alumni: Bryan Cranston attended briefly, Jorma Kaukonen (Jefferson Airplane), Jonathan Lethem, Ben Franklin (no, just kidding — but multiple US Senators)
- Distinctive features: Mount David (the campus hill), the Bates Mill complex, Coram Library, Bates Dance Festival (summer program)
- D3 athletics in NESCAC (peer with Williams/Amherst/Bowdoin/Middlebury/Colby/Wesleyan/Tufts/Trinity/CMS)
- 60% of students study abroad (one of highest rates among LACs)

## Aid + scholarship
- Need-blind for U.S. applicants
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need
- No-loan financial aid for families under ~$160K AGI
- Capped student loans for higher-income aid recipients
- Strong endowment per-student
- Hopkins Scholar + various competitive merit awards

## Recent essay shifts
Bates' "Why Bates" supplement rewards applicants who engage with the test-optional history + Short Term + NESCAC athletic + Maine outdoor culture. Generic "small LAC" essays underperform versus essays that reference Bates' first-mover status on testing, Short Term-specific projects the applicant would pursue, or the Mount David tradition.
