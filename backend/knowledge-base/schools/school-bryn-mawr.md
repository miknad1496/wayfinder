# Bryn Mawr College — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Private LAC, Seven Sisters women's college (admits women + nonbinary applicants), ~1,400 undergrad, Bryn Mawr PA (10 miles west of Philadelphia)
- Admit rate: ~32%
- Test policy: Test-optional permanently
- Application: Common App + Bryn Mawr supplements
- Early round: ED1 (Nov 15) + ED2 (Jan 1) — both binding
- Deadline: Nov 15 ED1 / Jan 1 ED2 + RD

## What admissions actually weights
Bryn Mawr is institutionally distinct in four ways: (1) **Tri-College Consortium** with Haverford and Swarthmore: full course cross-registration, shared library systems, dining privileges, campus shuttle running ~30 minutes between schools. This effectively gives Bryn Mawr students access to two more LAC campuses with no extra cost or admit barrier. (2) **First U.S. women's college to grant a PhD** (1888) — the Bryn Mawr PhD program is still active and one of few graduate programs at a women's college. The intellectual seriousness flows down to undergrads — Bryn Mawr is academically intense in a way that resembles a small research university more than a typical LAC. (3) **Honor Code is genuinely central**: Bryn Mawr's Honor Code is older than Davidson's (dates to 1892) and governs both academic AND social conduct. Students self-schedule final exams unproctored, take-home exams are common, the Self-Government Association governs all student life. (4) **Strong feeder pipeline to graduate school**: Bryn Mawr graduates pursue PhDs at higher per-capita rates than any other women's college and most coed LACs — particularly in classics, archaeology, and the sciences.

Strong programs: Classical and Near Eastern Archaeology (signature program — Bryn Mawr is one of very few undergraduate archaeology programs in the country), Biology, Chemistry, Mathematics, Greek + Latin (rigorous classics tradition), Italian, German, Art History (Bryn Mawr Special Collections includes major rare-book holdings), Geology.

The "Mawrtyr" identity (yes, that's the demonym) — intellectually serious, tradition-conscious, often quirky — shapes campus culture. The Lantern Night ceremony + Hell Week + May Day traditions are distinctive (and 100+ years old).

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1370-1500, ACT 31-33. Submission rate ~45-50% for admits.

## Strategic notes
- ED1 admit rate ~50-55%; ED2 admit rate ~40%; RD ~28% — ED is significant leverage
- Tri-College access: Haverford + Swarthmore courses are fully open
- Bi-College program with Haverford goes even deeper (shared majors, joint faculty, integrated registrar)
- Greek life: NONE (Seven Sisters)
- Bryn Mawr campus architecture is genuinely distinctive — Cope and Stewardson Collegiate Gothic, M. Carey Thomas Library, the Cloisters
- Walking distance to Bryn Mawr station (SEPTA Paoli/Thorndale Line, 25 min to downtown Philadelphia)
- Strong alumnae network in academia, sciences, public service (Katharine Hepburn, Hanna Holborn Gray attended)
- "Customs Week" (orientation) builds institutional identity from day 1

## Aid + scholarship
- Need-blind for U.S. applicants
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need
- No-loan policy for families under ~$60K AGI
- Capped loans for higher-income aid recipients
- Strong endowment per-student
- Hepburn Scholars + various competitive merit awards
- Posse Foundation partnership

## Recent essay shifts
Bryn Mawr's "Why Bryn Mawr" supplement rewards applicants who engage with the academic intensity, the Tri-College structure, and the Honor Code as INSTITUTIONAL features (not just "I want a small school"). Post-SFFA, the women's-college framing is once again strategically meaningful — applicants who articulate why a women-centered education matters score better than those who treat the gender-identity aspect as a secondary feature.
