# Haverford College — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Private LAC, ~1,400 undergrad, Haverford PA (Tri-College Consortium with Bryn Mawr + Swarthmore; 10 miles west of Philadelphia)
- Admit rate: ~13-14%
- Test policy: Test-optional permanently
- Application: Common App + Haverford supplements
- Early round: ED1 (Nov 15) + ED2 (Jan 1) — both binding
- Deadline: Nov 15 ED1 / Jan 1 ED2 + RD

## What admissions actually weights
Haverford is institutionally distinct in four ways: (1) **Quaker heritage** — founded 1833 by Philadelphia Quakers. While not religiously affiliated today, the Quaker values (consensus decision-making, social responsibility, intellectual honesty) shape institutional culture. (2) **Honor Code is genuinely the most stringent of any selective LAC** — Haverford's Honor Code is in some ways MORE comprehensive than Davidson's or Bryn Mawr's. It governs academic AND social conduct, requires self-reporting, requires mutual accountability. Final exams unproctored, take-home exams common, the Honor Council handles violations as student-run. The Honor Code has been refined and reaffirmed by students since 1896. (3) **Bi-College + Tri-College structure**: The Bi-College program with Bryn Mawr is genuinely deep (shared majors, faculty teaching at both, fully integrated registrar — students cross-register seamlessly). The broader Tri-College adds Swarthmore. (4) **No fraternities + no varsity football** — Haverford abolished varsity football in 1972 and Greek life predates that — these are deliberate institutional choices reflecting Quaker simplicity.

Strong programs: Biology, Mathematics, Computer Science (rigorous small program), Physics, Chemistry, English, History, Religion (signature program — Haverford's Quaker tradition makes religion + philosophy a core strength), Economics, Political Science, Anthropology, Music.

Haverford's "Customs Week" (orientation) builds Honor Code engagement from day 1. The "Haverfest" + "Customs Week" + ResidenceHall traditions are distinctive.

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1430-1530, ACT 32-34. Submission rate ~45-50% for admits.

## Strategic notes
- ED1 admit rate ~40-45%; ED2 admit rate ~30%; RD ~12% — ED is meaningful leverage
- Bi-College with Bryn Mawr: full course access + integrated majors with seamless registrar
- Tri-College adds Swarthmore (40-min drive, less integrated than Bi-College but courses cross-listable)
- Greek life: NONE (abolished decades ago, no current presence)
- Athletics: D3 in CC (Centennial Conference); Quaker simplicity = no varsity football
- Notable alumni: Walter Brown (founder of Brookings Institution), Dave Barry, Joshua Bell briefly
- Honor Code is the highest-leverage essay topic
- Distinctive features: Founders Hall, the Duck Pond, the Cricket Pitch (Haverford has a cricket pitch — first college cricket team in the U.S.)

## Aid + scholarship
- Need-blind for U.S. applicants
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need
- No-loan policy for low-income aid recipients
- Strong endowment per-student
- Customs Scholars + various competitive merit awards

## Recent essay shifts
Haverford's Honor Code essay is the highest-leverage prompt. AOs read for whether the applicant takes institutional honor codes seriously — generic "I value integrity" responses fail. Applicants who articulate why an institutional honor code matters in 21st-century college culture, who reference specific Haverford features (Quaker heritage, Bi-College access, Customs Week), and who signal collaborative + intellectually honest character score noticeably better.
