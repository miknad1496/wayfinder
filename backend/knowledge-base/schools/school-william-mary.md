# College of William & Mary — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Public R1 (officially) but operates like an elite LAC, ~6,500 undergrad, Williamsburg VA
- Admit rate: ~33% in-state, ~36% out-of-state
- Test policy: Test-optional through 2026-27
- Application: Common App + W&M supplements
- Early round: BOTH Early Decision (binding) AND Early Action (non-binding) — Nov 1 for both
- Deadline: Nov 1 ED/EA / Jan 1 RD

## What admissions actually weights
William & Mary is institutionally peculiar in three ways most applicants miss: (1) Founded 1693 — second-oldest college in the U.S. The institutional identity is genuinely traditional in a way most "old" schools aren't anymore — Honor Code, traditions like the Yule Log and Convocation walking under the Wren Building, small-class culture all feel like an LAC despite the public-university designation. (2) ED admit rate is meaningfully higher than RD (~40-45% ED vs ~33% RD). For applicants for whom W&M is clear top choice, ED is high-leverage. (3) The Mason School of Business uses a junior-year internal-admit model (not direct freshman admit) — applicants enter as undeclared liberal arts and apply to Business after sophomore year. The internal admit cut is competitive (~3.4 GPA in Business pre-reqs).

Strong programs: Government (W&M is feeder to State Department, intelligence community via DC proximity), International Relations, Public Policy, Economics, English, Classics, History (W&M was founded with Classics as core, retains strength). Pre-law pipeline strong (W&M Law School separate, but undergrad-to-law-school placement excellent).

W&M's "TWAMP" (Tribe Will Ask More Permission — informal student term for the rigorous workload culture) reputation is real. Heavy reading load, demanding writing requirements across the curriculum.

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1380-1510, ACT 30-34. ED admits cluster slightly lower than RD admits (typical for binding-ED schools).

## Strategic notes
- ED + EA both available — ED bump is real and significant; EA is a soft-yes signal but doesn't materially change odds
- Honor Code is genuine cultural feature — applicants who reference it authentically (not generically) score better
- Mason School of Business: internal admit junior year (not direct freshman); plan accordingly
- W&M and JMU/UVA-Tech rivalries are real — applicants from elsewhere in VA know what these mean
- Greek life ~30% — significant but not dominant; many strong students are unaffiliated
- Williamsburg is a small colonial town — the historic district and Colonial Williamsburg surround campus, which is charming for some applicants and "too quiet" for others (real fit factor)
- D1 athletics (Tribe) but in CAA Conference — mid-major presence, not power conference

## Aid + scholarship
- VA residents: meets full demonstrated need
- OOS: meets demonstrated need (W&M is need-aware for OOS)
- 1693 Scholars Program — full tuition + summer stipend + research access, ~10 awards/year, separate competitive application
- Murray Scholarship — full tuition for top in-state students
- Stamps Scholarship at W&M — full-ride + stipend
- Monroe Scholars — automatic for top admits, includes $3K research stipend across 4 years

## Recent essay shifts
W&M's "Why W&M" supplement explicitly asks applicants to engage with specific traditions, programs, or aspects of W&M culture. Generic "great academics" essays score noticeably worse than essays that reference specific traditions (Yule Log, Honor Code, the Wren Building, the Crim Dell legend, the W&M Bowl). Honor System engagement in particular is read for character — applicants who articulate why an institutional honor code matters score well.
