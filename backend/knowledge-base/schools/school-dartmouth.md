# Dartmouth College — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

> Wayfinder advisory file. Verify against current Dartmouth admissions site and Common Data Set before relying on production answers.

## Snapshot
- Type: Private R1 Ivy League, ~4,500 undergrad enrollment (smallest Ivy)
- Most recent admit rate: ~6.0-6.5% (Class of 2027 cycle)
- Test policy: REQUIRED — Dartmouth was first Ivy to reinstate, announcement Feb 2024 for 2025-26 cycle
- Application: Common App or Coalition; Dartmouth-specific supplements
- Early round: Early Decision (ED) — BINDING. Deadline Nov 1
- RD deadline: ~Jan 3

## What admissions actually weights
Dartmouth's smaller size and rural setting mean admissions reads heavily for "Dartmouth fit" — students who genuinely want a tight-knit, outdoorsy, intense-academic + intense-social environment in Hanover. The "D-Plan" (Dartmouth's quarter system with required summer term as sophomore) is a distinctive structural element students need to know about and like.

Test scores: median admit middle-50 SAT 1500-1560, ACT 33-35.

The Dartmouth philosophy emphasizes the "engaged generalist" — undergraduate teaching focus (faculty primarily teach undergrads, very rare grad TAs), small class sizes, strong residential community. Students who want the research-university breadth of Penn/Columbia/Cornell are often less happy at Dartmouth.

## Recent essay shifts (2024-2026)
Dartmouth's supplements include the famous "Why Dartmouth?" essay. AOs explicitly test for specific knowledge: D-Plan, residential life, specific academic programs, the outdoor culture. Generic "I love snow" or "small class size" answers read as superficial.

A 2024-25 supplement asked applicants about a meaningful relationship — answers that show specific reciprocity (what you give + what you receive) land best.

## Post-SFFA stance
Dartmouth's post-SFFA approach included expanded adversity-essay options and explicit messaging about context-sensitive reading.

## Demonstrated interest
Dartmouth does NOT formally track demonstrated interest, but ED commitment is a strong signal AND visiting Hanover (where feasible) is read as genuine fit-research given Hanover's relative isolation.

## Strategic notes for applicants
- ED at Dartmouth is binding. Unhooked ED admit lift is meaningful (~1.7-1.9x vs RD for comparable applicants — among the strongest Ivy ED bumps)
- The D-Plan: students take 3 of 4 quarters per year + are required to take summer term sophomore year. Plan for off-terms (when most students do internships, research, study abroad). Significant lifestyle implication
- Athletic recruiting ~25% of admits — among the highest in Ivy, due to small class size + strong DI athletics
- The outdoor + community culture is real — students who don't engage with it (Greek life ~60% of campus, Dartmouth Outing Club, etc.) often feel isolated

## Aid + scholarship policy
- Need-blind for U.S. citizens and permanent residents
- Need-blind for international applicants (since 2022 — second Ivy to go fully need-blind for international after Princeton)
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need
- Families under $125K income: zero parent contribution
- Loan-free for families under $125K; reduced loans above
- No merit-only scholarships at undergrad
- QuestBridge partner
