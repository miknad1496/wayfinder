# Davidson College — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Private LAC, ~2,000 undergrad, Davidson NC (20 miles north of Charlotte, on Lake Norman)
- Admit rate: ~17%
- Test policy: Test-optional permanently
- Application: Common App + Davidson supplements
- Early round: ED1 (Nov 15) + ED2 (Jan 2) — both binding
- Deadline: Nov 15 ED1 / Jan 2 ED2 / Jan 2 RD

## What admissions actually weights
Davidson is institutionally distinct in four ways most applicants don't appreciate: (1) **The Davidson Trust** (since 2007): Davidson was the first U.S. college to eliminate loans from financial aid packages entirely — every dollar of demonstrated need is met with grants and work-study, no loans. This makes Davidson uniquely affordable for middle-income families compared to peer LACs. (2) **Honor Code** is genuinely central: Davidson's honor code dates to 1837 and is one of the most stringent in U.S. higher education. Students self-schedule final exams (any time during exam week, in any room, unproctored), professors leave the classroom during in-class exams, dorm rooms are unlocked. AOs read Honor Code essays for whether the applicant takes this seriously — generic responses fail. (3) **Presbyterian heritage** (founded 1837): Davidson is officially affiliated with the Presbyterian Church (USA) and has religious-life infrastructure (chaplain, campus ministries) that's unusual for a top LAC. Students aren't required to be Presbyterian, but the religious heritage shapes the institutional culture (community service, mission orientation). (4) **D1 athletics in a LAC body**: Davidson is one of the few selective LACs in NCAA Division I (Atlantic 10 conference) — basketball especially (Stephen Curry led Davidson to the 2008 Elite Eight). For athlete-applicants, this is meaningfully different from D3 LAC peers.

Strong programs: Economics, Political Science, Pre-med pipeline (Davidson sends 80%+ of pre-meds to medical school — highest yield rate of any LAC), Biology + Chemistry, English, History, Mathematics, International Studies (Davidson is unusually well-connected in DC + diplomatic corps for a southern LAC).

The "Davidson type" — Honor Code-serious, athletic, southern + traditionalist + pre-professional — is a distinctive cultural fit that AOs explicitly read for.

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1370-1500, ACT 31-33. Submission rate ~50% for admits.

## Strategic notes
- ED1 admit rate ~38-42%; ED2 admit rate ~25%; RD ~13-15% — ED is meaningful leverage
- Honor Code engagement is the highest-leverage essay topic
- Greek life: ~38% (very high for a selective LAC — Greek system is socially central, similar to Hamilton)
- D1 basketball is a real campus rallying point
- Charlotte proximity: 20 miles to one of the fastest-growing southern metros, banking hub for Bank of America + Wells Fargo (strong finance internship pipeline)
- Pre-med pipeline is the strongest of any LAC (med school placement rate ~95%)
- Strong religious life infrastructure (Presbyterian heritage)
- Lake Norman recreational access (sailing, paddling, lakefront)
- Distinctive traditions: Cake Race, Frolics, Hummin' Bird (the campus newspaper), the C Shop ice cream

## Aid + scholarship
- Need-blind for U.S. applicants
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need
- DAVIDSON TRUST: NO LOANS in any financial aid package (industry-leading; Davidson was first)
- Strong endowment ($900M+, large for a school this size)
- John M. Belk Scholarship — full-tuition merit, ~10 awards/year, separate competitive application
- Bonner Scholars (community-service based merit)
- Athletic scholarships available (D1 athletics — unusual for a selective LAC)

## Recent essay shifts
Davidson's "Why Davidson" + Honor Code essays both reward authentic engagement. The Honor Code essay specifically: applicants who write generic "I value integrity" responses lose; applicants who articulate why an institutional honor code matters in 21st-century college culture (and what they'd contribute to / gain from it) score noticeably better. The Presbyterian heritage doesn't have to be aligned with applicant religion — but ignoring the institutional context entirely reads as poor fit research.
