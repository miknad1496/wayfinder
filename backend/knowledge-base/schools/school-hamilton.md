# Hamilton College — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Private LAC, ~2,000 undergrad, Clinton NY (Mohawk Valley, 45 min east of Syracuse, 4 hrs from NYC)
- Admit rate: ~12-14%
- Test policy: Test-optional permanently
- Application: Common App + Hamilton supplements
- Early round: ED1 (Nov 1) + ED2 (Jan 1) — both binding
- Deadline: Nov 1 ED1 / Jan 1 ED2 + RD

## What admissions actually weights
Hamilton is institutionally distinct in four ways: (1) **Open curriculum** (no general-education distribution requirements besides 1 writing-intensive course + 3 writing-intensive courses across the curriculum) — students design their own program. Combined with #2, this is the institutional identity. (2) **Writing intensity**: Hamilton was an early pioneer of "writing across the curriculum" and remains one of the most writing-focused LACs — the Nesbitt-Johnston Writing Center is central to the academic culture. Even STEM majors take writing-intensive courses. AOs read essays expecting writing maturity. (3) **The "Old Hamilton" + "New Hamilton" merger**: Hamilton merged with Kirkland College (women's college, founded 1968) in 1978 — the Kirkland legacy persists in some residential and academic distinctions, and the campus retains its "two-campus" feel (the Hamilton "stone campus" + the Kirkland "modern campus"). (4) Hamilton has the **highest faculty-student ratio in U.S. LACs** (1:8 historically), with no graduate students competing for faculty attention.

Strong programs: Government (top LAC government program — Hamilton is feeder to State Department + intelligence community + DC policy), History, English (writing-intensive curriculum lifts this), Economics, Public Policy, Mathematics + Statistics, Geosciences (signature small-LAC program with field-research access), Theater, Music.

Hamilton's "Continental Tradition" campus identity (named for Alexander Hamilton, who was an early supporter) shapes culture: traditionalist, emphasis on writing + speaking, pre-professional but rigorously liberal-arts.

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1430-1540, ACT 32-34. Submission rate ~50-60% for admits.

## Strategic notes
- ED1 admit rate ~35-40%; ED2 admit rate ~30%; RD ~10% — ED is critical leverage at Hamilton
- Hamilton has a notably traditional culture (preppy + East Coast oriented) compared to peer LACs like Vassar/Wesleyan
- Greek life: ~30% (notably high for a small LAC — Greek system is socially significant)
- Pre-law + government pipelines very strong (Hamilton + DC = real network)
- Cold/snowy upstate NY winters (Mohawk Valley gets significant lake-effect snow)
- Distinctive features: Hamilton's library collection includes 600,000+ volumes (large for a school of 2000), the Wellin Museum of Art, the Adirondack Adventure pre-orientation program
- Strong alumni network in finance, law, government

## Aid + scholarship
- Need-blind for U.S. applicants
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need
- No-loan policy for families under ~$125K (Hamilton replaces loans with grants)
- Capped student loans for higher-income aid recipients ($4-5K/year max)
- Strong endowment per-student (~$650K)

## Recent essay shifts
Hamilton's "Why Hamilton" supplement specifically asks how the open curriculum + writing emphasis fit the applicant's intellectual interests. AOs read for whether the applicant has actually thought about open curriculum (not all selective LACs have it — Brown, Amherst, Hamilton, Vassar do; Williams, Pomona, Swarthmore don't). Generic "small classes + great professors" essays underperform versus essays that reference specific writing-curriculum features or specific Hamilton departments.
