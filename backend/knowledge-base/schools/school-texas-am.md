# Texas A&M University (College Station) — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Public R1, ~57,000 undergrad (one of largest in U.S. — only UT Austin + UCF + ASU rival), College Station TX
- Admit rate: ~64% overall (TX residents see different mechanics — see below)
- Test policy: Test-optional through 2026-27 (Engineering + Mays Business effectively expect scores)
- Application: ApplyTexas (Texas-state portal) — Common App also accepted
- Early round: Early Action — non-binding (Oct 15 priority deadline)
- Deadline: Dec 1 RD (Oct 15 priority for Engineering + Mays + scholarships)

## What admissions actually weights
Texas A&M has three institutional features that drive admissions outcomes for TX residents in particular: (1) **Top 10% Auto-Admit Rule for TX residents**: Texas state law guarantees admission to TAMU for any TX high school student in the top 10% of their graduating class who applies by the priority deadline. This drives the ~63% in-state admit rate — for top-10% TX students, admit is automatic. For non-top-10% TX students AND OOS students, the actual admit profile is much more competitive. (2) **The Aggie Network is the single largest alumni association in the U.S.** (450,000+ members) — organized through "Aggie Rings" (a tradition since 1889), Aggie Muster, Aggie Bonfire (suspended after 1999 collapse but cultural memory persists), Reveille (the live collie mascot), and the Corps of Cadets. The cultural depth is genuine, not marketing. (3) **Corps of Cadets**: TAMU has the largest senior military college (ROTC) in the country outside service academies — ~2,500 students in the Corps, many become commissioned officers. Corps students live in distinct residential quads, follow military structure, and are a meaningful part of campus identity even for non-Corps students.

Strong programs: Mays Business School (top-25, particularly strong in supply chain, accounting), Engineering (College of Engineering — top-10 public, particularly Petroleum Engineering #1 nationally, Aerospace, Industrial), Veterinary Medicine (top-15 globally), Agricultural Sciences (signature land-grant strength), Architecture, Geosciences (especially geophysics, oil & gas adjacent).

The Aggie identity is genuinely central — "Howdy" greeting, "Whoop!" cheer, Aggie War Hymn, the Twelfth Man tradition, Silver Taps memorial. Applicants who don't engage with this tradition feel like outsiders.

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1190-1410, ACT 25-31. Engineering admits cluster ~1340-1500.

## Strategic notes
- TX top-10% automatic admit applies to TAMU + UT Austin (the two flagship publics)
- Apply by Oct 15 EA for scholarship consideration + engineering review
- Engineering: direct major admit, but General Engineering pathway available
- Corps of Cadets: separate residential commitment, scholarship pipeline, military officer pipeline
- Mays Business: direct freshman admit competitive
- Greek life ~13%, present but Aggie traditions overshadow Greek-system social structure
- College Station is a college town built around TAMU + Texas A&M Health Science Center
- Strong international student population, particularly Asian + Latin American

## Aid + scholarship
- TX residents: in-state tuition ~$13K
- OOS: ~$40K tuition, but TAMU offers significant OOS merit (notably more than UT Austin)
- President's Endowed Scholarship + Brown Foundation Scholarship — competitive merit
- Corps of Cadets scholarships (military service-linked, can lead to ROTC commissioning)
- Texas Aggie Graduate Grant + Aggie Promise (low-income TX residents)
- McNair Scholars + various Honors-track financial support

## Recent essay shifts
TAMU supplements via ApplyTexas reward applicants who engage with Aggie traditions authentically. The Common App version + supplements ask "Why Texas A&M" and "What contribution will you make to Aggieland?" — generic "great engineering school" essays underperform versus essays that reference specific traditions (Muster, Silver Taps, the Twelfth Man) or Corps of Cadets if relevant. Outsider applicants who don't research the culture stand out (negatively).
