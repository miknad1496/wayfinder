# Kenyon College — Wayfinder Deep Knowledge

## Snapshot
- Type: Private LAC, ~1,800 undergrad, Gambier OH (rural central Ohio, 50 miles northeast of Columbus)
- Admit rate: ~24%
- Test policy: Test-optional permanently
- Application: Common App + Kenyon supplements
- Early round: ED1 (Nov 15) + ED2 (Jan 15) — both binding
- Deadline: Nov 15 ED1 / Jan 15 ED2 + RD

## What admissions actually weights
Kenyon is institutionally distinct in four ways: (1) **English + Creative Writing is the institutional identity** — Kenyon is the home of the Kenyon Review (founded 1939, one of the most prestigious literary magazines in the U.S.) AND the Kenyon Review Young Writers Workshop (high school program that's a feeder for English majors). The college's English department is consistently top-3 among LACs. Famous Kenyon writers include John Crowe Ransom, James Wright, E.L. Doctorow, Robert Lowell, Allison Joseph. (2) **Founded 1824 as the Episcopal Theological Seminary of Ohio**, Kenyon is the second-oldest private college in Ohio. The Episcopal heritage shapes the institutional aesthetic (Old Kenyon Hall, Pierce Hall, the Church of the Holy Spirit) and there's a chaplaincy still active, but the college is no longer formally religious. (3) **Gambier OH is genuinely rural** — population ~2,500 (the college students roughly equal the town population). The setting is a real fit factor: cornfields surrounded by oak forests, no nightlife outside Kenyon itself. (4) **Middle Path** — the central campus walking path, originally cut by a Bishop in 1830s. The institutional metaphor: students walk the same path their predecessors have for nearly 200 years.

Strong programs: English (signature — top LAC English department), Creative Writing (Kenyon Review tradition), Classics (signature — strong Greek + Latin programs), History, Philosophy, Religious Studies (Episcopal heritage roots), Drama, Modern Languages, Biology, Mathematics, Music.

The "Lord/Lady" identity (yes, Kenyon's mascot is "Lords" and "Ladies" — reflecting the founding era) — traditional, intellectually serious, often quirky — shapes culture.

Test scores when submitted: middle-50 SAT 1370-1500, ACT 31-33. Submission rate ~50% for admits.

## Strategic notes
- ED1 admit rate ~35-40%; ED2 admit rate ~28%; RD ~22% — ED is meaningful leverage
- English/Creative Writing applicants should reference Kenyon Review specifically — major institutional asset
- Greek life: ~30% (high for a small LAC; Greek system is socially significant)
- Gambier OH: rural Ohio, real fit factor — students must accept the location
- D3 athletics in NCAC (North Coast Athletic Conference); Kenyon swimming is consistently top-3 nationally
- Notable alumni: Paul Newman (didn't graduate), E.L. Doctorow, Allison Janney, John Green, Carl Djerassi, Robert Lowell
- Distinctive features: Old Kenyon Hall (1827, oldest college building in Ohio), Middle Path, the Church of the Holy Spirit, Peirce Dining Hall
- Kenyon Review Young Writers Workshop: pipeline from high school summer program to admissions

## Aid + scholarship
- Need-blind for U.S. applicants
- Meets 100% of demonstrated need
- No-loan financial aid for families under ~$80K AGI
- Strong endowment per-student
- Trustee Scholarships + various competitive merit awards
- Kenyon Review Scholar Award for top creative writing applicants

## Recent essay shifts
Kenyon's "Why Kenyon" supplement rewards applicants who engage with the literary + Episcopal-heritage + rural setting. Creative writing applicants who reference specific Kenyon Review work or specific Kenyon professors score noticeably better. Generic "small LAC in countryside" essays underperform.
