# AP Macroeconomics — Wayfinder Study Guide

> Wayfinder advisory file. Surfaces when user asks about AP Macro prep, the
> 7 master graphs, or chain-of-reasoning FRQ technique.

## Math of a 5
~75% total. MCQ 45/60 correct + FRQ ~80% on each.

## THE differentiator (5-scorer vs 4-scorer)
**CHAIN OF REASONING.** Every Macro FRQ rewards a connected sequence: "action → market effect → interest rate → spending → AD/AS → real GDP/Price Level/Unemployment."

A 4-scorer stops at 1-2 links: "Fed lowers interest rate → AD increases."

A 5-scorer chains 4-6 links: "Fed buys bonds in open-market operations → bank reserves increase → money supply (MS) shifts right → nominal interest rate falls → cost of borrowing decreases → investment + interest-sensitive consumption (durables) increase → AD shifts right → real GDP increases AND price level increases AND unemployment falls (move down Phillips Curve in short run)."

The chain is THE entire game.

## The 7 master graphs (memorize cold — every FRQ uses 1-3)
1. **Production Possibilities Curve (PPC)** — opportunity cost, efficiency
2. **Business Cycle** — expansion / peak / recession / trough
3. **AD-AS** — short-run vs long-run AS, output gaps, inflationary/recessionary gaps
4. **Money Market** — vertical MS (Fed sets), MD downward sloping, nominal i on Y-axis
5. **Loanable Funds** — upward-sloping S, downward-sloping D, REAL r on Y-axis
6. **Phillips Curve** — short-run trade-off (down-sloping), long-run vertical at NRU
7. **Foreign Exchange (FOREX)** — supply/demand for currency, exchange rate

## Money Market vs Loanable Funds (#1 confused topic)
This is the SINGLE most-confused distinction. Get it right.

**Money Market (MM):**
- Y-axis: NOMINAL interest rate (i)
- Supply: VERTICAL (Fed sets MS regardless of interest rate)
- Use for: monetary policy (Fed actions, open market operations)
- Shifts: ΔMS shifts MS curve; ΔMD shifts MD curve

**Loanable Funds (LF):**
- Y-axis: REAL interest rate (r)
- Supply: UPWARD-sloping (people save more at higher r)
- Demand: DOWNWARD-sloping (firms borrow less at higher r)
- Use for: government deficits, crowding out, savings policy
- Shifts: gov deficit borrows from S → demand increases → real r ↑ → private investment ↓

**Memory hack:** Money Market = "How much money exists" (Fed controls). Loanable Funds = "Who's lending to whom and at what real rate."

## The Crowding Out chain (memorize this exact sequence)
Government deficit financed by borrowing →
Demand for Loanable Funds shifts RIGHT →
Real interest rate (r) increases →
Private investment (I) decreases →
Slower long-run growth (LRAS shifts right by less)

## Universal chain template (use this on every monetary/fiscal FRQ)
**Action → Direct Market Effect → Interest Rate or Wage Effect → Spending Component (C, I, G, NX) → AD or AS Shift → Real GDP, Price Level, Unemployment Outcome**

For monetary policy: action = Fed move; market = MM; interest rate = nominal i.
For fiscal: action = G or T change; market = LF; interest rate = real r (crowding out).
For supply shock: action = oil/wage change; market = LRAS or SRAS; outcome = stagflation if adverse.

## Key formulas
- **Real GDP** = Nominal GDP / GDP Deflator × 100
- **Real interest rate** = Nominal i − Expected Inflation (Fisher Equation)
- **Money multiplier** = 1 / Reserve Requirement
- **Tax multiplier** = MPC / (1 − MPC) [absolute value, negative direction]
- **Spending multiplier** = 1 / (1 − MPC) = 1 / MPS
- **Crowding out chain** (memorize sequence above)

## Top traps
- Confusing MM (nominal i) with LF (real r) — most common error
- Forgetting to label axis variable on graphs (must label both axes for full points)
- Stopping at 1-2 links in the chain (caps at 3/4 or 2/3 on most FRQs)
- Phillips Curve: forgetting the LR is VERTICAL at NRU (only short-run has trade-off)
