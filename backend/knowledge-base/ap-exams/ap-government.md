# AP Government & Politics — Wayfinder Study Guide

> Wayfinder advisory file. Surfaces when user asks about AP Gov prep, FRQ
> strategy, or required Foundational Documents / SCOTUS cases.

## Math of a 5
~75% total. MCQ 41/55 correct. FRQ scaled to ~80% on each.

## THE differentiator (5-scorer vs 4-scorer)
**Knowing the CONSTITUTIONAL CLAUSE behind each required SCOTUS case.** The SCOTUS Comparison FRQ asks you to match a non-required (novel) case to a required case via the SHARED CLAUSE. Students who memorized facts without the clause cannot answer this FRQ correctly.

A 4-scorer says "Marbury established judicial review." A 5-scorer says "Marbury established judicial review under Article III + the Supremacy Clause; therefore any case involving federal courts striking down a state or federal law shares that constitutional basis."

## Memorization fuel (highest-leverage prep in any AP)
**9 Foundational Documents** (every Argument FRQ MUST cite one):
1. Declaration of Independence
2. Articles of Confederation
3. US Constitution
4. Federalist 10 (factions, large republic)
5. Federalist 51 (separation of powers, "ambition counteracts ambition")
6. Federalist 70 (energetic executive, single president)
7. Federalist 78 (judicial review, "least dangerous branch")
8. Brutus 1 (Anti-Federalist, fears of central power)
9. Letter from Birmingham Jail (civil disobedience, MLK)

**15 Required SCOTUS Cases** (with the clause for each):
1. Marbury v. Madison (Article III, judicial review)
2. McCulloch v. Maryland (Necessary & Proper Clause + Supremacy Clause)
3. US v. Lopez (Commerce Clause limits)
4. Engel v. Vitale (Establishment Clause, school prayer)
5. Wisconsin v. Yoder (Free Exercise Clause)
6. Tinker v. Des Moines (1st Amendment Free Speech, schools)
7. NYT v. US (1st Amendment Press, prior restraint)
8. Schenck v. US (1st Amendment limits, "clear and present danger")
9. Gideon v. Wainwright (6th Amendment + 14th, right to counsel)
10. Roe v. Wade (originally Privacy/14th Due Process; OVERTURNED Dobbs 2022 — know historical context, current state)
11. McDonald v. Chicago (2nd Amendment incorporation via 14th)
12. Brown v. Board (14th Amendment Equal Protection)
13. Citizens United (1st Amendment Free Speech, campaign finance)
14. Baker v. Carr (justiciability + Equal Protection — "one person, one vote")
15. Shaw v. Reno (14th Amendment Equal Protection, racial gerrymandering)

## 5 Big Ideas (every FRQ tags 1+)
- **CON** — Constitutionalism
- **LOR** — Liberty + Order
- **PRD** — Civic Participation in a Representative Democracy
- **PMI** — Policy-Making Interests, Institutions, Behaviors
- **MPA** — Methods of Political Analysis (data interpretation)

## 4 FRQ Types (FIXED — same 4 every year, in this order)
1. **Concept Application (3 points)** — apply a concept to a scenario
2. **Quantitative Analysis (4 points)** — interpret data + identify trend + draw inference
3. **SCOTUS Comparison (4 points)** — compare a novel case to a required case via shared clause
4. **Argument Essay (6 points)** — defend a thesis using ≥1 Foundational Document + reasoning + perspective

## Top traps
- **Argument Essay must cite a Foundational Document** — most-missed point. If you don't reference a specific Federalist/Constitution/Brutus/MLK Letter clause, you cap at 4/6.
- **SCOTUS Comparison:** must explicitly identify the SHARED CLAUSE between novel + required case
- **Quant FRQ:** must explain a TREND from data, not just describe one data point

## Exam structure
- Section I: 55 MCQ, 80 minutes, 50% of score
- Section II: 4 FRQs, 100 minutes, 50% of score
- Total: 3 hours

## Score distribution
- 5: ~16% / 4: ~13% / 3: ~24% / 2: ~25% / 1: ~22%
