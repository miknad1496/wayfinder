# AP Calculus BC — Wayfinder Study Guide

> Wayfinder advisory file. Designed to surface in chat when user asks about
> AP Calculus BC prep, exam strategy, or score targets. Verify against
> current College Board exam structure before relying.

## Exam structure (current as of 2026 administration)

- **Total time:** 3 hours 15 minutes
- **Section I: Multiple Choice** (1h 45min, 50% of score)
  - Part A: 30 questions, 60 minutes, NO calculator
  - Part B: 15 questions, 45 minutes, calculator allowed
- **Section II: Free Response** (1h 30min, 50% of score)
  - Part A: 2 questions, 30 minutes, calculator
  - Part B: 4 questions, 60 minutes, NO calculator
- **Score scale:** 1-5. Most colleges grant credit for 4 or 5; many also accept 3.

## What's tested (10 units, organized in CED)

1. Limits and Continuity (4-7% of exam)
2. Differentiation: Definition and Fundamental Properties (4-7%)
3. Differentiation: Composite, Implicit, Inverse Functions (4-7%)
4. Contextual Applications of Differentiation (6-9%)
5. Analytical Applications of Differentiation (8-11%)
6. Integration and Accumulation of Change (17-20%)
7. Differential Equations (6-9%)
8. Applications of Integration (6-9%)
9. **Parametric, Polar, Vector-Valued Functions (BC ONLY)** (11-12%)
10. **Infinite Sequences and Series (BC ONLY)** (17-18%)

BC vs AB: BC covers everything in AB plus units 9 + 10. The series unit (taylor, maclaurin, lagrange error) is the unit BC students most often underestimate.

## Score distribution context (recent years)

- 5: ~43-45% of test-takers
- 4: ~17-18%
- 3: ~17-18%
- 2: ~14-15%
- 1: ~6-8%
- AP Calc BC has the HIGHEST 5-rate of any AP exam (highly self-selected pool)

## Strategy by score target

**Targeting a 5:**
- Master series (taylor, maclaurin, ratio test, alternating series test, lagrange error bound) — these dominate FRQ
- Memorize parametric/polar derivative + arc length formulas cold
- Practice FRQ rubrics — earning each point requires specific notation/justification
- Calculator: store programs for slope fields, parametric arc length, numerical integration. Know your TI-84 / Casio cold.
- Cycle through 5 full released exams in the 6 weeks before test

**Targeting a 4:**
- Strong fundamentals + can attempt all FRQ parts even if not all complete
- Don't skip series — even partial credit on series FRQs adds up
- Time management: don't get stuck on one MCQ for >2 minutes

**Targeting a 3:**
- Solid AB content + know the BC topics conceptually even if not fluently
- On FRQ, ALWAYS attempt every part — partial credit is generous
- Calculator strategy: know the basics (derivatives, integrals at a point, equation solver)

## Common mistakes that lose points

- **Notation:** writing "dy/dx" inconsistently, missing differential dx in integrals, forgetting "+ C"
- **Series convergence justification:** saying "by ratio test" without showing the limit
- **Calculator vs non-calculator confusion:** using calculator decimals on non-calculator FRQs
- **Reading:** parametric arc length vs polar arc length (different formulas) — students mix up
- **Lagrange error bound:** forgetting the (n+1)! denominator
- **Implicit differentiation:** forgetting product rule on terms like xy²

## Study timeline (suggested)

- **September-October (start of school year):** Build foundations alongside class. Review AB content if needed.
- **November-January:** Cover units 1-8 thoroughly. Most schools have done this by January.
- **February:** Hit the BC-only units 9 + 10 (parametric/polar/vector + series). These are make-or-break.
- **March:** Mock exam #1 (released 2017+). Review weakest topics.
- **April:** Mock exams #2-3. Drill FRQ rubrics. Memorize formulas.
- **First week of May:** 1-2 final mock exams. Practice pacing.
- **Exam day (early May):** Show up rested. Don't cram night-of.

## Calculator policy

- Approved: TI-83/84/Plus, TI-89 Titanium (can do symbolic), Casio fx-9750GIII, HP 39gII, NumWorks
- NOT approved: TI-Nspire CX II CAS — wait NO, CX II CAS IS approved actually
- Programmable but not communicate-with-each-other
- Bring batteries / fresh calc — no failures allowed

## College credit policy (varies by school)

- Most universities: 4 or 5 = credit for Calc I + Calc II (8 credits / one full year)
- Top schools (MIT, Caltech, Princeton): may not award credit; you place into multivariable
- Some schools: 3 = elective credit only, 4-5 = course credit
- ALWAYS check the school's specific AP credit policy on their registrar website
