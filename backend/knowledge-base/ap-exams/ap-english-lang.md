# AP English Language & Composition — Wayfinder Study Guide

> Wayfinder advisory file. Surfaces when user asks about AP Lang prep, essay
> strategy, or commentary depth.

## Math of a 5
~75% total. MCQ 33+/45 correct + average ~5/6 across the 3 essays. The 5 hinges on essay scoring more than MCQ.

## Universal Rubric (SAME on all 3 essays)
Each essay scored on 6 points:
- **Thesis (1 point)** — defensible thesis with line of reasoning
- **Evidence + Commentary (0-4 single scale)** — single combined score
  - **3/4 (4-zone)** = specific evidence + commentary explaining HOW it works
  - **4/4 (5-zone)** = sophisticated commentary explaining COMPLEXITY (multiple effects, tensions, qualifications)
- **Sophistication (1 point)** — only ~10% of essays earn it

## THE differentiator (5-scorer vs 4-scorer)
**Commentary depth.** A 4-scorer IDENTIFIES devices ("the author uses metaphor"). A 5-scorer EXPLAINS HOW devices CREATE meaning + advance argument ("by foregrounding the cracked walls' specific bodily detail, the author bypasses statistical abstraction and forces the reader into visceral identification with the displaced families").

The leap from "name the device" to "explain how the device functions" is the entire game.

## Body Paragraph Formula (Rhetorical Analysis)
Every body paragraph follows this 4-move structure:
1. **TOPIC SENTENCE** — name the device + connect to author's argument
2. **EVIDENCE** — brief quote (don't over-quote)
3. **COMMENTARY** — THE SCORING ZONE. Explain HOW the device works on the reader, what effect it creates, why the author chose this specifically.
4. **CONNECTION TO PURPOSE** — tie back to the broader argument or audience effect

The Commentary section is where 90% of points are won/lost.

## The 3 essays
1. **Synthesis (40 minutes)** — read 4-5 sources, weave them into a position. Cite at least 3, ideally 4-5. Take a position; don't just summarize.
2. **Rhetorical Analysis (40 minutes)** — analyze how an author achieves purpose through devices. Device → Effect → Purpose chain.
3. **Argument (40 minutes)** — defend / challenge / **qualify** a claim. **QUALIFY is the highest-sophistication ceiling** because it acknowledges complexity built into the response.

## The 4 paths to the Sophistication point
Only ~10% of essays earn this point. You earn it via ANY ONE of:
1. **Nuanced argument** acknowledging complexity (best path)
2. **Implications / limits** of your argument — explore consequences
3. **Style** — your own writing demonstrates rhetorical skill (controlled syntax, deliberate diction)
4. **Broader context** — connect to historical/cultural/literary context outside the prompt

## Phrases that score (drop these into commentary)
- "By foregrounding [specific detail]..."
- "This device creates [specific effect] by [mechanism]..."
- "While [counterposition], the author qualifies this by..."
- "The cumulative effect of [device] across the passage is..."
- "[Device] works in tandem with [other device] to..."

## Top traps
- Stopping at IDENTIFY without EXPLAIN ("the author uses ethos") — caps at 3/6
- Over-quoting (more than ~10 words) — wastes space, reduces commentary room
- Argument essay: pure defend/challenge with no qualification — limits sophistication ceiling
- Synthesis: summarizing sources instead of weaving them into your argument

## Exam structure
- Section I: 45 MCQ, 60 minutes, 45% of score
- Section II: 3 essays, 135 minutes (incl. 15 min reading), 55% of score
- Total: 3 hours 15 minutes

## Score distribution
- 5: ~10% / 4: ~22% / 3: ~32% / 2: ~26% / 1: ~10%
