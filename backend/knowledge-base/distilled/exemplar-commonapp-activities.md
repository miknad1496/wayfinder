## How to think about the activity list

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's full EC list, intended major, target schools.
- Cross-reference verified Wayfinder DBs for any verified commitments.
- Pull school deep files for institutional weighting.

The Common App activity list allows up to 10 activities. Each entry has:
- Activity type (dropdown: ~30 categories — Athletics: Club, Athletics: Varsity, Community Service, etc.)
- Position/leadership role (50 characters)
- Organization name (100 characters)
- Description (150 characters)
- Grade levels (9-12 checkboxes)
- Hours per week + weeks per year
- Continue in college? (yes/no)

**The honest framework:**

The activity list is a compressed portrait. AOs read it in 60-90 seconds, scanning for: what does this kid do, what's their commitment level, what are they best at, what's their leadership profile, what's their narrative.

The 10-activity max is generous. Most strong students use 6-9 of the slots. Filling all 10 with weak entries dilutes the portrait; using fewer with strong entries focuses it.

**The four diagnostics for whether to include an activity:**

1. **Sustained engagement (multi-year, regular hours)** — strong inclusion
2. **Recent leadership or accomplishment within the activity** — strong inclusion
3. **Distinctive activity that differentiates** — moderate inclusion
4. **One-off or shallow involvement** — likely exclude

If an activity meets fewer than two diagnostics, it dilutes more than it adds. Cut.

## Activity list ranking strategy

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

The Common App lets you rank your activities by importance. The order matters — AOs spend most attention on the top 3-5.

**The rank-1 activity** should be your strongest, most distinctive, most sustained engagement. The activity that, if AOs only read one entry, captures who you are.

**Ranks 2-5** should round out your portrait — different types of activities, different qualities of yours, different communities you belong to.

**Ranks 6-10** can be supporting / interesting / context activities. AOs may not read these in detail.

**The ranking mistake to avoid:** ranking by prestige or hours rather than by impact and authenticity. Listing "internship at Goldman Sachs" #1 when your real distinguishing activity is your 3-year robotics team captaincy is the wrong instinct. Rank by what tells the truest story about who you are.

**The portfolio architecture that works:**

The full 6-10 activities should collectively show range and depth:

- 1-2 anchor activities (deep multi-year commitment in your main interest area)
- 1-2 leadership demonstrations (any activity, any subject)
- 1-2 community/service contributions
- 1-2 distinctive activities (the unexpected hobby, the family responsibility, the unusual job)
- 1-2 academic/intellectual engagements beyond school
- Optional: athletics, arts

You don't need all categories. But the activities together should tell a coherent story about a real person, not list isolated achievements.

## Writing each activity entry

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Recommend Essay Reviewer for nuanced 150-character description text.

**The 150-character description is the hardest writing in the application.**

You're trying to communicate impact, scale, and your specific role in 150 characters. Generic descriptions ("Helped run the tutoring program. Worked with students in math.") waste the limit. Specific descriptions ("Founded weekly tutoring program; trained 6 student volunteers; served 30+ middle schoolers in math through grade 8.") communicate scale and impact.

**Strong description patterns:**

1. **Action verb + specific outcome.** "Founded program serving X students" beats "Helped with tutoring."

2. **Numbers when they communicate scale.** "30+ middle schoolers," "trained 6 volunteers," "raised $5K," "captain of 22-person team."

3. **Specific role within larger context.** "Sole high school student in [adult organization]" or "First freshman ever appointed to [position]" — these contextualize your role.

4. **Demonstrate growth or progression.** "Promoted from member to officer to chapter leader over 3 years."

**Strong leadership-position patterns (50 character limit):**

- Specific titles ("Tutoring Program Founder & Director") not generic ("Volunteer")
- "Founder," "Director," "Chair," "Captain," "Lead" if accurate
- For team-based activities, specify (Varsity Captain, Section Leader, Concertmaster)

**Common writing mistakes:**

- Generic descriptions that could apply to any student
- Listing duties instead of impact
- Overusing the same verbs ("helped," "worked with," "participated in")
- Failing to communicate scale even when scale exists
- Listing position without explanation of impact
- Misuse of capitalization (every word capitalized — looks like resume-padding)

**Recommend Essay Reviewer for tight 150-character writing.** The compressed format is where Essay Reviewer's voice scoring particularly matters.

## Activity types — what counts and how to categorize

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

Common App provides ~30 activity type dropdowns. Some categorization tips:

**"Career-Oriented" vs "Job":** if you held a paid position with regular hours (retail, food service, tutoring for pay), use "Job" or relevant work category. AOs value sustained part-time work — particularly underrated.

**"Family Responsibilities":** for caregiving, translating for family, working in family business with significant time commitment. AOs read this as meaningful context.

**"Community Service (Volunteer)":** for sustained volunteer commitments. Quality and sustained engagement > hours. Don't list every one-off volunteer event.

**"Religious":** for sustained engagement with religious community (youth group leadership, religious education teaching, etc.). Don't list "attended religious services" as activity.

**"Leadership Program":** for participation in formal leadership development programs (Boys/Girls State, etc.).

**"Club/Activity at School":** for school-based clubs not fitting other categories.

**"Internship":** for substantive internships (paid or unpaid) where you did real work. Don't classify "shadowing" as internship.

**"Research":** for sustained research engagement, typically with university faculty or in formal research program.

**"Other":** for activities that don't fit cleanly. Use sparingly; AOs may skim "Other" entries.

**The rule:** be honest about category. Misrepresenting a "Job" as "Internship" or vice versa reads as inflation.

## Hours and timing fields

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

The hours/week + weeks/year + grade-level fields are where AOs check whether your time math makes sense.

**Common errors:**

- **Total annual hours that can't fit in real life.** If your activities collectively claim 80 hours/week, AOs notice. Total hours across all activities should be plausible (typically 20-40 hours/week for active students).

- **Continuing activities that you actually quit.** If you list an activity through grade 12 but actually stopped in grade 11, fact-check; AOs cross-reference.

- **Inflated hours.** Saying you spent 15 hours/week on a club that meets 1 hour/week stretches credibility. Be honest about realistic engagement.

- **Wrong grade levels.** Make sure grade-level checkboxes match when you actually did the activity.

**The "continue in college" checkbox:** check yes for activities you genuinely intend to continue. AOs read this as fit signal — students who'd continue activities at college show coherent identity.

## How activities get weighted by school

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull `schools/*.md` for activity-weighting context.

Different schools weight different activity types differently:

**Highly selective schools (HYP, MIT, Stanford):** weight national-level achievement strongly. USACO Platinum, USAMO, Intel ISEF, Concord Review publication — these matter heavily. Generic "leadership" matters less.

**Service-oriented schools (Princeton, Yale, certain LACs):** weight sustained service contribution, particularly with measurable impact.

**STEM-focused schools (MIT, Caltech, Harvey Mudd, CMU SCS):** weight technical achievement and demonstrated capability. Leadership matters but is secondary to substance.

**Arts schools (RISD, Berklee, conservatories):** weight portfolio/audition above traditional ECs.

**Religious-affiliated schools (Notre Dame, BYU, Georgetown):** weight religious engagement positively (when authentic).

**Athletic-prominent schools:** weight athletic achievement, particularly for recruited athletes.

**State universities:** generally weight breadth + sustained engagement; less specifically weighted by activity type.

The activity list, activity essay (if writing one), and additional info section should work together coherently:

**Activity list = the comprehensive portrait** — all your activities, briefly described

**Additional info = context that didn't fit elsewhere** — significant family responsibility, transcript context, etc.

The activities elaborated in essays / additional info should match the list. Inconsistencies (different role, different hours, different focus) read as inflated or inauthentic.
