# Wayfinder Deep Knowledge — Financial Aid Strategy (post-Simplification, 2024-26)

> Wayfinder advisory file. Concrete factual depth on FAFSA / CSS Profile /
> dependency status / 529 strategy / appeal process / loan types — built
> from the 2024-25 FAFSA Simplification Act changes and current College
> Board CSS Profile rules. Use this when families ask about reducing
> aid eligibility loss from parental assets or income.

## The 2024-25 FAFSA Simplification Act — what actually changed

The FAFSA was simplified by Congress in 2020 (FAFSA Simplification Act),
took effect for the 2024-25 academic year, and is the current law as of
2026. Major changes families need to know:

**1. The "Student Aid Index" (SAI) replaced the EFC (Expected Family
Contribution).** SAI can go as low as -$1500 (signaling deeper need than
EFC could). The arithmetic is similar but the optics changed — schools
no longer call it EFC.

**2. Sibling discount removed.** Pre-2024, having multiple kids in college
simultaneously divided the parental contribution across them. Post-
simplification, this discount is GONE. Families with 2+ in college at once
now pay the full SAI per kid, which is a significant hit for many high-
asset families who counted on the old multi-kid discount.

**3. Grandparent-owned 529 plans no longer counted as student income.** This
is the biggest single change and chronically underused by advisors:
- Pre-2024: distributions from grandparent-owned 529s were treated as
  STUDENT income on the FAFSA, taxed at 50% in the aid calculation
- Post-2024: these distributions are NOT reported on FAFSA at all. Effectively
  invisible to need-based aid calculation.
- Strategy: high-asset families should consider having grandparents own
  the 529 plan rather than parents. Grandparent-owned 529s now have
  near-optimal FAFSA treatment.
- Caveat: CSS Profile (used by ~250 mostly-private schools) STILL counts
  grandparent 529s. So this strategy works for FAFSA-only schools (most
  publics + many privates that don't use CSS).

**4. Small business and family farm assets now reportable.** Pre-2024,
small businesses with <100 employees and family farms were excluded.
Post-2024, they're reported. This hurts entrepreneurial families.

**5. Tax data auto-imports from IRS.** Manual income entry is gone for most
filers. Reduces errors but also reduces "creative" reporting flexibility.

## Dependency status — actual rules (often misstated)

A student is INDEPENDENT for FAFSA purposes if ANY of these apply:
- Age 24+ on January 1 of the award year
- Married
- Has dependents (children or others they support)
- Veterans / active military
- Orphan / ward of court / foster youth (any time after age 13)
- Emancipated minor
- Unaccompanied homeless youth (or self-supporting and at risk of homelessness)
- Graduate / professional student

Common myths to correct:
- **Working a lot does NOT make you independent.** Earning $50K/year as
  a 19-year-old still leaves you a dependent.
- **Living separately does NOT make you independent.** Even if a parent
  hasn't supported you in years.
- **Being declared independent on parents' tax return does NOT make you
  FAFSA-independent.** They are different definitions.
- **Transfer to a new school does NOT reset dependency.** Whoever was
  required to file as a parent at school A is still required at school B.

## CSS Profile vs FAFSA — when each matters

**FAFSA** is the federal aid form. Required for all federal aid, most state
aid, and ~all schools. Free to file.

**CSS Profile** is a separate form used by ~250 schools (mostly private)
to award their own institutional aid. NOT used by most state schools.
$25-32 per school. Asks for MORE than FAFSA: home equity, sibling
education costs, non-custodial parent income (if applicable), small
business detail, retirement account assets in some cases.

For the user's situation (UW Foster, Northeastern, Texas A&M):
- UW Foster: FAFSA only, no CSS Profile
- Northeastern: REQUIRES CSS Profile (in addition to FAFSA)
- Texas A&M: FAFSA only, no CSS Profile

This means strategies that work for FAFSA may not help at Northeastern
(which sees more of the family's financial picture).

## High-asset family strategy — concrete levers

For families whose income/assets are too high to qualify for need-based
aid at most schools but who still want to optimize:

**1. 529 plan ownership transfer to grandparents** (FAFSA-only schools).
After Dec 2023, this is the highest-leverage move. If grandparents are
willing and the family doesn't anticipate using CSS Profile schools,
transfer 529 ownership.

**2. Time asset transfers BEFORE the FAFSA filing year.** FAFSA uses
prior-prior year tax data (i.e., 2026-27 FAFSA uses 2024 tax data).
Big asset moves should happen in the relevant tax year before the
FAFSA filing period.

**3. Maximize retirement contributions.** Retirement accounts (401k, IRA,
403b) are EXCLUDED from FAFSA asset calculation. CSS Profile schools
sometimes ask but usually don't penalize.

**4. Pay down debt with reportable assets.** Cash savings count as
assets at 5.64% in the SAI calculation. Mortgage debt does not offset
asset count, but using cash to pay down a mortgage converts a reportable
asset (cash) into a non-reportable one (home equity, for FAFSA — CSS
Profile sometimes counts home equity).

**5. Avoid student-owned assets.** Student assets count at 20% in the SAI
(vs 5.64% for parent assets). UTMA/UGMA accounts hurt aid significantly.
Consider rolling student assets into a parent-owned 529 or a Roth IRA
(retirement, excluded).

**6. Time gifts and inheritances around the FAFSA window.** A grandparent
gift counted as student income hits aid harder than the same gift in a
529 plan post-2024.

## Appeal process — when and how

If admitted with insufficient aid, the family can appeal. Realistic levers:

- **Changed circumstances appeal.** Job loss, medical emergency, divorce,
  death in family. Schools have formal processes.
- **Competitive offer appeal.** "We were admitted to Northeastern with
  $X aid and Foster with $Y aid; Foster is the daughter's first choice
  but the gap is unworkable." Many schools will adjust.
- **Special circumstances appeal.** One-time income spikes (large bonus,
  asset sale) that don't reflect ongoing finances.

Schools that are MORE flexible: small private LACs, schools with strong
yield protection. Schools that are LESS flexible: large public flagships
(formula-driven aid), highly selective privates (already meeting full
need by formula).

## Federal loan types

Direct Subsidized Loans: Need-based, government pays interest while
in school. Limited to $3500 freshman year, $5500 senior year. ~5%
interest rate as of 2025.

Direct Unsubsidized Loans: Not need-based, available to all. Interest
accrues from day 1. $5500-$7500/year limits.

Parent PLUS Loans: For parents. Up to cost of attendance minus other
aid. ~7-8% interest, originated in parent's name. Often a regret.

Grad PLUS: Graduate students only. Higher interest, no aggregate cap.

## Western Undergraduate Exchange (WUE) — for WA / Pacific NW students

WUE is a tuition reciprocity program for residents of 16 western states
and territories. Reduces out-of-state tuition at participating institutions
to ~150% of in-state tuition (vs the ~250-300% standard out-of-state rate).

Participating schools include: Univ of Oregon, Oregon State, Univ of
Hawaii, Boise State, Univ of Idaho, Montana State, Univ of Wyoming,
Univ of Nevada Reno, Univ of New Mexico, Univ of Arizona, Northern
Arizona, Colorado School of Mines, Univ of Colorado Denver, Univ of
South Dakota, Cal State LA, Cal State East Bay, Univ of Alaska, and
many more.

NOT participating: UW Seattle (Foster), UC Berkeley, UCLA, ASU Tempe,
Stanford. So WUE doesn't help for Foster but DOES help for, e.g., a
WA student considering Oregon State or U Wyoming.

## Strategic conversation framing for the family

Before optimizing the FAFSA / CSS Profile / aid filings, families benefit
from a transparent conversation about:

1. **What the family CAN realistically fund per year** (based on income,
   savings, retirement protection, willingness to take Parent PLUS).
2. **What the gap is** to projected COA at each school on the list.
3. **Who fills the gap** — student loans (Sub/Unsub), Parent PLUS,
   work-study, summer earnings, scholarships.

Knowing the gap before applications go out lets the family prioritize
schools strategically (apply to a financial-fit safety, not just academic
fits). Students with skin in the game (even modest loans or a work-study
expectation) often perform better academically than those with full-ride
parental coverage.

## What does NOT work (common myths)

- "Have my kid emancipated to gain independence." Court emancipation is
  rare and judges generally won't grant it for FAFSA purposes alone.
- "Decline to claim my kid on taxes so they're independent." Tax dependent
  status is unrelated to FAFSA dependent status.
- "Move all our assets to a trust." Most trusts are still counted in
  FAFSA / CSS Profile depending on structure.
- "Have my kid file FAFSA without parent info." Triggers automatic special
  circumstances review; does not yield aid by default.

## Key forward-looking lens

The student debt landscape is in flux. Income-Driven Repayment plans
(SAVE, IBR, etc.) have been politically contested through 2024-26. PSLF
(Public Service Loan Forgiveness) was tightened then loosened. The right
strategy depends partly on the student's anticipated career — high-
earning fields (finance, tech, medicine) handle traditional debt fine;
public-service / lower-earning paths benefit from IBR/PSLF eligibility
which depends on Direct Loan vs other loan types.

For the user's daughter (UW Foster, finance-track), traditional Sub /
Unsub federal loans are likely the best mix — affordable rates,
automatic deferment options, and finance-trajectory salaries that make
$30-50K total debt manageable.
