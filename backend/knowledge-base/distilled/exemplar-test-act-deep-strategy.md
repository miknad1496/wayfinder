The honest framework for ACT strategy:

**The ACT structure:**

- 4 mandatory sections + optional Writing
- English: 75 questions, 45 minutes (36 sec/question — fastest pacing on the test)
- Math: 60 questions, 60 minutes (60 sec/question)
- Reading: 40 questions, 35 minutes (52 sec/question)
- Science: 40 questions, 35 minutes (52 sec/question)
- Writing (optional): 40 minutes for 1 essay; some schools require, some don't

**Composite scoring (1-36):**

- Each section scored 1-36
- Composite = average of 4 section scores, rounded
- 36 composite = perfect; ~3,000 students per year nationally
- 33-35: top 1%; competitive at top schools
- 30-32: top 5%; competitive at top-25 schools
- 27-29: top 13%; competitive at top-50 schools
- 24-26: top 25%; competitive at top-100
- 21-23: top 40%
- Below 21: open admissions or some moderately-selective schools

**Score targets by tier of school:**

- HYPSM tier: 34+ competitive, 33+ acceptable with strong rest of profile
- Top 10-25: 32+ competitive
- Top 25-50: 30+ competitive
- Top 50-100: 28+ competitive
- State flagships: 26+ competitive (varies by state)

## Section-by-section strategy

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**English section (45 min, 75 questions):**

The fastest-paced section. 36 seconds per question. Many students finish under time only with strong pacing discipline.

Question types:
- Production of Writing (~30%): main idea, organization, transitions, style
- Knowledge of Language (~15%): word choice, tone
- Conventions of Standard English (~55%): grammar, punctuation, sentence structure

Strategic notes:
- Dense practice of grammar rules essential — punctuation, comma rules, semicolons, dashes, sentence boundaries
- The "shortest answer is usually right" heuristic works often (ACT favors concise constructions)
- Practice pacing rigorously — 36 seconds per question is hard for slow readers
- Know the standard grammar errors tested (subject-verb agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, modifier placement, parallelism, comma splices)

**Math section (60 min, 60 questions):**

Calculator allowed throughout. Broader content range than SAT math.

Question types:
- Pre-Algebra and Elementary Algebra (~25%): basic algebra, percentages
- Intermediate Algebra and Coordinate Geometry (~25%): functions, equations, lines
- Plane Geometry (~25%): shapes, area, volume, congruence, similarity
- Trigonometry (~15%): basic trig, identities
- Advanced Math (~10%): logarithms, matrices, complex numbers, sequences

Strategic notes:
- ACT math has more memorized formulas than SAT math (no formula sheet provided)
- Stronger math vocabulary than SAT (matrices, logs, complex numbers all appear)
- Geometry is more tested than SAT
- Pacing is more humane (60 seconds/question)
- Strong calculator skills help
- Memorize key formulas: area/volume of common shapes, special right triangles, basic trig

**Reading section (35 min, 40 questions):**

Fast pacing, 52 seconds/question. Long passages (typically 4 passages of ~750 words each).

Question types:
- Key Ideas and Details (~55%): main idea, details, inferences, sequence
- Craft and Structure (~25%): word in context, structure, point of view
- Integration of Knowledge and Ideas (~20%): comparing texts, evaluating evidence

Passage types:
- Prose fiction
- Social science
- Humanities
- Natural science

Strategic notes:
- Practice with long-form reading critical
- Skimming + active rereading often better than slow first reading
- Question order in some passages roughly mirrors passage chronology
- Practice all 4 passage types — students often have a weakest type

**Science section (35 min, 40 questions):**

Despite the name, NOT a test of science knowledge. It's a test of reading scientific tables, charts, conflicting hypotheses, and experimental design. No biology/chemistry/physics knowledge required to answer most questions.

Question types:
- Data Representation (~38%): reading graphs and tables
- Research Summaries (~45%): interpreting experimental results
- Conflicting Viewpoints (~17%): comparing hypothetical scientific positions

Strategic notes:
- Most students fear this section but it's often one of the highest-scoring with practice
- Practice reading tables and graphs quickly
- Skip extensive reading of background paragraphs initially; go straight to questions and refer back to data
- Conflicting Viewpoints questions are the hardest for many; budget time accordingly

**Writing section (optional):**

40 minutes, single essay. Three perspectives provided on a contemporary issue; you discuss the perspectives and argue your own view.

- Required at: Some schools require it (declining list); check each school's policy. Most schools no longer require it.
- Scoring: 2-12 scale; doesn't affect composite score
- Strategic notes: if not required by any of your schools, skip it (saves stress and time)

## ACT prep timeline

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's grade for timeline calibration.

Similar to SAT timeline:

**For students starting prep in spring of sophomore year:**
- Take baseline timed practice ACT
- Light prep over summer
- Junior year fall: take official PreACT or ACT diagnostic
- Junior year winter: ramp prep
- Junior year spring: take official ACT
- Junior year summer / senior fall: optional retake

**For students starting prep in summer before junior year:**
- Summer: structured prep 5-10 hours/week
- Junior year fall: maybe take official ACT (October date is good)
- Junior year winter-spring: continue prep, retake if needed
- Junior year summer: optional further retake

**For students starting prep junior fall:**
- Fall diagnostic, intense prep through winter
- Spring official ACT
- Summer retake if needed

## Retake decisions

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's current ACT and target schools.

**When to retake:**
- Score below 25th percentile of admits at top target schools
- Score below merit-aid threshold at target schools
- Specific section dragged composite (e.g., 36/36/30/36 = 35 composite, but Reading drag could be improved)
- Bad test day (illness, distraction)

**When NOT to retake:**
- Score at 50th percentile+ at top target schools
- Multiple retakes already with no improvement
- Stress about score affecting other application areas

**Score Choice and superscoring:**

Most schools allow you to send specific test dates (Score Choice). Many schools also superscore — combining your highest section scores across multiple test dates into a "best composite." Check each target school's policy:

- Schools that superscore ACT: Yale, Stanford, Princeton, MIT, Duke, many others (list growing)
- Schools that don't superscore ACT: some schools take only highest single sitting

If your target schools superscore, retaking strategically (focus on weakest section) maximizes superscored composite.

## Section-specific improvement strategies for retakes

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**Improving English:**
- Drill grammar rules systematically
- Practice with timed sections to build pacing
- Review every error from practice tests; understand why correct answer is correct
- Vocabulary in context can be improved with focused vocab work

**Improving Math:**
- Identify content gaps via practice tests; fill with targeted study
- Practice calculator efficiency
- Memorize key formulas (especially geometry and trig)
- Time per question is more forgiving than English; practice can address speed

**Improving Reading:**
- Practice timed reading; build endurance
- Identify weakest passage type; target practice
- Practice referring back to passage rather than relying on memory
- Active reading techniques (annotation if testing on paper, mental annotation if digital)

**Improving Science:**
- Practice with table/graph reading exercises
- Don't read background paragraphs in depth on first pass
- Time management is critical; don't spend too long on Conflicting Viewpoints
