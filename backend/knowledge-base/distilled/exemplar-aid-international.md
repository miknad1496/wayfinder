## How to think about international student aid

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

International student financial aid in US higher ed is fundamentally different from domestic aid. Three core points most families don't understand:

**1. International students cannot file FAFSA.** FAFSA is only for US citizens, permanent residents, and certain eligible non-citizens. International students (F-1 visa applicants) need to use whatever international financial aid form each school requires (often the CSS Profile or a school-specific international form).

**2. "Need-aware" is the default for international students at most US schools.** Schools that are need-blind for domestic applicants are typically need-aware for internationals. This means: applying for financial aid AFFECTS your admission chances. The school may admit you only if they can afford to fund you.

**3. The list of schools that are BOTH need-blind for internationals AND meet full demonstrated need is small.** This is the critical strategic information for international families needing aid.

## Need-aware schools that meet full need (the second tier)

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

These schools are need-aware for internationals (applying for aid hurts admit chances) BUT will fully meet need IF they admit and decide to fund:

- Most other Ivies (Penn, Brown, Cornell)
- Duke, Northwestern, Vanderbilt, Wash U, JHU, Rice, Notre Dame, Emory, Tufts
- Many top LACs (Williams, Wesleyan, Carleton, Grinnell, Macalester, Bates, Bowdoin's peers)

**The strategic tradeoff:** at need-aware schools, applying for aid:
- Reduces admit probability by maybe 30-50% for internationals (varies by school and applicant strength)
- BUT if admitted, full funding is typically provided
- vs not applying for aid (declaring full-pay), where admit probability is higher but family must pay $80K-$95K/year

For families with realistic full-pay capability, declaring full-pay at need-aware schools dramatically improves admit chances. For families needing aid, the need-aware penalty is real and worth understanding.

## Schools where international aid is genuinely scarce

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**Public universities (UC system, UMich, UVA, UNC, all state flagships):**

Very limited aid for international students. International tuition is full-rate ($60K-$80K/year). Most public universities do not offer significant aid to internationals beyond a few highly competitive scholarships. Even strong international applicants who would qualify for full aid at private universities typically face full COA at public universities.

**Most other private universities outside the top tier:**

Need-aware for internationals AND don't fully meet need. The result: international admits often receive partial aid that leaves $20K-$50K/year unfunded, forcing family contribution that may not be feasible.

**Practical implication:** for international students needing substantial aid, the right strategic list is heavy on the need-blind-meets-full-need list, supplemented by full-need-meeting LACs (which have higher admit rates than HYP-tier and similar aid generosity if admitted).

## The strategic application list for international students needing aid

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's profile, country, financial situation.

**The right list construction for an international student needing significant aid:**

**Layer 1 — Heavy on need-blind-meets-full-need schools:** apply broadly across HYPSM + Amherst + Bowdoin + Dartmouth + Columbia + Stanford + Pomona. Even though admission rates are 2-5% for internationals at these schools, this is the highest-yield path to (admit AND fully funded).

**Layer 2 — Need-aware top schools (apply selectively):** apply to need-aware top schools (Penn, Duke, Northwestern, etc.) only if you have unusually strong profile that can absorb the need-aware penalty. The aid burden is similar if admitted.

**Layer 3 — Full-need-meeting LACs:** Bates, Carleton, Grinnell, Macalester, Smith, Wellesley, Vassar, Bryn Mawr, Wesleyan. Lower nominal admit rates than HYPSM (often 8-15% for internationals vs 2-5%). If admitted, often fully funded. Strong "sweet spot" for international applicants needing aid.

**Layer 4 — In-country backup:** every international applicant needing aid should have an in-country plan that doesn't depend on US admission. Strong universities in the home country, pathway programs (community college → transfer to US, foundation year → degree in another country), etc.

**Avoid:** applying primarily to need-aware top-30 schools assuming "significant aid" is available. The need-aware penalty + inadequate aid combination produces worse outcomes than the focused-on-need-blind approach.

## Application elements specific to international applicants

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

Beyond aid form mechanics, international applicants need to handle several elements US applicants don't:

**1. English proficiency demonstration:**
- TOEFL: typical top-school target 110+, required at most schools
- IELTS: 7.5+ equivalent
- Duolingo English Test: increasingly accepted but verify per school
- SAT EBRW score: serves as proxy for English at most schools (high score can sometimes waive TOEFL requirement)

**2. School context document:**
- Have your school counselor or principal write a "school profile" explaining the academic context
- What classes are available, how grades work, percentile/rank if available
- Essential for AOs unfamiliar with non-US educational systems

**3. Recommendation letters in English:**
- Teachers who can write fluent academic English
- Ideally with awareness of US application norms
- Translated letters from non-English-fluent recommenders are usually accepted but read as less compelling

**4. External validation:**
- International competitions (Olympiads, ISEF, World Schools Debating, FRC robotics)
- IB Diploma if available (very portable signal)
- A-Levels, Abitur, French Baccalauréat — recognizable to AOs
- Published work in English-language outlets

**5. Demonstrated financial reality:**
- Be honest in financial aid forms; international financial aid offices have seen everything
- Misrepresentation is detected and disqualifying
- Schools verify income claims through documentation
