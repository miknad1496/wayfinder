## How to think about arts admissions

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's discipline, portfolio status, target schools.
- Pull `schools/*.md` for arts programs.

The honest framework:

**Arts admissions is a parallel track.** Students applying to BFA programs, conservatories, or with arts portfolios face a process that prioritizes artistic ability over standard academic profile.

**The categories of arts-focused programs:**

1. **Pure conservatories** (Juilliard, Curtis, Cleveland Institute of Music, Eastman, Berklee, NEC, MannHattan School of Music): admissions almost entirely determined by audition. Academic profile matters but secondary.

2. **University arts programs (BFA degree-granting)** (NYU Tisch, USC Cinematic Arts, RISD, Pratt, Parsons, ArtCenter, MICA, SCAD): admissions weight portfolio/audition heavily but academic profile matters more than at conservatories.

3. **Liberal arts colleges with strong arts** (Williams, Amherst, Wesleyan, Vassar, Smith, Wellesley, Kenyon, Sarah Lawrence, Bennington): admit primarily on standard academic application; arts portfolio submitted as supplemental and considered.

4. **Universities with arts schools** (Yale College, Stanford, Northwestern, Carnegie Mellon, Michigan, Brown, Columbia): apply to general university; arts portfolio supplemental; some specific BFA programs (CMU School of Art, Northwestern theater).

## Visual arts portfolios

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For students applying to visual arts programs (RISD, Pratt, ArtCenter, MICA, SCAD, NYU Tisch art tracks, university arts schools):

**Portfolio standards:**

- Typically 12-20 pieces (school varies)
- Mix of media (drawing, painting, photography, digital, mixed media, sculpture if applicable)
- Foundation drawing strongly emphasized — at least some traditional drawings showing observation skills
- Personal voice / vision evident across pieces
- Variety AND coherence — show range without seeming scattered

**Submission platforms:**

- SlideRoom (most common)
- School-specific portals
- Some schools require physical submission

**The honest framework:**

- Portfolio is THE deciding factor at top art schools
- Academic profile secondary but still considered
- Specific institutional fit matters — RISD vs Pratt vs ArtCenter prize different qualities
- Strong portfolios show artistic point of view, not just technical skill

**Pre-college options:**

- Pre-college summer programs at RISD, Pratt, MICA, etc. give exposure (often pay-to-play, modest admissions value)
- Sustained engagement with art teacher mentor
- Building portfolio through high school art classes + independent practice
- Showing in juried local exhibitions
- Scholastic Art Awards (Gold/Silver Key, Honorable Mention) — meaningful national recognition

## Music auditions

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For students applying to music programs (Juilliard, Berklee, Eastman, Manhattan School of Music, university music schools):

**Audition requirements:**

- Pre-screen recording (typically 10-20 minutes; submitted with application)
- If pre-screen passes, live audition (in-person or recorded)
- Repertoire requirements specific to instrument and program (typically published on school websites)
- Theory/aural skills sometimes assessed

**The honest framework:**

- Conservatory admissions almost entirely about audition
- University music school admissions more weighted by academic profile
- Specific institutional fit varies — Juilliard prizes technical perfection, Berklee prizes contemporary versatility, Eastman prizes classical depth, etc.
- Pre-college engagement matters: youth orchestras, master classes, competitions, summer programs

**Pre-college music programs:**

- Tanglewood Institute (Boston Symphony's program)
- Aspen Music Festival
- Interlochen Arts Camp
- Brevard Music Center
- Sphinx Performance Academy (for Black/Latino classical musicians)
- Music conservatory pre-college programs (Juilliard Pre-College, Manhattan School Pre-College, etc.)

## Theater auditions

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For students applying to BFA acting programs (Carnegie Mellon, Yale Drama, Juilliard Drama, Tisch, etc.):

**Audition format:**

- Two contrasting monologues (typically one classical, one contemporary)
- Sometimes additional material (a song, a movement piece)
- Live audition, often in cycles (regional auditions in major cities)
- Callbacks for top schools

**The honest framework:**

- BFA acting admissions are highly competitive (~5-10% admit at top programs)
- Audition is THE deciding factor
- Academic profile secondary
- Specific institutional fit varies dramatically: classical training (Juilliard, NCSA), contemporary commercial (Tisch, USC), interdisciplinary (Yale Drama), etc.

**Pre-college engagement:**

- High school theater participation (lead roles, sustained engagement)
- Summer theater programs (Stagedoor Manor, Northwestern Cherubs, Carnegie Mellon Pre-College Drama)
- Acting classes outside school
- Showcase/competition results

## Dance auditions

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For students applying to BFA dance programs:

**Audition format:**

- Live class audition (ballet, modern technique)
- Sometimes choreography solo
- Sometimes interview
- Cycles in major cities

**The honest framework:**

- Dance admissions are physically demanding and competitive
- Audition determines admit; academic profile secondary
- Programs prize different aesthetics — Juilliard contemporary/classical, Tisch commercial/modern, etc.

## Creative writing portfolios

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For students applying to creative writing programs (Iowa Writers' Workshop undergrad equivalents, Columbia, Brown, Hopkins Writing Seminars):

**Portfolio submissions:**

- Typically 10-20 pages of work (poetry, fiction, creative nonfiction)
- Specific page counts vary by school
- Mix of pieces vs single longer work depends on program

**The honest framework:**

- Pure undergrad writing programs are rare; most schools admit to general college
- Writing supplement allows showcasing creative writing
- Quality of writing matters; sustained voice and craft mature beyond age expectations valued

**Recognition that matters:**

- Scholastic Writing Awards (Gold/Silver Key)
- Iowa Young Writers' Studio
- Kenyon Young Writers Workshop
- Sewanee Young Writers' Conference
- Concord Review (for nonfiction)
- Foyle Young Poets (international, free)
- YoungArts (national)

## Liberal arts with arts (the most common scenario)

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

Most arts students don't apply to conservatories — they apply to liberal arts colleges or research universities and submit arts supplement materials.

**Arts supplement strategy:**

- Most top schools accept arts supplements (separate from main application)
- Quality and selectivity matter — submit only if work is strong
- AOs use faculty review for arts supplements
- Strong arts supplement can move admit decision; weak supplement can hurt

**Submission via:**

- Common App arts supplement
- SlideRoom (visual arts)
- School-specific portals

**What to submit:**

- 5-10 strongest pieces (visual arts)
- 1-2 highest-quality recordings (music)
- 1-2 strongest writing samples (creative writing)
- Director's reel or performance footage (theater/dance/film)

**The honest framework:**

- Don't submit if work is mediocre — adds noise without signal
- Quality over quantity
- Each supplement reviewed by faculty, who give detailed feedback to admissions

6. **Acknowledge audition stress.** Sustained preparation matters.
