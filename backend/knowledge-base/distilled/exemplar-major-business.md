## How to think about the business pathway

**[SLM data retrieger for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's grade, intended business major, target schools.
- Pull `schools/*.md` for any business schools the user is considering.

The honest framework for business pathway has three core paths:

**Path 1: Direct undergraduate business admission.** Apply directly to a business school within a university (Wharton at Penn, Ross at Michigan, Haas at Berkeley — though Haas requires internal application after 2 years, McIntire at UVA — same, McDonough at Georgetown, Stern at NYU, Mendoza at Notre Dame, Marshall at USC, McCombs at Texas, Kelley at Indiana).

**Path 2: General undergraduate + MBA later.** Major in anything (econ, math, stats, engineering, liberal arts), then pursue MBA after 3-7 years of work experience. This is the dominant path for top-tier MBA programs (Harvard Business School, Stanford GSB, Wharton MBA, Booth, Kellogg).

**Path 3: Specialty undergraduate business school.** Babson (entrepreneurship-focused), Bentley, Cooper Union (separate model), Bryant.

**Strategic implication:** the right path depends on what the student wants to do.

- **Going into investment banking, consulting, or corporate finance from undergrad:** direct undergrad business admit (Wharton, Ross, Stern, McIntire) provides the strongest pipeline. These programs have direct recruiting relationships with banks and consulting firms.
- **Building a startup or entrepreneurial path:** Babson, Stanford (CS + entrepreneurship culture), Penn (Wharton + general engagement), or general schools with strong startup ecosystems work.
- **Going into business as eventual long-term career, with possible interim career:** general undergrad in econ/math/STEM/liberal arts → 3-7 years of work → MBA is the dominant path.
- **Becoming an academic in business/economics:** general undergrad with strong econ/math foundations → PhD program.

## Direct undergraduate business admission

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's profile for stats and ECs.
- Pull `schools/*.md` for the specific business schools.

The major direct-admit business schools at top universities:

**Wharton (Penn):** the most selective undergrad business program. Looking for students with demonstrated quantitative ability, leadership, and business interest. Strong in finance recruiting. Admit rate ~6-7%.

**Ross (Michigan):** strong overall business program; particularly strong in consulting recruiting. Both freshman direct admit and sophomore application paths. Admit rate for direct-from-HS path ~16%.

**Haas (Berkeley):** internal application after 2 years at Berkeley (you can't apply directly from HS to Haas). Very competitive internal admission; admit rate ~10-15% from Berkeley students who apply.

**McIntire (UVA):** internal application after 2 years at UVA. Very competitive internal admission.

**Stern (NYU):** direct admit from HS; strong NYC recruiting pipeline. Admit rate ~12%.

**McDonough (Georgetown):** direct admit; strong DC connections. Admit rate ~14%.

**Mendoza (Notre Dame):** direct admit; strong Catholic culture; competitive accounting program. Admit rate ~17%.

**Marshall (USC):** direct admit; strong LA connections. Admit rate ~12%.

**McCombs (Texas):** direct admit; strong Texas pipeline. Admit rate for OOS ~20%.

**Kelley (Indiana):** direct admit; strong overall business program; moderate selectivity for OOS ~25%.

**Babson:** direct admit; entrepreneurship-focused; FME program is distinctive; ~23% admit rate. See `exemplar-chat-school-specific.md` for Babson deep dive.

**The application requirements that vary:**

- Some schools require a separate business school essay or supplement (Wharton, McDonough, etc.)
- Some require demonstrated business engagement (Babson particularly)
- Some require math through pre-calculus or calculus
- Most weigh the same general factors as the broader university: GPA, test scores, ECs, essays

## What undergrad business admissions look for

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Cross-reference user's ECs against business-fit signals.
- Recommend Wayfinder verified programs/internships for any gaps.

**Demonstrated business interest and engagement:**

This is where business-school applications differ most from general university applications. AOs at business schools want to see authentic business interest.

What counts as authentic business interest:

- **Started a business** (even small): Etsy shop, lawn care service, tutoring business, consulting for local businesses, etc.
- **Sustained internship in a business:** family business, local business, startup
- **DECA or FBLA national-level participation:** competitive business clubs are recognizable signals
- **Investment club leadership:** managing real or virtual portfolios with sustained engagement
- **Business-adjacent academic work:** independent study in economics, finance, or business
- **Quantitative capability:** strong math/stats grades, AMC/AIME participation, Math Olympiad
- **Entrepreneurship competitions:** local pitch competitions, business plan contests

What doesn't count as authentic business interest:

- "I'm interested in business" without action
- Generic Yale Young Global Scholars or Stanford Pre-Collegiate Studies (pay-to-play)
- "Took an economics class and liked it"
- Family business reference without active engagement

The Wayfinder workflow: cross-reference verified Wayfinder DBs for business-aligned summer programs (LaunchX, CEO Summer, JA programs, NFTE, FBLA/DECA national competitions) that strengthen authentic business signal.

## The MBA path (vs direct undergrad business)

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For students considering the longer-horizon MBA path:

**Top MBA programs:**

- Harvard Business School (HBS), Stanford GSB, Wharton MBA, Booth (Chicago), Kellogg (Northwestern), MIT Sloan, Columbia, Berkeley Haas MBA, NYU Stern MBA, Yale SOM, Tuck (Dartmouth)

**Typical MBA applicant profile:**

- 3-7 years of work experience post-undergrad (typically 5)
- 700+ GMAT or 320+ GRE
- Undergrad GPA 3.5+
- Significant career progression in current role
- Strong recommendations from supervisors and senior leaders
- Clear post-MBA career goals (often ambitious career pivot or acceleration)

**Common pre-MBA careers:**

- Investment banking analyst (2-3 years) → MBA
- Management consulting analyst (2-3 years) → MBA
- Tech (PM, engineer, business operations) → MBA
- Private equity analyst → MBA
- Non-profit management → MBA (less common but real path)
- Military officer → MBA (significant pipeline)
- Founder of startup that didn't work → MBA

**The undergrad implications:**

If MBA is the long-term goal, undergrad strategy is:
- Major in something rigorous and quantitative (econ, math, engineering, CS, stats)
- Earn high GPA (3.7+ at minimum for top MBAs)
- Aim for prestigious entry-level role at top firms (IB analyst, MBB consulting, top tech, top startups)
- Build a coherent career arc that an MBA would clearly accelerate

Undergrad institution prestige matters more for the MBA path than for general business undergrad path because top MBA programs heavily over-represent students from prestige undergrads + top early-career firms.

## Specific career pipelines and what they want

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**Investment banking:**

- Wharton, Stern, Ross, McIntire, Haas have direct pipelines
- Princeton, Harvard, Yale, MIT (general majors with finance interest) also strong
- Want: high GPA, finance interest demonstrated through clubs/internships, strong quantitative skills, leadership, potentially relevant ECs
- Recruiting: starts sophomore year for sophomore summer; full-time recruitment senior fall
- Cross-reference verified Wayfinder finance-related internships

**Management consulting (MBB and other top firms):**

- Top business schools and top general undergrads
- Want: very high GPA, demonstrated leadership, case-style problem-solving capability, strong communication
- Recruiting: junior summer for full-time; senior fall for full-time
- Cross-reference verified Wayfinder consulting/leadership programs

**Tech (product management, technical roles):**

- Top CS programs, top general schools with CS interest, business schools with tech focus
- Want: technical capability, product thinking, leadership in technical projects
- Cross-reference exemplar-major-engineering.md for tech-heavy paths

**Entrepreneurship:**

- Not a single recruiting pipeline; founders come from diverse backgrounds
- Want: demonstrated initiative, founding experience (even small), risk tolerance
- Babson is entrepreneurship-focused; Stanford's startup culture is real; Wharton has strong startup founder pipeline; many other paths work
