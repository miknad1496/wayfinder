The UC system has 9 undergraduate campuses ranging from Berkeley/UCLA (highly selective) to Merced (more accessible). All use the same application (UC Application, separate from Common App) and the same essay set (4 PIQs from 8 prompts). But selectivity, culture, and major strength vary dramatically across the 9 campuses.

**The fundamental insight:** the UC application is one application, but it's effectively 9 different decisions. Each campus makes its own admit decision based on the same application materials. Strong students often get admitted to some UCs and rejected from others.

**The 9 UC undergraduate campuses, roughly ordered by selectivity:**

1. **UC Berkeley** — most selective overall; Engineering and Business (Haas) most competitive. ~10% admit rate.
2. **UCLA** — second most selective; CS, biology, business pre-med most competitive. ~9% admit rate.
3. **UC San Diego** — strong selective; particularly competitive for CS, engineering, biology. ~24% admit rate.
4. **UC Santa Barbara** — selective; College of Creative Studies most selective subset. ~26% admit rate.
5. **UC Davis** — strong on agriculture, biology, applied sciences. ~37% admit rate.
6. **UC Irvine** — particularly strong CS, business, health sciences. Significant Korean community. ~29% admit rate.
7. **UC Santa Cruz** — counter-cultural; rewards self-direction. ~58% admit rate.
8. **UC Riverside** — increasingly investing institutionally; growing programs. ~69% admit rate.
9. **UC Merced** — newest UC, most accessible. ~85% admit rate.

## The UC capped GPA — the most-misunderstood UC mechanic

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

UC uses a unique GPA calculation that differs from most US college applications. Three GPAs are calculated:

**1. Unweighted UC GPA:** all academic courses (a-g requirements) from grades 10-11, with standard 4.0 scale.

**2. Capped Weighted UC GPA:** same courses, with weighting for honors/AP/IB courses, BUT capped at 8 weighted points (4 honors classes' worth of weighting). This is the GPA used most heavily in UC admissions.

**3. Fully Weighted UC GPA:** uncapped weighted version. Some campuses look at this in addition to capped.

**Why the cap matters:** at high schools that offer many AP/honors courses, students can rack up extensive weighting. The UC cap means that taking 8+ AP courses doesn't show up as 5.0+ GPA in admissions. The system was designed to level the playing field across schools with very different course offerings.

**Strategic implications:**

- For students at high schools with many AP options: there's diminishing return after ~6 weighted courses (you've maxed out the cap).
- For students at high schools with few AP options: the cap is largely irrelevant; take what's available and earn As.
- The UC capped GPA tends to be lower than the GPA other applications calculate. Don't be alarmed when your UC GPA looks lower than your school GPA.

**The UC GPA does NOT include 9th grade.** Only 10th and 11th grade academic courses count. This is different from most US applications and helps students who improved after a slow freshman year.

## In-state vs out-of-state UC considerations

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's state.

**For California in-state students:**

- UCs are designed primarily to serve California residents.
- Admit rates are higher for in-state at all UCs (UCLA in-state ~10%, OOS ~6%).
- Tuition is dramatically lower (~$15K vs ~$48K).
- The strategic question is which UCs to apply to (often: all of them, given the same application).

**For out-of-state students:**

- UCs have OOS quotas (UC system policy roughly limits OOS to ~18% of new admits, varies by campus).
- Admit rates are lower than in-state.
- Tuition is full-rate (~$48K + room/board ~$80K total).
- Limited OOS aid; UCs do not generally offer significant aid to OOS students.
- Strategic question: is the UC experience worth the OOS premium? For most OOS students, the answer is "only if the specific UC has unique programmatic offerings or you have specific reason to be in California."

**For international students:**

- Treated as OOS for tuition (full-rate).
- Very limited international aid.
- Same OOS admit rate considerations.
- See `exemplar-aid-international.md` for the international aid landscape.
- For Korean students specifically: UCs (especially UC Irvine, UCLA, UC San Diego) have substantial Korean communities; pull `intl/korea/us-pathway/` for Korean-applicant UC strategy.

## By-campus and by-major selectivity

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's intended major and target UCs.
- Pull `schools/uc-*.md` deep files for major-specific selectivity at each.

UC campuses don't always admit at the campus level only — many campuses admit by major or by college, with significantly different selectivity by major.

**UC Berkeley:**
- College of Letters & Science (general) — campus admit rate
- College of Engineering — much more competitive (~6-8% for OOS)
- Haas School of Business — extremely competitive; usually admits via internal transfer after 2 years
- College of Chemistry, EECS — very competitive

**UCLA:**
- College of Letters and Science — campus admit rate
- Henry Samueli School of Engineering — more competitive
- School of the Arts and Architecture — competitive (audition/portfolio)
- Pre-major in CS — competitive even outside engineering school

**UC San Diego:**
- Six undergraduate colleges (Revelle, Muir, Marshall, Warren, Roosevelt, Sixth) — each with its own GE requirements
- Competitive for CS, engineering, biology, cognitive science
- UCSD allows you to rank college preferences in application

**UC Davis:**
- College of Letters & Science — campus admit rate
- College of Agricultural and Environmental Sciences — strong reputation, varied selectivity
- College of Engineering — competitive
- College of Biological Sciences — competitive

**UC Irvine:**
- Bren School of ICS (Computer Science) — very competitive
- Paul Merage School of Business — competitive
- School of Biological Sciences — competitive

**Strategic implication:** if you're applying for a competitive major at a competitive UC, consider applying with a different major and switching after admission (where allowed). Some students apply to UC Irvine ICS as "biological sciences" (more accessible) and switch to ICS after admission, for example. Verify school-by-school whether internal major switching is allowed.

## The UC application timing and structure

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**Key dates:**

- **Application opens:** August 1 of senior year
- **Application deadline:** November 30 of senior year (for fall admission the following year)
- **Decisions released:** March (UCs notify on a rolling basis March 1 - March 31, generally)
- **SIR (Statement of Intent to Register) deadline:** May 1

**Application components:**

- 4 PIQs (350 words each) — see `exemplar-uc-piq.md`
- 20-activity list (slightly longer than Common App)
- Up to 5 awards/honors
- A-G course requirements verification
- Self-reported transcript

**No supplements at most UCs.** You write the 4 PIQs once and they go to all UCs you apply to. Some specific majors (architecture, music, etc.) have additional portfolio/audition requirements.

**No demonstrated interest tracking at UCs.** They don't track visits, emails, or interest signals. Apply broadly without worrying about whether you've shown enough interest.

**One application, multiple UCs:** unlike Common App + supplements model, the UC application goes to all UCs you select with no additional materials. Applying to all 9 vs applying to 1 has the same writing burden — only the application fee differs ($70/campus or fee waivers for income-eligible).

**Strategic implication:** for in-state students, applying to multiple UCs (often 4-6) is the dominant strategy because the marginal cost is low. For OOS students, more strategic selection makes sense given the OOS premium.

## The right UC application list for different applicant profiles

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's profile (state, GPA, major, financials).

**For California in-state, strong stats:**

Apply broadly across UCs. Reach: Berkeley, UCLA. Match: UCSD, UCSB, Davis, Irvine. Likely: Santa Cruz, Riverside. Safety: Merced (essentially open admit for in-state).

**For California in-state, moderate stats:**

Skip Berkeley/UCLA reach (low probability without other distinguishing factors). Match: Davis, Irvine, UCSD (perhaps), UCSB. Likely: Santa Cruz, Riverside. Safety: Merced.

**For OOS strong stats:**

Strategic selection. Berkeley and UCLA are very competitive for OOS at any stat level. UCSD and UCSB are accessible but pricey. Davis and Irvine offer strong programs at slightly more accessible OOS admit rates.

The honest tradeoff for OOS: $80K/year for UC experience vs comparable private universities with potential aid. For students choosing UCs over private universities OOS, the reason should be programmatic (specific major strength, location, fit) rather than generic prestige.

**For international students:**

Similar to OOS calculation. UC system has growing international population but limited aid. The right list construction often emphasizes UC Irvine, UCLA, UCSD (which have strong international communities) over Berkeley (very competitive for international).
