The honest framework:

**The application is ~6 months of sustained work.** Most students underestimate the time commitment.

**Critical milestones (working backwards from January 1 RD deadlines):**

- **Junior summer:** identify school list, draft Common App essay
- **September of senior year:** Common App essay polished, supplements drafted
- **October:** finalize ED/EA applications (typical Nov 1 deadlines)
- **November-December:** finalize RD applications
- **January 1:** typical RD deadline at most selective schools
- **February-March:** mid-year grade reports, supplements for late-deadline schools
- **March-April:** decisions arrive; spring decisions
- **May 1:** SIR deadline

## The senior summer to senior fall sequence

**Junior summer (June-August):**

This is when strong applications get built. Most students underuse this period.

- **Finalize school list.** Use Wayfinder school deep files to research and decide.
- **Draft Common App essay.** Multiple drafts. Use Essay Reviewer.
- **Begin researching school supplements.** Pull school deep files for each.
- **Take/retake SAT/ACT if needed.**

- **Reach out to recommenders if you didn't already in junior spring.**

**Senior September:**

- Common App essay finalized
- Supplements for ED/EA schools drafted
- Recommenders confirmed and provided materials

- FAFSA and CSS Profile filed (if applicable)

**Senior October:**

- ED/EA applications submitted (typical Nov 1 deadline; aim for Oct 25 at latest)

**Senior November:**

- ED/EA submitted

**Senior December:**

- RD applications mostly complete

**Senior January:**

- Final RD applications submitted
- Mid-year grade reports sent

## Rolling admissions vs deadline

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull `schools/*.md` for each target school's deadline policy.

**Rolling admissions:**

- Some schools (many state flagships, some private universities) accept and review applications throughout admissions cycle
- Apply EARLY in cycle for higher admit chances
- Earlier applicants get earlier decisions
- Beyond optimal window, late applicants face significantly lower admit chances

**Examples of rolling schools:**
- Most Big 10 state schools (UMich is exception — has deadlines)
- Many SUNY schools
- Indiana University, Penn State, Michigan State
- Some private universities (Tulane, etc.)

**Deadline admissions:**

- Most selective private universities
- Specific deadlines for ED, EA, RD, ED2
- Decisions arrive at single-time releases

## Managing multiple application deadlines

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For students applying to many schools:

**Build a calendar.** List every school with:
- Application opens
- Application due
- Materials needed (recs, transcripts, scores, financial aid forms)
- Supplement essay topics and word counts
- Decision date

**Group by deadline.** Schools with similar deadlines can be worked simultaneously.

**Prioritize ED/EA preparation.** These come first; quality of November submission affects everything else.

**Don't submit early.** Many schools accept submissions early but it doesn't help admissions. Better to submit polished application closer to deadline than early but rough.

**Don't submit late.** Same-day rushed submissions produce errors. Aim for 2-3 days before deadline.

**Use Common App "submit" tracking.** Common App shows which schools have received your materials.

## Common timing mistakes

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

1. **Underestimating Common App essay time.** Most students underestimate. Plan for 4-6 weeks of drafting and revision.

2. **Asking recommenders late.** Late spring junior or early fall senior is right window. Mid-October is too late at many schools.

3. **Rushing supplements.** Supplements matter. A polished supplement at Yale ED beats unpolished supplements at Yale + Stanford + 5 other schools.

4. **Ignoring financial aid timeline.** FAFSA / CSS Profile have school-specific priority deadlines, often before application deadlines.

5. **Forgetting senior year matters.** Senior fall grades visible to schools via mid-year report; light senior year reads as senioritis.

6. **Missing scholarship-specific deadlines.** Many merit aid scholarships have separate (often earlier) deadlines than admission.

7. **Procrastinating on test score sending.** Test scores need to be officially sent from College Board / ACT; takes 2-3 weeks for processing.
