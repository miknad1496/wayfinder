## How to think about athletics recruitment

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's grade, sport, current level, target schools.

The honest framework:

**Athletics recruitment is a different admissions track.** Recruited athletes at all levels (from D1 athletic scholarships to Ivy League "likely letters" to D3 coach-supported admissions) follow a process largely separate from regular admissions.

**The four NCAA divisions:**

- **D1:** highest level. Athletic scholarships available (full or partial). Major commitment. ~351 schools.
- **D2:** competitive but smaller scale than D1. Partial scholarships available. ~308 schools.
- **D3:** competitive athletics without athletic scholarships (financial aid through standard need + merit). ~439 schools.
- **NAIA:** smaller association of mostly smaller schools. Athletic scholarships available.

**Plus:**
- **Ivy League athletics** — D1 conference but Ivy League schools don't offer athletic scholarships (institutional rule). Aid through standard need-based.
- **Service academies** — recruit athletes; aid through service-academy-specific structure (free education + service obligation).

## The recruiting timeline (NCAA D1/D2)

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

NCAA recruiting rules vary by sport but general timeline:

**Freshman/sophomore year of HS:**
- Coaches can watch athletes at events but most direct contact restricted
- Athletes can attend camps and showcases
- Build athletic resume — measurable performance metrics

**June 15 after sophomore year:**
- For most sports, this is when D1 coaches can begin direct contact (calls, emails, texts)
- For some sports (football, basketball), different dates apply
- Athletes can call coaches at any time

**Junior year:**
- Heavy contact period with college coaches
- Official visits become possible (limited per athlete)
- Verbal commitments can happen
- Critical year for competitive performance

**Senior year:**
- National Letter of Intent (NLI) signing periods (specific to each sport)
- Official commitments
- Final official visits

**For Ivy League and academic-heavy schools:**
- Pre-read process where coach gets indication from admissions whether athlete would be admissible
- Likely letter from admissions before official admit decision
- Different timeline than NCAA athletic recruitment generally

## What recruiters look for

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**Athletic talent (the obvious factor):**

- Performance metrics specific to the sport (times, distances, statistics, footage)
- Competition level (state, regional, national)
- Recognition (rankings, awards)
- Coachability and coach references

**Academic qualifications (varies by school):**

- For service academies and Ivies: academics matter heavily
- For top D3 (Williams, Amherst, Tufts): athletes still need to be academically competitive
- For most D1: NCAA Eligibility Center minimums + school-specific requirements
- "Academic Index" (AI) at Ivy schools quantifies academic profile; recruited athletes need to fall within acceptable AI range

**Character signals:**

- Coach recommendations
- Captain experience
- Off-field maturity signals
- Social media (coaches check)

## Ivy League athletics specifically

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull `schools/*.md` for Ivy schools the user is targeting.

The Ivy League has unique athletics admissions:

**No athletic scholarships.** Aid is need-based only. Recruited athletes pay what their family's financial situation determines.

**The "Likely Letter" process:**

- Coach identifies recruit
- Coach submits academic credentials to admissions for "pre-read"
- If pre-read positive, athlete and admissions develop relationship
- Likely letter (essentially advance acceptance) issued in October-December of senior year
- Formal acceptance follows in regular admit cycle

**Academic Index (AI):**

The Ivy League uses an Academic Index formula combining GPA and test scores to ensure recruited athletes are within reasonable academic range of student body.

- Each school has a recruit AI minimum (varies by school)
- Recruited athletes' average AI must equal or exceed school's overall student average AI minus a specified amount
- Schools track AI carefully across recruiting classes

**The implications:**

Recruited Ivy athletes have somewhat lower academic profiles than non-athlete admits but still need to be academically competitive. A recruited athlete with 3.7 GPA and 1450 SAT may be admissible to Yale where a non-athlete with same profile would be a long shot.

## D3 athletics

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

D3 doesn't offer athletic scholarships but coach support can significantly affect admissions:

**At top academic D3 schools (Williams, Amherst, Tufts, Bowdoin, Wesleyan, Middlebury, etc.):**

- Coach support = "tipped" admit chances at otherwise-similar academic profile
- Coaches have recruiting "slots" with admissions
- Process similar to Ivy: pre-read, recruitment, possibly likely letter equivalent

**For non-recruited D3 walk-ons:**

- Apply through standard admissions
- Try out for team after admission
- Many D3 teams are largely walk-on rosters (especially less-popular sports)

## How to be recruited

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For athletes wanting to be recruited:

**Step 1: Build measurable athletic profile.**

- Compete at state level minimum
- For most sports, ranked nationally helps
- Document everything (times, statistics, video)
- Compete at events where coaches scout

**Step 2: Create athletic profile / highlight video.**

- 2-4 minute highlight video showing skills
- Include game footage with you identifiable
- For team sports, include multiple game situations
- Post on YouTube unlisted, share link

**Step 3: Identify target schools and reach out.**

- Email coaches at target schools
- Brief intro: name, year, sport, position, key stats, video link
- Specific reasons you're interested in their program
- Academic profile briefly

**Step 4: Attend camps and showcases at target schools.**

- Many sports have college-hosted camps where coaches evaluate
- Showcases (regional events) give exposure to multiple coaches
- Cost can be significant; prioritize schools you have realistic interest

**Step 5: Develop relationships with coaches.**

- Sustained contact, not one-and-done
- Ask thoughtful questions about program fit
- Show you're seriously considering the school, not just the sport

**Step 6: Manage academic profile.**

- Recruited athlete still needs to be academically admissible
- Keep grades up
- Take competitive courses
- Manage standardized testing

## When athletics recruitment doesn't work out

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

For athletes who don't end up recruited at desired level:

**Walk-on options:**

- Many D1 and D2 teams accept walk-on tryouts
- Make team contact you advance ("preferred walk-on")
- D3 teams are largely walk-on
- Senior year/post-acceptance walk-on contact common

**Down-shifting:**

- Many D1-caliber athletes thrive at D2 or D3 level
- Smaller school may be better academic and social fit anyway
- Athletic experience often more rewarding at lower division (more playing time, closer team, less commercialized)

**Stopping competitive athletics:**

- Sometimes the right call is to focus on academics, club sports, or other interests
- Honest reassessment of how much time/energy athletics has been consuming
