## How to think about waitlists

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull user's confirmed admits + waitlist offers + target schools.
- Pull deep files for waitlist schools if available.

**The honest framework starts with the reality of waitlist conversion rates:**

Waitlist conversion (the percentage of waitlisted students ultimately admitted) varies dramatically by school and by year. General patterns:

- **HYP and similar (Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT, Stanford):** typically <2% conversion. Most years, almost no one comes off waitlist.
- **Top 10-20 schools:** typically 1-10% conversion, varies dramatically year to year
- **Top 20-50 schools:** 5-20% typical
- **Less selective schools:** waitlists smaller and convert more often

**Why the variation is so high:**

Waitlist movement depends on the school's yield (% of admitted students who enroll). If yield is exactly as predicted, the school doesn't go to waitlist. If yield is below prediction (more admits than expected decline), the school goes to waitlist to fill seats.

Some years are "high yield" years (few admits decline) — almost no waitlist movement.
Some years are "low yield" years — significant waitlist movement.

There's no way to predict which year you're applying in.

**The honest implication:** treat waitlist as a long shot. Plan as if you'll attend your best confirmed admit. If a waitlist comes through, that's bonus.

## What to do when waitlisted

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**Step 1: Decide if you actually want to stay on the waitlist.**

Not every waitlist is worth staying on. Some questions:

- Would I genuinely choose this school over my best confirmed admit?
- If admitted off waitlist, would I make the switch (forfeiting deposit at confirmed school)?
- Is this school worth several months of uncertainty hanging over my summer plans?

If yes to all three, stay on waitlist. If no to any, decline the waitlist gracefully (most schools provide an opt-in form; not opting in declines).

**Step 2: Commit to your best confirmed admit.**

By May 1, you must SIR (Statement of Intent to Register) at the school you'll attend if no waitlist comes through. Don't delay this decision — schools don't extend deadlines for waitlist uncertainty.

**Step 3: Send a Letter of Continued Interest (LOCI) to the waitlist school.**

LOCI is a brief letter (typically 1 page or under) reaffirming interest, sharing any new accomplishments since application, and articulating specific institutional fit.

See LOCI structure below.

**Step 4: Continue strong senior year.**

Bad senior year grades can affect waitlist consideration. Maintain rigor through final transcripts.

**Step 5: Wait.**

Waitlist movement typically happens between May 1 and June or July. Some schools continue admitting off waitlist into August. Be patient; don't pester.

## How to write the LOCI (Letter of Continued Interest)

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull `schools/school-<slug>.md` for institutional context to weave into LOCI.
- Recommend Essay Reviewer for drafting.

The LOCI structure that works:

**Paragraph 1: Re-affirm enthusiasm specifically.**

Brief opening that confirms continued interest in the school. NOT generic "I love your school." Specific: "[School] remains my first choice because [specific institutional features that drew you originally]."

**Paragraph 2: Update with new substantive content.**

What's happened since you applied that strengthens your case?
- New academic accomplishments (acceptance to selective summer program, completion of difficult senior project, awards)
- New extracurricular accomplishments (leadership roles, competition results, founded organizations)
- Continued strong grades (mention if final senior grades are strong)
- Specific intellectual development (new interest deepened, new project started)

This is the heart of the LOCI. New material is what gives admission committee something to work with.

**Paragraph 3: Specific institutional fit detail.**

Pull from the school deep file. Reference specific programs, courses, communities you'd engage with. NOT generic "I want to attend X" — specific "I want to study with Professor Y in Z department because their work on [specific topic] aligns with my [specific work]."

**Paragraph 4: Commitment statement.**

If you would commit to attend if admitted, say so explicitly. "If admitted off the waitlist, I will absolutely commit to enrolling at [school]." This commitment is the strongest single sentence in a LOCI — schools admit off waitlist only when they're confident the student will enroll.

DON'T make this commitment if you wouldn't actually commit. Schools track commit-and-decline patterns and counselors notice.

**Length:** 1 page maximum. Concise + specific beats long + general.

## What NOT to do with waitlist

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**1. Send multiple letters / emails / packages.** One LOCI is appropriate. Multiple submissions read as desperate.

**2. Have parents reach out to admissions office.** Backfires. Admissions doesn't want parental advocacy.

**3. Submit additional recommendation letters or supplementary materials beyond what the school requests.** Adds to AO workload without adding signal.

**4. Pester admissions office with status inquiries.** They'll let you know.

**5. Ignore the waitlist entirely.** If you want it, send a LOCI. Silent waitlist applicants get admitted last (if at all) because schools assume they're not interested anymore.

**6. Stay on multiple waitlists hoping one converts.** Spreads your interest signal thin. Pick the 1-2 waitlists where you'd genuinely commit and focus on those.

## Specific waitlist strategies by school type

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull school deep files for institutional waitlist patterns.

**Highly selective private universities (HYP, Stanford, MIT):**

- Very low conversion. Most years, no waitlist movement.
- LOCI rarely changes outcome but doesn't hurt.
- Only stay on waitlist if you'd genuinely choose this school over confirmed admits.

**Top 10-20 schools:**

- Variable conversion. Worth sending LOCI.
- Schools with stronger yield protection may move waitlist more (Northeastern, Vanderbilt historically have higher waitlist movement than peer schools).

**Top 20-50 schools:**

- Generally more waitlist movement than top schools.
- LOCI more likely to make a difference.

**Top LACs:**

- Variable; depends on yield year.
- Smaller class sizes mean fewer waitlist seats but proportionally similar movement.

**State universities (out-of-state applicants particularly):**

- Often more waitlist movement than peer privates because state policy may shift OOS quotas.
- Less personalized review of LOCI.

## When waitlist comes through

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

If admitted off waitlist after May 1:

**Step 1:** Confirm the offer in writing.

**Step 2:** Compare to your committed school carefully. The decision is now real and irreversible (in most cases).

**Step 3:** If choosing waitlist school, withdraw from committed school formally and forfeit deposit.

**Step 4:** Notify other schools you've been considering (other waitlists you're on, etc.).

**Step 5:** Move forward. Don't second-guess once decided.

The honest framework: most students who get off waitlist are genuinely happy with the outcome. The angst of waiting is often worse than the actual decision.
