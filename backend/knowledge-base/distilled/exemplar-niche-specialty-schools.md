## US Service academies (USNA, USMA, USAFA, USCGA, USMMA)

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull `schools/school-usna.md`, `school-usma.md`, `school-usafa.md`, etc.

**The five US service academies:**

- **USNA (US Naval Academy)** — Annapolis, MD; commissioning Navy and Marine Corps officers
- **USMA (US Military Academy)** — West Point, NY; commissioning Army officers
- **USAFA (US Air Force Academy)** — Colorado Springs, CO; commissioning Air Force and Space Force officers
- **USCGA (US Coast Guard Academy)** — New London, CT; commissioning Coast Guard officers (does NOT require Congressional nomination)
- **USMMA (US Merchant Marine Academy)** — Kings Point, NY; commissioning Merchant Marine officers and military reserve

**The deal:**

- Free education (tuition, room, board, books, monthly stipend)
- 5-year active-duty service obligation post-graduation
- Total scholarship value over 4 years: ~$500K+
- Reserve commitment continues after active service

**Admission requirements (USMA, USNA, USAFA, USMMA — USCGA different):**

1. **Congressional nomination** — typically from your US Senator or House Representative. Apply directly to their offices early junior year. Each member can nominate limited number per academy per year.
2. **Strong academic profile** — typical SAT 1300+, GPA 3.7+, rigor expected
3. **Fitness assessment** — Candidate Fitness Assessment (push-ups, sit-ups, run, etc.)
4. **Medical evaluation** — DoDMERB physical examination
5. **Character and leadership demonstration** — recommendations, ECs, interviews
6. **Application essay** — typically focused on motivation for military service

**Strategic timing:**

- Junior year: explore service academies, attend Summer Seminar (highly competitive, similar to admissions process), engage in NJROTC/JROTC/CAP/Sea Cadets if available, build athletic profile
- Junior spring/summer: apply for Congressional nomination (multiple offices possible)
- Senior fall: complete academy application, fitness assessment, medical, interviews
- Senior winter/spring: decisions arrive

**The honest insight:**

Service academy admissions is highly selective (USMA admit rate ~10-12%, similar to top private universities). The 5-year service obligation is real and substantial. Going to a service academy is choosing a military career path, not just a free education. Students unsure about military service should NOT apply.

**Cross-reference:**

For students interested in military but uncertain about full service academy commitment:
- ROTC scholarships at civilian universities (4-year scholarships at any participating university; service obligation 4-8 years)
- Military colleges (Citadel, VMI, Norwich, Texas A&M Corps of Cadets) — military culture without full DoD commission requirement

- Parsons School of Design (NYC) — fashion, design
- MICA (Maryland Institute College of Art)
- SCAD (Savannah College of Art and Design)

**The honest framework:**

Conservatory admissions is almost entirely about audition/portfolio. Academic profile matters but secondary. Your audition is your application.

For students with strong audition AND strong academic profile, choosing between conservatory and university with arts is a real decision:
- Conservatory: deeper artistic immersion, smaller community, narrower educational scope, professional pipeline
- University with arts: broader education, more options if interests shift, larger community, less intense artistic immersion

## Women's colleges

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**
- Pull `schools/school-wellesley.md`, `school-smith.md`, `school-bryn-mawr.md`, `school-mount-holyoke.md`, `school-barnard.md`.

**The Seven Sisters** (historically; some no longer all-women):
- Wellesley, Smith, Mount Holyoke, Bryn Mawr, Barnard (still women's colleges)
- Vassar (became co-ed 1969)
- Radcliffe (absorbed into Harvard)

**The honest framework:**

Women's colleges have distinctive admissions and culture. Strategic students should engage with the women's college mission in their applications.

**Modern women's college policies:**

- Generally admit women, transgender women, non-binary students assigned female at birth
- Specific policies vary by school (Smith, Mount Holyoke have explicit trans-inclusive policies; Wellesley has policy on trans women applicants)
- Cross-reference school deep files for current policy
- See also exemplar-chat-edge-cases.md for trans-supportive school context

**Why students choose women's colleges:**

- Distinctive academic culture (often more rigorous than coed peer schools)
- Leadership opportunities (no male peers competing for student leadership)
- Strong alumna networks
- Specific institutional traditions and community

**Application strategy:**

- "Why women's college" should be specific to that institution's distinctive features, not generic women's-college appeal
- Engage with the school's specific traditions, history, and culture
- Pre-college engagement with women's leadership programs strengthens fit
- Consider application to multiple women's colleges if interest is genuine

**Cross-college consortium opportunities:**

- Smith / Mount Holyoke / Hampshire / Amherst / UMass — Five-College Consortium
- Bryn Mawr / Haverford / Swarthmore / Penn — Quaker Consortium (cross-registration)
- Barnard / Columbia — full integration with Columbia University

## Religious-affiliated colleges

**[SLM data retrieval for this exemplar]**

**Notable religious-affiliated schools:**

- **Catholic:** Notre Dame, Georgetown, Boston College, Holy Cross, Villanova, Fordham, Loyola Marymount, Catholic University, Saint Louis University, Marquette
- **Mormon:** BYU
- **Jesuit:** Georgetown, BC, Loyola, Marquette, Fordham, etc.
- **Other Christian:** Wheaton (IL), Calvin College, Pepperdine, Baylor, TCU
- **Jewish:** Yeshiva University, Brandeis (originally Jewish-founded but secular)

**The honest framework:**

Religious-affiliated schools vary dramatically in how religious culture pervades student experience:

- **Strongly religious culture:** BYU, Wheaton — significant code-of-conduct expectations, religion required in coursework
- **Religious roots, secular culture:** Notre Dame, Georgetown, BC — Catholic identity present but doesn't dominate; many non-Catholic students; coursework includes some religion options
- **Historically religious, fully secular now:** Yale, Princeton, Brown — historical religious roots no longer functionally affect culture

**Application strategy at religious schools:**

- Authentic religious engagement strengthens application at strongly religious schools
- For BYU specifically: Mormon faith/conduct alignment important; ecclesiastical endorsement required
- For Catholic schools: Catholic faith engagement helpful but not required; service orientation valued
- Religion essays welcome at religious schools, more tentative at secular peer schools
