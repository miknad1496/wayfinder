# AP German Language and Culture — Overview — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this exam.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Themes (six):** Family/Community, Identity, Beauty/Aesthetics, Science/Tech, Global Challenges, Contemporary Life — same structure as other AP language exams.
- **Speaking world:** Germany, Austria, Switzerland (German Swiss areas), Liechtenstein, parts of Belgium/Italy/Luxembourg.

## Big Ideas

1. **German** spoken by ~95 million native speakers; major in EU.
2. **Cases:** German has 4 noun cases (Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ, Genitiv) — like Latin.
3. **Word order:** verb-second (V2) in main clauses; verb-final in subordinate.
4. **Cultural icons:** Bach, Beethoven, Goethe, Einstein, BMW/Mercedes/VW.
5. **Modern Germany:** unified 1990 (East+West); EU leader; refugee policy debates.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **German cases:**
  - **Nominativ:** subject ("Der Mann ist groß").
  - **Akkusativ:** direct object ("Ich sehe den Mann").
  - **Dativ:** indirect object ("Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch").
  - **Genitiv:** possession ("das Buch des Mannes" — increasingly replaced by "von dem Mann").
- **Articles change with case AND gender:**
  - **Masculine:** der/den/dem/des.
  - **Feminine:** die/die/der/der.
  - **Neuter:** das/das/dem/des.
  - **Plural:** die/die/den/der.
- **Word order:**
  - **V2 in main clauses:** verb in 2nd position. *Heute gehe ich zur Schule.* (Today go I to school.)
  - **V-final in subordinate:** *...weil ich müde bin* (...because I tired am).
- **Compound words:** German loves long compounds. *Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaftskapitänsmütze* = "Danube steamship company captain's cap."
- **Family vocabulary:** Vater, Mutter, Bruder, Schwester, Großvater (Opa), Großmutter (Oma).
- **Cultural figures:**
  - **Composers:** Bach, Beethoven, Brahms, Mozart (Austrian), Wagner.
  - **Writers:** Goethe (*Faust*), Schiller, Kafka (Czech-German), Mann (Thomas, Heinrich), Brecht.
  - **Philosophers:** Kant, Hegel, Marx, Nietzsche, Heidegger.
  - **Scientists:** Einstein, Heisenberg, Planck.
- **Holidays:**
  - **Weihnachten (Christmas):** especially Christmas Eve (Heiliger Abend).
  - **Ostern (Easter).**
  - **Tag der Deutschen Einheit (Oct 3):** German Reunification Day.
  - **Karneval/Fasching:** pre-Lent (Cologne, Munich).
- **Cuisine:** Brot (bread varieties), Wurst (sausages), Bier (beer purity law), Sauerkraut, Bretzel.

### Adds for [4]

- **German-speaking countries:**
  - **Germany (Deutschland):** ~83M.
  - **Austria (Österreich):** ~9M.
  - **Switzerland (Schweiz):** German Swiss areas; ~5M speakers.
- **History:**
  - **Holy Roman Empire** (medieval to 1806).
  - **Unified Germany** under Bismarck (1871).
  - **WWI, Weimar Republic, Nazi Germany, WWII.**
  - **Divided 1949-1990** (West/East).
  - **Reunification 1990.**
  - **EU leader today.**

### Adds for [5]

- **Why German verb structure tricky:** verb in 2nd position with subject often after; verb at end in subordinate clauses; separable verbs split.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — German articles

For "the man" in each case:
(a) Nominativ.
(b) Akkusativ.
(c) Dativ.

- (a) **der Mann.**
- (b) **den Mann.**
- (c) **dem Mann.**

### Example 2 [4] — Word order

How would you say "I learn German because it is interesting"?
- ***Ich lerne Deutsch, weil es interessant ist.***
- **Note:** in subordinate clause (weil = because), verb (ist) goes to end.

### Example 3 [5] — Reunification

What was German Reunification?
- **East Germany (GDR) + West Germany (FRG)** had been separate since 1949.
- **Berlin Wall fell Nov 9, 1989.**
- **Formal reunification Oct 3, 1990.**
- **Now celebrated as Tag der Deutschen Einheit.**
- **Major political/economic project** of integration; ongoing East/West differences (income, voting patterns).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **4 cases** — must memorize article/adjective endings.
2. **Word order V2** in main clauses (verb second).
3. **Verb to end** in subordinate clauses.
4. **Compound words can be very long.**

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Deutsch hat vier Fälle (Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ, Genitiv), die mit der Funktion eines Substantivs im Satz wechseln; Artikel und Adjektive ändern sich entsprechend."
2. "Im Hauptsatz steht das Verb an zweiter Stelle (V2): 'Heute gehe ich zur Schule.' Im Nebensatz wandert das Verb ans Ende: '...weil ich müde bin.'"
3. "Die Wiedervereinigung Deutschlands am 3. Oktober 1990 — nach dem Mauerfall am 9. November 1989 — bleibt ein zentrales nationales Ereignis, gefeiert als Tag der Deutschen Einheit."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Exam

*Master 4 cases. Master V2 word order. Master Reunification. Master cultural figures (Goethe, Beethoven). Master family vocabulary.*
