# AP French Language and Culture — Unit 1: Les Familles et les Communautés — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Theme:** Family Structures, Customs, Citizenship, Education, Social Networks
- **Where this theme appears on the exam:** Authentic French-language sources from France, Quebec, francophone Africa, Caribbean.

## Big Ideas

1. **France-centric vs francophone diversity** — France is one of many.
2. **Francophone world:** France, Belgium, Switzerland, Quebec, ~25 African countries, Caribbean (Haiti, Martinique, Guadeloupe), Pacific.
3. **French family** structures evolving (PACS civil unions, blended families).
4. **Education:** baccalauréat, grandes écoles.
5. **Citizenship** in France: secular republicanism (laïcité); immigration debates.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Vocabulary:**
  - **La famille:** parents, enfants, frères, sœurs, grands-parents, oncle, tante, cousin/cousine.
  - **Familles modernes:** familles monoparentales, recomposées, PACS (Pacte Civil de Solidarité), mariage pour tous (same-sex marriage, 2013).
  - **La communauté:** voisins, citoyen, citoyenneté, banlieue, quartier.
  - **L'éducation:** école primaire, collège, lycée, baccalauréat (bac), université, grande école, classe préparatoire (prépa).
- **French education distinctives:**
  - **Baccalauréat (bac):** national exam at end of lycée.
  - **Grandes écoles:** elite institutions (Polytechnique, ENS, HEC, Sciences Po).
  - **Public education** free through university (modest fees).
- **Francophone world:**
  - **France** + Belgium, Switzerland (partially) — European.
  - **Quebec** (Canada).
  - **West Africa:** Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, etc.
  - **Central Africa:** DR Congo, Cameroon, Gabon, etc.
  - **North Africa:** Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria (also Arabic).
  - **Caribbean:** Haiti, Martinique, Guadeloupe.
  - **Indian Ocean:** Madagascar, Réunion.
  - **Pacific:** New Caledonia, French Polynesia.
- **Laïcité:** French secularism; strict separation of religion and state.

### Adds for [4]

- **PACS (1999):** civil union; broader than marriage initially.
- **Mariage pour tous (2013):** same-sex marriage.
- **Citoyenneté française:** can be acquired by birth in France (jus soli) or naturalization.
- **Banlieue:** French suburbs; often associated with social problems but not monolithic.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why francophone identity complex:**
  - **Colonial legacy** in Africa, Caribbean.
  - **Tensions** between France and former colonies.
  - **Quebec's distinct identity** within Canada.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Vocabulary

Translate to French:
(a) high school.
(b) grandfather.
(c) blended family.

- (a) **lycée.**
- (b) **grand-père.**
- (c) **famille recomposée.**

### Example 2 [4] — Education

What is the baccalauréat?
- **National exam at end of lycée** (typically age 18).
- **Required for university entrance.**
- **Different streams** (général, technologique, professionnel).
- **Major life milestone** in France.

### Example 3 [5] — Francophone diversity

Compare French in France and Quebec.
- **Pronunciation, vocabulary differ** (Quebec preserves some older forms).
- **Quebec uses anglicisms differently** (some adopted; some resisted).
- **Quebec French has distinct accent.**
- **Examples:**
  - "Char" (car) Quebec vs "voiture" France.
  - "Magasiner" (to shop) Quebec vs "faire les courses" France.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **France ≠ all francophone.** Many distinct francophone cultures.
2. **Laïcité is unique** to France's strict secularism.
3. **Bac is exam,** not degree.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Le baccalauréat marque la fin du lycée et constitue le passage obligé pour l'enseignement supérieur en France; ses différentes filières (général, technologique, professionnel) orientent les parcours futurs."
2. "Le monde francophone dépasse largement la France : du Québec à l'Afrique de l'Ouest, des Caraïbes au Pacifique, plus de 300 millions de personnes parlent français, héritage à la fois culturel et colonial."
3. "La laïcité française — la séparation stricte entre religion et État — distingue le modèle républicain français et alimente les débats actuels sur le port de symboles religieux dans l'espace public."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Theme

*Master French education system (bac, grandes écoles). Master francophone world geography. Master laïcité. Master family vocabulary.*
