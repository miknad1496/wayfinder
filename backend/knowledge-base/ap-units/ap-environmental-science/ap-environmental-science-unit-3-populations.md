# AP Environmental Science — Unit 3: Populations — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP Environmental Science exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** population characteristics; carrying capacity; population growth (exponential, logistic); human population (DTM, age structure, total fertility rate); Earth's carrying capacity for humans.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Logistic vs exponential growth tested constantly. Carrying capacity (K). Demographic Transition Model (DTM) overlaps with AP Human Geo.

## Big Ideas

1. **Populations grow exponentially** when resources unlimited, **logistically** when limited.
2. **Carrying capacity (K)** is max population an environment can sustainably support.
3. **r-strategists** (boom-bust) and **K-strategists** (stable near K) follow different patterns.
4. **Human population** follows demographic transition as countries develop.
5. **Total fertility rate (TFR)** below 2.1 = eventual population decline.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Population characteristics:**
  - **Size:** number of individuals.
  - **Density:** individuals per unit area.
  - **Distribution:** clumped, uniform, random.
  - **Age structure:** proportion in different age classes.
  - **Sex ratio.**
- **Population growth models:**
  - **Exponential growth (J-curve):**
    - **dN/dt = rN** (rate of change = intrinsic growth rate × population).
    - Unlimited resources.
    - Rapid; doubles repeatedly.
    - **Examples:** bacteria in petri dish, invasive species early.
  - **Logistic growth (S-curve):**
    - **dN/dt = rN(K-N)/K**.
    - K = carrying capacity.
    - Growth slows as approaches K.
    - Levels off at K.
    - **Examples:** most natural populations near equilibrium.
- **Carrying capacity (K):**
  - Maximum population environment can sustainably support.
  - Determined by **limiting factors**: food, water, space, predation, disease.
  - **Population overshoots K** can cause crash.
- **Limiting factors:**
  - **Density-dependent:** intensify as population grows (disease, competition, predation).
  - **Density-independent:** affect regardless of density (weather, natural disasters).
- **r-strategists (review):**
  - **Many offspring; little parental care; short lifespan.**
  - **Boom and bust** populations.
  - Examples: insects, weeds, mice.
- **K-strategists (review):**
  - **Few offspring; high parental care; long lifespan.**
  - **Stable near K.**
  - Examples: elephants, whales, humans.
- **Survivorship curves:**
  - **Type I:** high survival until old age (humans, large mammals).
  - **Type II:** constant mortality (rodents, birds).
  - **Type III:** high early mortality, few survive to adulthood (fish, insects).
- **Population growth metrics:**
  - **Crude Birth Rate (CBR):** births per 1,000 per year.
  - **Crude Death Rate (CDR):** deaths per 1,000 per year.
  - **Natural increase rate (NIR):** (CBR − CDR) / 10 = % growth per year.
  - **Doubling time:** **70/r** (where r = % growth rate). E.g., r = 2% → doubling = 35 years.
- **Total Fertility Rate (TFR):**
  - **Average children per woman.**
  - **Replacement level: ~2.1** (accounts for child mortality).
  - **Sub-replacement (<2.1):** eventual decline.
  - **Above 2.1:** growth.
- **Demographic Transition Model (DTM):**
  - **Stage 1:** high birth, high death, slow growth (pre-industrial).
  - **Stage 2:** high birth, falling death, rapid growth (early development).
  - **Stage 3:** falling birth, low death, slowing growth (developing).
  - **Stage 4:** low birth, low death, stable (developed).
  - **Stage 5:** birth < death, decline (some developed countries today: Japan, Germany, Italy).
- **Age structure diagrams:**
  - **Wide base:** rapid growth (Stage 2).
  - **Even distribution:** stable (Stage 4).
  - **Narrow base:** declining (Stage 5).
- **Earth's human carrying capacity:**
  - **Estimated 9-12 billion** sustainably (varies by lifestyle).
  - **Currently ~8 billion** and growing.
  - **Resource consumption** matters as much as population number.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why exponential vs logistic:**
  - **Exponential** when resources unlimited (early stages, fresh resources).
  - **Logistic** when limiting factors kick in.
  - **Real populations** often start exponential, then logistic.
- **Overshoot and crash:**
  - **Population exceeds K** temporarily.
  - **Resources depleted.**
  - **Crash** below K.
  - **May oscillate** around K.
  - **Reindeer on St. Matthew Island:** classic case (introduced 1944, peaked 1963, crashed near 0 by 1966).
- **Why DTM Stages:**
  - **Stage 2 (early):** medical advances reduce death rate; cultural lag in birth rate.
  - **Stage 3 (developing):** women's education + urbanization + contraception → birth rate drops.
  - **Stage 4 (developed):** both rates low; stable.
  - **Stage 5 (some developed):** TFR well below 2.1 (Japan ~1.3, Korea ~0.8); aging populations.
- **Age structure consequences:**
  - **Wide base:** future workforce expansion AND need for jobs/education.
  - **Even:** demographic stability.
  - **Narrow base:** workforce shrinkage; pension/healthcare burden.
- **Pronatalist vs antinatalist policies:**
  - **Pronatalist:** France, Singapore (incentives for births).
  - **Antinatalist:** China One-Child Policy (1979-2015); India sterilization.
  - **Difficult to engineer** demographic outcomes.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why population growth slowed.**
  - **Global TFR fell from ~5 (1960s) to ~2.3 (today).**
  - **Education, urbanization, contraception, women's empowerment.**
  - **Most countries below or near replacement.**
  - **Africa exception** — still high TFR.
- **Why aging populations matter.**
  - **Stage 5 challenges:** smaller workforce supports larger elderly population.
  - **Pension systems strained.**
  - **Need for immigration** to fill labor (politically resisted).
  - **Japan, Italy, Germany** facing now; **China** following.
- **Why human carrying capacity contested.**
  - Depends on **technology, lifestyle, equity**.
  - **Earth could feed more if we ate less meat, wasted less, distributed equitably.**
  - **Environmental footprints** vary widely (US ~5x global average).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Growth model

A bacterial population in a petri dish doubles every hour. What's the model?
- **Exponential growth.**
- **Resources (food, space) unlimited initially.**
- Will become logistic when resources deplete.

### Example 2 [3] — Doubling time

A population grows at 3.5% per year. Doubling time?
- **70/3.5 = 20 years.**

### Example 3 [4] — Logistic curve

Describe phases of logistic growth.
- **Lag phase:** slow start.
- **Exponential phase:** rapid growth (resources still abundant).
- **Deceleration:** growth slows as approaches K.
- **Stable phase:** population fluctuates around K.

### Example 4 [4] — DTM identification

For each, identify DTM stage:
(a) Niger: TFR ~6.6.
(b) Mexico: TFR ~1.9.
(c) Japan: TFR ~1.3.

- (a) **Stage 2** (rapid growth).
- (b) **Stage 4** (stable; just below replacement).
- (c) **Stage 5** (decline).

### Example 5 [5] — Stage 5 challenges

What challenges does Japan face as a Stage 5 country?
- **Population declining;** workforce shrinking.
- **Aging:** larger elderly proportion to support.
- **Pension and healthcare** burden on workers.
- **Need immigration** to fill labor — politically resisted.
- **Government incentives** for births largely ineffective.
- **Economic implications:** reduced consumer demand, slower growth.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing exponential and logistic.** Exponential: J-curve, unlimited resources. Logistic: S-curve, leveling at K.
2. **Wrong doubling time formula.** **70/r** (where r is % growth rate).
3. **Confusing K and r strategists.** K: few, slow, parental care, near K. r: many, fast, boom-bust.
4. **Wrong replacement TFR.** **~2.1** (accounts for mortality).
5. **Confusing density-dependent and -independent factors.** Density-dependent intensify with population; density-independent don't.
6. **Wrong DTM stage assignments.** Match TFR/growth to stage.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For growth questions:**
- Identify model (exponential or logistic).
- Identify limiting factors.
- Calculate doubling time if r given.

**For DTM questions:**
- Identify stage.
- Connect TFR/growth to stage.

**For age structure questions:**
- Identify shape and stage.
- Predict trajectory.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Exponential growth (dN/dt = rN) characterizes populations with unlimited resources, producing a J-curve. Logistic growth (dN/dt = rN(K-N)/K) reflects limiting factors, producing an S-curve that levels at carrying capacity K."
2. "Carrying capacity is determined by limiting factors — food, water, space, predation, disease. Density-dependent factors intensify as population grows; density-independent factors (weather, natural disasters) affect regardless of density."
3. "Doubling time is calculated as 70/r where r is the percentage growth rate; a population growing at 2% per year doubles in 35 years."
4. "The Demographic Transition Model describes population change in stages: Stage 1 (high birth/high death), Stage 2 (high birth/falling death — rapid growth), Stage 3 (falling birth/low death), Stage 4 (low birth/low death — stable), and Stage 5 (birth below death — decline, e.g., Japan)."
5. "Total Fertility Rate (TFR) below 2.1 — the replacement level — indicates eventual population decline. Japan's TFR (~1.3), South Korea's (~0.8), and many European countries' (<1.7) signal aging populations and shrinking workforces."
