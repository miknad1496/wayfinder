## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 7–10% of the AP Environmental Science exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** primary vs secondary pollutants; smog (industrial vs photochemical); thermal inversion; acid deposition; noise pollution; indoor air pollution; reduction strategies.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** 6 criteria air pollutants tested. Photochemical smog formation. Acid rain. CFCs/ozone (related to Unit 9). Indoor air contaminants.

## Big Ideas

1. **Primary pollutants** emitted directly; **secondary** form via reactions in atmosphere.
2. **Two smog types:** industrial (sulfurous, from coal) and photochemical (Los Angeles, from cars + sunlight).
3. **Thermal inversion** traps pollution near ground.
4. **Acid deposition** damages forests, lakes, structures.
5. **Indoor air** can be more polluted than outdoor.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Primary vs secondary pollutants:**
  - **Primary:** emitted directly (CO, SO₂, NOx, particulates, VOCs, lead).
  - **Secondary:** form in atmosphere from primary (ozone, sulfuric acid, nitric acid, PAN).
- **Six "criteria" air pollutants (US EPA):**
  - **Carbon monoxide (CO):** colorless, odorless; from incomplete combustion; binds hemoglobin (asphyxiation).
  - **Sulfur dioxide (SO₂):** from coal combustion, smelters; → sulfuric acid → acid rain.
  - **Nitrogen oxides (NOx):** from combustion (vehicles, power plants); contribute to smog and acid rain.
  - **Particulate matter (PM10, PM2.5):** dust, smoke, soot; respiratory disease.
  - **Ground-level ozone (O₃):** secondary pollutant; main component of photochemical smog.
  - **Lead (Pb):** historically from leaded gasoline (banned 1996 US), paint; neurotoxin.
- **Industrial smog (gray air):**
  - **From coal combustion** (SO₂ + particulates).
  - **Cool, humid climates** (London historically; Beijing today).
  - **Famous:** London "Great Smog" (1952), ~12,000 deaths.
- **Photochemical smog (brown air):**
  - **Forms in sunlight** from NOx + VOCs.
  - **Produces** ozone, PAN.
  - **Warm, sunny climates** (Los Angeles classic).
  - **Worsens with traffic.**
- **Thermal inversion:**
  - **Normally:** temperature decreases with altitude → warm air at surface rises → pollution disperses.
  - **Inversion:** layer of warm air sits above cooler surface air → pollution trapped.
  - **Common in valleys** (LA basin, Mexico City).
  - **Worsens smog.**
- **Acid deposition:**
  - **Wet deposition (acid rain):** SO₂ + NOx → sulfuric/nitric acid → falls as rain/snow.
  - **Dry deposition:** acid particles fall directly.
  - **Effects:** acidifies lakes/streams (kills fish), damages forests, weathers buildings/statues.
  - **Region of origin different from impact** (Midwest US emissions → New England acid rain; English emissions → Scandinavian acid rain).
- **CFCs and ozone (Unit 9 connection):**
  - **CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)** used in refrigeration, aerosols.
  - **Destroy stratospheric ozone** layer.
  - **Montreal Protocol (1987)** phased out CFCs; ozone hole recovering.
- **Indoor air pollution:**
  - Often worse than outdoor.
  - **Radon:** radioactive gas from soil; lung cancer risk; basements.
  - **Asbestos:** insulation; mesothelioma.
  - **Lead:** from old paint.
  - **VOCs:** from paint, carpet, cleaners.
  - **Carbon monoxide:** from gas appliances, generators.
  - **Mold:** moist environments.
  - **Tobacco smoke.**
  - **Combustion smoke** in developing countries (cooking with wood/dung).
- **Noise pollution:**
  - **Hearing damage** at high decibels.
  - **Stress, sleep disruption** at lower levels.
  - **Marine noise** (ship engines, sonar) disrupts whale communication.
- **Reduction strategies:**
  - **Catalytic converters** (cars): convert CO, NOx, hydrocarbons to less harmful.
  - **Scrubbers** (coal plants): remove SO₂.
  - **Electrostatic precipitators**: remove particulates.
  - **Fuel switching:** coal → natural gas → renewables.
  - **Cap-and-trade** (acid rain program 1990 in US worked well for SO₂).
  - **Vehicle emission standards.**
  - **Public transit, cycling.**

### Adds for [4]

- **Photochemical smog formation:**
  - **NOx (from cars)** + **VOCs (from gasoline, paints)** + **sunlight** + **heat** → ozone, PAN.
  - **Worse in summer.**
  - **LA, Mexico City, Beijing** chronic problems.
- **Why thermal inversion bad:**
  - **Pollution accumulates** near surface.
  - **Vehicles, factories continue emitting.**
  - **No vertical mixing** until inversion breaks (storm or temperature change).
- **Acid rain mechanism:**
  - **SO₂ + H₂O + O₂ → H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid).**
  - **NOx + H₂O → HNO₃ (nitric acid).**
  - **Falls as rain.**
  - **Buffering:** limestone neutralizes; granite doesn't (NE US lakes vulnerable).
- **Acid Rain Program (1990 Clean Air Act):**
  - **Cap-and-trade** for SO₂ from power plants.
  - **Successful:** reduced SO₂ ~60% from 1990 to 2010.
  - **Cost lower** than predicted.
- **Radon:**
  - **Naturally occurring** radioactive gas from uranium decay in soil.
  - **Seeps into basements.**
  - **Second leading cause of lung cancer** in US (after smoking).
  - **Test homes; mitigate** with ventilation.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why air quality has improved in US.**
  - **Clean Air Act (1970, amended 1990)** drove major reductions.
  - **Lead:** ~99% reduction with leaded gas phase-out.
  - **SO₂:** ~90% reduction with coal scrubbers, switching.
  - **Particulates, NOx, ozone:** significant reductions.
  - **Major public health benefits** — lower asthma, heart disease, premature death.
- **Why developing world has worse air.**
  - **Less stringent regulations.**
  - **Coal still major energy source.**
  - **Indoor cooking** with biomass.
  - **WHO estimates** ~7 million premature deaths annually globally from air pollution.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Primary vs secondary

For each, identify primary or secondary:
(a) SO₂.
(b) Ground-level ozone.
(c) Particulates.
(d) Sulfuric acid.

- (a) **Primary.**
- (b) **Secondary** (forms from NOx + VOCs in sunlight).
- (c) **Primary.**
- (d) **Secondary** (from SO₂ + water).

### Example 2 [3] — Smog type

For each, identify industrial or photochemical:
(a) London Great Smog (1952).
(b) Los Angeles smog.
(c) Coal-burning city in winter.

- (a) **Industrial** (sulfurous, from coal).
- (b) **Photochemical** (sunny, traffic).
- (c) **Industrial.**

### Example 3 [4] — Photochemical smog

How does photochemical smog form?
- **NOx** (from vehicles, power plants) + **VOCs** (from gasoline, paints, vegetation) react in **sunlight**.
- Forms **ground-level ozone** and **PAN**.
- **Worse in:** sunny weather, traffic congestion, valleys (inversion).
- **LA, Mexico City, Beijing** chronic.

### Example 4 [4] — Acid rain mechanism

Trace acid rain from emission to lake.
- **Coal-fired power plants** emit SO₂.
- **SO₂ + H₂O + O₂ → H₂SO₄** (sulfuric acid).
- **Acid rain falls** downwind (often hundreds of miles).
- **Acidifies lakes/streams.**
- **Fish die** (aquatic life sensitive to pH).
- **Forests damaged** (especially conifers).
- **Statues/buildings weathered.**

### Example 5 [5] — Indoor air

What are major indoor air pollutants?
- **Radon** (radioactive gas from soil; basements).
- **Asbestos** (older insulation).
- **Lead** (older paint).
- **VOCs** (paint, carpet, cleaners).
- **CO** (gas appliances, generators).
- **Mold** (moisture).
- **Tobacco smoke.**
- **Combustion smoke** (developing world cooking).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing primary and secondary pollutants.** Primary: emitted directly. Secondary: forms in atmosphere.
2. **Confusing smog types.** Industrial: gray, coal, cool/humid. Photochemical: brown, cars, sunny.
3. **Wrong direction of thermal inversion.** Warm above cold near surface (opposite of normal); traps pollution.
4. **Wrong acid rain sources.** SO₂ (mostly coal) and NOx (mostly vehicles).
5. **Forgetting indoor air.** Often worse than outdoor.
6. **Confusing stratospheric and tropospheric ozone.** Stratospheric: GOOD (blocks UV). Tropospheric (ground-level): BAD (smog).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For pollutant questions:**
- Identify primary or secondary.
- Identify source.
- Address health/environmental effect.

**For smog questions:**
- Identify type.
- Address conditions favoring formation.

**For policy questions:**
- Identify regulatory tools (cap-and-trade, emission standards).
- Address effectiveness.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Primary pollutants are emitted directly (CO, SO₂, NOx, particulates, VOCs, lead); secondary pollutants form in the atmosphere from primary ones (ground-level ozone from NOx + VOCs + sunlight; sulfuric acid from SO₂ + water)."
2. "Industrial smog (gray air) develops from coal combustion in cool, humid climates and was responsible for the London Great Smog of 1952. Photochemical smog (brown air) forms in warm, sunny climates from vehicle emissions (NOx + VOCs + sunlight) — Los Angeles is the classic case."
3. "Thermal inversion traps pollutants near the ground when a layer of warm air sits above cooler surface air, preventing vertical mixing — common in valleys like the LA basin and Mexico City."
4. "Acid deposition occurs when SO₂ (mostly from coal combustion) and NOx (mostly from vehicles) form sulfuric and nitric acids in the atmosphere, falling as acid rain that acidifies lakes (killing fish), damages forests, and weathers buildings — often hundreds of miles downwind from emission sources."
5. "Major indoor air pollutants include radon (radioactive gas from soil; second leading lung cancer cause in US after smoking), asbestos, lead, VOCs, and CO — often making indoor air more polluted than outdoor."
