## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 6–8% of the AP Environmental Science exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** biodiversity (genetic, species, ecosystem); ecosystem services; island biogeography; ecological tolerance; ecological succession; natural disruptions; adaptation.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Three levels of biodiversity tested constantly. Ecosystem services. Succession (primary vs secondary). Island biogeography.

## Big Ideas

1. **Biodiversity has three levels:** genetic, species, ecosystem.
2. **Ecosystem services** are benefits from ecosystems to humans.
3. **Island biogeography** explains diversity on islands (size + distance).
4. **Ecological succession:** community develops over time after disturbance.
5. **Natural disruptions** can be beneficial; human disruptions often harmful.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Three levels of biodiversity:**
  - **Genetic diversity:** variation within species (gene pool).
  - **Species diversity:** number of different species (and their abundance).
  - **Ecosystem diversity:** variety of ecosystems in region.
- **Why biodiversity matters:**
  - **Resilience to disturbance.**
  - **Source of food, medicine, materials.**
  - **Aesthetic, cultural, recreational value.**
  - **Ethical value.**
- **Ecosystem services:**
  - **Provisioning:** food, water, materials.
  - **Regulating:** climate, water purification, pollination, decomposition.
  - **Supporting:** soil formation, photosynthesis, nutrient cycling.
  - **Cultural:** recreation, aesthetic, spiritual.
- **Island biogeography:**
  - **Larger islands** support more species.
  - **Closer islands** have higher immigration.
  - **Equilibrium** between immigration and extinction.
  - **Smaller, more isolated islands** = fewer species.
- **Ecological tolerance:**
  - **Range of conditions** organism can survive in.
  - **Optimum range** for thriving.
  - **Stress range** for survival.
  - **Outside tolerance** = death.
  - **Generalists** (raccoons): wide tolerance.
  - **Specialists** (panda, koala): narrow tolerance; vulnerable.
- **Ecological succession:**
  - **Primary succession:** on bare rock/no soil (volcanic eruption, glacier retreat).
    - **Pioneer species:** lichens, mosses (no soil needed).
    - Build soil over time.
    - Eventually grasses, shrubs, trees.
  - **Secondary succession:** on disturbed but soil-intact land (after fire, abandoned farm).
    - Faster than primary.
    - Pioneer species: grasses, weeds.
    - Eventually shrubs, trees, mature forest.
  - **Climax community:** stable, self-sustaining community at end of succession.
- **K-strategists vs r-strategists:**
  - **K-strategists:** few offspring; high parental care; long lifespan; stable environment. (Elephants, humans.)
  - **r-strategists:** many offspring; little parental care; short lifespan; variable environment. (Insects, weeds.)
- **Natural disruptions:**
  - **Fire:** clears underbrush, releases nutrients; some ecosystems fire-dependent (chaparral).
  - **Storms, floods, volcanic eruptions:** create new habitats.
  - **Periodic disruption** maintains biodiversity (intermediate disturbance hypothesis).
- **Adaptation and natural selection:**
  - **Adaptation:** trait that improves survival/reproduction.
  - **Natural selection:** differential survival/reproduction → traits frequencies change.
  - **Coevolution:** species evolve in response to each other (predator-prey, plant-pollinator).
- **Keystone species:**
  - **Disproportionate impact** on ecosystem relative to abundance.
  - **Examples:** sea otters (eat sea urchins → maintain kelp forests); beavers (engineer wetlands); wolves in Yellowstone.
  - **Removal collapses** ecosystem.
- **Indicator species:**
  - **Sensitive to environmental change.**
  - **Presence/absence indicates** ecosystem health.
  - **Examples:** amphibians (sensitive to pollution); lichens (air quality).
- **Invasive species:**
  - Non-native species that disrupts ecosystem.
  - **Examples:** zebra mussels (Great Lakes); kudzu (US South); Burmese pythons (Everglades).
  - **Often outcompete** natives lacking defenses.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why biodiversity provides resilience:**
  - **Multiple species** can fill similar niches; if one declines, others compensate.
  - **Genetic diversity** within species buffers against pathogens.
  - **Ecosystem services** more robust with diverse providers.
- **Island biogeography applied:**
  - **Habitat fragmentation** = "islands" of habitat in sea of disturbance.
  - **Smaller fragments** support fewer species.
  - **Connected fragments** (corridors) support more.
- **Specialist vulnerability:**
  - Pandas eat almost only bamboo → bamboo loss = panda decline.
  - Koalas eat eucalyptus → fire vulnerability.
  - Climate change especially threatens specialists.
- **Succession examples:**
  - **Mt. St. Helens (1980):** primary succession after eruption.
  - **Yellowstone fires (1988):** secondary succession in burned areas.
  - **Hurricane-cleared forests:** secondary succession.
- **Fire-dependent ecosystems:**
  - **Chaparral, longleaf pine, prairie:** fire essential.
  - **Fire suppression** allows fuel buildup → catastrophic wildfires.
  - **Prescribed burns** mimic natural cycle.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why ecosystem services matter (economic).**
  - **Estimated value of global ecosystem services:** trillions of dollars annually.
  - **Pollination services** alone valued at hundreds of billions.
  - **Loss is economic AND ecological.**
- **Why keystone species matter.**
  - **Removal cascades** through ecosystem.
  - **Wolves in Yellowstone:** removal led to elk overgrazing, river bank erosion. Reintroduction (1995) restored balance.
  - **Apex predators often keystone.**
- **Why invasive species spread.**
  - **No natural predators** in new environment.
  - **Often outcompete** natives.
  - **Globalization** accelerates spread.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify biodiversity level

For each, identify level:
(a) Different breeds of dogs.
(b) Variety of fish in a coral reef.
(c) Forest, prairie, and wetland in same region.

- (a) **Genetic diversity** (within species).
- (b) **Species diversity.**
- (c) **Ecosystem diversity.**

### Example 2 [3] — Identify ecosystem service

For each, identify service type:
(a) Pollination by bees.
(b) Lumber from forests.
(c) Hiking in national park.
(d) Soil formation.

- (a) **Regulating.**
- (b) **Provisioning.**
- (c) **Cultural.**
- (d) **Supporting.**

### Example 3 [4] — Primary vs secondary succession

Compare primary and secondary succession.
- **Primary:** on bare rock; no soil; pioneer species (lichens, mosses) build soil.
- **Secondary:** soil intact (after fire, farm abandonment); pioneer species (grasses) grow faster.
- **Both lead to** climax community over time.
- **Secondary much faster** because soil already present.

### Example 4 [4] — Keystone species

What happened in Yellowstone after wolves were reintroduced (1995)?
- **Wolves** were keystone predator (removed early 20th c.).
- **Without wolves:** elk overgrazed willows along streams.
- **Reintroduction:** wolves controlled elk; willows recovered; beavers returned (need willows); river banks stabilized; biodiversity increased.
- **"Trophic cascade"** illustrates keystone species impact.

### Example 5 [5] — Invasive species

Why are invasive species so destructive?
- **No natural predators or pathogens** in new environment.
- **Native species lack adaptations** to compete.
- **Often r-strategists** (rapid reproduction).
- **Examples:** zebra mussels clog water systems, eat native plankton; kudzu smothers native vegetation; Burmese pythons devastate Everglades small mammals.
- **Hard to eradicate** once established.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing levels of biodiversity.** Genetic (within), species (between), ecosystem (between communities).
2. **Wrong succession types.** Primary: bare rock, no soil. Secondary: soil intact.
3. **Confusing keystone and indicator species.** Keystone: disproportionate impact. Indicator: shows ecosystem health.
4. **Confusing K and r strategists.** K: few, slow, parental care. r: many, fast, no care.
5. **Misidentifying ecosystem services.** Provisioning (goods), regulating (processes), supporting (foundation), cultural (non-material).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For biodiversity questions:**
- Identify level (genetic/species/ecosystem).
- Connect to resilience/services.

**For succession questions:**
- Identify primary or secondary.
- Identify pioneer species.

**For ecosystem service questions:**
- Identify type.
- Provide example.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Biodiversity at three levels — genetic (variation within species), species (variety between species), and ecosystem (variety of ecosystems) — provides resilience against disturbance, pathogens, and environmental change."
2. "Ecosystem services include provisioning (food, materials), regulating (pollination, climate), supporting (soil formation, nutrient cycling), and cultural (recreation, aesthetic) — collectively valued at trillions of dollars annually."
3. "Primary succession begins on bare rock without soil; lichens and mosses serve as pioneer species, gradually building soil over decades to centuries. Secondary succession follows disturbance where soil remains; pioneer species are typically grasses, with succession proceeding much faster."
4. "Keystone species exert disproportionate effects on ecosystems relative to abundance — their removal cascades through trophic levels (Yellowstone wolves' removal led to elk overgrazing; their reintroduction in 1995 restored riparian ecosystems)."
5. "Invasive species are non-native species that disrupt ecosystems by outcompeting natives lacking defenses — zebra mussels in the Great Lakes, kudzu in the US South, and Burmese pythons in the Everglades illustrate the disruption."
