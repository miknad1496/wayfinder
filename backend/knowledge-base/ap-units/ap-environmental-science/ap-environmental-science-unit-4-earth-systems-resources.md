## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP Environmental Science exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** plate tectonics; soil formation and properties; atmospheric structure; weather; global wind patterns; ocean circulation; ENSO (El Niño/La Niña); watersheds.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Plate boundaries (convergent, divergent, transform). Soil layers (O-A-B-C). Atmospheric layers. Coriolis effect. ENSO impacts.

## Big Ideas

1. **Plate tectonics** drive Earth's geology — earthquakes, volcanoes, mountain building.
2. **Soil forms** through weathering, biological activity, climate; varies in fertility.
3. **Atmosphere is layered** (troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere).
4. **Wind patterns** driven by uneven solar heating + Coriolis effect.
5. **ENSO** (El Niño/La Niña) cycles affect global weather.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Plate tectonics:**
  - **Earth's crust** = plates floating on mantle.
  - **Convection currents** in mantle drive movement.
  - **Boundary types:**
    - **Convergent:** plates collide. Subduction (denser plate dives under) creates trenches and volcanoes (Andes, Cascades). Continental collision creates mountains (Himalayas).
    - **Divergent:** plates separate. New crust forms (mid-Atlantic ridge; East African Rift).
    - **Transform:** plates slide past. Earthquakes (San Andreas Fault).
- **Soil formation:**
  - **Weathering** breaks down rock (physical: temperature, water, ice; chemical: water, oxygen).
  - **Biological** activity adds organic matter.
  - **Time** — soil forms slowly (1 cm in centuries).
- **Soil horizons (layers):**
  - **O horizon:** organic litter (leaves, dead matter).
  - **A horizon (topsoil):** mineral + organic; most biological activity; dark.
  - **E horizon:** leached layer (some soils).
  - **B horizon (subsoil):** accumulates leached materials.
  - **C horizon:** weathered parent material.
  - **R horizon:** bedrock.
- **Soil texture:**
  - **Sand:** large particles; drains fast; low water/nutrient retention.
  - **Silt:** medium.
  - **Clay:** small particles; retains water; can be waterlogged.
  - **Loam:** balanced sand-silt-clay; ideal for agriculture.
- **Soil characteristics:**
  - **Permeability:** rate water moves through.
  - **Porosity:** % of pore space.
  - **Fertility:** nutrient content.
  - **pH:** acidity/alkalinity.
- **Atmospheric layers (bottom to top):**
  - **Troposphere:** 0-12 km; weather happens here; temperature DECREASES with altitude.
  - **Stratosphere:** 12-50 km; **ozone layer**; temperature INCREASES with altitude.
  - **Mesosphere:** 50-85 km; coldest layer.
  - **Thermosphere:** 85+ km; very hot but thin air.
- **Atmospheric composition:**
  - **Nitrogen (N₂):** 78%.
  - **Oxygen (O₂):** 21%.
  - **Argon, CO₂, water vapor, etc.:** ~1%.
- **Solar heating and wind:**
  - **Equator receives most direct sunlight** → warmest.
  - **Poles receive indirect sunlight** → coldest.
  - **Heated air rises;** cool air sinks.
  - **Convection cells** (Hadley, Ferrel, Polar) circulate air.
  - **Coriolis effect:** Earth's rotation deflects winds (right in Northern Hemisphere; left in Southern).
- **Global wind patterns:**
  - **Trade winds:** equatorial.
  - **Westerlies:** mid-latitudes.
  - **Polar easterlies:** poles.
  - **Jet streams:** fast, high-altitude winds.
- **Ocean circulation:**
  - **Surface currents** driven by wind + Coriolis.
  - **Deep currents** driven by density (salinity + temperature) — **thermohaline circulation**.
  - **Gulf Stream** transports warm water from tropics to N. Atlantic; warms Europe.
- **ENSO (El Niño Southern Oscillation):**
  - **Normal:** trade winds push warm water westward; cold water upwells off Peru.
  - **El Niño:** trade winds weaken; warm water spreads east; reduced upwelling off Peru; collapsed fishing; droughts in Australia/Indonesia; floods in Peru/California.
  - **La Niña:** trade winds intensify; cool water spreads west; opposite effects.
  - **Cycles every 2-7 years.**
- **Watersheds:**
  - **Land area** that drains to common body of water.
  - **Mississippi watershed** drains ~40% of continental US.
  - **Activities upstream affect downstream** water quality.

### Adds for [4]

- **Plate boundary examples:**
  - **Convergent (oceanic-continental):** Andes (Nazca-South American), Cascades (Juan de Fuca-N. American).
  - **Convergent (continental-continental):** Himalayas (Indian-Eurasian).
  - **Divergent:** Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift.
  - **Transform:** San Andreas Fault.
- **Soil formation factors (CLORPT):**
  - **Cl**imate.
  - **O**rganisms.
  - **R**elief (topography).
  - **P**arent material.
  - **T**ime.
- **Coriolis effect detail:**
  - **Causes** rotation of cyclones (counterclockwise N. hemisphere; clockwise S.).
  - **Affects** trade winds (deflected westward).
  - **Doesn't affect** small-scale things (toilet flushing — myth).
- **ENSO impacts:**
  - **El Niño:** California floods, drought in SE Asia/Australia, weakened Atlantic hurricane season.
  - **La Niña:** drought in California, more Atlantic hurricanes, wetter SE Asia.
  - **Climate change** may intensify ENSO patterns.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why plate tectonics matter.**
  - **Drives geological hazards** (earthquakes, volcanoes, tsunamis).
  - **Shapes landscapes** (mountains, ocean basins).
  - **Drives long-term carbon cycle** (volcanism releases; subduction recycles).
- **Why soil matters.**
  - **Foundation of agriculture.**
  - **Forms slowly;** lost rapidly through erosion.
  - **Topsoil loss = food security threat.**
- **Why ocean circulation matters.**
  - **Distributes heat** globally.
  - **Climate change** could disrupt thermohaline circulation (Gulf Stream weakening could cool Europe paradoxically).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Plate boundary identification

For each, identify boundary type:
(a) San Andreas Fault.
(b) Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
(c) Andes Mountains.
(d) Himalayas.

- (a) **Transform.**
- (b) **Divergent.**
- (c) **Convergent (oceanic-continental):** Nazca subducts under South American.
- (d) **Convergent (continental-continental):** Indian + Eurasian.

### Example 2 [3] — Soil layers

Order soil horizons from top to bottom.
- **O** (organic litter).
- **A** (topsoil).
- **E** (leached, in some soils).
- **B** (subsoil).
- **C** (weathered parent material).
- **R** (bedrock).

### Example 3 [4] — Atmospheric layers

Why does temperature increase with altitude in stratosphere but decrease in troposphere?
- **Troposphere:** heated from below by Earth's surface; air cools with altitude.
- **Stratosphere:** ozone absorbs UV radiation, heating upper layer; warmer at top than bottom.
- **Inversion** prevents convection between layers.

### Example 4 [4] — ENSO

What happens in El Niño?
- **Trade winds weaken.**
- **Warm Pacific water** that normally piles up in west spreads eastward.
- **Reduced upwelling off Peru** → cold-water fish die; Peruvian fishing collapses.
- **Drought in Australia/Indonesia.**
- **Floods in Peru/California.**
- **Weakened Atlantic hurricane season.**
- **Cycles every 2-7 years** with La Niña.

### Example 5 [5] — Coriolis effect

Why do hurricanes rotate counterclockwise in Northern Hemisphere?
- **Hurricanes are low-pressure systems.**
- **Air rushes in toward center.**
- **Coriolis effect** deflects this incoming air to the right.
- **Net result:** counterclockwise rotation (in Northern Hemisphere).
- **Reverse in Southern Hemisphere** (clockwise).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing plate boundaries.** Convergent (collide), divergent (separate), transform (slide).
2. **Wrong soil horizon order.** O (organic) → A (topsoil) → E → B (subsoil) → C → R (bedrock).
3. **Wrong atmospheric layer.** Troposphere first; ozone is stratospheric.
4. **Confusing El Niño and La Niña.** El Niño: weakened trades, warm east. La Niña: stronger trades, cool east.
5. **Wrong Coriolis direction.** Right in N. hemisphere; left in S.
6. **Forgetting watershed concept.** Includes ALL land draining to water body.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For tectonics questions:**
- Identify boundary type and example.
- Connect to geological feature (mountain, volcano, fault).

**For soil questions:**
- Identify layer.
- Identify formation factor (CLORPT).

**For atmospheric questions:**
- Identify layer.
- Note temperature behavior with altitude.

**For ENSO questions:**
- Identify phase (El Niño or La Niña).
- Identify regional impacts.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Plate tectonics drives geological activity through three boundary types: convergent (plates collide, forming mountains and volcanoes), divergent (plates separate, creating new crust), and transform (plates slide past, generating earthquakes like San Andreas)."
2. "Soil forms through weathering of parent material combined with biological activity, organized in horizons from O (organic litter) through A (topsoil) to C (weathered parent material) — formation requires centuries but loss can happen rapidly through erosion."
3. "The atmosphere has four major layers: troposphere (weather, decreasing temperature), stratosphere (ozone layer, increasing temperature), mesosphere (coldest), and thermosphere (very hot but thin)."
4. "The Coriolis effect — the deflection of moving air and water by Earth's rotation (right in Northern Hemisphere, left in Southern) — drives global wind patterns including trade winds, westerlies, and the rotation direction of hurricanes."
5. "ENSO (El Niño Southern Oscillation) cycles between El Niño phase (weakened trade winds, warm water spreads east, reduced Peruvian upwelling, droughts in SE Asia, floods in California) and La Niña phase (intensified trades, opposite effects), every 2-7 years with global weather impacts."
