# AP Environmental Science — Unit 9: Global Change — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 15–20% of the AP Environmental Science exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** stratospheric ozone depletion (CFCs); greenhouse effect and climate change; ocean warming and acidification; invasive species; HIPPCO drivers of biodiversity loss; sustainability and human responses.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Largest single unit. Climate change tested constantly. Ozone vs greenhouse confusion is a perennial trap. HIPPCO framework. Ocean acidification. Mass extinction.

## Big Ideas

1. **Stratospheric ozone depletion** (CFCs) is DIFFERENT from greenhouse effect (CO₂).
2. **Greenhouse gases trap heat;** CO₂ is the most important driver.
3. **Climate change effects:** warming, sea level rise, extreme weather, ecosystem disruption.
4. **Ocean acidification** = "the other CO₂ problem."
5. **HIPPCO** drives biodiversity loss; we're in a 6th mass extinction.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Stratospheric ozone depletion (DIFFERENT from greenhouse):**
  - **Ozone (O₃) layer in stratosphere** absorbs UV radiation.
  - **CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)** released → drift up to stratosphere → break down ozone.
  - **Cl atom** can destroy ~100,000 ozone molecules.
  - **Ozone "hole"** discovered over Antarctica (1985).
  - **UV penetration** → skin cancer, eye damage, ecosystem damage.
  - **Montreal Protocol (1987):** phased out CFCs internationally.
  - **Most successful environmental treaty.**
  - **Ozone layer recovering** (slowly).
- **Greenhouse effect (DIFFERENT from ozone):**
  - **Greenhouse gases (GHGs)** absorb infrared radiation.
  - **Trap heat** in lower atmosphere.
  - **Natural greenhouse effect** keeps Earth ~33°C warmer than otherwise.
  - **Without it,** Earth would be uninhabitable.
  - **Enhanced greenhouse effect** = climate change.
- **Major greenhouse gases:**
  - **CO₂:** ~76% of human GHG emissions; from fossil fuels, deforestation.
  - **Methane (CH₄):** ~16%; from livestock, rice paddies, landfills, natural gas leaks. ~80x more potent than CO₂ over 20 years.
  - **Nitrous oxide (N₂O):** ~6%; from agriculture, fertilizers. ~270x more potent.
  - **Fluorinated gases:** ~2%; refrigerants. Very long-lived.
  - **Water vapor:** powerful but cycles quickly; amplifies CO₂ effect.
- **Climate change evidence:**
  - **Atmospheric CO₂:** ~280 ppm pre-industrial → ~420 ppm today.
  - **Global average temperature:** ~1.1°C warmer since 1880.
  - **Arctic sea ice declining.**
  - **Glaciers retreating.**
  - **Sea level rising** (~20 cm since 1900).
  - **Ocean warming.**
  - **More extreme weather** (heatwaves, droughts, storms).
- **Climate change effects:**
  - **Sea level rise** from thermal expansion + ice melt.
  - **Coastal flooding;** small island nations existentially threatened.
  - **Extreme weather** intensifying.
  - **Coral bleaching** from warm + acidic water.
  - **Species range shifts;** some can't adapt fast enough.
  - **Crop yield changes** (mostly negative for staples).
  - **Disease range expansion** (mosquitoes northward).
  - **Disrupted seasons** (earlier spring, later autumn).
- **Ocean acidification:**
  - **CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid).**
  - **Ocean pH** has dropped ~0.1 since pre-industrial (~30% increase in H⁺).
  - **Threatens calcifying organisms** (corals, oysters, plankton with calcium carbonate shells).
  - **Coral reefs** dying globally.
- **Invasive species (review from Unit 2):**
  - **Threat magnified** by climate change (species ranges shifting).
- **HIPPCO** framework for biodiversity loss:
  - **H**abitat destruction (deforestation, development).
  - **I**nvasive species.
  - **P**ollution.
  - **P**opulation (human).
  - **C**limate change.
  - **O**verexploitation (overfishing, hunting).
- **Sixth mass extinction:**
  - **Current extinction rate** ~100-1,000x background rate.
  - **Driven by HIPPCO.**
  - **Scientists name** current epoch the **Anthropocene** (informal).
- **International responses:**
  - **Montreal Protocol (1987):** phased out CFCs.
  - **Kyoto Protocol (1997):** GHG targets for developed countries.
  - **Paris Agreement (2015):** national pledges; goal to limit warming to 1.5-2°C.
  - **IPCC** (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change) synthesizes science.
- **Mitigation vs adaptation:**
  - **Mitigation:** reducing GHG emissions (renewable energy, efficiency, reforestation).
  - **Adaptation:** adjusting to climate change effects (sea walls, drought-resistant crops, etc.).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why ozone depletion is NOT greenhouse effect:**
  - **Ozone:** stratospheric; depletion lets in MORE UV (harms organisms).
  - **Greenhouse:** tropospheric; gases trap heat (warms Earth).
  - **Different chemistry, different effects.**
  - **Common confusion.**
- **Why methane more potent:**
  - **Better at absorbing infrared.**
  - **Shorter atmospheric lifetime** (~12 years) but more powerful per molecule.
  - **Reducing methane has fast climate benefit.**
- **Climate feedback loops:**
  - **Positive feedback (amplifies warming):**
    - **Albedo:** ice melts → less reflection → more absorption → more melt.
    - **Permafrost:** thaws → releases methane → more warming.
    - **Water vapor:** warmer air holds more water (also a GHG).
  - **Negative feedback:** clouds (some cool by reflecting sunlight).
- **Tipping points:**
  - **Ice sheet collapse** (West Antarctic, Greenland).
  - **Amazon dieback.**
  - **Permafrost methane release.**
  - **Coral reef collapse.**
  - **Risk of crossing irreversibly.**
- **Climate justice:**
  - **Developed countries** historically emitted most.
  - **Developing countries** suffer worst impacts.
  - **Poor populations** within all countries most vulnerable.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Montreal Protocol succeeded:**
  - **Limited number of substitutes** needed (CFCs replaced by HFCs, then less harmful).
  - **Limited industries** affected.
  - **Wide scientific consensus.**
  - **Visible "hole"** mobilized public.
- **Why climate harder than ozone:**
  - **GHGs from every economic sector.**
  - **Fossil fuels embedded** in entire industrial economy.
  - **No simple substitutes.**
  - **Decades-long delays** between emissions and full effects.
- **What humans can do.**
  - **Mitigation:** transition to renewables; energy efficiency; reforestation; reduce meat; carbon capture.
  - **Adaptation:** resilient infrastructure; agriculture shifts; relocation in vulnerable zones.
  - **Both needed.**

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Ozone vs greenhouse

What's the difference between ozone depletion and greenhouse effect?
- **Ozone depletion:** **stratospheric** ozone destroyed by **CFCs**; lets in more **UV** radiation; causes skin cancer, eye damage.
- **Greenhouse effect:** **tropospheric** GHGs (CO₂, CH₄, etc.) trap heat; cause **climate change/warming**.
- **Different layers, different chemistry, different effects.**

### Example 2 [3] — GHG identification

Identify the largest source of each:
(a) CO₂.
(b) CH₄.
(c) N₂O.

- (a) **Fossil fuel combustion** (and deforestation).
- (b) **Livestock, rice paddies, landfills, natural gas leaks.**
- (c) **Agriculture (fertilizers).**

### Example 3 [4] — Climate feedback

How does Arctic sea ice loss create positive feedback?
- **Ice reflects ~80%** of sunlight (high albedo).
- **As ice melts, exposes dark ocean water** (low albedo, ~10%).
- **More absorption of sunlight** → more warming.
- **More warming** → more ice melt.
- **Positive feedback amplifies** initial warming.

### Example 4 [4] — Ocean acidification

Why does CO₂ acidify the ocean?
- **CO₂ dissolves in water:** CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid).
- **Carbonic acid releases H⁺:** lowers pH.
- **Ocean pH** dropped from ~8.2 to ~8.1 since pre-industrial (~30% increase in H⁺).
- **Calcifying organisms** (corals, oysters, plankton) struggle to build calcium carbonate shells.
- **Coral reefs dying** combined with warming.

### Example 5 [5] — HIPPCO application

Identify HIPPCO factors threatening polar bears.
- **H** (Habitat): Arctic sea ice melting; lose hunting platform.
- **I** (Invasive): less direct concern.
- **P** (Pollution): bioaccumulation of POPs in fat.
- **P** (Population): human encroachment on Arctic.
- **C** (Climate change): primary threat — sea ice loss.
- **O** (Overexploitation): historically hunted, now mostly regulated.
- **Climate change is the primary driver** of polar bear decline.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing ozone and greenhouse.** Different layers (stratosphere vs troposphere), different chemistry, different effects.
2. **Wrong GHG ranking.** CO₂ ~76%, CH₄ ~16%, N₂O ~6% of human emissions.
3. **Confusing mitigation and adaptation.** Mitigation: reduce emissions. Adaptation: adjust to impacts.
4. **Wrong international treaty pairings.** Montreal (CFCs/ozone). Kyoto (GHG/climate). Paris (GHG/climate).
5. **Underestimating ocean acidification.** Major threat to marine ecosystems beyond just warming.
6. **Forgetting HIPPCO.** Six drivers of biodiversity loss.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For climate change questions:**
- Identify GHG and source.
- Identify effect (warming, sea level, etc.).
- Identify mitigation/adaptation.

**For biodiversity questions:**
- Apply HIPPCO.
- Address specific threats.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Stratospheric ozone depletion (CFCs destroying O₃) and the greenhouse effect (GHGs trapping heat in the troposphere) are distinct phenomena. The Montreal Protocol (1987) successfully phased out CFCs; the Paris Agreement (2015) attempts to address greenhouse gases."
2. "Major anthropogenic greenhouse gases include CO₂ (~76% of emissions, from fossil fuels and deforestation), methane (~16%, from livestock, landfills, natural gas — ~80x more potent than CO₂ over 20 years), nitrous oxide (~6%, from agriculture), and fluorinated gases."
3. "Climate change drives sea level rise (thermal expansion + ice melt), extreme weather intensification, ocean warming, ecosystem disruption, range shifts, and altered crop yields — with disproportionate impact on developing countries and poor populations."
4. "Ocean acidification — 'the other CO₂ problem' — occurs when atmospheric CO₂ dissolves in seawater forming carbonic acid, lowering ocean pH (~30% increase in H⁺ since pre-industrial) and threatening calcifying organisms (corals, oysters, plankton)."
5. "HIPPCO — Habitat destruction, Invasive species, Pollution, Population, Climate change, Overexploitation — drives the current biodiversity crisis. Extinction rates are estimated at 100-1,000 times background rates, marking a sixth mass extinction event."
