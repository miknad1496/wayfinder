## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 6–8% of the AP Environmental Science exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** ecosystem structure; trophic levels; energy flow; biogeochemical cycles (water, carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, sulfur); productivity; symbiosis.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Foundational; biogeochemical cycles tested constantly. 10% rule of energy transfer. Trophic level structure.

## Big Ideas

1. **Ecosystems include biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components.**
2. **Energy flows through ecosystems** in trophic levels (with ~90% loss at each step).
3. **Matter cycles through ecosystems** in biogeochemical cycles (water, C, N, P, S).
4. **Productivity** measures rate of biomass production.
5. **Species interact** through competition, predation, symbiosis.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Ecosystem components:**
  - **Biotic:** living things (plants, animals, microbes).
  - **Abiotic:** non-living (water, air, soil, sunlight, temperature, minerals).
  - **Biome:** large region with characteristic climate, plants, animals (forest, desert, tundra, etc.).
- **Trophic levels:**
  - **Producers (autotrophs):** plants, algae; make own food via photosynthesis.
  - **Primary consumers:** herbivores eat producers.
  - **Secondary consumers:** carnivores eat primary consumers.
  - **Tertiary consumers:** top predators.
  - **Decomposers:** break down dead matter (bacteria, fungi).
- **Energy flow:**
  - **Source:** sun (for most ecosystems).
  - **10% rule:** ~10% of energy passes to next trophic level; 90% lost as heat.
  - **Top predators few** because energy diminishes up pyramid.
- **Photosynthesis equation:**
  - **6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**
  - Producers fix carbon and produce oxygen.
- **Cellular respiration:**
  - **C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP**
  - Releases energy; reverse of photosynthesis.
- **Productivity:**
  - **Gross primary productivity (GPP):** total energy fixed by producers.
  - **Net primary productivity (NPP):** GPP − respiration.
  - **NPP = energy available** to consumers.
  - **High productivity:** tropical rainforests, estuaries, swamps.
  - **Low productivity:** deserts, deep ocean, tundra.
- **Biogeochemical cycles:**
  - **Water cycle:** evaporation → condensation → precipitation → runoff/infiltration.
  - **Carbon cycle:** photosynthesis fixes CO₂; respiration/decomposition/combustion releases.
  - **Nitrogen cycle:**
    - **N₂ in atmosphere** (78%).
    - **Nitrogen fixation** (bacteria, lightning) → ammonia (NH₃/NH₄⁺).
    - **Nitrification** (bacteria) → nitrites (NO₂⁻) → nitrates (NO₃⁻).
    - **Plants absorb nitrates;** animals eat plants.
    - **Denitrification** returns N to atmosphere.
  - **Phosphorus cycle:** **no atmospheric phase**; weathering of rock; through soil and biota.
  - **Sulfur cycle:** through volcanoes, atmosphere, soil; major source of acid rain.
- **Species interactions:**
  - **Competition:** for resources.
  - **Predation:** one eats other.
  - **Mutualism:** both benefit (bees and flowers).
  - **Commensalism:** one benefits, other unaffected (barnacles on whale).
  - **Parasitism:** one benefits, other harmed (tick on dog).
- **Niche vs habitat:**
  - **Habitat:** where organism lives.
  - **Niche:** organism's role (job).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why 10% rule:**
  - Energy lost as heat at each transfer.
  - Lost in metabolism, movement, reproduction.
  - Limits food chain length to ~4-5 levels.
- **Why nitrogen cycle complex:**
  - **N₂ atmospheric** but plants can't use directly.
  - **Bacteria essential** for fixation.
  - **Human disruption** (fertilizers) doubled N input → eutrophication, dead zones.
- **Carbon cycle and climate:**
  - **Burning fossil fuels** releases CO₂ stored over millions of years.
  - **Net atmospheric CO₂ increase** since Industrial Revolution.
  - Drives **climate change** (Unit 9).
- **Symbiosis examples:**
  - **Mutualism:** mycorrhizae (fungi-roots); coral-zooxanthellae; gut bacteria.
  - **Commensalism:** epiphytes on tree (use as platform).
  - **Parasitism:** mistletoe; tapeworm.
- **Ecological efficiency variation:**
  - **Aquatic ecosystems** sometimes higher (~20%).
  - **Endotherms (warm-blooded)** less efficient than ectotherms.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why biogeochemical cycles matter.**
  - **Sustain life;** disrupted cycles disrupt ecosystems.
  - **Human impact** (carbon, nitrogen) drives major environmental issues.
  - **Phosphorus may be limiting nutrient** for agriculture in future.
- **Why productivity varies.**
  - **Light, water, temperature, nutrients** drive productivity.
  - **Limiting factors** constrain.
  - **Tropical rainforest:** all conditions favorable.
- **Why energy/matter distinction matters.**
  - **Energy flows** (one-way; lost as heat).
  - **Matter cycles** (recycled).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Trophic level identification

For each, identify trophic level:
(a) Grass.
(b) Cow.
(c) Wolf eating cow.
(d) Vulture eating dead wolf.
(e) Bacteria decomposing vulture.

- (a) **Producer.**
- (b) **Primary consumer.**
- (c) **Secondary consumer.**
- (d) **Tertiary consumer (or scavenger).**
- (e) **Decomposer.**

### Example 2 [3] — 10% rule

A producer level has 10,000 kcal of energy. How much reaches tertiary consumers?
- **Producer:** 10,000 kcal.
- **Primary consumer (10%):** 1,000 kcal.
- **Secondary consumer (10%):** 100 kcal.
- **Tertiary consumer (10%):** **10 kcal.**

### Example 3 [4] — Nitrogen cycle

Trace nitrogen from atmosphere to plant tissue.
- **N₂** in atmosphere (78%).
- **Nitrogen-fixing bacteria** (Rhizobium, lightning) convert N₂ → ammonia (NH₃) or ammonium (NH₄⁺).
- **Nitrification** by bacteria: NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻.
- **Plants absorb NO₃⁻** through roots.
- **Plants incorporate N into amino acids/proteins.**

### Example 4 [4] — Carbon cycle disruption

How are humans disrupting the carbon cycle?
- **Burning fossil fuels** releases stored carbon as CO₂.
- **Deforestation** reduces carbon sinks.
- **Net atmospheric CO₂** increasing since Industrial Revolution (~280 ppm pre-industrial → ~420 ppm today).
- **Drives climate change.**

### Example 5 [5] — Productivity

Why are tropical rainforests highly productive but tundra is not?
- **Tropical rainforest:** abundant light (year-round), water, warmth, nutrients (rapidly cycled). NPP very high.
- **Tundra:** limited light (winter), cold, frozen soil prevents nutrient cycling. NPP low.
- **Productivity reflects** which abiotic factors are limiting.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Wrong trophic levels.** Producer (plant), primary consumer (herbivore), secondary (carnivore eats herbivore), tertiary (top predator).
2. **Ignoring decomposers.** Essential for nutrient cycling.
3. **Confusing GPP and NPP.** GPP: total. NPP: GPP − respiration.
4. **Wrong nitrogen cycle steps.** Fixation → ammonification → nitrification → assimilation → denitrification.
5. **Forgetting phosphorus has no atmospheric phase.**
6. **Confusing energy flow and matter cycling.** Energy: one-way (lost). Matter: cycles.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For ecosystem questions:**
- Identify trophic level.
- Apply 10% rule for energy.

**For cycle questions:**
- Identify steps in order.
- Identify human disruption.

**For productivity questions:**
- Identify limiting factor.
- Distinguish GPP and NPP.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Energy flows through ecosystems in trophic levels with ~90% loss at each transfer (10% rule), constraining food chains to 4-5 levels and limiting top predator populations."
2. "Photosynthesis (6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂) fixes carbon into organic matter; cellular respiration reverses this, releasing CO₂ and energy."
3. "The nitrogen cycle moves N from atmospheric N₂ through nitrogen-fixing bacteria to ammonia/ammonium, then through nitrification to nitrates that plants absorb. Human fertilizer use has roughly doubled the nitrogen entering this cycle, causing eutrophication and dead zones."
4. "The carbon cycle has been disrupted by fossil fuel combustion releasing carbon stored over geological time, raising atmospheric CO₂ from ~280 ppm pre-industrial to ~420 ppm today and driving climate change."
5. "Net Primary Productivity (NPP = GPP − respiration) determines energy available to consumers, with tropical rainforests and estuaries highly productive and deserts and deep ocean low-productivity due to limiting abiotic factors."
