## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP Environmental Science exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Tragedy of the Commons; agriculture (Green Revolution, GMOs, irrigation issues); meat production; mining; fishing; urbanization; ecological footprints; sustainable practices.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Tragedy of the Commons foundational. CAFOs and feedlots. Aquaculture. Mining types (strip, surface, subsurface). Urban sprawl. Sustainability frameworks.

## Big Ideas

1. **Tragedy of the Commons** explains overuse of shared resources.
2. **Modern agriculture** uses irrigation, fertilizers, pesticides, GMOs — productivity gains AND environmental costs.
3. **CAFOs (Concentrated Animal Feeding Operations)** efficient but environmentally costly.
4. **Mining methods** vary in disturbance intensity.
5. **Urbanization** and sprawl have environmental costs; smart growth offers alternatives.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Tragedy of the Commons (Garrett Hardin, 1968):**
  - **Shared resource (common)** without regulation.
  - **Each user benefits individually** from extracting more.
  - **Costs distributed across all users.**
  - **Result:** overuse, depletion.
  - **Examples:** overfishing oceans, atmospheric pollution, groundwater depletion.
  - **Solutions:** regulation, privatization, community management.
- **Agricultural practices:**
  - **Industrial monoculture:** single crop on large area; efficient but vulnerable to pests, soil depletion.
  - **Polyculture:** multiple crops; more resilient.
  - **Crop rotation:** prevents soil depletion (alternating nitrogen-fixing legumes).
  - **Intercropping:** multiple crops in same field.
  - **Agroforestry:** trees integrated with crops.
- **Green Revolution (1940s-1970s):**
  - **High-yield seeds, synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, irrigation.**
  - Dramatic yield increases.
  - **Costs:** soil/water depletion, biodiversity loss, dependence on inputs.
- **GMOs (Genetically Modified Organisms):**
  - Modified DNA for desired traits.
  - **Examples:** Bt corn (insect resistance); Roundup Ready soy (herbicide resistance); Golden Rice (vitamin A).
  - **Controversy:** safety, ecological impact, corporate control of seeds.
- **Irrigation:**
  - **Furrow irrigation:** flood furrows; ~60% efficient; high evaporation.
  - **Spray (center-pivot):** ~75% efficient; common in US Great Plains.
  - **Drip irrigation:** delivers water to root zone; ~90% efficient.
  - **Salinization:** salt buildup in irrigated soil; eventually destroys productivity.
  - **Aquifer depletion:** Ogallala Aquifer (US Great Plains) depleting fast.
- **Pesticides:**
  - **Insecticides** (kill insects), **herbicides** (kill weeds), **fungicides**, etc.
  - **Broad-spectrum vs targeted.**
  - **Bioaccumulation:** pesticides build up in tissues over time.
  - **Biomagnification:** concentration increases up food chain.
  - **DDT:** banned for thinning bird eggshells (Rachel Carson, *Silent Spring*).
  - **Pesticide resistance:** insects evolve resistance; treadmill of new pesticides.
  - **Integrated Pest Management (IPM):** combine biological controls + targeted chemicals + cultural methods.
- **CAFOs (Concentrated Animal Feeding Operations):**
  - **Large-scale livestock production.**
  - **Efficient meat production.**
  - **Costs:** waste lagoons (water pollution), antibiotic resistance, methane emissions, animal welfare.
- **Mining methods:**
  - **Surface mining:** strip mining; mountaintop removal (Appalachia); open-pit. Massive disturbance.
  - **Subsurface mining:** tunnels; less surface disturbance but dangerous to workers, acid mine drainage.
  - **Placer mining:** sediment from rivers (gold).
  - **Reclamation:** restoring mined land (often inadequate).
- **Fishing:**
  - **Overfishing:** harvest exceeds reproduction.
  - **Bycatch:** non-target species caught.
  - **Trawling:** drag nets; destroys seafloor.
  - **Aquaculture:** farming fish; can pollute water, escape and outcompete wild fish.
  - **Sustainable certifications** (MSC).
- **Urbanization:**
  - **Movement** of populations to cities.
  - **Urban sprawl:** low-density, car-dependent development.
  - **Heat island effect:** urban areas hotter (concrete, less vegetation).
  - **Impervious surfaces** reduce groundwater recharge; increase runoff.
- **Ecological footprint:**
  - **Land/water area** needed to provide resources and absorb waste for given population.
  - **US footprint** ~5x sustainable global average.
  - **Differences by lifestyle, country.**
- **Sustainable practices:**
  - **Smart growth:** dense, walkable, transit-oriented.
  - **Sustainable agriculture:** crop rotation, IPM, no-till, cover crops.
  - **Sustainable forestry:** selective cutting, reforestation.
  - **LEED certification** for buildings.

### Adds for [4]

- **Tragedy of the Commons solutions:**
  - **Government regulation:** quotas, licenses (fisheries).
  - **Privatization:** assign property rights.
  - **Community management:** Elinor Ostrom's work on community-managed commons.
- **Pesticide treadmill:**
  - Apply pesticide → kills susceptible insects.
  - Resistant insects survive, reproduce.
  - Population becomes resistant.
  - **New pesticide needed.**
  - **IPM** breaks cycle.
- **Salinization mechanism:**
  - **Irrigation water** has dissolved salts.
  - **Water evaporates;** salts remain.
  - **Salt accumulates** over years.
  - **Ancient Mesopotamia** abandoned cities due to salinization.
  - **Fixes:** drainage systems; periodic flushing.
- **Mountaintop removal:**
  - **Appalachian coal mining.**
  - **Tops blown off mountains;** fill dumped in valleys.
  - **Destroys watersheds, biodiversity, communities.**
- **Smart growth principles:**
  - **Mixed-use development.**
  - **Walkable.**
  - **Transit-oriented.**
  - **Greenbelts** preserve open space.
  - **In-fill development** rather than greenfield sprawl.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Tragedy of the Commons matters.**
  - **Many environmental problems** are commons problems (climate, oceans, atmosphere).
  - **Solutions require collective action.**
- **Why sustainable agriculture difficult.**
  - **Conventional agriculture more profitable short-term.**
  - **Sustainable practices require knowledge, investment.**
  - **Subsidies often favor** conventional.
- **Why urbanization both helps and hurts.**
  - **Helps:** density reduces per-capita resource use; transit possible.
  - **Hurts:** concentrates pollution; sprawl is worst of both worlds.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Tragedy of the Commons example

How is overfishing a Tragedy of the Commons?
- **Ocean fisheries** = shared resource.
- **Each fisher benefits** from larger catch.
- **Cost (depletion)** distributed across all.
- **Result:** overfishing, collapsed stocks (e.g., Atlantic cod, 1990s).
- **Solutions:** quotas (regulation), MPAs (marine protected areas).

### Example 2 [3] — Mining method identification

For each, identify mining method:
(a) Strip away surface to reach coal seams.
(b) Tunnels deep underground for gold.
(c) Sift sand for gold flecks.

- (a) **Surface (strip) mining.**
- (b) **Subsurface mining.**
- (c) **Placer mining.**

### Example 3 [4] — Bioaccumulation/biomagnification

Trace DDT through a food chain.
- **DDT applied to crops.**
- **Crops absorb low levels.**
- **Insects eat crops; concentrate slightly.**
- **Small fish eat insects; concentrate more (biomagnification).**
- **Large fish eat small fish; concentrate further.**
- **Birds (ospreys, eagles) eat fish; very high DDT levels.**
- **Caused eggshell thinning;** near-extinction of bald eagles in US.

### Example 4 [4] — Aquifer depletion

What's happening to the Ogallala Aquifer?
- **Underlies US Great Plains** (NE, KS, OK, TX, etc.).
- **Heavily pumped** for irrigation since 1950s.
- **Recharge much slower** than withdrawal.
- **Already depleted** in some areas.
- **Threatens future agriculture** in Plains.

### Example 5 [5] — Sustainable agriculture

How might agriculture become more sustainable?
- **Crop rotation** maintains soil fertility.
- **Cover crops** prevent erosion, fix nitrogen.
- **Reduced/no-till** preserves soil.
- **Drip irrigation** reduces water use.
- **Integrated Pest Management** reduces pesticide use.
- **Polyculture/agroforestry** increases resilience.
- **Organic methods** eliminate synthetic chemicals.
- **Reduced meat consumption** dramatically reduces land/water/emissions.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Misunderstanding Tragedy of the Commons.** Shared resource + individual benefit + distributed cost = overuse.
2. **Confusing bioaccumulation and biomagnification.** Bioaccumulation: builds up in individual over lifetime. Biomagnification: concentration up food chain.
3. **Wrong mining method classifications.** Strip, mountaintop removal = surface. Tunnels = subsurface. Sediment = placer.
4. **Ignoring water efficiency differences.** Drip > spray > furrow.
5. **Forgetting salinization.** Long-term threat to irrigated agriculture.
6. **Confusing Green Revolution date.** **1940s-1970s.** Norman Borlaug.

**For mining questions:**

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The Tragedy of the Commons (Hardin, 1968) describes overexploitation of shared resources when individuals benefit from extraction while costs are distributed — illustrated by overfishing, atmospheric pollution, and groundwater depletion."
2. "Industrial agriculture's Green Revolution (1940s-1970s) tripled yields through high-yield varieties, synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, and irrigation — but at costs of soil and aquifer depletion (e.g., Ogallala Aquifer), biodiversity loss, and pesticide resistance."
3. "Bioaccumulation concentrates persistent chemicals in individual organisms over time; biomagnification compounds this up food chains, with apex predators carrying the highest concentrations — DDT's effects on bald eagles illustrate this devastating dynamic."
4. "Concentrated Animal Feeding Operations (CAFOs) provide efficient meat production but generate concentrated waste lagoons that pollute groundwater, contribute to antibiotic resistance, and produce significant methane emissions."
5. "Sustainable land use combines smart growth (dense, walkable, transit-oriented), sustainable agriculture (crop rotation, IPM, drip irrigation), and ecological footprint reduction — replacing unsustainable practices that exceed Earth's carrying capacity."
