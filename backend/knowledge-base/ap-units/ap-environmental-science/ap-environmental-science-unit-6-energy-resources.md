# AP Environmental Science — Unit 6: Energy Resources and Consumption — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP Environmental Science exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** energy units; non-renewable energy (fossil fuels, nuclear); renewable energy (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, biomass, biofuels); energy efficiency.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Fossil fuel impacts. Nuclear (fission, waste, accidents). Renewable types and tradeoffs. Energy efficiency.

## Big Ideas

1. **Energy sources** divide into non-renewable (fossil, nuclear) and renewable (solar, wind, etc.).
2. **Fossil fuels** dominate today but cause climate change and pollution.
3. **Nuclear** is low-carbon but creates waste and accident risk.
4. **Renewables** are growing rapidly; each has tradeoffs.
5. **Energy efficiency** is often "cheapest source" of energy.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Energy units:**
  - **Joule (J):** SI unit.
  - **Watt (W):** J/s; rate of energy use.
  - **Kilowatt-hour (kWh):** unit on electric bills.
- **Energy sources:**
  - **Non-renewable:** fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas), nuclear (uranium).
  - **Renewable:** solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, biomass, biofuels.
- **Fossil fuels:**
  - **Formed from buried organic matter** over millions of years.
  - **Coal:** mostly carbon; high CO₂ per energy. Mining hazards (mountaintop removal, black lung).
  - **Oil (petroleum):** transportation fuel; refined into gasoline, diesel, etc. Spills (Deepwater Horizon 2010).
  - **Natural gas (methane):** cleaner-burning than coal; methane leaks are potent greenhouse gas. **Fracking** controversies.
- **Combustion impacts:**
  - **CO₂** drives climate change.
  - **SO₂** causes acid rain.
  - **NOx** causes smog, acid rain.
  - **Particulates** cause respiratory disease.
  - **Mercury** from coal combustion.
- **Nuclear power:**
  - **Fission:** splitting uranium-235 (or plutonium-239) releases energy.
  - **No CO₂ at point of generation.**
  - **Waste:** highly radioactive; storage problem (Yucca Mountain proposal in US).
  - **Accidents:** Three Mile Island (1979), Chernobyl (1986), Fukushima (2011).
  - **Decommissioning expensive.**
  - **France:** ~70% nuclear; US: ~20%.
- **Solar:**
  - **Photovoltaic (PV) cells:** convert sunlight to electricity.
  - **Solar thermal:** focuses sunlight to heat fluid.
  - **Passive solar design:** building orientation/materials use sun.
  - **Costs falling rapidly.**
  - **Limitations:** intermittent (no sun at night, cloudy days); land use.
- **Wind:**
  - **Turbines** convert wind to electricity.
  - **Onshore and offshore.**
  - **Limitations:** intermittent; bird/bat mortality; aesthetics.
  - **Increasingly competitive** with fossil fuels on cost.
- **Hydroelectric:**
  - **Dams** generate electricity from falling water.
  - **Largest renewable source globally.**
  - **Limitations:** ecosystem disruption (fish migration, sediment); few suitable sites left.
- **Geothermal:**
  - **Heat from Earth's interior.**
  - **Power plants** in volcanic regions (Iceland, Philippines).
  - **Heat pumps** for buildings (most places).
- **Biomass:**
  - **Burning organic matter** (wood, crops).
  - **Carbon-neutral** in theory (regrowth absorbs CO₂).
  - **Practice:** can be unsustainable.
- **Biofuels:**
  - **Ethanol** (corn, sugarcane): blended with gasoline.
  - **Biodiesel** (soybeans, palm oil).
  - **Critique:** competes with food; land conversion.
- **Energy efficiency:**
  - **Cheaper than producing new energy.**
  - **LED bulbs** vs incandescent (10x more efficient).
  - **Hybrid/EV cars.**
  - **Building insulation.**
  - **Energy Star ratings.**
- **Energy mix (US, ~2024):**
  - **Petroleum:** ~36%.
  - **Natural gas:** ~33%.
  - **Renewables:** ~13%.
  - **Coal:** ~10%.
  - **Nuclear:** ~8%.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why coal is dirtiest:**
  - **Highest CO₂** per unit energy (more carbon per joule).
  - **More sulfur** → SO₂ → acid rain.
  - **Heavy metals** (mercury) in flue gas.
  - **Mining destruction** (mountaintop removal).
- **Fracking (hydraulic fracturing):**
  - High-pressure water + chemicals injected to crack shale; release oil/gas.
  - **Boom in US natural gas production** (~2010s).
  - **Concerns:** water contamination, induced earthquakes, methane leaks.
- **Nuclear tradeoffs:**
  - **Pro:** no operational CO₂; reliable baseload; small footprint.
  - **Con:** waste storage; accident risk; weapons proliferation; cost.
  - **Climate change** is reviving nuclear interest in some countries.
- **Renewable intermittency solutions:**
  - **Storage** (batteries, pumped hydro).
  - **Grid integration** across regions.
  - **Demand response.**
  - **Smart grid.**
- **Net energy:**
  - **Energy returned vs energy invested (EROI).**
  - **Conventional oil:** historically high EROI (~30:1).
  - **Tar sands, deep oil:** much lower EROI.
  - **Solar PV:** improving rapidly.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why energy transition difficult.**
  - **Massive infrastructure** to replace.
  - **Existing investments** in fossil fuels.
  - **Political economy** of fossil fuel industries.
  - **Intermittency challenges** for renewables.
- **Why energy efficiency cheapest.**
  - **Negawatts vs megawatts.**
  - **Often payback in years.**
  - **Reduces need for new generation.**
  - **Amory Lovins** popularized concept.
- **Why nuclear contested.**
  - **Climate-friendly** but waste/accident concerns.
  - **France's success** (70% nuclear) vs **Germany's exit** (Fukushima reaction).
  - **Small modular reactors** newer technology.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify renewable/non-renewable

For each, identify renewable or non-renewable:
(a) Coal.
(b) Solar PV.
(c) Uranium.
(d) Wind.
(e) Natural gas.

- (a) **Non-renewable.**
- (b) **Renewable.**
- (c) **Non-renewable.**
- (d) **Renewable.**
- (e) **Non-renewable.**

### Example 2 [3] — Fossil fuel impact

For each, identify pollutant:
(a) Drives climate change.
(b) Causes acid rain.
(c) Causes respiratory disease.

- (a) **CO₂.**
- (b) **SO₂** (and NOx).
- (c) **Particulates** (and ozone, NOx).

### Example 3 [4] — Nuclear tradeoffs

What are pros and cons of nuclear power?
- **Pros:**
  - **No CO₂** at point of generation.
  - **Reliable baseload.**
  - **High energy density** — small footprint.
  - **Existing fleet** generates significant electricity.
- **Cons:**
  - **Radioactive waste** for thousands of years.
  - **Accident risk** (Chernobyl, Fukushima).
  - **Weapons proliferation** risk.
  - **High capital cost.**
  - **Decommissioning expensive.**

### Example 4 [4] — Solar tradeoffs

What are pros and cons of solar PV?
- **Pros:**
  - **Renewable; no fuel cost.**
  - **No CO₂ emissions** in operation.
  - **Distributed generation** possible (rooftops).
  - **Costs falling rapidly.**
- **Cons:**
  - **Intermittent** (no sun at night, less in winter, cloudy).
  - **Land use** for utility-scale.
  - **Manufacturing** uses energy and produces some pollution.
  - **Storage needed** for grid reliability.

### Example 5 [5] — Energy efficiency

How does energy efficiency function as "energy resource"?
- **Reducing demand** is equivalent to producing energy.
- **LED bulbs** use 10x less energy than incandescent.
- **Building insulation** reduces heating/cooling needs.
- **Often cheapest, fastest** way to reduce emissions.
- **"Negawatts"** (Amory Lovins) — energy not used.
- **Pays back** quickly in lower bills.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing renewable and clean.** Nuclear is low-carbon but not renewable. Biomass is renewable but can be polluting.
2. **Wrong fossil fuel impacts.** CO₂ → climate. SO₂ → acid rain. NOx → smog + acid rain.
3. **Confusing fission and fusion.** Nuclear power = fission. Fusion is experimental (sun's process).
4. **Underestimating renewables intermittency.** Real challenge requiring storage/grid solutions.
5. **Forgetting energy efficiency.** Often cheapest way to "produce" energy.
6. **Wrong US energy mix.** Petroleum ~36%, natural gas ~33%, renewables ~13%, coal ~10%, nuclear ~8% (2024).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For energy source questions:**
- Identify type.
- Address pros AND cons.

**For sustainability questions:**
- Compare options.
- Address efficiency.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas) provide ~80% of global energy but emit CO₂ driving climate change, SO₂ causing acid rain, NOx causing smog, particulates causing respiratory disease, and mercury (especially from coal)."
2. "Nuclear power generates electricity through fission of uranium-235, producing no CO₂ at operation but creating long-lived radioactive waste and accident risks (Three Mile Island 1979, Chernobyl 1986, Fukushima 2011)."
3. "Solar photovoltaic and wind have become cost-competitive with fossil fuels, but their intermittency requires storage (batteries, pumped hydro), grid integration, and smart grid technologies for reliable power."
4. "Hydroelectric is the largest renewable globally but disrupts ecosystems (blocks fish migration, alters sediment flow); few suitable new sites remain."
5. "Energy efficiency — the 'negawatt' — is often the cheapest 'energy source.' LED bulbs use one-tenth the energy of incandescent; building insulation, heat pumps, and EVs reduce energy demand cost-effectively."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master fossil fuel types and impacts. Master nuclear pros/cons. Master each renewable type and tradeoffs. Master energy efficiency. Know rough US energy mix.*
