## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 18–22% of the AP Physics 2 exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** charge; Coulomb's law; electric field; electric potential; potential energy; field lines; equipotential surfaces; conductors and insulators.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Coulomb's law calculations. Electric field of point charge. Potential difference and energy. Field-potential relationship.

## Big Ideas

1. **Charge is conserved and quantized** (e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C).
2. **Coulomb's law:** F = kq₁q₂/r² (k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²).
3. **Electric field:** E = F/q (force per unit charge).
4. **Electric potential:** V = U/q (potential energy per unit charge).
5. **Field points from high to low potential.**

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Charge:**
  - **Two types:** positive and negative.
  - **Like charges repel; opposite attract.**
  - **Conservation:** total charge of isolated system constant.
  - **Quantization:** charge is integer multiple of e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C.
- **Coulomb's law:**
  - **F = kq₁q₂/r²** (magnitude).
  - **k = 1/(4πε₀) = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C².**
  - **Direction:** along line between charges.
- **Electric field:**
  - **E = F/q** (force per unit positive test charge).
  - **Field of point charge:** E = kq/r².
  - **Direction:** away from positive, toward negative.
  - **Vector quantity.**
  - **Units:** N/C or V/m.
- **Field lines:**
  - **Direction = direction of E** at each point.
  - **Density = field strength.**
  - **Start on positive charges; end on negative.**
  - **Never cross.**
- **Electric potential energy:**
  - **U = kq₁q₂/r** (point charges).
  - **Positive when** like charges (repel; want to fly apart, "uphill").
  - **Negative when** opposite charges (bound; "downhill").
- **Electric potential (voltage):**
  - **V = U/q** (potential energy per unit charge).
  - **Units:** Volt (V) = J/C.
  - **Point charge:** V = kq/r.
  - **Scalar** (no direction).
- **Potential difference:** ΔV = V_b − V_a.
  - **Work to move charge q:** W = qΔV.
- **Equipotential surfaces:**
  - **Surfaces of constant V.**
  - **Perpendicular to E field.**
  - **No work to move charge along** equipotential.
- **Conductors:**
  - **Charges free to move.**
  - **In equilibrium:** field inside = 0; charges on surface.
- **Insulators:** charges don't move freely.

### Adds for [4]

- **E and V relationship:**
  - **E = −dV/dx** (gradient).
  - **Field points** in direction of decreasing potential.
- **Capacitor (introduced):**
  - **Two parallel plates** with opposite charge.
  - **C = Q/V** (capacitance).
  - **Parallel-plate:** C = ε₀A/d.
- **Charging by induction:** redistribute charges in conductor without contact.
- **Force from field:** F = qE.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why field important:** propagates effect of charges through space.
- **Why potential useful:** scalar (easier than vector field).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Coulomb's law

Two point charges +2 μC and +3 μC are 0.1 m apart. Force?
- **F = kq₁q₂/r² = (9×10⁹)(2×10⁻⁶)(3×10⁻⁶)/(0.1)² = (9×10⁹)(6×10⁻¹²)/0.01 = 5.4 N (repulsive).**

### Example 2 [3] — Field of point charge

Field 0.1 m from +2 μC charge?
- **E = kq/r² = (9×10⁹)(2×10⁻⁶)/(0.01) = 1.8×10⁶ N/C** (away from charge).

### Example 3 [4] — Potential

Potential 0.1 m from +2 μC charge?
- **V = kq/r = (9×10⁹)(2×10⁻⁶)/(0.1) = 1.8×10⁵ V.**

### Example 4 [4] — Work

Move +1 μC charge from V = 100 V to V = 50 V. Work by field?
- **W = q(V_initial − V_final) = (1×10⁻⁶)(100 − 50) = 5×10⁻⁵ J.**
- **Field does positive work** moving positive charge to lower potential.

### Example 5 [5] — Field lines

Sketch field lines for two opposite charges (+, −).
- **Lines go from + to −.**
- **Curve toward each other.**
- **Symmetric about midpoint axis.**
- **Density highest near each charge.**

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Sign in Coulomb's law.** Use magnitudes; determine direction separately.
2. **Confusing E (vector) and V (scalar).**
3. **Wrong direction of E.** From + to − (away from +, toward −).
4. **Forgetting r² in E and r in V.**
5. **Field lines never cross.**

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Coulomb's law:** apply formula with magnitudes; direction by inspection.
**For fields and potentials:** distinguish vector vs scalar.
**For conductors:** field inside = 0 in equilibrium.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Coulomb's law: F = kq₁q₂/r², where k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C². Like charges repel; unlike attract; force along line between charges."
2. "Electric field E = F/q is the force per unit positive test charge — a vector pointing away from positive and toward negative charges. For a point charge: E = kq/r²."
3. "Electric potential V = U/q is the potential energy per unit charge — a scalar quantity in Volts. For a point charge: V = kq/r."
4. "Equipotential surfaces are perpendicular to electric field lines; no work is required to move a charge along an equipotential surface."
5. "In a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, the electric field inside is zero and excess charge resides on the surface."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master Coulomb's law (F = kq₁q₂/r²). Master E = kq/r² and V = kq/r. Master field lines and equipotentials. Master W = qΔV. Conductor in equilibrium has E = 0 inside.*
