## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–12% of the AP Physics 2 exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** photoelectric effect; photon energy E = hf; matter waves (de Broglie); atomic models; energy levels; nuclear binding energy; alpha/beta/gamma decay; mass-energy equivalence (E = mc²).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Photoelectric effect (KE_max = hf − φ). Photon E = hf. de Broglie wavelength. Decay types. Half-life calculations.

## Big Ideas

1. **Light is both wave and particle** (wave-particle duality); photon energy E = hf.
2. **Photoelectric effect:** light ejects electrons; KE_max = hf − φ (work function).
3. **Matter waves:** λ = h/p (de Broglie wavelength).
4. **Atoms have discrete energy levels;** transitions emit/absorb photons.
5. **Nuclear processes:** decay (α, β, γ); E = Δmc².

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Photon:**
  - **Light quantum.**
  - **Energy:** E = hf = hc/λ (h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s).
- **Photoelectric effect:**
  - **Photon hits metal; ejects electron** if hf > work function (φ).
  - **KE_max = hf − φ** (Einstein's equation).
  - **Threshold frequency:** f₀ = φ/h (below this, no emission).
  - **Increasing intensity:** more photons → more electrons (but same KE_max).
  - **Increasing frequency:** higher KE_max.
- **de Broglie wavelength:**
  - **Matter has wave properties.**
  - **λ = h/p = h/(mv).**
- **Atomic energy levels:**
  - **Discrete (quantized).**
  - **Transitions:** photon emitted (downward) or absorbed (upward).
  - **ΔE = hf** for transition.
  - **Hydrogen levels:** E_n = −13.6/n² eV.
- **Nuclear notation:** ²³⁸U (mass number 238).
- **Decay types:**
  - **Alpha (α):** emit ⁴He nucleus. Mass number −4, atomic number −2.
  - **Beta (β⁻):** emit electron + antineutrino. Atomic number +1.
  - **Beta (β⁺):** emit positron + neutrino. Atomic number −1.
  - **Gamma (γ):** photon emitted; no change in N or Z.
- **Half-life:**
  - **Time for half of nuclei to decay.**
  - **N(t) = N₀(½)^(t/T_½).**
- **Mass-energy equivalence:**
  - **E = mc².**
  - **Mass defect** (binding energy): difference between sum of nucleon masses and nucleus mass.

### Adds for [4]

- **Photoelectric explained particle nature:**
  - **Wave model predicted:** more intense light → more KE.
  - **Particle model:** more intense → more electrons (each carries hf − φ).
  - **Einstein's 1905 explanation** earned him Nobel.
- **Compton scattering:** photon scatters off electron, loses energy.
- **Wave-particle duality:** light AND matter exhibit both.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why quantum revolutionized physics:** ended classical determinism at small scales.
- **Why nuclear binding energy matters:** fission/fusion energy.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Photon energy

Photon λ = 500 nm. Energy?
- **E = hc/λ = (6.63×10⁻³⁴)(3×10⁸)/(500×10⁻⁹) = 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 2.5 eV.**

### Example 2 [3] — Photoelectric

Metal φ = 2.0 eV. Light hf = 5.0 eV. KE_max?
- **KE_max = hf − φ = 5.0 − 2.0 = 3.0 eV.**

### Example 3 [4] — de Broglie

Electron at v = 10⁶ m/s. Wavelength?
- **p = mv = (9.11×10⁻³¹)(10⁶) = 9.11×10⁻²⁵ kg·m/s.**
- **λ = h/p = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / 9.11×10⁻²⁵ = 7.3×10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.73 nm.**

### Example 4 [4] — Half-life

A sample has 1000 atoms; half-life 5 hours. Atoms after 15 hours?
- **t/T_½ = 15/5 = 3.**
- **N = 1000 · (½)³ = 1000/8 = 125 atoms.**

### Example 5 [5] — Alpha decay

²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th + ?
- **Mass number: 238 → 234** (lost 4).
- **Atomic number: 92 → 90** (lost 2).
- **Emitted:** ⁴He (alpha particle) — atomic number 2, mass 4.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Forgetting work function** in photoelectric.
2. **Using v in de Broglie:** use p = mv (or relativistic for fast particles).
3. **Wrong decay type signature.** α: −4, −2. β⁻: 0, +1. β⁺: 0, −1.
4. **Half-life vs decay rate.**
5. **E = hf:** use SI units; h in J·s.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For photon questions:** E = hf or hc/λ.
**For photoelectric:** KE_max = hf − φ.
**For decay:** balance mass and atomic numbers.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Light exhibits wave-particle duality: photons carry energy E = hf = hc/λ. The photoelectric effect demonstrates the particle nature: KE_max = hf − φ, where φ is the work function (minimum energy to eject an electron)."
2. "de Broglie hypothesized matter waves: λ = h/p = h/(mv). Electron diffraction confirmed this experimentally."
3. "Atoms have discrete energy levels. Photons are emitted when electrons transition from higher to lower levels with energy ΔE = hf."
4. "Radioactive decay follows N(t) = N₀(½)^(t/T_½), where T_½ is the half-life. Alpha decay emits a ⁴He nucleus (mass −4, atomic number −2); beta-minus decay emits an electron (atomic number +1); gamma decay emits a photon."
5. "Mass-energy equivalence E = mc² explains nuclear energy: mass defect (the difference between sum of nucleon masses and bound nucleus mass) is converted to binding energy."
