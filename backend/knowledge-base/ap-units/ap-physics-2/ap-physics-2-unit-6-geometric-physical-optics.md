## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–17% of the AP Physics 2 exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** electromagnetic spectrum; reflection; refraction (Snell's law); total internal reflection; lenses and mirrors (thin-lens equation); interference (double-slit, thin films); diffraction.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Snell's law constantly. Mirror/lens equations. Double-slit interference (d sin θ = mλ). Thin-film interference.

## Big Ideas

1. **Light is electromagnetic wave** with wavelength λ and frequency f (c = λf).
2. **Reflection:** angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
3. **Refraction:** Snell's law (n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂).
4. **Lenses/mirrors:** 1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i.
5. **Interference:** constructive when path difference = mλ; destructive when (m+½)λ.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Light:**
  - **Speed in vacuum:** c = 3×10⁸ m/s.
  - **In medium:** v = c/n (n = index of refraction).
  - **c = λf** (wavelength × frequency).
- **Reflection:**
  - **Angle of incidence = angle of reflection** (both from normal).
- **Refraction:**
  - **Snell's law:** n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.
  - **Light bends toward normal** entering denser medium.
- **Total internal reflection:**
  - **Critical angle:** n₁ sin θ_c = n₂ sin 90° = n₂.
  - **θ_c = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁).**
  - **Beyond θ_c:** no refraction; all reflection.
  - **Used in:** fiber optics.
- **Mirrors:**
  - **Concave (converging):** focus parallel rays at focal point.
  - **Convex (diverging):** spread parallel rays.
  - **Mirror equation:** 1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i.
  - **Magnification:** m = −d_i/d_o = h_i/h_o.
- **Lenses:**
  - **Convex (converging):** focus parallel rays.
  - **Concave (diverging):** spread parallel rays.
  - **Thin-lens equation:** 1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i.
  - **f positive** for converging; **negative** for diverging.
- **Image properties:**
  - **Real image:** light actually converges; can be projected.
  - **Virtual image:** light appears to diverge from; can't project.
  - **d_i > 0:** real (same side as observer or on opposite side of lens).
  - **d_i < 0:** virtual (same side as object).
  - **m > 0:** upright; **m < 0:** inverted.
- **Double-slit interference (Young):**
  - **d sin θ = mλ** for constructive (bright fringes; m = 0, ±1, ±2...).
  - **d sin θ = (m + ½)λ** for destructive.
  - **Fringe spacing:** y = λL/d (small angle).
- **Diffraction:**
  - **Light bends around obstacles.**
  - **Single-slit:** central maximum, secondary maxima decreasing.
- **Thin-film interference:**
  - **Light reflects off front and back surface** of thin film.
  - **Path difference + phase changes** determine constructive/destructive.

### Adds for [4]

- **Refraction examples:**
  - **Glass:** n ≈ 1.5.
  - **Water:** n ≈ 1.33.
  - **Diamond:** n ≈ 2.4.
- **Total internal reflection requires** going from higher n to lower n.
- **Image formation by ray tracing:**
  - **Rays parallel to axis** → through focal point.
  - **Rays through focal point** → parallel.
  - **Rays through center** → straight through (lens) or reflect (mirror).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why thin films colorful:** different wavelengths interfere differently.
- **Why dispersion:** n depends on λ; different colors refract differently (prism, rainbow).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Snell's law

Light passes from air (n=1) into water (n=1.33) at 30° from normal. Angle in water?
- **n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.**
- **1 · sin 30° = 1.33 · sin θ₂.**
- **sin θ₂ = 0.5/1.33 = 0.376.**
- **θ₂ ≈ 22°.**

### Example 2 [3] — Critical angle

Light from glass (n=1.5) to air (n=1). Critical angle?
- **sin θ_c = n₂/n₁ = 1/1.5 = 0.667.**
- **θ_c ≈ 42°.**

### Example 3 [4] — Lens

Object 30 cm from converging lens with f = 10 cm. Image distance?
- **1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i.**
- **1/10 = 1/30 + 1/d_i.**
- **1/d_i = 1/10 − 1/30 = 3/30 − 1/30 = 2/30 = 1/15.**
- **d_i = 15 cm** (real, inverted).

### Example 4 [4] — Double-slit

Slits separated d = 0.1 mm; screen L = 2 m away. Light λ = 500 nm. First-order bright fringe location?
- **For m=1: d sin θ = λ → sin θ = 500×10⁻⁹/10⁻⁴ = 5×10⁻³.**
- **y = L tan θ ≈ L sin θ = 2 · 5×10⁻³ = 0.01 m = 1 cm.**

### Example 5 [5] — Diffraction

Single-slit diffraction creates pattern. Where are first dark fringes?
- **a sin θ = mλ (m = ±1, ±2, ...) for dark.**
- **First dark:** sin θ = λ/a.
- **Width of central max ≈ 2λL/a.**

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Snell's law direction.** sin θ inversely proportional to n: denser medium has smaller angle.
2. **Sign conventions for lenses/mirrors.** Memorize.
3. **Total internal reflection requires going** from higher n to lower n.
4. **Confusing converging/diverging.** Convex lens converges; concave diverges.
5. **Wrong interference condition.** Constructive: mλ. Destructive: (m+½)λ.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Snell's law:** identify which medium is n₁; angles from normal.
**For lenses:** sign convention; ray diagrams.
**For interference:** identify d, λ, L; apply formula.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Snell's law n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ governs refraction. Light bends toward the normal entering a denser medium (higher n)."
2. "Total internal reflection occurs when light traveling from a higher-index medium hits the boundary at angle greater than critical angle θ_c = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁) — used in fiber optics."
3. "Thin-lens equation: 1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i. Magnification m = −d_i/d_o. Sign conventions: f positive for converging lenses, negative for diverging; d_i positive for real images, negative for virtual."
4. "Double-slit interference: bright fringes at d sin θ = mλ (constructive); dark at d sin θ = (m+½)λ. Fringe spacing y = λL/d (small angle)."
5. "Diffraction is the bending of light around obstacles or through apertures. Single-slit produces a central maximum with first dark fringes at sin θ = λ/a."
