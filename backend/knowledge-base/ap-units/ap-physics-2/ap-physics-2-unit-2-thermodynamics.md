## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–18% of the AP Physics 2 exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** temperature; thermal energy; ideal gas law; kinetic theory; heat transfer (conduction, convection, radiation); first and second laws of thermodynamics; PV diagrams; entropy.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Ideal gas law calculations. PV diagrams (work as area). First law (ΔU = Q − W). Heat engines. Entropy as direction of spontaneous change.

## Big Ideas

1. **Temperature is average kinetic energy** of molecules.
2. **Ideal Gas Law:** PV = nRT (or PV = NkT).
3. **First Law of Thermodynamics:** ΔU = Q − W (energy conservation).
4. **Second Law:** entropy of isolated system increases.
5. **Heat engines** can't be 100% efficient (Carnot bound).

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Temperature:**
  - **Average translational KE** of molecules.
  - **K (Kelvin) = °C + 273.**
- **Thermal energy:** total energy in molecular motion.
- **Heat (Q):** energy transferred due to temperature difference.
- **Specific heat:** Q = mcΔT (c = specific heat capacity).
- **Heat transfer modes:**
  - **Conduction:** through solid contact.
  - **Convection:** via fluid motion.
  - **Radiation:** electromagnetic waves (no medium needed).
- **Ideal gas law:**
  - **PV = nRT** (n = moles, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)).
  - **PV = NkT** (N = molecules, k = Boltzmann constant 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K).
  - **Average KE per molecule:** (3/2)kT.
- **PV diagrams:**
  - **Plot pressure vs volume** for gas process.
  - **Work done BY gas:** W = area under curve (positive for expansion, negative for compression).
  - **Cycle:** area enclosed = net work.
- **Processes:**
  - **Isothermal:** constant T (PV = const, hyperbola).
  - **Isobaric:** constant P (horizontal line).
  - **Isochoric (isovolumetric):** constant V (vertical line).
  - **Adiabatic:** no heat (Q = 0).
- **First Law:** ΔU = Q − W.
  - **U:** internal energy.
  - **Q:** heat added to system.
  - **W:** work done BY system.
- **Second Law:** entropy of isolated system increases (ΔS ≥ 0).
- **Heat engine:** converts heat to work; efficiency = W/Q_h.
- **Carnot efficiency (max):** η = 1 − T_c/T_h (T in Kelvin).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why isothermal hyperbolic:** PV = nRT = const at fixed T.
- **Adiabatic curves steeper than isothermal** for same starting point.
- **Sign conventions:** positive Q (heat in), positive W (work by gas).
- **Heat pumps and refrigerators:** reverse heat engines (work in, heat out of cold).
- **Entropy:** measure of disorder; ΔS = Q/T (reversible).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why second law fundamental:** gives "arrow of time"; spontaneous direction.
- **Why Carnot limit:** any real engine has more losses.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Ideal gas law

2 moles of ideal gas at 300 K, 1 atm (10⁵ Pa). Find volume.
- **PV = nRT → V = nRT/P = 2·8.314·300/10⁵ = 0.0499 m³ ≈ 50 L.**

### Example 2 [3] — Specific heat

How much heat to warm 1 kg water from 20°C to 100°C? c_water = 4186 J/(kg·K).
- **Q = mcΔT = 1·4186·80 = 334,880 J ≈ 335 kJ.**

### Example 3 [4] — First law

Gas absorbs 500 J heat and does 200 J work. Find ΔU.
- **ΔU = Q − W = 500 − 200 = 300 J.**

### Example 4 [4] — Carnot efficiency

Heat engine operates between 600 K and 300 K. Max efficiency?
- **η = 1 − 300/600 = 0.5 = 50%.**

### Example 5 [5] — Cycle on PV diagram

A cycle traces clockwise loop with enclosed area 200 J. What's net work?
- **Clockwise = positive work BY gas.**
- **Net work = enclosed area = 200 J.**

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Forgetting Kelvin** (T in K for gas law and Carnot).
2. **Sign confusion in first law.** ΔU = Q − W (W is work BY gas).
3. **Wrong direction on PV cycle.** Clockwise = engine (positive work). Counterclockwise = refrigerator.
4. **Confusing heat and temperature.** Heat is energy transfer; temperature is property.
5. **Carnot only sets max efficiency** — real engines lower.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For PV diagrams:** identify processes; calculate work as area.
**For first law:** identify Q and W; ΔU = Q − W.
**For Carnot:** use Kelvin temperatures.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The Ideal Gas Law PV = nRT relates pressure, volume, moles, and temperature (Kelvin) for an ideal gas."
2. "Temperature is proportional to average translational kinetic energy: ⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)kT per molecule."
3. "First Law of Thermodynamics: ΔU = Q − W, where Q is heat added to system and W is work done by system. Energy is conserved."
4. "On a PV diagram, work done by the gas equals the area under the curve. For a cyclic process, net work equals the enclosed area (positive if clockwise, negative if counterclockwise)."
5. "Carnot efficiency η = 1 − T_c/T_h sets the maximum possible efficiency for a heat engine operating between hot and cold reservoirs (temperatures in Kelvin)."
