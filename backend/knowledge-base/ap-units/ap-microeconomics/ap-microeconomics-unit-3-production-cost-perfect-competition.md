## Big Ideas

1. **Profit = Total Revenue − Total Cost.** Firms maximize profit by producing the quantity where MARGINAL REVENUE = MARGINAL COST (MR = MC).
2. **Costs split into fixed (constant regardless of output) and variable (depend on output).** Average costs and marginal cost are derived from these.
3. **The cost curves have characteristic shapes.** ATC is U-shaped. MC intersects ATC and AVC at their MINIMUM points.
4. **In perfect competition, firms are PRICE TAKERS.** Demand for each firm is HORIZONTAL at market price. P = MR = AR for the firm.
5. **In long run, perfect competition leads to ZERO ECONOMIC PROFIT.** New firms enter when there are profits, exit when there are losses, until profits approach zero.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Production function:** relationship between inputs (labor, capital) and output. Often denoted Q = f(L, K).
- **Short run vs long run:**
  - **Short run:** at least one factor (usually capital) is fixed.
  - **Long run:** all factors variable. Firms can adjust everything.
- **Marginal product (MP):** additional output from one more unit of input.
  - **Diminishing marginal returns:** as more variable input added (with fixed input constant), MP eventually decreases. (Increasing returns at first, then diminishing.)
- **Total cost (TC) = Fixed Cost (FC) + Variable Cost (VC).**
  - **FC:** doesn't change with output (rent, machinery, salaries).
  - **VC:** changes with output (materials, hourly wages).
- **Average costs (per unit):**
  - **AFC = FC/Q** (average fixed cost — declines as Q increases).
  - **AVC = VC/Q** (average variable cost — usually U-shaped).
  - **ATC = TC/Q = AFC + AVC** (average total cost — U-shaped).
- **Marginal Cost (MC) = ΔTC/ΔQ.** Cost of producing one more unit. Often U-shaped (or initially decreasing then increasing).
- **Relationship between MC and average costs:**
  - When MC < ATC, ATC is decreasing.
  - When MC > ATC, ATC is increasing.
  - **MC intersects ATC and AVC at their minimum points.**
- **Profit:**
  - **Total profit = TR − TC.**
  - **Per-unit profit = (P − ATC) at given Q.**
- **Types of profit:**
  - **Accounting profit** = TR − explicit costs (only).
  - **Economic profit** = TR − explicit costs − implicit costs.
  - **Implicit costs** include opportunity cost of owner's time, foregone alternative investments.
  - In economics, "profit" usually means ECONOMIC PROFIT.
- **Profit maximization rule:** produce where MR = MC.
- **Shutdown decision:** in short run, produce if P ≥ AVC. Otherwise, shut down (still pay fixed costs).
- **Exit decision:** in long run, exit if P < ATC. Otherwise, stay.
- **Perfect Competition characteristics:**
  - **Many sellers and buyers** (no individual can affect price).
  - **Identical products** (no differentiation).
  - **Free entry and exit.**
  - **Perfect information.**
  - Examples (close to): agricultural markets, foreign exchange.
- **Firm in perfect competition:**
  - **Price taker:** accepts market price.
  - **Demand curve for firm:** HORIZONTAL at market price.
  - **P = MR = AR.**
- **Profit maximization in perfect competition:** produce where P = MC (since P = MR).
- **Short-run vs long-run equilibrium in perfect competition:**
  - **Short-run:** firms can earn profit, loss, or break even. Whatever P happens to be.
  - **Long-run:** firms enter (when profits exist) or exit (when losses) until P = ATC at minimum (zero economic profit).

### Adds for [4]

- **Production function shape:**
  - At first: increasing marginal returns (more inputs → output increases more than proportionally).
  - Then: diminishing marginal returns (more inputs → output increases less).
  - Eventually: negative marginal returns.
- **Cost curves drawn together:**
  - MC starts low, falls slightly, then rises (U-shape or J-shape).
  - AVC and ATC are U-shaped.
  - MC crosses AVC and ATC at their minimum points.
  - AFC declines monotonically (1/Q shape).
- **Why MC intersects ATC at minimum:**
  - When MC < ATC: each additional unit costs less than the average → ATC decreases.
  - When MC > ATC: each additional unit costs more than the average → ATC increases.
  - When MC = ATC: ATC at minimum.
- **Long-run cost curves:**
  - Each short-run ATC corresponds to a specific plant size.
  - Long-run ATC is the envelope of all short-run ATCs (the lower bound).
  - Shows economies of scale (LRATC decreasing), diseconomies (increasing), constant returns (flat).
- **Profit maximization for perfect competition:**
  - P = MR (since price taker).
  - Set P = MC, solve for Q*.
  - At Q*: profit = (P − ATC) × Q*.
  - If P > ATC: profit positive.
  - If P = ATC: zero economic profit (break-even).
  - If P < ATC: loss.
- **Short-run shutdown rule:**
  - Continue producing as long as P ≥ AVC.
  - If P < AVC: SHUT DOWN. Producing only adds to losses.
  - If P = AVC: indifferent (covering variable costs but losing fixed costs).
- **Long-run exit decision:**
  - Exit if P < ATC (ALWAYS losing money in long run).
  - If P = ATC at minimum: zero economic profit, but firms stay.
  - If P > ATC: economic profit, more firms enter, P falls.
- **Long-run equilibrium in perfect competition:**
  - All firms operate at minimum ATC.
  - P = MR = MC = ATC at minimum.
  - Zero economic profit.
  - Allocative efficiency (P = MC = MB to society).
  - Productive efficiency (firms produce at minimum ATC).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why diminishing returns happens.** With fixed capital, each additional unit of variable input has less capital to work with. Eventually adding workers without more equipment/space slows productivity.
- **Why MC = MR is the profit-max rule.** If MR > MC, producing one more unit adds to profit. If MR < MC, producing one more unit reduces profit. Profit is maximized where MR = MC.
- **Why economic profit drives long-run dynamics.** In perfect competition, positive economic profit attracts new firms (free entry). Their entry shifts market supply right, lowering price. Process continues until P = ATC at minimum and economic profit = 0. Symmetrical exit when losses occur.
- **Economic vs accounting profit.**
  - Accounting profit shows up on income statements.
  - Economic profit ALSO subtracts opportunity costs (implicit costs).
  - A firm with $100,000 accounting profit may have NEGATIVE economic profit if owner's time/money would earn more elsewhere.
  - **Zero economic profit = NORMAL profit** (covering all opportunity costs).
- **Why perfect competition produces efficient outcomes.**
  - **Allocative efficiency:** P = MC = marginal benefit to society. Right amount produced.
  - **Productive efficiency:** firms produce at minimum ATC. Most efficient production.
  - **Distributive efficiency:** consumer surplus + producer surplus maximized.
  - This is the "benchmark" against which other market structures are compared.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Cost calculations

A firm produces 100 units. Total fixed cost = $500. Total variable cost = $300.

(a) Calculate TC, AFC, AVC, ATC.
(b) If MC for the 100th unit was $5, what does that tell us?

- **(a)** TC = FC + VC = 500 + 300 = $800.
  - AFC = 500/100 = $5/unit.
  - AVC = 300/100 = $3/unit.
  - ATC = 800/100 = $8/unit (or AFC + AVC = 5 + 3 = $8).
- **(b)** MC for 100th unit = $5. Since MC ($5) < ATC ($8), ATC is DECREASING. Producing more would lower average cost.

### Example 2 [3][4] — Perfect competition profit maximization

In a perfectly competitive market, a firm faces market price P = $20. The firm's MC = 2Q (where Q is quantity).

(a) Find profit-maximizing Q.
(b) If ATC at this Q is $15, calculate profit.

- **(a)** P = MC: 20 = 2Q → Q = 10.
- **(b)** Profit per unit = P − ATC = 20 − 15 = $5. Total profit = 5 × 10 = $50.

### Example 3 [4] — Short-run shutdown

A firm faces price P = $5. At its profit-maximizing quantity, ATC = $7 and AVC = $4.

(a) Should the firm produce? Continue or shut down?
(b) Compute losses if continuing.

- **(a)** P ($5) ≥ AVC ($4). Continue producing.
- **(b)** Losses per unit = P − ATC = 5 − 7 = −$2. Firm loses $2 per unit.
  - But fixed costs = ATC × Q − VC × Q = (7-4) × Q = $3 × Q.
  - If shut down, firm loses ALL fixed costs.
  - By producing, firm covers part of fixed costs (revenue covers AVC plus some).
  - Therefore, continue producing.
- **Key insight:** even at a loss, producing minimizes losses if P ≥ AVC.

### Example 4 [4][5] — Long-run equilibrium in perfect competition

In a perfectly competitive market, current price P = $25. Firms are earning economic profit. Predict long-run outcome.

- **Step 1.** Positive profit → NEW FIRMS ENTER market.
- **Step 2.** Increased supply shifts market supply curve RIGHT.
- **Step 3.** Market price falls.
- **Step 4.** Continue until P = ATC at minimum (zero economic profit).
- **Step 5.** At long-run equilibrium:
  - Firms produce at minimum ATC.
  - Each firm has zero economic profit.
  - P = MR = MC = ATC at minimum.
  - Allocative AND productive efficiency.

### Example 5 [5] — Economies of scale

A firm finds that as it doubles inputs, output more than doubles.

(a) What does this indicate?
(b) What about long-run cost curve shape?

- **(a)** **Economies of scale:** producing larger quantities lowers average cost. Output increases more than proportionally to inputs.
- **(b)** Long-run ATC is DECREASING in this region. Big firms have a cost advantage over small firms.
- **Real-world example:** automotive manufacturing — large-scale production lowers per-unit costs through specialization, technology, bulk purchasing.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing fixed and variable costs.** FC: doesn't change with Q. VC: changes with Q.
2. **Wrong shape of cost curves.** ATC is U-shaped. MC is U-shaped. MC intersects ATC and AVC at their MINIMUMS.
3. **Forgetting profit max rule.** MR = MC.
4. **Wrong shutdown rule.** Shut down if P < AVC. Otherwise produce.
5. **Confusing accounting and economic profit.** Economic profit subtracts implicit costs too.
6. **Treating perfect competition as monopoly.** Perfect competition: many firms, identical product, price takers.
7. **Wrong demand curve for perfect competition firm.** Firm's demand is HORIZONTAL at market price.
8. **Wrong long-run equilibrium prediction.** Long-run: ZERO economic profit.
9. **Confusing per-unit profit with total profit.** Per-unit = P − ATC. Total = (P − ATC) × Q.
10. **Forgetting that fixed costs are sunk in short run.** If you've paid for the building, that cost is sunk.
11. **Wrong relationship between MC and ATC.** MC < ATC → ATC falling. MC > ATC → ATC rising.
12. **Not labeling cost curves correctly.** ATC, MC, AVC must be distinguished.
13. **Treating firm and market levels as same.** Firm's demand is horizontal. Market demand is downward-sloping.
14. **Forgetting why long-run profit = 0 in perfect competition.** Free entry/exit drives profit to zero.
15. **Misreading profit/loss areas on graph.** Box from price to ATC times quantity.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For cost curves:**
- Label correctly: AFC, AVC, ATC, MC.
- Show MC crossing ATC and AVC at minimums.
- ATC = AFC + AVC.

**For profit maximization:**
- Set P = MC (perfect competition).
- Compute Q*.
- Profit = (P − ATC) × Q.

**For shutdown:**
- Compare P to AVC (short-run).
- Compare P to ATC (long-run).

**For long-run perfect competition:**
- Recognize zero economic profit.
- Firms operate at minimum ATC.
- Show entry/exit dynamics.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "A perfectly competitive firm is a PRICE TAKER, facing a horizontal demand curve at the market price. P = MR = AR for the firm."
2. "A firm maximizes profit by producing the quantity where MARGINAL REVENUE = MARGINAL COST. Below this quantity, MR > MC (more profit); above this quantity, MC > MR (less profit)."
3. "In the short run, a firm should continue producing as long as P ≥ AVC, even at a loss, because it covers some of the fixed costs."
4. "In the long run in perfect competition, free entry and exit drive economic profits to ZERO. Firms operate at the minimum of their average total cost curve, achieving both productive and allocative efficiency."
5. "Marginal cost equals average total cost at the MINIMUM of ATC. When MC < ATC, ATC is decreasing; when MC > ATC, ATC is increasing."
6. "Total cost = Fixed Cost + Variable Cost. Average cost (per unit) = Total Cost / Quantity. AFC = FC/Q (declines with Q). AVC = VC/Q. ATC = AFC + AVC."
7. "Economic profit subtracts both explicit costs (accounting) AND implicit costs (opportunity cost of owner's time, foregone alternatives). Zero economic profit means firms cover all opportunity costs — they're earning a NORMAL profit."
