# AP Art History — Unit 5: Indigenous Americas (1000 BCE-1980 CE) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~6% of the AP Art History exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Mesoamerica (Olmec, Maya, Aztec, Teotihuacan); Andes (Chavín, Moche, Inka); North America (Eastern Woodlands, Southwest, Plains, Northwest Coast).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** ~14 of 250 images. Major ceremonial centers, ceramics, textiles.

## Big Ideas

1. **Mesoamerican civilizations** built monumental ceremonial centers (pyramids, ball courts).
2. **Andean civilizations** mastered textiles, ceramics, metallurgy; Inka built Machu Picchu.
3. **North American native art** varies by region: Eastern mounds, SW pueblos, Plains hide painting, NW coastal carving.
4. **Religion drives much of art:** rulers, gods, ancestors.
5. **Continuity through colonialism:** indigenous artistic traditions persist.

### Adds for [4]

- **Aztec sacrifice and Templo Mayor:**
  - **Twin temples** to Tlaloc (rain god) and Huitzilopochtli (war/sun god).
  - **Ritual sacrifice** central to ceremonies.
  - **Spanish destroyed** but archaeological excavation continues.
- **Inka achievements:**
  - **Quipu:** knotted cord recording system (no writing).
  - **Stoneworking:** precise fitting without mortar.
  - **Roads** linked vast empire (~25,000 miles).
- **Pueblo cliff dwellings:**
  - **Built into cliffs** for defense, climate.
  - **Mesa Verde, Chaco Canyon** major sites.
  - **Continuity:** modern Pueblo peoples descend from "Ancestral Puebloans."

### Adds for [5]

- **Why ceremonial centers reflect cosmology:**
  - **Pyramids = sacred mountains** in many Mesoamerican cosmologies.
  - **Astronomical alignments** common.
- **Why textiles central in Andes:**
  - **Status, religious, exchange functions.**
  - **Sumptuous textiles** offered to deities.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Match work to culture

For each, identify culture:
(a) Olmec colossal head.
(b) Machu Picchu.
(c) Cahokia mounds.

- (a) **Olmec (Mesoamerica, ~900 BCE).**
- (b) **Inka (Andes, ~1450 CE).**
- (c) **Mississippian (North America, ~1100 CE).**

### Example 2 [4] — Templo Mayor

Describe Templo Mayor.
- **Aztec ceremonial center** in Tenochtitlán (today Mexico City).
- **Pyramid with twin temples** at top.
- **Tlaloc (rain god) + Huitzilopochtli (war/sun god).**
- **Site of ritual sacrifice.**
- **Destroyed by Spanish (1521+);** rediscovered through 20th-century excavation.

### Example 3 [5] — Inka stonework

What was distinctive about Inka stonework (e.g., Machu Picchu)?
- **Precise fitting** of massive irregular stones without mortar.
- **Earthquake-resistant.**
- **Stones polished smooth.**
- **Astronomical alignments** built into structures.
- **No iron tools** — achieved through stone hammering.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Distinguish Mesoamerican civilizations:** Olmec → Maya → Teotihuacan → Aztec.
2. **Andean civilizations:** Chavín → Moche → Wari → Inka.
3. **Inka had no writing** — used quipu (knotted cords).
4. **Templo Mayor = Aztec** (not Maya).
5. **Diverse North American traditions** by region.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For attribution:** identify culture, region.
**For function:** religious/ceremonial usually central.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Mesoamerican ceremonial centers (Teotihuacan's Pyramid of the Sun, Maya temples, Aztec Templo Mayor) functioned as ritual focal points where pyramids served as sacred mountains, often aligned with astronomical events and dedicated to specific deities."
2. "Inka builders at Machu Picchu fit massive irregular stones together without mortar with such precision that the structures have survived earthquakes for centuries — achieved without iron tools, using stone hammering."
3. "North American indigenous art varies dramatically by region: the Mississippian mound complexes (Cahokia), Ancestral Puebloan cliff dwellings (Mesa Verde), Northwest Coast totem poles and masks (Tlingit, Haida), and Plains beadwork and ledger drawings."
