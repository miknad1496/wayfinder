# AP Art History — Unit 10: Global Contemporary (1980-Present) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~11% of the AP Art History exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** post-1980 art globally; new media (video, installation, performance); identity politics; globalization; key artists (Wodiczko, Anatsui, Mutu, Kngwarreye).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** ~27 of 250 images. Diverse global contemporary art; engagement with identity, politics, technology.

## Big Ideas

1. **Contemporary art is global** — not just Euro-American.
2. **New media:** video, performance, installation, digital, conceptual.
3. **Identity** (race, gender, sexuality, nation) prominent theme.
4. **Globalization** both subject and condition of contemporary art.
5. **Postmodernism** rejects single narrative; pluralism, irony, appropriation.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Key works:**
  - **The Gates (Christo and Jeanne-Claude, 2005, Central Park):** environmental installation.
  - **Pink Panther (Jeff Koons, 1988):** kitsch high art.
  - **The Crossing (Bill Viola, 1996):** video.
  - **Earth's Creation (Emily Kame Kngwarreye, 1994):** Aboriginal painter.
  - **Trade (Gifts for Trading Land with White People) (Jaune Quick-to-See Smith, 1992).**
  - **Untitled (1993, Lin Tianmiao):** Chinese contemporary.
  - **Old Man's Cloth (El Anatsui, 2003):** sculpture from bottle caps.
  - **A Book from the Sky (Xu Bing, 1987-1991):** invented Chinese characters.
  - **Pure Land (Mariko Mori, 1998):** Japanese contemporary, technology + Buddhism.
  - **Stadia II (Julie Mehretu, 2004):** abstract painting referencing global capital.
  - **Preying Mantra (Wangechi Mutu, 2006):** Kenyan-American collage.
- **Movements/themes:**
  - **Identity art:** race, gender, sexuality (Kara Walker, Glenn Ligon, Fred Wilson).
  - **Postmodernism:** pastiche, irony, appropriation.
  - **Installation art.**
  - **Video and digital art** (Bill Viola, Pipilotti Rist).
  - **Performance art.**
  - **Earthworks** (Christo, Smithson).
  - **Street art:** Banksy, Basquiat (earlier).

### Adds for [4]

- **El Anatsui:**
  - **Ghanaian sculptor.**
  - **Tapestries from bottle caps and metal.**
  - **References trade, colonialism, environment.**
- **Wangechi Mutu:**
  - **Kenyan-American.**
  - **Collage and sculpture exploring** Black female body, hybridity.
- **Kara Walker:**
  - **African American.**
  - **Cut paper silhouettes** depicting antebellum violence.
  - **Confronts viewer with racial history.**
- **Banksy:**
  - **Anonymous British street artist.**
  - **Stenciled political/social commentary.**

### Adds for [5]

- **Why contemporary art global:**
  - **Internet, biennials, art markets** connect.
  - **No single center** (Paris/NYC dominance fading).
- **Why "postmodern":**
  - **Skepticism toward grand narratives.**
  - **Pastiche, appropriation, irony.**
  - **"High" and "low" culture mixed.**

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify artists

For each work, identify artist:
(a) The Gates (Central Park).
(b) Old Man's Cloth (bottle cap tapestry).
(c) A Book from the Sky.

- (a) **Christo and Jeanne-Claude.**
- (b) **El Anatsui.**
- (c) **Xu Bing.**

### Example 2 [4] — Identity art

How does Kara Walker engage racial history?
- **Cut paper silhouettes** in deceptively delicate medium.
- **Depicts antebellum violence** (slavery, sexual abuse).
- **Forces viewer confrontation** with historical horror.
- **Works at room scale,** wraps walls, immersive.

### Example 3 [5] — Globalization

How does El Anatsui's work address globalization?
- **Materials:** discarded bottle caps and metal — global consumer waste.
- **Form:** evokes Kente cloth (Ghanaian heritage).
- **Process:** assembled by hand, by community.
- **Trade reference:** historic trade routes brought goods, materials.
- **Tension:** local craft + global trade systems.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Contemporary art is global** — don't focus only on Western artists.
2. **Identity is major theme** but not the only one.
3. **El Anatsui = Ghanaian** (not generic "African").
4. **Many media coexist** — painting, sculpture, installation, video, performance.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For contemporary art:** identify artist, materials, themes.
**For analysis:** address how form/medium serves content.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Contemporary art (1980-present) is genuinely global, with major artists working across continents — El Anatsui (Ghana) creating tapestries from bottle caps, Xu Bing (China) inventing illegible Chinese characters, Kara Walker (US) cutting paper silhouettes of antebellum violence."
2. "Identity art examines how race, gender, sexuality, and nationality shape experience. Kara Walker's silhouettes confront American racial history; Wangechi Mutu's collages explore the Black female body across cultures."
3. "El Anatsui's monumental tapestries — assembled from thousands of discarded bottle caps and aluminum strips — evoke Ghanaian Kente cloth while engaging globalization, trade, and consumer waste."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master that contemporary art is global. Master identity art (Walker, Mutu). Master El Anatsui, Xu Bing, Christo. Master varieties of media (installation, video, performance).*
