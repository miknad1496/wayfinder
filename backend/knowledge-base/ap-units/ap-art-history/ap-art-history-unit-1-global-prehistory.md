# AP Art History — Unit 1: Global Prehistory (30,000-500 BCE) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~4% of the AP Art History exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Paleolithic, Neolithic, Bronze Age art; cave paintings; megalithic structures; small-scale figurines; pottery.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** ~250-image curriculum includes 11 prehistoric works. Tested for materials, function, cultural context.

## Big Ideas

1. **Earliest art** dates back ~30,000+ years (cave paintings).
2. **Function:** ritual, communication, status — rarely "art for art's sake."
3. **Materials shaped by technology:** stone, bone, clay → eventually metals.
4. **Megalithic structures** (Stonehenge, Tlatilco) reflect organized labor.
5. **Continuity:** prehistoric forms influence later art.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **AP Art History 250 prehistoric works (key examples):**
  - **Apollo 11 Stones** (Namibia, 25,500-25,300 BCE): earliest known painting in Africa.
  - **Great Hall of the Bulls (Lascaux)** (France, 15,000-13,000 BCE): cave paintings.
  - **Camelid sacrum in shape of canine** (Mexico, 14,000-7,000 BCE): repurposed bone.
  - **Running horned woman, Tassili n'Ajjer** (Algeria, 6,000-4,000 BCE): rock painting.
  - **Beaker with ibex motifs** (Susa, Iran, 4,200-3,500 BCE): pottery.
  - **Anthropomorphic stele** (Saudi Arabia, 4,000 BCE): early figurative.
  - **Jade cong** (China, 3,300-2,200 BCE).
  - **Stonehenge** (England, 2,500-1,600 BCE): megalithic.
  - **Ambum stone** (Papua New Guinea, 1,500 BCE): zoomorphic stone.
  - **Tlatilco female figurine** (Mexico, 1,200-900 BCE).
  - **Terra cotta fragment** (Lapita peoples, 1,000 BCE).
- **Prehistoric periods:**
  - **Paleolithic (Old Stone Age):** ~30,000-10,000 BCE; hunter-gatherers; cave paintings, small figurines.
  - **Neolithic (New Stone Age):** ~10,000-3,000 BCE; agriculture, settlements, pottery, megaliths.
  - **Bronze Age:** ~3,000-1,200 BCE; metallurgy.
- **Cave painting techniques:**
  - **Pigments:** ochre, charcoal, hematite.
  - **Application:** brushes, blowing through reeds.
  - **Subject:** mostly animals (bison, horses, deer); few humans.
  - **Locations:** deep caves, suggesting ritual.
- **Function debates:**
  - **Ritual/shamanic** (most common interpretation).
  - **Hunting magic.**
  - **Storytelling/teaching.**
  - **Aesthetic** (less likely sole motive).
- **Stonehenge:**
  - **Stones brought from far** (some bluestones from Wales, ~150 miles).
  - **Astronomical alignment** (summer solstice).
  - **Function:** ceremonial, calendar, burial site.
  - **Massive labor** organization.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why cave paintings deep:** preservation; sacred space.
- **Lascaux's Great Hall of Bulls:** ~600 painted images; some animals 15+ feet long; multiple superimposed.
- **Why female figurines (Tlatilco etc.) emphasized:** likely fertility.
- **Megalithic architecture beyond Stonehenge:** dolmens, Avebury, Carnac (France).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why prehistoric art matters:** challenges assumption that "civilization" = sophisticated art; demonstrates universal human aesthetic impulse.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify period

For each, identify period:
(a) Lascaux cave paintings.
(b) Stonehenge.
(c) Tlatilco figurine.

- (a) **Paleolithic** (~15,000 BCE).
- (b) **Neolithic/Bronze Age transition** (~2,500 BCE).
- (c) **Neolithic Mesoamerica** (~1,200-900 BCE).

### Example 2 [4] — Function

Why might Lascaux paintings be deep in caves?
- **Preservation:** away from weather.
- **Sacred space:** ritual context away from daily life.
- **Difficult access** suggests special occasions.
- **Possibly shamanic:** dark, sensory-deprived environments altered states.

### Example 3 [5] — Cultural significance

Why is Stonehenge significant?
- **Massive labor coordination** — implies social organization.
- **Astronomical alignments** — knowledge of cosmic cycles.
- **Multi-generational construction.**
- **Ritual/ceremonial site:** burials nearby.
- **Stone transport** (bluestones from Wales, ~150 miles) — engineering feat.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Wrong period assignments.** Paleolithic before Neolithic before Bronze Age.
2. **Cave paintings predate Stonehenge** by ~10,000+ years.
3. **Function debates** — don't assert single function with certainty.
4. **Materials matter** for context (cave wall vs portable figurine).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For attribution questions:** identify culture/period from materials/style.
**For function questions:** address possible meanings; cite evidence.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The Apollo 11 Stones (Namibia, ~25,500 BCE) and Lascaux Great Hall of Bulls (France, ~15,000 BCE) demonstrate that aesthetic and symbolic expression are tens of thousands of years old."
2. "Stonehenge (~2,500 BCE) reflects substantial social organization: the bluestones were transported from Wales (~150 miles); the structure aligns with the summer solstice; the site served ritual, calendrical, and burial functions."
3. "Prehistoric art's function — ritual, shamanic, calendrical, or commemorative — is debated, but the placement of paintings deep in caves and the labor invested in megaliths suggest meanings beyond mere decoration."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master ~5 key prehistoric works (Lascaux, Stonehenge, Tlatilco figurine, Apollo 11 Stones). Master Paleolithic/Neolithic distinction. Master function debates.*
