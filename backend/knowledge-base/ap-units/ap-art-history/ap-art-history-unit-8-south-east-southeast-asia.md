# AP Art History — Unit 8: South, East, and Southeast Asia (300 BCE-1980 CE) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~8% of the AP Art History exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Hindu and Buddhist art (India, Cambodia, Indonesia); Chinese painting, calligraphy, ceramics; Japanese painting, prints, gardens; Korean.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** ~21 of 250 images. Buddhist iconography, Chinese landscape painting, Japanese woodblock prints.

## Big Ideas

1. **Buddhist art** spread across Asia from India.
2. **Hindu art** in India, Cambodia (Angkor), Indonesia.
3. **Chinese landscape painting** highly developed; tradition of literati artists.
4. **Japanese woodblock prints (ukiyo-e)** influenced European Impressionism.
5. **Calligraphy** elevated as art form (China, Japan, Korea).

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Key works:**
  - **Great Stupa, Sanchi (Buddhist, India)** (~150 BCE-50 CE).
  - **Buddha statues** (Gupta India, Tang China, etc.).
  - **Borobudur (Buddhist, Java)** (~750-825 CE): massive stupa.
  - **Angkor Wat (Hindu, Cambodia)** (~1113-1150).
  - **Forbidden City, Beijing** (~1406-1420 onwards).
  - **Travelers Among Mountains and Streams (Fan Kuan)** (~1000): Northern Song landscape.
  - **The Great Wave off Kanagawa (Hokusai)** (~1830-1832).
  - **Chinese ceramics** (porcelain, Ming blue-and-white).
  - **Japanese tea ceremony bowls.**
- **Buddhism in art:**
  - **Buddha iconography:** ushnisha (cranial protrusion), urna (forehead mark), mudras (hand gestures), elongated earlobes.
  - **Mudras:** dhyana (meditation), bhumisparsha (earth-touching, enlightenment), abhaya (fearlessness).
- **Hinduism in art:**
  - **Multiple gods:** Shiva, Vishnu, Brahma, Devi.
  - **Iconography:** multiple arms, weapons, symbols.
- **Chinese painting traditions:**
  - **Landscape (shan shui — "mountain water"):** dominant since Song dynasty.
  - **Calligraphy:** highest art form.
  - **Literati painting:** by educated officials.
- **Japanese woodblock prints (ukiyo-e):**
  - **"Floating world"** — urban entertainment districts.
  - **Hokusai, Hiroshige.**
  - **Influenced** Van Gogh, Monet, Degas, others.

### Adds for [4]

- **Stupa structure:**
  - **Hemispherical mound** (anda).
  - **Square railing on top** (harmika).
  - **Pole with parasols** (yashti).
  - **Encloses relics; circumambulated.**
- **Angkor Wat:**
  - **Originally Hindu** (Vishnu); later Buddhist.
  - **Symbolic of Mount Meru** (cosmic mountain).
  - **Concentric galleries; central towers.**
  - **Complex bas-reliefs** depict Hindu epics.
- **Chinese landscape (shan shui):**
  - **Atmospheric perspective** (mist, distance).
  - **Tiny human figures** dwarfed by nature.
  - **Daoist influence:** harmony with nature.
- **Hokusai's Great Wave:**
  - **From series Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji.**
  - **Mt. Fuji small, framed** by giant wave.
  - **Influenced Western art.**

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Asian art shaped European modernism:** Japanese prints (Japonisme) influenced Impressionism, Art Nouveau.
- **Why China retained continuous tradition:** thousand-year painting lineage.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify works

For each, identify culture and approximate date:
(a) Great Stupa Sanchi.
(b) Angkor Wat.
(c) The Great Wave off Kanagawa.

- (a) **Indian Buddhist, ~150 BCE-50 CE.**
- (b) **Khmer Empire (Cambodia), Hindu/later Buddhist, ~1113-1150.**
- (c) **Japanese ukiyo-e by Hokusai, ~1830-1832.**

### Example 2 [4] — Buddha iconography

Identify iconographic features of seated Buddha.
- **Ushnisha:** cranial protrusion (wisdom).
- **Urna:** forehead mark (third eye / spiritual vision).
- **Elongated earlobes** (former princely jewelry).
- **Mudra:** specific hand gesture (varies).
- **Robe:** monastic robes.
- **Lotus posture:** seated meditation.

### Example 3 [5] — Japonisme

How did Japanese art influence European Impressionism?
- **Trade with Japan reopened** (1850s+).
- **Ukiyo-e prints reached Europe;** affordable, vivid color.
- **Influenced:** Van Gogh, Monet, Degas, Whistler.
- **Compositional features adopted:** asymmetry, flat color planes, cropping, bird's-eye perspective.
- **Hokusai's Great Wave** especially influential.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Buddhism started in India,** then spread.
2. **Angkor Wat originally Hindu,** then Buddhist.
3. **Chinese landscape (shan shui) ≠ Western landscape** (different conventions, atmospheric not linear perspective).
4. **Ukiyo-e = woodblock prints** of "floating world" entertainment.
5. **Mudras have specific meanings** (don't confuse).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Buddhist art:** identify iconography (mudra, ushnisha).
**For Chinese landscape:** identify Daoist/Confucian influence.
**For Japanese prints:** identify ukiyo-e features.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The Great Stupa at Sanchi (~150 BCE-50 CE), with its hemispherical mound (anda) enclosing relics and surrounded by a railing (harmika) topped with parasols (yashti), exemplifies early Buddhist architecture; circumambulation around the stupa was central practice."
2. "Angkor Wat (~1113-1150) — originally a Hindu temple to Vishnu, later Buddhist — symbolically represents Mount Meru, the cosmic mountain, with concentric galleries and central towers reflecting the Hindu cosmos."
3. "Chinese landscape painting (shan shui — 'mountain water'), dominant since the Song dynasty, uses atmospheric perspective and tiny human figures dwarfed by nature, reflecting Daoist harmony between humans and nature."
4. "Hokusai's *Great Wave off Kanagawa* (~1830-1832), from the series *Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji*, exemplifies ukiyo-e woodblock printing; its dramatic asymmetry and flat color planes profoundly influenced Western Impressionists (Japonisme)."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master Buddhist art (stupa, Buddha iconography). Master Hindu art (Angkor Wat). Master Chinese landscape painting. Master Japanese ukiyo-e and Japonisme.*
