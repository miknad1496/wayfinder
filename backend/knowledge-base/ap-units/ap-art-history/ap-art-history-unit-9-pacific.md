# AP Art History — Unit 9: The Pacific (700-1980 CE) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~4% of the AP Art History exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Polynesia (Hawaii, Samoa, Aotearoa/NZ Maori, Easter Island); Melanesia (Papua New Guinea); Micronesia; Australian Aboriginal art.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** ~11 of 250 images. Distinctive cultural traditions; tattoo, carving, body decoration.

## Big Ideas

1. **Pacific Islander art** is functional, ceremonial, often portable.
2. **Tattoo and body decoration** integral in many Pacific cultures.
3. **Maori (Aotearoa/NZ)** carving traditions sophisticated.
4. **Easter Island moai** monumental but enigmatic statues.
5. **Aboriginal Australian art** has continuous traditions ~50,000 years.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Key works:**
  - **'Ahu 'ula (feathered cloak), Hawaii** (~late 1700s).
  - **Moai, Rapa Nui (Easter Island)** (~1100-1600 CE): monumental volcanic stone heads/torsos.
  - **Maori meeting house (Te Hau-ki-Turanga)** (1842-1843).
  - **Marae (Polynesian sacred meeting space).**
  - **Malagan masks/figures (Papua New Guinea).**
  - **Aboriginal "dreaming" paintings.**
- **Polynesian features:**
  - **Long sea voyages;** advanced navigation.
  - **Stratified societies.**
  - **Tattoo (tatau)** marks status, lineage.
  - **Sacred objects** (mana, tapu).
- **Maori:**
  - **Wood carving** with spiral patterns (koru).
  - **Greenstone (pounamu) jewelry.**
  - **Moko (facial tattoo)** signified lineage, status.
  - **Meeting houses (wharenui)** carved with ancestor figures.
- **Rapa Nui (Easter Island):**
  - **~1000+ moai** statues.
  - **Volcanic rock; quarried at Rano Raraku.**
  - **Likely ancestor representations.**
  - **Society collapsed** (~1600s).
- **Aboriginal Australian art:**
  - **Dreaming/Dreamtime stories** depicted.
  - **Aerial perspective:** view from above.
  - **Dot painting** (modern continuation of ancient practice).
  - **Songlines** = paths through landscape with ritual significance.
- **Melanesian:**
  - **Masks for ceremonies** (initiation, funerals).
  - **Bilum (string bag).**
  - **Highly varied** by island.

### Adds for [4]

- **Hawaiian feathered cloaks ('ahu 'ula):**
  - **Made of thousands** of bird feathers.
  - **Worn by chiefs** (ali'i).
  - **Symbolize divine descent;** rare/precious.
- **Moko (Maori facial tattoo):**
  - **Each design unique** to individual and lineage.
  - **Signifies social standing.**
  - **Modern revival.**
- **Easter Island collapse:**
  - **Resource depletion** (deforestation, soil erosion).
  - **Population crash** before European contact.
  - **Cautionary ecological tale** (debated).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Pacific often ignored in art history:**
  - Eurocentric biases.
  - Colonial collection often stripped objects from context.
- **Contemporary Pacific artists** continue traditions while engaging modernity.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify works

For each, identify culture:
(a) Moai.
(b) Wharenui meeting house.
(c) 'Ahu 'ula feathered cloak.

- (a) **Rapa Nui (Easter Island).**
- (b) **Maori (Aotearoa/New Zealand).**
- (c) **Hawaiian.**

### Example 2 [4] — Maori carving

Describe Maori meeting house carving traditions.
- **Carved with ancestor figures** depicted along walls and supporting beams.
- **Meeting house = embodied ancestor** (head at gable, ribs as rafters, body as interior).
- **Spiral (koru) patterns.**
- **Maintains genealogical and spiritual continuity.**

### Example 3 [5] — Aboriginal "dreaming"

What is the Dreaming in Aboriginal Australian art?
- **Spiritual/cultural concept:** the time when ancestral beings created the world.
- **Stories embedded in landscape;** songlines mark paths.
- **Painted on bodies, rock, bark, modern canvas.**
- **Aerial perspective** common.
- **Continuous tradition** ~50,000 years.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Pacific is huge and diverse** — distinguish Polynesia, Melanesia, Micronesia, Australia.
2. **Moai are not just heads;** many have torsos buried.
3. **Aboriginal art continuous** for tens of thousands of years.
4. **Tattoo (tatau) is sacred,** not just decoration.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Pacific art:** identify region (Polynesia/Melanesia/Micronesia/Australia).
**For function:** address ceremonial/social purposes.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Polynesian art — Hawaiian feathered cloaks ('ahu 'ula), Maori carved meeting houses, Rapa Nui moai — reflects highly stratified societies with sophisticated craft traditions, often functioning to mark status, lineage, and connection to ancestors."
2. "Maori meeting houses (wharenui) embody ancestors: the gable represents the head, rafters represent ribs, and the interior the ancestor's body. Carved with spiral koru patterns, they serve as living genealogical and spiritual centers."
3. "Aboriginal Australian art has the longest continuous artistic tradition in the world (~50,000 years), depicting Dreaming stories — the time when ancestral beings shaped the landscape — through aerial perspective and symbolic forms continuing in modern dot painting."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master Polynesia/Melanesia/Micronesia/Australia regional distinctions. Master moai, Maori meeting houses, Hawaiian feathered cloaks, Aboriginal Dreaming.*
