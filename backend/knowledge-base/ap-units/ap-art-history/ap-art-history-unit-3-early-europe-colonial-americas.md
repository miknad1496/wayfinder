# AP Art History — Unit 3: Early Europe and Colonial Americas (200-1750 CE) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~21% of the AP Art History exam (largest unit)
- **Sub-topics covered:** Early Christian/Byzantine; Romanesque; Gothic; Italian Renaissance; Northern Renaissance; Baroque; Rococo; colonial Americas.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** ~52 of 250 images (largest single unit). Renaissance to Baroque = exam workhorse.

## Big Ideas

1. **Christianity** shapes Western art for ~1500 years (Early Christian → Gothic → Renaissance → Baroque).
2. **Renaissance** rediscovers classical ideals; Italian (~1400-1500) then Northern (~1450-1600).
3. **Baroque** (~1600-1750): drama, emotion, theatrical lighting (Caravaggio, Bernini, Rembrandt).
4. **Rococo** (~1700s): playful, decorative, aristocratic.
5. **Colonial Americas** synthesize European, indigenous, African traditions.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Early Christian/Byzantine:**
  - **Catacomb of Priscilla** (Rome, 200-400 CE).
  - **Hagia Sophia, Constantinople** (532-537 CE): Byzantine basilica + dome; Anthemius and Isidorus.
  - **Justinian Mosaic, San Vitale, Ravenna** (~547 CE).
- **Islamic art** (covered separately in Unit 7 — Islamic art tradition).
- **Romanesque (~1000-1200):**
  - **Church of Sainte-Foy, Conques** (~1050).
  - **Bayeux Tapestry** (~1066-1080): Norman conquest narrative.
- **Gothic (~1140-1500):**
  - **Chartres Cathedral** (begun ~1145, rebuilt 1194-1220): pointed arches, flying buttresses, stained glass.
  - **Notre Dame de Paris.**
- **Italian Renaissance (~1400-1500):**
  - **Brunelleschi:** Florence Cathedral dome (1417-1436); linear perspective.
  - **Donatello:** *David* (~1440): first freestanding nude since antiquity.
  - **Leonardo da Vinci:** *Last Supper* (1495-1498); *Mona Lisa* (~1503).
  - **Michelangelo:** *David* (1501-1504); Sistine Chapel ceiling (1508-1512); *Pietà*.
  - **Raphael:** *School of Athens* (1509-1511).
- **Northern Renaissance (~1450-1600):**
  - **Jan van Eyck:** *Arnolfini Portrait* (1434): oil on panel, naturalism, symbolism.
  - **Albrecht Dürer:** *Adam and Eve* (1504): Northern + Italian synthesis.
  - **Hieronymus Bosch:** *Garden of Earthly Delights* (~1500).
- **Baroque (~1600-1750):**
  - **Caravaggio:** *Calling of Saint Matthew* (1599-1600): dramatic chiaroscuro.
  - **Bernini:** *Ecstasy of Saint Teresa* (1647-1652); *David* (1623-1624).
  - **Rembrandt:** *The Night Watch* (1642); *Self-Portrait*.
  - **Velázquez:** *Las Meninas* (1656).
  - **Vermeer:** *Woman Holding a Balance* (~1664).
  - **Versailles** (Louis XIV's palace).
- **Rococo (~1720s-1770s):**
  - **Watteau, Fragonard, Boucher:** playful, pastel, aristocratic.
  - **The Swing** (Fragonard).
- **Colonial Americas:**
  - **Cuzco school painting (Andes).**
  - **Casta paintings (Mexico):** depicted racial mixing.
  - **Virgin of Guadalupe.**
  - **Synthesized European, indigenous, African.**

### Adds for [4]

- **Linear perspective:**
  - **Brunelleschi developed** (~1410s).
  - **Vanishing point** creates illusion of depth.
  - **Renaissance hallmark.**
- **Why Renaissance "rebirth":**
  - **Rediscovered classical (Greek/Roman) ideals.**
  - **Humanism:** human-centered worldview.
  - **Scientific approach** to art (anatomy, perspective).
- **Caravaggio's chiaroscuro:**
  - **Strong contrast** between light and dark.
  - **Dramatic, theatrical.**
  - **Influenced 17th-century European painting** (Caravaggisti).
- **Rembrandt's distinct qualities:**
  - **Psychological depth.**
  - **Mastery of light.**
  - **Self-portraits** chronicled aging, self-examination.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Italian Renaissance flourished in Florence:**
  - **Wealth (banking, Medici).**
  - **Civic competition.**
  - **Classical heritage.**
- **Why Baroque drama:**
  - **Counter-Reformation:** Catholic Church commissioned emotive art to compete with Protestant simplicity.
- **Casta paintings:**
  - **Encoded colonial racial hierarchy.**
  - **Complex hybrid identity** — proto-modern racial categories.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Period attribution

For each, identify period:
(a) Hagia Sophia.
(b) Chartres Cathedral.
(c) Sistine Chapel ceiling.
(d) Las Meninas.

- (a) **Byzantine** (532-537 CE).
- (b) **Gothic** (12th-13th c.).
- (c) **Italian Renaissance** (1508-1512, Michelangelo).
- (d) **Spanish Baroque** (1656, Velázquez).

### Example 2 [4] — Renaissance vs Baroque

Compare Renaissance and Baroque styles.
- **Renaissance:** balanced, harmonious, idealized.
- **Baroque:** dramatic, emotional, theatrical lighting (chiaroscuro).
- **Renaissance figures:** calm.
- **Baroque figures:** in motion, mid-action.

### Example 3 [5] — Colonial America

What characterizes colonial Latin American religious painting (Cuzco school)?
- **Synthesis** of European Catholic iconography + indigenous Andean elements.
- **Madonna in Andean dress** (e.g., Virgin of Cocharcas).
- **Local landscapes** as backgrounds.
- **Continued indigenous artistic traditions** under colonial Catholic veneer.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Wrong attributions:** Michelangelo did Sistine ceiling AND David; Leonardo did Mona Lisa AND Last Supper.
2. **Italian vs Northern Renaissance** — Northern has detailed naturalism, oil painting precision; Italian has classical balance, fresco.
3. **Caravaggio's chiaroscuro** vs Renaissance balanced lighting.
4. **Colonial American art** is hybrid, not just "European in Americas."

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For attribution:** style, materials, subject matter clues.
**For analysis:** formal qualities + cultural context.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Italian Renaissance art (~1400-1500) integrated rediscovered classical ideals — proportion, balance, idealized human form — with new techniques: Brunelleschi's linear perspective and humanist study of anatomy. Florence's wealth and Medici patronage drove production."
2. "Baroque art (~1600-1750) embraced drama, emotion, and theatrical lighting (Caravaggio's chiaroscuro). The Counter-Reformation Catholic Church commissioned works that engaged viewer emotion — Bernini's Ecstasy of Saint Teresa, Caravaggio's Calling of Saint Matthew."
3. "Northern Renaissance painters like Jan van Eyck (Arnolfini Portrait) achieved unprecedented naturalism through oil paint's translucency, building up layers of glazes — a different sensibility from Italian fresco emphasis on idealized form."
4. "Colonial American art — Cuzco school painting in the Andes, casta paintings in Mexico — synthesized European Catholic iconography with indigenous and African elements, reflecting the cultural complexity of colonial societies."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master Italian Renaissance (Brunelleschi, da Vinci, Michelangelo, Raphael). Master Northern Renaissance (van Eyck, Dürer). Master Baroque (Caravaggio, Bernini, Rembrandt). Master colonial American synthesis. Master perspective and chiaroscuro.*
