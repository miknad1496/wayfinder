# AP Art History — Unit 4: Later Europe and Americas (1750-1980 CE) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~21% of the AP Art History exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Neoclassicism; Romanticism; Realism; Impressionism; Post-Impressionism; modern movements (Cubism, Fauvism, Expressionism, Surrealism, Abstract Expressionism, Pop, Minimalism, Photorealism).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Massive coverage. ~52 of 250 images. Major movements and key works.

## Big Ideas

1. **18th-19th century:** Neoclassicism → Romanticism → Realism → Impressionism.
2. **Late 19th-early 20th century:** Post-Impressionism → Modernism (Cubism, etc.).
3. **Early 20th century:** Expressionism, Surrealism, Dada.
4. **Mid-20th century:** Abstract Expressionism (NYC), Pop Art.
5. **Late 20th century:** Minimalism, Photorealism, Conceptual.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Neoclassicism (~1750-1820):**
  - **Jacques-Louis David:** *Oath of the Horatii* (1784); *Death of Marat* (1793).
  - **Inspired by classical Greek/Roman.**
  - **Reaction against Rococo.**
- **Romanticism (~1800-1850):**
  - **Caspar David Friedrich:** *Wanderer Above the Sea of Fog* (~1818).
  - **Théodore Géricault:** *Raft of the Medusa* (1818-1819).
  - **Eugène Delacroix:** *Liberty Leading the People* (1830).
  - **J.M.W. Turner:** sublime landscapes.
- **Realism (~1840s-1880s):**
  - **Gustave Courbet:** *The Stone Breakers* (1849).
  - **Jean-François Millet:** *The Gleaners* (1857).
  - **Honoré Daumier.**
- **Impressionism (~1870s-1890s):**
  - **Claude Monet:** *Impression, Sunrise* (1872); *Water Lilies* series.
  - **Auguste Renoir:** *Luncheon of the Boating Party* (1881).
  - **Edgar Degas:** ballerinas.
  - **Mary Cassatt:** mother and child scenes.
  - **Quick brushwork; outdoor (en plein air); light effects.**
- **Post-Impressionism (~1880s-1900s):**
  - **Vincent van Gogh:** *The Starry Night* (1889); *Sunflowers*.
  - **Paul Cézanne:** *Mont Sainte-Victoire* (multiple); foundation of Cubism.
  - **Paul Gauguin:** Tahiti paintings.
  - **Georges Seurat:** *A Sunday on La Grande Jatte* (1884-1886): pointillism.
- **Cubism (1907-1920s):**
  - **Pablo Picasso:** *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon* (1907); *Guernica* (1937).
  - **Georges Braque.**
  - **Multiple viewpoints simultaneously.**
- **Fauvism, Expressionism:**
  - **Henri Matisse:** *The Dance* (1909-1910).
  - **Edvard Munch:** *The Scream* (1893).
  - **Wassily Kandinsky:** abstract.
- **Surrealism (1920s+):**
  - **Salvador Dalí:** *The Persistence of Memory* (1931).
  - **René Magritte:** *The Treachery of Images* (1929) — "Ceci n'est pas une pipe."
  - **Frida Kahlo:** self-portraits.
- **Abstract Expressionism (1940s-1950s, NYC):**
  - **Jackson Pollock:** drip paintings (*Number 1A, 1948*).
  - **Mark Rothko:** color field.
  - **Willem de Kooning, Helen Frankenthaler.**
- **Pop Art (1950s-1960s):**
  - **Andy Warhol:** *Marilyn Diptych* (1962); *Campbell's Soup Cans*.
  - **Roy Lichtenstein:** comic-book inspired.
  - **Reaction against Abstract Expressionism;** popular culture imagery.
- **Minimalism, Conceptual, Photorealism (late 20th):**
  - **Donald Judd:** untitled stacks.
  - **Sol LeWitt** (Conceptual).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why Impressionism revolutionary:**
  - **Painted light** rather than fixed objects.
  - **Outdoor (en plein air).**
  - **Quick brushwork; visible strokes.**
  - **Modern subjects** (Parisian leisure).
- **Cubism's break:**
  - **Multiple simultaneous viewpoints.**
  - **Fragmented planes.**
  - **Departed from single-point perspective** (which had ruled since Renaissance).
- **Why Pollock significant:**
  - **All-over composition** (no central focus).
  - **Drip technique** = canvas as field of energy.
  - **Process art** = act of painting matters.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why so many movements 1850-1950:** rapid social change, photography (released painting from documentation), exposure to non-Western art.
- **Modernism's trajectory:** progressive abstraction; loss of common visual culture; eventual reaction (Pop = return to imagery).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Match artist to work

For each, identify artist:
(a) Oath of the Horatii.
(b) The Starry Night.
(c) Guernica.
(d) The Persistence of Memory.

- (a) **Jacques-Louis David.**
- (b) **Vincent van Gogh.**
- (c) **Pablo Picasso.**
- (d) **Salvador Dalí.**

### Example 2 [4] — Movement features

What characterizes Impressionism?
- **Outdoor painting** (en plein air).
- **Quick brushwork; visible strokes.**
- **Light as primary subject.**
- **Modern Parisian life.**
- **Reaction against academic style.**

### Example 3 [5] — Cubism's break

How did Cubism break from Renaissance perspective?
- **Renaissance:** single vanishing point; coherent illusionistic space.
- **Cubism:** multiple simultaneous viewpoints.
- **Fragmented planes;** subject visible from many angles at once.
- **Picasso's *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon*** (1907) inflection point.
- **Influenced by African masks** and Cézanne's planar analysis.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing Impressionism and Post-Impressionism.**
2. **David painted Oath of the Horatii AND Death of Marat** (both Neoclassical).
3. **Picasso's Guernica is response to Spanish Civil War** (1937 bombing of Basque town).
4. **Pollock's "drip" technique** isn't random; deliberate process.
5. **Pop Art reacted against** abstract expressionism's elitism.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For movement attribution:** identify hallmarks (subject, technique, period).
**For specific works:** know context, artist, date approximately.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Neoclassicism (~1750-1820), exemplified by Jacques-Louis David's *Oath of the Horatii*, drew on classical Greek and Roman ideals as a deliberate reaction against the perceived frivolity of Rococo."
2. "Impressionism revolutionized painting by treating light itself as subject — Monet's *Impression, Sunrise* lent the movement its name. Painted en plein air with quick brushwork, Impressionists captured fleeting effects of light on Parisian leisure scenes."
3. "Cubism (Picasso, Braque, ~1907+) broke decisively from Renaissance perspective by depicting subjects from multiple simultaneous viewpoints, fragmenting form into planes — laying foundation for 20th-century abstraction."
4. "Picasso's *Guernica* (1937) responded to the bombing of the Basque town of Guernica during the Spanish Civil War, using a monumental black-white-gray composition with fractured Cubist forms to convey wartime horror."
5. "Abstract Expressionism (NYC, 1940s-1950s) — Pollock's drip paintings, Rothko's color fields — emphasized the act of painting and emotional expression. Pop Art (Warhol, Lichtenstein) reacted against Abstract Expressionism's perceived elitism by embracing popular culture imagery."
