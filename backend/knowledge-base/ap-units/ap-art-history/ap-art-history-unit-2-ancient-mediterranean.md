# AP Art History — Unit 2: Ancient Mediterranean (3500 BCE-300 CE) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~15% of the AP Art History exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Ancient Near East (Mesopotamia, Persia); Egypt; Greek (Archaic, Classical, Hellenistic); Etruscan; Roman.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Largest classical content area. ~36 of 250 images. Greek vase periods, sculpture canon, Roman architecture (Pantheon, Colosseum).

## Big Ideas

1. **Mesopotamian art** = ziggurats, cuneiform-inscribed steles (Code of Hammurabi).
2. **Egyptian art** = highly conventional, geometric; for eternity (tombs, temples).
3. **Greek art** = idealized human form; progresses Archaic → Classical → Hellenistic.
4. **Roman art** = practical, naturalistic portraits; engineering (arches, domes, concrete).
5. **All shaped by religion, politics, technology** of their cultures.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Mesopotamian (key works):**
  - **White Temple and ziggurat, Uruk** (Sumerian, ~3500 BCE).
  - **Statues of votive figures, Eshnunna** (~2700 BCE): wide-eyed worshipers.
  - **Standard of Ur** (~2600 BCE): inlaid wood.
  - **Stele of Hammurabi** (~1792 BCE): Babylonian law code.
  - **Lamassu, Persepolis** (~720 BCE Assyrian): winged human-headed bull, palace guardian.
- **Egyptian (key works):**
  - **Palette of King Narmer** (~3000 BCE): unification of Egypt; iconographic conventions.
  - **Great Pyramids of Giza** (~2550 BCE): tombs of Khufu, Khafre, Menkaure.
  - **Seated scribe** (~2400 BCE): naturalistic.
  - **Temple of Amun-Re, Karnak** (~1550 BCE).
  - **Akhenaten, Nefertiti, daughters relief** (~1350 BCE): Amarna style — softer, naturalistic.
  - **Innermost coffin of Tutankhamun** (~1323 BCE).
- **Greek (key works):**
  - **Archaic (~600-480 BCE):** **Anavysos Kouros** — frontal, "archaic smile."
  - **Classical (~480-323 BCE):**
    - **Doryphoros (Spear-bearer), Polykleitos** (~440 BCE): canon of human proportion.
    - **Parthenon, Athens** (~447-432 BCE): Doric temple; Iktinos and Kallikrates architects; sculpted by Phidias.
    - **Athenian Agora.**
  - **Hellenistic (~323-31 BCE):**
    - **Winged Victory of Samothrace** (~190 BCE): dynamic, dramatic.
    - **Laocoön and his Sons** (~25 BCE-1st CE): emotion, suffering.
    - **Pergamon Altar** (~166-156 BCE).
- **Greek vase painting:**
  - **Black-figure** (Athens, 600s-500s BCE).
  - **Red-figure** (Athens, 530s+ BCE).
- **Roman (key works):**
  - **Augustus of Prima Porta** (early 1st CE): idealized portrait of emperor.
  - **Colosseum, Rome** (70-80 CE): amphitheater; arches, vaults, concrete.
  - **Pantheon, Rome** (118-125 CE): dome with oculus.
  - **Forum Romanum.**
  - **Mosaics, frescoes** (Pompeii preserved many).

### Adds for [4]

- **Egyptian conventions:**
  - **Frontal eye, profile face** for figures.
  - **Hierarchical scale** — important figures larger.
  - **Stable, eternal style** for ~3,000 years.
  - **Amarna period brief departure** (Akhenaten, ~1350 BCE).
- **Greek progression:**
  - **Archaic:** stiff, frontal, smile.
  - **Classical:** contrapposto (weight shift), idealized realism.
  - **Hellenistic:** dramatic emotion, motion, complex compositions.
- **Roman engineering innovations:**
  - **Concrete** allowed massive vaults, domes.
  - **Arches** distribute weight.
  - **Aqueducts, basilicas, baths.**
- **Roman portraiture vs Greek:**
  - **Greek:** idealized.
  - **Roman:** verism (warts and all) for older portraits; idealized for emperors.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Greek art "classical":** ideal of human beauty, balance, proportion shaped Western art for 2500 years.
- **Why Pantheon enduring:** dome (largest unreinforced concrete dome until 20th c.); oculus; perfect geometry.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify culture and period

For each, identify culture and approximate date:
(a) Stele of Hammurabi.
(b) Doryphoros.
(c) Pantheon.

- (a) **Babylonian, ~1792 BCE.**
- (b) **Greek Classical, ~440 BCE.**
- (c) **Roman, 118-125 CE.**

### Example 2 [4] — Greek progression

Trace stylistic progression: Archaic → Classical → Hellenistic.
- **Archaic:** stiff, frontal, archaic smile (Anavysos Kouros).
- **Classical:** contrapposto, idealized realism, balance (Doryphoros, Parthenon sculptures).
- **Hellenistic:** dramatic emotion, motion, complex (Winged Victory, Laocoön).

### Example 3 [5] — Roman engineering

How does the Pantheon demonstrate Roman engineering?
- **Concrete dome:** ~142 ft diameter; largest unreinforced concrete dome until 20th century.
- **Oculus** at top: only light source; structural element.
- **Coffered ceiling** lightens dome's weight.
- **Walls thicken toward base** to support load.
- **Geometry:** dome's diameter equals its height — perfect sphere fits inside.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Egyptian conventions** stable ~3,000 years; brief Amarna departure.
2. **Greek classical = ~480-323 BCE.**
3. **Confusing Pantheon (Rome) and Parthenon (Athens).**
4. **Black-figure precedes red-figure.**
5. **Roman concrete revolutionary** — enabled massive structures.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For attribution:** identify culture and period from style.
**For analysis:** identify formal elements + cultural context.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Egyptian art's conventions — frontal eye in profile face, hierarchical scale, rigid frontality — remained remarkably stable for 3,000 years, with the Amarna period under Akhenaten (~1350 BCE) a brief naturalistic departure."
2. "Greek sculpture progressed from the rigid frontality and 'archaic smile' of the Archaic period (Anavysos Kouros), to the idealized contrapposto of Classical (Polykleitos's Doryphoros), to the dramatic emotion and motion of Hellenistic (Winged Victory, Laocoön)."
3. "Roman engineering enabled architectural innovations — the Colosseum's arches and vaults, the Pantheon's unprecedented concrete dome with oculus, sprawling aqueducts — that would influence Western architecture for two millennia."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master Egyptian conventions. Master Greek Archaic/Classical/Hellenistic distinction. Master Roman engineering (Pantheon, Colosseum). Master Stele of Hammurabi, Palette of Narmer, Doryphoros, Parthenon, Pantheon as touchstones.*
