# AP Art History — Unit 7: West and Central Asia (500 BCE-1980 CE) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~4% of the AP Art History exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Persian; Islamic art (early, medieval, Ottoman); Mughal Indian; Central Asian.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** ~10 of 250 images. Islamic architecture (Dome of the Rock, Alhambra) and Mughal art common.

## Big Ideas

1. **Islamic art** generally avoids figurative representation in religious contexts (aniconism).
2. **Geometric, vegetal patterns; calligraphy** dominate.
3. **Major Islamic dynasties:** Umayyad, Abbasid, Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal.
4. **Mosques:** distinctive features (mihrab, minaret, qibla wall, courtyard).
5. **Mughal art (India)** synthesizes Islamic and Hindu traditions.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Key works:**
  - **Petra (Jordan, Nabataean,** ~400 BCE-100 CE): rock-cut architecture.
  - **Dome of the Rock, Jerusalem** (691 CE): early Umayyad Islamic monument.
  - **Great Mosque of Córdoba** (~785+ CE).
  - **Alhambra (Granada, Spain)** (~1354+).
  - **Topkapi Saray (Istanbul, Ottoman).**
  - **Süleymaniye Mosque, Istanbul** (~1550-1557): Sinan architect.
  - **Taj Mahal, Agra (Mughal)** (1632-1653): Shah Jahan for Mumtaz Mahal.
  - **Persian miniatures** (illuminated manuscripts).
- **Islamic architectural features:**
  - **Mosque elements:** mihrab (niche pointing to Mecca), qibla wall, minbar (pulpit), minaret (tower), sahn (courtyard).
  - **Domes** (often onion-shaped in Mughal).
  - **Pointed arches.**
  - **Tilework, mosaic** patterns.
- **Calligraphy:**
  - **Highest art form in Islam** (writing word of God).
  - **Quranic verses** decorate buildings.
  - **Multiple scripts** (Kufic, Naskh, Diwani).
- **Geometric and vegetal patterns:**
  - **Arabesque:** intertwined vegetal patterns.
  - **Geometric tessellations** (precursor to M.C. Escher).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why aniconism:**
  - **Quranic concern** about idolatry.
  - **Religious contexts avoid figural images.**
  - **Secular contexts** (palaces, manuscripts) often include figures.
  - **Persian/Mughal miniatures** include figures.
- **Sinan, Ottoman master architect:**
  - **Designed ~300 buildings** for Suleiman the Magnificent.
  - **Süleymaniye Mosque** rivals Hagia Sophia.
- **Taj Mahal:**
  - **Mausoleum for Mumtaz Mahal** (Shah Jahan's wife who died in childbirth).
  - **White marble inlaid with semi-precious stones.**
  - **Symmetrical Persian-style garden.**
  - **Mughal synthesis** of Islamic + Indian elements.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Islamic art often misrepresented:**
  - Western framing as "decorative" misses theological depth.
  - Calligraphy as highest art (writing the divine word).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify works

For each, identify location/period:
(a) Dome of the Rock.
(b) Alhambra.
(c) Taj Mahal.

- (a) **Jerusalem, Umayyad Islamic, 691 CE.**
- (b) **Granada, Spain, Nasrid Islamic, ~1354+.**
- (c) **Agra, India, Mughal, 1632-1653.**

### Example 2 [4] — Mosque features

Identify standard mosque features.
- **Mihrab:** niche in qibla wall pointing to Mecca.
- **Qibla wall:** wall facing Mecca.
- **Minbar:** raised pulpit for sermons.
- **Minaret:** tower for call to prayer.
- **Sahn:** open courtyard for ablutions and prayer.
- **Dome** (often).

### Example 3 [5] — Taj Mahal

Discuss the Taj Mahal as Mughal synthesis.
- **Mughal Indian Islamic** emperor's mausoleum.
- **Persian elements:** symmetrical char bagh garden, iwan archway.
- **Indian elements:** white marble inlay (pietra dura), local stone-carving.
- **Built for Mumtaz Mahal,** Shah Jahan's wife.
- **Cultural fusion** characteristic of Mughal patronage.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Aniconism applies to RELIGIOUS contexts** primarily; secular Islamic art often has figures.
2. **Dome of the Rock is shrine, not mosque.**
3. **Mughal = Indian Islamic dynasty** (~1526-1857).
4. **Taj Mahal = mausoleum,** not mosque.
5. **Sinan and Süleymaniye = Ottoman** (not Persian).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Islamic architecture:** identify mosque features.
**For attribution:** Umayyad/Abbasid/Ottoman/Safavid/Mughal periods.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Islamic art in religious contexts emphasizes calligraphy (writing the word of God), geometric patterns (arabesques and tessellations), and architectural features — mihrab, minaret, qibla wall, sahn — while generally avoiding figural representation."
2. "The Dome of the Rock (Jerusalem, 691 CE) is the earliest surviving major Islamic monument, built atop the Temple Mount; its dome and octagonal plan influenced subsequent Islamic architecture."
3. "The Taj Mahal (1632-1653), Shah Jahan's mausoleum for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, exemplifies Mughal synthesis of Persian (symmetrical char bagh garden, iwan archways) and Indian (white marble pietra dura inlay) elements."
4. "Ottoman master architect Sinan (~1490-1588) designed approximately 300 buildings for Suleiman the Magnificent; his Süleymaniye Mosque in Istanbul rivals the nearby Byzantine Hagia Sophia."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master Dome of the Rock, Alhambra, Taj Mahal as touchstones. Master mosque features. Master aniconism (and its limits). Master Mughal synthesis.*
