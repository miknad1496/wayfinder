# AP Music Theory — Unit 6: Harmony and Voice Leading II — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~13% of the AP Music Theory exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** non-chord tones (passing tones, neighbor tones, suspensions, anticipations, escape tones); 6/4 chord types; figured bass realization.

## Big Ideas

1. **Non-chord tones (NCTs)** decorate harmony.
2. **Categorize NCTs** by approach + departure direction.
3. **Suspensions:** strong dissonances on strong beats; resolve down by step.
4. **6/4 chords** have specific functional uses.
5. **Realize figured bass** by adding upper voices.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Non-chord tones (NCTs):**
  - **Passing tone (P):** stepwise approach AND departure same direction.
  - **Neighbor tone (N):** stepwise away then back to same note.
  - **Suspension (S):** held note from previous chord; dissonant on strong beat; resolves down by step.
  - **Retardation:** like suspension but resolves up.
  - **Anticipation (A):** rhythmic — note arrives early.
  - **Escape tone (échappée):** stepwise then leap opposite direction.
  - **Appoggiatura:** leap then step opposite direction.
  - **Pedal tone:** sustained or repeated note (often bass) through changing harmony.
- **Suspension types:**
  - **4-3:** common; 4 above bass resolves to 3.
  - **7-6:** 7 above bass resolves to 6.
  - **9-8:** 9 above bass resolves to 8.
  - **2-3 (bass suspension):** 2 above resolves to 3.
- **6/4 chord uses:**
  - **Cadential 6/4:** ornaments V before resolving to V.
  - **Passing 6/4:** between two root-position chords step apart.
  - **Pedal (neighbor) 6/4:** over sustained bass.
  - **Arpeggiated 6/4:** from arpeggiation.

### Adds for [4]

- **Suspension preparation, suspension, resolution:**
  - **Preparation:** consonant note tied/repeated.
  - **Suspension:** dissonant.
  - **Resolution:** by step down.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why NCTs:** create melodic interest while preserving harmonic logic.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify NCT

Soprano: C-D-E over chord C-E-G. The D is what kind of NCT?
- **Approached by step (C-D); departs by step (D-E); both up.**
- **Passing tone.**

### Example 2 [4] — Suspension

Soprano holds C from previous chord while bass moves to G (chord G-B-D-G). Then C resolves to B. What suspension?
- **C above G = 4th.**
- **C resolves down to B = 3rd.**
- **4-3 suspension.**

### Example 3 [5] — Cadential 6/4

In V → I cadence, why insert I6/4 before V?
- **I6/4 (C-E-G with G in bass) over dominant bass G.**
- **Functions as ornamented V** (the C is appoggiatura to B; E to D).
- **Heightens dominant resolution** to I.
- **Standard cadence:** I6/4 - V - I.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Misclassifying NCTs.** Identify approach AND departure intervals/directions.
2. **Suspensions resolve DOWN** by step (retardations resolve up).
3. **Cadential 6/4 has dominant function,** not tonic.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Non-chord tones decorate harmony. Passing tones move stepwise through; neighbor tones step away and back; suspensions are held from prior chord, dissonant on strong beat, resolving down by step."
2. "Suspensions are labeled by intervals above the bass at suspension and resolution: 4-3 (4th above bass resolves to 3rd), 7-6, 9-8, with 2-3 bass suspensions where the bass itself is the suspended note."
3. "Cadential 6/4 chords occur on strong beats before V, with bass as dominant scale degree; functionally, they are ornamented dominants — I6/4 - V - I is the prototype."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master NCT types (passing, neighbor, suspension, anticipation, escape, appoggiatura, pedal). Master suspension labels (4-3, 7-6, 9-8). Master 6/4 chord types.*
