# AP Music Theory — Unit 5: Harmony and Voice Leading I — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~14% of the AP Music Theory exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** four-part voice leading basics; SATB ranges; common voice-leading rules; introduction to part-writing.

## Big Ideas

1. **SATB voicing:** Soprano, Alto, Tenor, Bass.
2. **Smooth voice leading:** small intervals; common tones held.
3. **No parallel 5ths or octaves** between voices.
4. **Resolve tendency tones** properly (leading tone up; chordal 7th down).
5. **Spacing:** no more than octave between adjacent upper voices; bass can be wider.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **SATB ranges (typical):**
  - **Soprano:** middle C to G above high C.
  - **Alto:** G below middle C to D above middle C.
  - **Tenor:** C below middle C to G above middle C.
  - **Bass:** E below low C to middle C.
- **Voice leading principles:**
  - **Smallest intervals possible** between same voice in successive chords.
  - **Common tones held** in same voice when possible.
  - **No parallel perfect 5ths** between voices.
  - **No parallel perfect octaves** between voices.
  - **Avoid crossing voices** (alto going above soprano).
  - **Resolve leading tone up** to tonic.
  - **Resolve chordal 7th down** by step.
- **Doubling:**
  - **Major and minor triads:** double root usually.
  - **First inversion:** double soprano typically.
  - **Don't double leading tone or chordal 7th.**
- **Spacing:**
  - **Between SAT:** within an octave each.
  - **Between bass and tenor:** can be wider (more than octave OK).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why no parallel 5ths/octaves:** weaken independence of voices; sound "thin."
- **Hidden 5ths/8ves:** when both outer voices move in similar motion to perfect interval. Usually avoided.

### Adds for [5]

- **Voice leading is craft;** classical conventions stylistic.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Smooth motion

Voice-lead I to V in C major.
- **I = C (C-E-G); V = G (G-B-D).**
- **Common tone:** G (held).
- **Other voices:** C → B (step down); E → D (step down).
- **Bass:** C → G (5th down or 4th up).

### Example 2 [4] — Avoid parallels

C → G in two voices (soprano: G→D; alto: C→G).
- **Soprano G to D = perfect 5th down.**
- **Alto C to G = perfect 5th up.**
- **Wait:** the INTERVAL between soprano and alto changed from 5th (G-C) to 5th (D-G) — **parallel 5ths!** AVOID.

### Example 3 [5] — Resolve leading tone

In C major, V chord is G-B-D. The leading tone is B. Where should B resolve?
- **Leading tone B resolves up** to C (tonic) by half-step.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Parallel 5ths/octaves** — easy to write inadvertently.
2. **Failing to resolve leading tone up.**
3. **Doubling leading tone** — bad.
4. **Voice crossing.**

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Common-practice voice leading prefers smooth motion: smallest intervals possible in each voice, common tones held when feasible, leading tone resolved up to tonic, chordal 7th resolved down by step."
2. "Parallel perfect 5ths and parallel perfect octaves between any two voices are avoided in common-practice voice leading because they weaken voice independence."
3. "Standard SATB doublings: double the root in major or minor triads in root position; double the soprano in first inversion; never double the leading tone or chordal 7th."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master SATB ranges. Master voice-leading rules (no parallel 5ths/octaves, resolve leading tone, smooth motion). Master doubling.*
