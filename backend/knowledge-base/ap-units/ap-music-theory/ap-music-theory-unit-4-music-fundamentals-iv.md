# AP Music Theory — Unit 4: Music Fundamentals IV — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~14% of the AP Music Theory exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** modes (Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, Locrian); pentatonic and blues scales; chromatic scale; introduction to non-diatonic.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Identifying modes; modal characteristics in melodies.

## Big Ideas

1. **Modes** = scales beyond major/minor; rooted in church music.
2. **Seven modes** based on starting note of major scale.
3. **Pentatonic** (5-note) common in folk music globally.
4. **Blues scale** uses "blue notes" (flatted 3rd, 5th, 7th).
5. **Modes shape melodic character.**

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Seven modes (start on each white key from C major scale):**
  - **Ionian (C-C):** identical to major scale.
  - **Dorian (D-D):** minor with raised 6th.
  - **Phrygian (E-E):** minor with lowered 2nd.
  - **Lydian (F-F):** major with raised 4th.
  - **Mixolydian (G-G):** major with lowered 7th.
  - **Aeolian (A-A):** identical to natural minor.
  - **Locrian (B-B):** rare; minor with lowered 2nd and 5th.
- **Pentatonic scales:**
  - **Major pentatonic:** 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 (C-D-E-G-A).
  - **Minor pentatonic:** 1, ♭3, 4, 5, ♭7 (C-E♭-F-G-B♭).
  - **Common in folk, Asian, blues music.**
- **Blues scale:** minor pentatonic + ♭5 (blue note).
  - **C blues:** C-E♭-F-G♭-G-B♭.
- **Chromatic scale:** all 12 half steps.
- **Whole-tone scale:** all whole steps (6 notes).

### Adds for [4]

- **Modal character:**
  - **Dorian (raised 6th):** "minor but happier" (Scarborough Fair).
  - **Phrygian (lowered 2nd):** Spanish/exotic (Flamenco).
  - **Lydian (raised 4th):** "ethereal," dreamy.
  - **Mixolydian (lowered 7th):** Celtic, rock (Norwegian Wood).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why modes matter:** alternatives to major-minor system; jazz, rock, folk extensively use.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify mode

Scale: D-E-F-G-A-B-C-D. Mode?
- **Starts on D, uses C major scale notes.**
- **Dorian** mode.

### Example 2 [4] — Build mode

Build E Phrygian.
- **Phrygian: minor with lowered 2nd.**
- **From E:** E-F-G-A-B-C-D-E (white keys from E).
- **All half steps E-F (lowered 2nd already in white key sequence).**

### Example 3 [5] — Modal character

Why does Phrygian sound "Spanish"?
- **Lowered 2nd degree** creates Andalusian cadence.
- **Common in Flamenco** music.
- **F-major chord over E pedal** characteristic.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Memorize mode order:** I-D-P-L-M-A-L (Ionian-Dorian-Phrygian-Lydian-Mixolydian-Aeolian-Locrian).
2. **Pentatonic = 5 notes,** not "missing" notes.
3. **Blues scale** has 6 notes (minor pentatonic + ♭5).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The seven church modes — Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, Locrian — are scales built on each note of the major scale, each with characteristic intervallic patterns."
2. "Dorian mode (minor with raised 6th) gives 'Scarborough Fair' its bittersweet quality. Mixolydian mode (major with lowered 7th) characterizes much rock music ('Norwegian Wood')."
3. "Pentatonic scales contain five notes per octave: major pentatonic (1, 2, 3, 5, 6) and minor pentatonic (1, ♭3, 4, 5, ♭7) appear in folk traditions globally and ground much blues, rock, and pop melody."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master 7 modes. Master pentatonic and blues scales. Master modal character associations.*
