## Big Ideas

1. **Triads:** stacked thirds (root, 3rd, 5th).
2. **Four triad qualities:** major, minor, augmented, diminished.
3. **Seventh chords:** add 7th above triad.
4. **Inversions:** rearrange chord tones; figured bass notation.
5. **Roman numerals** identify chord function in key.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Triad:** three notes stacked in thirds.
  - **Root:** lowest in root position.
  - **3rd:** above root.
  - **5th:** above 3rd.
- **Triad qualities:**
  - **Major:** M3 + m3 (e.g., C-E-G).
  - **Minor:** m3 + M3 (C-E♭-G).
  - **Augmented:** M3 + M3 (C-E-G#).
  - **Diminished:** m3 + m3 (C-E♭-G♭).
- **Seventh chords:** add 7th above triad.
  - **Major 7th (M7):** major triad + M7 (CM7: C-E-G-B).
  - **Dominant 7th (V7):** major triad + m7 (G7: G-B-D-F).
  - **Minor 7th (m7):** minor triad + m7 (Cm7: C-E♭-G-B♭).
  - **Half-diminished (ø7):** dim triad + m7.
  - **Fully diminished (°7):** dim triad + dim7.
- **Inversions and figured bass:**
  - **Root position:** root in bass.
  - **First inversion (6):** 3rd in bass.
  - **Second inversion (6/4):** 5th in bass.
  - **Third inversion (4/2 or 2):** 7th in bass (only seventh chords).
  - **Figured bass numbers** indicate intervals above bass.
- **Roman numeral analysis:**
  - **Capital roman = major chord** (I, IV, V).
  - **Lowercase = minor chord** (ii, iii, vi).
  - **Lowercase + ° = diminished** (vii°).
  - **Capital + augmented sign** for augmented.
  - **In major key:** I (M), ii (m), iii (m), IV (M), V (M), vi (m), vii° (dim).
  - **In minor key:** i (m), ii° (dim), III (M), iv (m), v (m) or V (M, with raised leading tone), VI (M), VII (M) or vii° (dim).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why V is dominant:** strong tendency to resolve to I.
- **Leading tone (7th degree):** wants to resolve up to tonic.
- **Tendency tones:** 7th up to 1st; 4th down to 3rd.

### Adds for [5]

- **Function vocabulary:**
  - **Tonic (I, vi, iii):** rest.
  - **Subdominant (IV, ii):** away.
  - **Dominant (V, vii°):** tension; resolves to tonic.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify triad

C-E♭-G: quality?
- **C-E♭ = m3; E♭-G = M3.**
- **Minor triad.**

### Example 2 [3] — Roman numeral

In C major, what is the F major triad?
- **F is 4th degree of C major.**
- **F major** is **IV** (major chord on 4th).

### Example 3 [4] — Inversion

A C major triad with E in bass?
- **E is the 3rd of C major.**
- **First inversion** — figured bass: **6**.

### Example 4 [4] — Seventh chord

Build dominant 7th on G.
- **G major triad: G-B-D.**
- **Add minor 7th above G: F.**
- **G7: G-B-D-F.**

### Example 5 [5] — Function

In C major, identify chord functions:
(a) C major.
(b) F major.
(c) G major.

- (a) **I — tonic** (rest).
- (b) **IV — subdominant** (away).
- (c) **V — dominant** (tension; resolves to I).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing major and minor triads.** Major: M3+m3. Minor: m3+M3.
2. **Seventh chord types:** know M7, V7, m7, ø7, °7.
3. **Inversion figured bass:** 6 (first), 6/4 (second), 4/2 (third for V7).
4. **Roman numeral case** matters: I major, i minor.
5. **Vii° (diminished) in major key.**

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Triads stack thirds: major (M3 + m3), minor (m3 + M3), augmented (M3 + M3), diminished (m3 + m3). The triad's root, third, and fifth define its quality."
2. "Roman numeral analysis identifies chords by scale degree and quality: capital for major (I, IV, V), lowercase for minor (ii, iii, vi), lowercase with ° for diminished (vii°)."
3. "Functional harmony groups chords: tonic (I, vi), subdominant (IV, ii), dominant (V, vii°). Dominant has strong pull to tonic; the leading tone (7th degree) drives resolution."
