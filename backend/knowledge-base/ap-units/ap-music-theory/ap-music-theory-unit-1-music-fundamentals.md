## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~16% of the AP Music Theory exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** pitch, intervals, scales, key signatures, time signatures, rhythm.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Foundation. Read on every exam section.

## Big Ideas

1. **Pitch** notated on staff with clefs (treble, bass, alto, tenor).
2. **Intervals** = distance between two pitches (quality + quantity).
3. **Scales:** major, minor (natural, harmonic, melodic).
4. **Key signatures** identify scale; circle of fifths.
5. **Time signatures** organize beats; rhythm subdivides beats.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Staff and clefs:**
  - **Treble clef (G clef):** middle C below staff.
  - **Bass clef (F clef):** middle C above staff.
  - **Alto, tenor (C clefs).**
- **Pitch names:** A through G; sharps (#), flats (♭), naturals (♮).
- **Intervals:**
  - **Quantity:** generic count (2nd, 3rd, etc.).
  - **Quality:** major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished.
  - **Perfect intervals:** P1 (unison), P4, P5, P8 (octave).
  - **Major/minor intervals:** 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th.
- **Scales:**
  - **Major:** W-W-H-W-W-W-H (whole/half steps).
  - **Natural minor:** W-H-W-W-H-W-W.
  - **Harmonic minor:** raised 7th (W-H-W-W-H-A2-H).
  - **Melodic minor:** raised 6th and 7th ascending; natural minor descending.
- **Key signatures:**
  - **Circle of fifths:** Sharp keys: G, D, A, E, B, F#, C#. Flat keys: F, B♭, E♭, A♭, D♭, G♭, C♭.
  - **Order of sharps:** F# C# G# D# A# E# B# (Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle).
  - **Order of flats:** B♭ E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ C♭ F♭ (reverse).
  - **Identify major key** from sharps: last sharp is leading tone (raise to find tonic).
  - **Identify major key** from flats: second-to-last flat is tonic.
- **Relative minor:** 6th degree of major (or major's tonic + 3 semitones down).
- **Time signatures:**
  - **Top number:** beats per measure.
  - **Bottom number:** note value of beat (4 = quarter, 8 = eighth, 2 = half).
  - **Simple meter:** beats divide into 2 (2/4, 3/4, 4/4).
  - **Compound meter:** beats divide into 3 (6/8, 9/8, 12/8).
- **Rhythm:**
  - **Whole, half, quarter, eighth, sixteenth notes.**
  - **Dotted notes** add half value.
  - **Rests** equivalent silence durations.

### Adds for [4]

- **Enharmonic equivalents:** F#=G♭, C#=D♭, etc.
- **Compound interval > octave** (9th, 10th, etc.).
- **Inverting intervals:** sum to 9 (3rd inverts to 6th); quality flips except perfect (P4 ↔ P5).
- **Augmented and diminished intervals:** alter from perfect/major/minor by chromatic semitone.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why circle of fifths organizes keys:** each key adds one sharp going up by P5; subtracts going down (adds flats).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify interval

C to E?
- **Quantity:** 3rd (C-D-E = 3 letter names).
- **Quality:** Major (4 semitones).
- **Major 3rd (M3).**

### Example 2 [3] — Key signature

What major key has 3 sharps?
- **3 sharps = F#, C#, G#.**
- **Last sharp G#; raise to A.**
- **Key of A major.**

### Example 3 [4] — Scale

Write A natural minor.
- **A B C D E F G A** (no sharps/flats).
- **Pattern W-H-W-W-H-W-W: A-B (W), B-C (H), C-D (W), D-E (W), E-F (H), F-G (W), G-A (W).** ✓

### Example 4 [4] — Time signature

In 6/8, how many beats per measure and what kind?
- **Top 6:** 6 eighth notes per measure.
- **Compound duple:** 2 beats, each beat = dotted quarter (3 eighth notes).

### Example 5 [5] — Relative minor

Relative minor of D major?
- **D major has 2 sharps.**
- **6th degree of D major: B.**
- **B minor** is relative (also 2 sharps).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing interval quantity and quality.** Quantity is # of letter names; quality is exact distance.
2. **Wrong key signature identification.** Memorize circle of fifths.
3. **Compound vs simple meter.** 6/8 is compound (beats subdivide in 3).
4. **Relative vs parallel minor.** Relative shares key signature; parallel shares tonic.
5. **Forgetting accidentals in harmonic/melodic minor.**

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For interval ID:** quantity by letter count; quality by half-step count.
**For key signature:** circle of fifths memorized.
**For time signature:** simple/compound; duple/triple.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "An interval has both quantity (the generic count by letter names — 2nd, 3rd, etc.) and quality (major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished). Perfect intervals are unison, 4th, 5th, octave; major/minor intervals are 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th."
2. "Major scale follows W-W-H-W-W-W-H pattern; natural minor follows W-H-W-W-H-W-W. Harmonic minor raises the 7th degree; melodic minor raises 6th and 7th ascending and reverts to natural descending."
3. "The circle of fifths organizes keys by P5 intervals: G major has 1 sharp, D has 2, A has 3, etc. The last sharp is the leading tone (raise by half-step to find tonic). The second-to-last flat names the major tonic."
4. "Simple meter divides each beat into two (2/4, 3/4, 4/4); compound meter divides into three (6/8 = compound duple, 9/8 = compound triple, 12/8 = compound quadruple)."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master pitch reading. Master intervals (quantity + quality). Master major/minor scales. Master circle of fifths. Master simple vs compound meter.*
