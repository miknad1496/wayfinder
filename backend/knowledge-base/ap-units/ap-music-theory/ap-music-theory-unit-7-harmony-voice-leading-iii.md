# AP Music Theory — Unit 7: Harmony and Voice Leading III — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~13% of the AP Music Theory exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** seventh chords in part-writing; secondary dominants; cadence types in detail; modulation introduction.

## Big Ideas

1. **Seventh chords** add complexity; chordal 7th resolves down.
2. **Secondary dominants** tonicize non-tonic chords (V/V, V/IV, etc.).
3. **Modulation** = change of key.
4. **Common modulation methods:** common chord, direct.
5. **Pivot chords** ease modulation.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Seventh chord voice leading:**
  - **Chordal 7th resolves DOWN** by step.
  - **Often resolves to chord whose 3rd it becomes** (e.g., V7's F resolves to I's E in C major).
- **Secondary dominants:**
  - **V/V (five of five):** dominant of the dominant.
    - In C major: V/V = D major (D-F#-A), the dominant of G.
  - **V/IV, V/vi, V/ii** common.
  - **Notation:** V7/V means V7 of V.
- **Tonicization** vs modulation:
  - **Tonicization:** brief emphasis of non-tonic chord (one or two chords).
  - **Modulation:** sustained change of key.
- **Modulation types:**
  - **Common chord (pivot):** chord serves both keys, smoothly transitions.
  - **Direct (phrase):** ends one key, begins next without preparation.
  - **Common-tone:** single tone bridges keys.
- **Closely related keys:**
  - **Tonic + dominant + subdominant + their relative minors/majors.**
  - **Easiest modulations** to closely related keys.

### Adds for [4]

- **Common pivot chords** include vi (in major to its relative).
- **Modulation typically by P5** (to dominant) or to relative minor/major.

### Adds for [5]

- **Tonicization vs modulation distinction can blur.**

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — V/V

What is V/V in C major?
- **V of C is G.**
- **V of G is D major (D-F#-A).**
- **D major** with raised F (F#).

### Example 2 [4] — Pivot chord modulation

Modulate from C major to G major. Possible pivot?
- **C major's V (G) = G major's I.**
- **C major's vi (Am) = G major's ii (Am).**
- **Use** Am as pivot: function in C major as vi, in G major as ii.

### Example 3 [5] — Chordal 7th resolution

V7 in C is G7 (G-B-D-F). The 7th is F. Where does it resolve to I?
- **F → E (down by step).**
- **E is the 3rd of I (C major).**
- **Standard resolution.**

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Chordal 7th MUST resolve down** by step.
2. **Secondary dominant requires raised tone** (e.g., V/V in C needs F#).
3. **Modulation involves sustained new key,** not brief tonicization.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Secondary dominants tonicize non-tonic chords by approaching them with their own dominant. V7/V in C major (D7) creates a temporary dominant pull toward V (G major)."
2. "Modulation can occur via common chord (pivot — chord shared by both keys), direct (phrase ends in old key, begins in new), or common-tone (single shared note bridges keys)."
3. "Closely related keys — tonic, dominant, subdominant, and their relative minors/majors — provide the smoothest modulations because they share many common chords and notes."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master secondary dominants (V/V, V/IV, V/vi). Master modulation types (pivot, direct). Master closely related keys. Master chordal 7th resolution.*
