## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~14% of the AP Music Theory exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** texture (monophony, homophony, polyphony); melody (motive, phrase, period); cadences (authentic, plagal, half, deceptive); melodic dictation skills.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Identifying texture from listening; cadence ID; melodic structure analysis.

## Big Ideas

1. **Texture:** monophonic (one line), homophonic (melody + accompaniment), polyphonic (multiple independent lines).
2. **Phrase:** musical sentence, often 4-8 measures.
3. **Period:** two phrases (antecedent + consequent).
4. **Cadences** end phrases: authentic (V-I), plagal (IV-I), half (any-V), deceptive (V-vi).
5. **Motive:** smallest meaningful musical unit; building block.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Texture:**
  - **Monophonic:** single melodic line (Gregorian chant).
  - **Homophonic:** melody + accompaniment (most pop, hymns).
  - **Polyphonic:** multiple independent melodic lines (Bach fugue).
  - **Heterophonic:** simultaneous variations of same melody.
- **Melody:**
  - **Motive:** small musical unit (e.g., Beethoven's 5th opening).
  - **Phrase:** musical sentence; usually 4-8 measures; ends with cadence.
  - **Period:** two phrases (antecedent ends weakly, consequent ends strongly).
- **Cadences:**
  - **Authentic (V-I):**
    - **Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC):** V-I, both root position, soprano on tonic. Strongest.
    - **Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC):** V-I but inversion or non-tonic soprano.
  - **Plagal (IV-I):** "Amen" cadence; gentler.
  - **Half Cadence (HC):** ends on V; sounds incomplete.
  - **Deceptive Cadence (DC):** V-vi; resolves unexpectedly.
- **Phrase structure:**
  - **Parallel period:** consequent begins like antecedent.
  - **Contrasting period:** consequent begins differently.

### Adds for [4]

- **Melodic dictation strategies:**
  - **Listen for cadences first** (anchors).
  - **Identify scale degrees** by interval from tonic.
  - **Sing or hum** as you write.
- **Common melodic shapes:**
  - **Arch:** rises then falls.
  - **Ascending/descending lines.**
- **Sequence:** repeats motive at different pitch.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why texture matters:** distinguishes styles (Renaissance polyphony vs Classical homophony).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Texture identification

For each, identify texture:
(a) Gregorian chant.
(b) Pop song with vocal + guitar chords.
(c) Bach fugue.

- (a) **Monophonic.**
- (b) **Homophonic.**
- (c) **Polyphonic.**

### Example 2 [4] — Cadence ID

A passage ends V-I in C major: G major chord (root position) to C major chord (root position), soprano ends on C. Identify cadence.
- **Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC).**

### Example 3 [5] — Period structure

Antecedent ends on V (half cadence); consequent ends on I (PAC). Both phrases begin similarly. What is this?
- **Parallel period:**
  - Two phrases.
  - Similar beginnings (parallel).
  - Antecedent: weak ending (HC).
  - Consequent: strong ending (PAC).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **PAC requires** root position V-I AND soprano on tonic.
2. **Half cadence ends on V,** not at V.
3. **Confusing parallel and contrasting periods** by phrase beginnings.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Texture: monophonic (single melodic line), homophonic (melody with accompaniment), polyphonic (multiple independent lines), heterophonic (simultaneous variations of one melody)."
2. "Cadences mark phrase endings: Perfect Authentic (V-I, root position, soprano on tonic — strongest), Imperfect Authentic (V-I but inversion or non-tonic soprano), Plagal (IV-I, 'Amen'), Half (ends on V — incomplete), Deceptive (V-vi)."
3. "A period consists of two phrases — antecedent (ends weakly, often HC) and consequent (ends strongly, often PAC). Parallel periods have similar phrase beginnings; contrasting periods differ."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master textures (monophonic/homophonic/polyphonic). Master cadences (PAC, IAC, plagal, half, deceptive). Master phrase and period structure.*
