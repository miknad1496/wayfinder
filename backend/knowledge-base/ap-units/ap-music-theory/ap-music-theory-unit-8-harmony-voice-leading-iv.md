# AP Music Theory — Unit 8: Harmony and Voice Leading IV — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~13% of the AP Music Theory exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** modal mixture (borrowed chords); Neapolitan 6 (♭II6); augmented 6th chords (Italian, French, German); other chromatic harmony.

## Big Ideas

1. **Modal mixture:** borrow chords from parallel minor (or major).
2. **Neapolitan 6 (N6 or ♭II6):** flatted 2nd in first inversion; pre-dominant function.
3. **Augmented 6th chords:** chromatic pre-dominants resolving to V.
4. **Italian, French, German aug 6** differ by additional notes.
5. **Chromatic harmony** expands tonal palette.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Modal mixture (borrowed chords):**
  - **Major key borrowing from minor:** common.
  - **Examples in C major:** iv (Fm), ♭VI (A♭), ♭VII (B♭), i (Cm).
  - **Adds melancholy or color.**
- **Neapolitan 6 (N6 = ♭II6):**
  - **♭II in first inversion** (so ♭scale 4 in bass).
  - **In C major or minor:** N6 = D♭/F.
  - **Pre-dominant function** — resolves to V (or V7).
- **Augmented 6th chords:**
  - **Pre-dominant chromatic chords;** resolve to V.
  - **Three types:**
    - **Italian (It+6):** ♭6, 1, #4. Three notes; doubled tonic.
    - **French (Fr+6):** ♭6, 1, 2, #4. Four notes.
    - **German (Ger+6):** ♭6, 1, ♭3, #4. Four notes; sounds like dominant 7th but resolves differently.
  - **Augmented 6th between ♭6 and #4** is the chord's identifier.
- **Other chromatic chords:**
  - **Chromatic mediants** (chords related by 3rd with chromatic alteration).
  - **Common tone diminished chords.**

### Adds for [4]

- **Why augmented 6th resolves to V:** ♭6 → 5 (down half-step); #4 → 5 (up half-step).
- **German +6** can be respelled as dominant 7th (enharmonic).

### Adds for [5]

- **Modal mixture used by:** Schubert, Brahms, others for emotional color.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify modal mixture

In C major, use of A♭ major chord. What is it?
- **A♭ = ♭VI** (borrowed from C minor).
- **Modal mixture.**

### Example 2 [4] — Build N6 in C major

What is N6 in C major?
- **♭II = D♭ major (D♭-F-A♭).**
- **First inversion: F-A♭-D♭.**
- **N6 in C major.**

### Example 3 [5] — Augmented 6th

Build It+6 in C major.
- **♭6 (A♭), 1 (C), #4 (F#).**
- **Voicing typically:** A♭ in bass, C and F# above (with C doubled).
- **Resolves to V (G):** A♭ → G; F# → G; C stays or moves to B.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **N6 in first inversion** specifically (♭scale 4 in bass).
2. **Italian +6 has THREE notes**; French and German have four.
3. **All aug 6 chords resolve to V.**

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Modal mixture borrows chords from the parallel mode (e.g., C major borrowing iv = Fm or ♭VI = A♭ from C minor) to add color and emotional depth."
2. "The Neapolitan 6 (N6 = ♭II6) is a pre-dominant chord — flatted second in first inversion (so ♭scale 4 in bass) — that resolves to V (often via V7)."
3. "Augmented 6th chords are chromatic pre-dominants resolving to V via voice leading: ♭6 down to 5 and #4 up to 5. The Italian +6 has three notes (♭6, 1, #4); French adds 2; German adds ♭3 and resembles a dominant 7th enharmonically."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master modal mixture (borrowed chords). Master N6 (♭II6). Master It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6. Master pre-dominant function and resolution to V.*
