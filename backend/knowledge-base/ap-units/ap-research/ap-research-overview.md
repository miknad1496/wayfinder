# AP Research — Overview — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this exam.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Capstone exam:** part of AP Capstone Diploma (with AP Seminar).
- **Format:** Year-long independent research project culminating in 4,000-5,000 word academic paper + presentation/oral defense.
- **Prerequisite:** AP Seminar (recommended).
- **NOT** a content exam; tests independent research skills.

## Big Ideas

1. **AP Research = independent academic research project** over a year.
2. **Choose research question, methodology, conduct study, write paper, present.**
3. **Original research** — collect data or analyze existing in new way.
4. **Academic paper** (4,000-5,000 words) modeled on undergraduate thesis.
5. **Oral defense** assesses depth of understanding.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Components:**
  - **Academic paper:** 4,000-5,000 words, ~75% of score.
  - **Presentation and oral defense:** ~25% of score.
- **Research process:**
  - **Identify research question** — narrow, specific, answerable.
  - **Literature review** of existing research.
  - **Choose methodology** appropriate to question.
  - **Conduct research** (interviews, surveys, experiments, content analysis, etc.).
  - **Analyze data.**
  - **Write paper** with standard academic structure.
  - **Present and defend.**
- **Standard paper structure:**
  - **Introduction:** research question, significance.
  - **Literature review:** what's known; gaps your research addresses.
  - **Methodology:** how you researched.
  - **Results/Findings:** what you found.
  - **Discussion:** what it means; limitations.
  - **Conclusion:** synthesis; implications.
- **Methodologies:**
  - **Qualitative:** interviews, focus groups, ethnography, content analysis.
  - **Quantitative:** surveys, experiments, statistical analysis.
  - **Mixed methods:** combination.
- **Inquiry Proposal Form (IPF):** initial proposal.
- **Process and Reflection Portfolio (PREP):** documentation throughout.

### Adds for [4]

- **Choosing research question:**
  - **Original** (not just rehashing).
  - **Narrow enough** to address in scope.
  - **Important enough** to be worth pursuing.
  - **Feasible** with available time/resources.
- **IRB considerations:** if working with human subjects, ethical review.
- **Citation styles:** MLA, APA, Chicago — choose appropriate to discipline.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why AP Research valuable:**
  - **Genuine research experience** before college.
  - **Develops independent learning** skills.
  - **Strong addition to college applications.**

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Research components

What are the major components of AP Research?
- **Year-long independent research project.**
- **4,000-5,000 word academic paper** (~75%).
- **Presentation and oral defense** (~25%).

### Example 2 [4] — Choose research question

What makes a strong research question?
- **Specific and narrow** (not "how does technology affect society?").
- **Original** (extends existing research; addresses gap).
- **Answerable** with available methods.
- **Important** (so what?).
- **Example:** "How does TikTok use among Gen Z affect their attention span during academic study, compared to non-users?"

### Example 3 [5] — Methodology selection

When to use qualitative vs quantitative methods?
- **Qualitative:** when exploring complex experiences, meanings, why-questions; interviews, focus groups, ethnography.
- **Quantitative:** when measuring frequencies, correlations, comparisons; surveys with statistical analysis.
- **Mixed:** when both depth and measurement needed.
- **Choose based on question** (not preference).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Research question too broad** — narrow it.
2. **Skipping literature review** — must show you know existing scholarship.
3. **Methodology mismatch** with question.
4. **Citation lapses** — credit all sources properly.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For paper:** follow standard academic structure rigorously.
**For presentation:** prepare for tough questions in defense.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "AP Research culminates in an original year-long inquiry: a 4,000-5,000 word academic paper plus presentation and oral defense, modeled on undergraduate thesis work."
2. "A strong research question is specific, original, answerable, and important — extending existing research rather than rehashing settled questions, and feasible within available time and methods."
3. "Methodology must match question: qualitative methods (interviews, ethnography) for exploring meanings and experiences; quantitative methods (surveys, experiments) for measuring patterns and testing hypotheses; mixed methods for both."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Exam

*Master research process (question → lit review → method → data → paper → defense). Choose narrow original question. Match methodology to question. Practice oral defense.*
