## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 8–13% of the AP Physics 1 exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** rotational kinetic energy; angular momentum; conservation of angular momentum; rolling without slipping; combined translational and rotational motion.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Builds on Unit 5. Conservation of angular momentum is essential. Rolling motion combines translation and rotation.

## Big Ideas

1. **Rotational kinetic energy: KE_rot = (1/2)Iω².** Parallel to translational KE = (1/2)mv².
2. **Angular momentum: L = Iω.** Parallel to linear momentum p = mv.
3. **Conservation of angular momentum:** in the absence of external torques, L is conserved.
4. **Rolling without slipping: v_cm = rω.** Both translational and rotational kinetic energy.
5. **Total KE for rolling object: KE = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)Iω².**

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Rotational kinetic energy (KE_rot):** energy of rotation.
  - **KE_rot = (1/2)Iω².**
  - Parallel to translational KE = (1/2)mv².
- **Angular momentum (L):**
  - **L = Iω** (rotational analog of linear momentum p = mv).
  - Vector quantity.
  - Units: kg·m²/s.
- **Conservation of angular momentum:**
  - In an isolated system (no external torques), L is conserved.
  - L_before = L_after.
  - Often: I_i·ω_i = I_f·ω_f.
- **Rolling motion:**
  - **Rolling without slipping:** v_cm = rω (no slipping at contact point).
  - **Pure rolling:** combination of translation (center of mass) and rotation about center of mass.
- **Total kinetic energy of rolling object:**
  - **KE = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)Iω²** (translational + rotational).

### Adds for [4]

- **Conservation of angular momentum classic example:**
  - **Ice skater spins:** pulls arms in (reduces I), so ω increases (L = Iω constant).
  - **Spinning planet:** if it loses mass to space (reduces I), it spins faster.
  - **Helicopter blade:** spinning provides angular momentum; tail rotor needed to counteract.
- **Rolling motion details:**
  - For rolling without slipping, the contact point with ground is INSTANTANEOUSLY at rest.
  - This means kinetic friction does NO work on rolling object.
  - Energy method: KE_initial = KE_final (if conservative forces).
- **Rolling down incline:**
  - Object loses PE, gains KE.
  - **PE_lost = KE_gained = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)Iω².**
  - With v = rω: PE_lost = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)I·(v/r)² = (1/2)mv²(1 + I/(mr²)).
  - Different shapes (different I) reach different speeds.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why conservation of angular momentum matters.**
  - In closed systems with no external torques, L is conserved.
  - Used in spinning sports figures, planetary motion, helicopter design.
- **Why rolling without slipping has v_cm = rω.**
  - At the contact point, velocity = v_cm - rω = 0 (otherwise slipping).
  - Hence v_cm = rω for rolling without slipping.
- **Why hollow vs solid affects rolling.**
  - Hollow has higher I/mr² ratio.
  - For same starting height, solid object reaches higher v at bottom.
  - Less of the energy goes into rotation; more into translation.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Rotational KE

A wheel (I = 2 kg·m²) spins at 5 rad/s. Find rotational KE.
- KE_rot = (1/2)(2)(25) = 25 J.

### Example 2 [3] — Conservation of angular momentum

A figure skater with arms out has I = 4 kg·m² and spins at 2 rad/s. She pulls arms in (I = 1 kg·m²). Find new angular velocity.
- L conserved: I_i·ω_i = I_f·ω_f.
- 4·2 = 1·ω_f → ω_f = 8 rad/s.

### Example 3 [4] — Rolling motion

A solid sphere (I = (2/5)mr²) rolls down a ramp. Initial height = 2 m. Find speed at bottom.
- PE_lost = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)Iω².
- mgh = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)·(2/5)mr²·(v/r)² = (1/2)mv²·(1 + 2/5) = (1/2)·(7/5)mv².
- gh = (7/10)v² → v² = 10gh/7 = 10·9.8·2/7 = 28 → v ≈ 5.29 m/s.

### Example 4 [4] — Comparing rolling objects

A solid sphere and a hollow sphere of equal mass and radius roll down the same ramp from the same height. Which reaches the bottom first?

- Solid sphere: I = (2/5)mr².
- Hollow sphere: I = (2/3)mr².
- Hollow has more rotational inertia → more energy goes to rotation, less to translation.
- **Solid sphere arrives first** (higher final v).

### Example 5 [5] — Conservation of angular momentum (planet collapse)

A star collapses, reducing radius by factor of 100. If initial angular velocity was 1 rev/year, find new angular velocity.

- L conserved: I·ω constant.
- For solid sphere: I ∝ R². If R reduces by 100, I reduces by 100² = 10,000.
- ω increases by factor of 10,000.
- New ω = 10,000 rev/year ≈ 27 rev/day. Very fast.
- (This is why neutron stars rotate so quickly!)

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing translational and rotational KE.** Both contribute for rolling object.
2. **Forgetting v_cm = rω condition for pure rolling.**
3. **Misapplying conservation of angular momentum.** Requires NO external torques.
4. **Wrong direction for angular momentum.** Vector quantity.
5. **Forgetting that I depends on mass distribution.**

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For rolling problems:**
- Use both translational and rotational KE.
- Apply v = rω for rolling without slipping.
- Use energy conservation.

**For angular momentum:**
- Identify if isolated (no external torques).
- Apply L_i = L_f.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Rotational kinetic energy: KE_rot = (1/2)Iω². Parallel to translational KE = (1/2)mv²."
2. "Angular momentum L = Iω is conserved in absence of external torques. L_i = L_f."
3. "For rolling without slipping, v_cm = rω. Total KE = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)Iω²."
4. "When the figure skater pulls in her arms, her moment of inertia decreases. By conservation of angular momentum (Iω constant), her angular velocity increases."
