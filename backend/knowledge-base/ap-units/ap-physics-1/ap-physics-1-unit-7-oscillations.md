## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 5–10% of the AP Physics 1 exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** simple harmonic motion (SHM); springs; pendulums; period, frequency, amplitude; energy in SHM.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Oscillations (especially SHM) appear regularly. Spring and pendulum systems are common. Period formulas must be memorized.

## Big Ideas

1. **Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM):** repetitive motion where restoring force is proportional to displacement.
2. **For ideal spring system:** F = -kx, leading to SHM with period T = 2π√(m/k).
3. **For pendulum (small angles):** T = 2π√(L/g). Period independent of mass and amplitude.
4. **Energy in SHM:** total energy is constant; converts between KE and PE during oscillation.
5. **Frequency and period are inverses.** f = 1/T.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM):**
  - Restoring force proportional to displacement.
  - F = -kx (Hooke's Law for spring).
  - Resulting motion: sinusoidal oscillation about equilibrium.
- **Period (T):** time for one complete cycle. Units: seconds.
- **Frequency (f):** cycles per second. Units: Hz.
  - **f = 1/T.**
- **Angular frequency (ω):** ω = 2πf = 2π/T. Units: rad/s.
- **Amplitude (A):** maximum displacement from equilibrium.
- **For mass-spring system:**
  - **T = 2π√(m/k).**
  - Period depends on mass and spring constant.
  - Period INDEPENDENT of amplitude.
- **For simple pendulum (small angles):**
  - **T = 2π√(L/g).**
  - Period depends on length and gravitational acceleration.
  - Period INDEPENDENT of mass and amplitude.
- **Energy in SHM:**
  - **Total energy** = constant.
  - **At equilibrium:** all kinetic energy. KE = (1/2)kA² (max KE).
  - **At amplitude:** all potential energy. PE = (1/2)kA² (max PE).
  - **In between:** energy is converting between KE and PE.

### Adds for [4]

- **Position, velocity, acceleration in SHM:**
  - **Position:** x(t) = A·cos(ωt + φ), where φ is phase.
  - **Velocity:** v(t) = -Aω·sin(ωt + φ).
  - **Acceleration:** a(t) = -Aω²·cos(ωt + φ) = -ω²·x(t).
  - **Maximum speed:** v_max = Aω (at equilibrium).
  - **Maximum acceleration:** a_max = Aω² (at amplitude).
- **Why pendulum period depends only on L and g.**
  - For small angles, restoring force = mg·sin(θ) ≈ mg·θ.
  - This is linear restoring force → SHM.
  - Mass cancels out: T = 2π√(L/g).
- **Why mass-spring period depends only on m and k.**
  - Spring force F = -kx; Newton: F = ma → ma = -kx.
  - This is SHM with ω² = k/m, so T = 2π√(m/k).
- **Damping:** in real systems, friction or air resistance cause amplitude to decrease over time.
- **Resonance:** large amplitude oscillations when driving frequency matches natural frequency.

### Adds for [5]

- **The "small angle" requirement for pendulums.** sin(θ) ≈ θ only for small angles. For large angles, motion is not simple harmonic; period varies with amplitude.
- **Why all SHM has same form.** Any system with restoring force proportional to displacement (F = -kx) gives SHM. Different systems (springs, pendulums, electrical circuits) follow same mathematical form.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Mass-spring period

A 0.5 kg mass on a spring (k = 200 N/m). Find period.
- T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(0.5/200) = 2π√0.0025 = 2π·0.05 = 0.314 s.

### Example 2 [3] — Pendulum

A pendulum with length 1 m. Find period (g = 9.8 m/s²).
- T = 2π√(L/g) = 2π√(1/9.8) = 2π·0.319 = 2.0 s.

### Example 3 [4] — Energy in SHM

Mass-spring system: k = 100 N/m, m = 0.5 kg, A = 0.1 m.
- Total energy = (1/2)kA² = (1/2)(100)(0.01) = 0.5 J.
- Max speed: v_max = √(2·E/m) = √(2·0.5/0.5) = √2 ≈ 1.41 m/s.
- Or: v_max = Aω = (0.1)·√(k/m) = (0.1)·√(100/0.5) = (0.1)·√200 = 1.41 m/s ✓.

### Example 4 [4] — Frequency calculation

A spring (k = 50 N/m) with 0.2 kg mass. Find frequency.
- T = 2π√(0.2/50) = 2π·0.0632 = 0.397 s.
- f = 1/T = 1/0.397 = 2.52 Hz.

### Example 5 [5] — Pendulum on Mars

A pendulum has period 2 s on Earth. What's its period on Mars (g_Mars = 3.7 m/s²)?
- T_Earth = 2π√(L/9.8) = 2 → L = (2/2π)² · 9.8 = 0.993 m.
- T_Mars = 2π√(0.993/3.7) ≈ 3.26 s.
- (Same length pendulum has longer period on Mars due to weaker gravity.)

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing period and frequency.** T (seconds), f (Hz). Inverses.
2. **Wrong period formulas.** Spring: T = 2π√(m/k). Pendulum: T = 2π√(L/g). Don't mix them.
3. **Forgetting period is independent of amplitude.** For ideal SHM (small angles for pendulum).
4. **Pendulum mass appears in wrong place.** For simple pendulum, mass DOESN'T affect period.
5. **Wrong amplitude calculation.** Maximum displacement from equilibrium.
6. **Confusing angular and translational frequency.** ω = 2πf.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For SHM problems:**
- Identify type (spring or pendulum).
- Use appropriate period formula.
- Apply energy conservation if needed.

**For pendulum problems:**
- Verify small angle (otherwise SHM doesn't strictly apply).
- Period independent of mass and amplitude.
