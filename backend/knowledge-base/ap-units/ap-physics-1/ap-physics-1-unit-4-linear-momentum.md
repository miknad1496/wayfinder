## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–18% of the AP Physics 1 exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** linear momentum; impulse-momentum theorem; conservation of momentum; types of collisions (elastic, inelastic, perfectly inelastic).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Momentum and collisions are perennial topics. Conservation of momentum applies even when energy is not conserved (inelastic collisions). Combined with energy methods for elastic collisions.

## Big Ideas

1. **Momentum (p) = mass × velocity.** Vector quantity.
2. **Conservation of momentum:** in a closed system, total momentum is conserved.
3. **Impulse-momentum theorem:** impulse equals change in momentum. J = F·Δt = Δp.
4. **Collisions:**
   - **Elastic:** momentum AND kinetic energy conserved.
   - **Inelastic:** only momentum conserved (KE not conserved).
   - **Perfectly inelastic:** momentum conserved; objects stick together.
5. **Use conservation of momentum even when energy isn't.** Particularly powerful for inelastic collisions.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Momentum (p):** product of mass and velocity. p = mv.
  - Units: kg·m/s.
  - **VECTOR quantity** — direction matters.
- **Impulse (J):** change in momentum.
  - **J = Δp = F·Δt** (force times time).
  - Units: N·s = kg·m/s (same as momentum).
- **Impulse-Momentum Theorem:**
  - The impulse on an object equals its change in momentum.
  - F_avg · Δt = Δ(mv) = mv_f - mv_i.
- **Conservation of momentum:**
  - In an isolated system (no external net force), total momentum is conserved.
  - Total p_before = total p_after.
  - For two objects: m₁v₁_i + m₂v₂_i = m₁v₁_f + m₂v₂_f.
- **Types of collisions:**
  - **Elastic:** momentum AND kinetic energy both conserved.
    - Examples: billiard balls (approximately), gas molecules.
  - **Inelastic:** momentum conserved; KE NOT conserved (some KE lost to heat, sound, deformation).
    - Examples: car crashes that don't bounce.
  - **Perfectly Inelastic:** momentum conserved; objects STICK TOGETHER.
    - Examples: two cars locking bumpers, bullet embedded in target.
- **2D collisions:** momentum conserved in EACH direction independently.
  - x-momentum_i = x-momentum_f.
  - y-momentum_i = y-momentum_f.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why impulse matters.**
  - Force × time = impulse.
  - Same impulse can come from large force × small time OR small force × large time.
  - **Implication:** to reduce force in collision (e.g., car safety), increase collision time (airbags, crumple zones).
- **Elastic collision math:**
  - For 1D elastic collision: both momentum AND kinetic energy conserved. Two equations, two unknowns.
  - **Equal masses 1D elastic collision:** velocities EXCHANGE. (m collides with stationary M = m → m stops, M moves at original velocity.)
- **Perfectly inelastic collision math:**
  - Objects move together with common final velocity.
  - m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v_f.
  - Solve for v_f.
- **Energy lost in inelastic collisions:**
  - KE_lost = KE_initial - KE_final.
  - Goes to heat, deformation, sound.
- **Center of mass:** for system of particles, the center of mass moves as if all mass were concentrated there with all forces applied to it.
  - Position: x_cm = Σ(m_i·x_i)/M_total.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why momentum is conserved.** Conservation follows from Newton's 3rd Law. When two objects interact, force on each is equal and opposite. So change in momentum of each is equal and opposite, and net change in total momentum is zero.
- **Connection to energy.**
  - Momentum is conserved even when energy isn't.
  - This is what makes momentum so powerful for inelastic collisions.
- **The "use ENERGY first when asked about FINAL SPEED" advice.** True for SINGLE-OBJECT problems with conservative forces. For multi-object collisions, START WITH MOMENTUM CONSERVATION.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Conservation of momentum

A 1500 kg car at 20 m/s collides with stationary 1000 kg car. They stick together. Find final velocity.
- **Perfectly inelastic collision.**
- m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v_f.
- 1500·20 + 1000·0 = 2500·v_f.
- v_f = 30000/2500 = 12 m/s.

### Example 2 [3] — Impulse calculation

A 0.5 kg ball moving at 10 m/s is hit by a bat, reversing direction at 15 m/s. Find impulse.
- Δp = m(v_f - v_i) = 0.5(-15 - 10) = -12.5 kg·m/s.
- Impulse = -12.5 N·s (or 12.5 N·s in opposite direction of original motion).

### Example 3 [4] — Elastic collision (equal masses)

Cue ball (1 kg) at 5 m/s strikes stationary 1 kg target ball (1D elastic collision). Find final velocities.
- Equal masses elastic collision: VELOCITIES EXCHANGE.
- Cue ball: 0 m/s (stops).
- Target: 5 m/s (moves at original velocity of cue ball).

### Example 4 [4] — Recoil

A 50 kg cannon fires a 5 kg projectile at 100 m/s. Find recoil velocity of cannon.
- Conservation of momentum: 0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂.
- 0 = 5·100 + 50·v_cannon.
- v_cannon = -500/50 = -10 m/s. (Cannon recoils at 10 m/s in opposite direction.)

### Example 5 [4][5] — 2D collision

Two ice hockey pucks of equal mass collide. Initial: puck A moves right at 4 m/s; puck B at rest. After collision: puck A moves at 30° above horizontal at v_A m/s; puck B moves at 60° below horizontal.

Use conservation of momentum in x and y directions (KE conservation gives third equation if elastic).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Forgetting momentum is a vector.** Direction matters in calculations.
2. **Confusing types of collisions.** Elastic (KE conserved); inelastic (KE not); perfectly inelastic (stick together).
3. **Wrong sign for impulse.** Pay attention to direction.
4. **Forgetting external forces.** Conservation of momentum requires NO external forces (or impulse from external is small).
5. **Using KE conservation in inelastic collisions.** WRONG. Only momentum conserved.
6. **Wrong impulse formula.** J = Δp = F·Δt.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For collision problems:**
- Identify type of collision.
- Use conservation of momentum.
- For elastic: also use conservation of KE.
- For perfectly inelastic: objects move together.

**For impulse:**
- Identify forces and time interval.
- Use J = F·Δt = Δp.

**For 2D collisions:**
- Conserve momentum in x AND y separately.
