## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 5–10% of the AP Physics 1 exam (newly added in 2024-25 redesign)
- **Sub-topics covered:** density; pressure; Pascal's Principle; Archimedes' Principle (buoyancy); fluid flow (continuity equation, Bernoulli's equation).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Unit 8 was added in 2024-25 redesign. Pressure, buoyancy, and Bernoulli's equation are key topics.

## Big Ideas

1. **Pressure (P) = Force/Area.** Units: Pa (N/m²).
2. **Pressure in fluid increases with depth.** P = P₀ + ρgh.
3. **Pascal's Principle:** pressure applied to confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.
4. **Archimedes' Principle (buoyancy):** buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid.
5. **Continuity equation:** in incompressible flow, A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ (cross-sectional area times velocity is constant).

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Density (ρ):** mass per unit volume.
  - **ρ = m/V.**
  - Units: kg/m³.
  - Water: ρ = 1000 kg/m³.
- **Pressure (P):** force per unit area.
  - **P = F/A.**
  - Units: pascals (Pa = N/m²).
  - Atmospheric pressure: ~101,325 Pa = 1 atm.
- **Pressure in a fluid at depth:**
  - **P = P₀ + ρgh,** where P₀ is pressure at surface and h is depth below.
  - Pressure increases with depth.
- **Pascal's Principle:** pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.
  - **Application:** hydraulic systems multiply force.
  - F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂ (pressure constant, force depends on area).
- **Archimedes' Principle (Buoyancy):**
  - **Buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid.**
  - **F_b = ρ_fluid · V_displaced · g.**
  - **Floating:** buoyant force = weight of object.
  - **Sinking:** weight > buoyant force at full submersion.
- **Continuity Equation:**
  - For incompressible fluid in steady flow: **A₁v₁ = A₂v₂.**
  - As cross-sectional area decreases, velocity increases (and vice versa).
- **Bernoulli's Equation:**
  - For ideal fluid (incompressible, non-viscous, steady flow):
  - **P + (1/2)ρv² + ρgh = constant.**
  - Pressure, kinetic energy per volume, and gravitational PE per volume sum to constant.
- **Implications of Bernoulli:**
  - Higher velocity → lower pressure.
  - Used in airplane lift, atomizers, etc.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why pressure increases with depth.**
  - Each layer of water above pushes down on layers below.
  - Pressure = ρgh from weight of water column above.
- **Hydraulic systems:**
  - Apply small force to small piston (large pressure due to small area).
  - Same pressure transmits to large piston (large force due to large area).
  - F₂ = F₁·(A₂/A₁). Mechanical advantage.
- **Why some objects float and others sink.**
  - Compare object density to fluid density.
  - Object density < fluid density: floats.
  - Object density = fluid density: stays at any depth.
  - Object density > fluid density: sinks.
- **Buoyancy worked example:**
  - 1 kg block, density 500 kg/m³, in water (1000 kg/m³).
  - Block volume: 1/500 = 0.002 m³.
  - If fully submerged, displaces 0.002 m³ of water.
  - Buoyant force = 1000·0.002·9.8 = 19.6 N.
  - Weight = 1·9.8 = 9.8 N.
  - Buoyant > weight: object floats.
  - Floats with half submerged (since density is half of water).
- **Continuity in real systems:**
  - Garden hose nozzle: smaller opening = higher velocity.
  - Heart and arteries: flow rate (A·v) constant.
- **Bernoulli applications:**
  - **Airplane lift:** air faster over wing top → lower pressure → upward lift.
  - **Atomizer:** high-velocity air creates low pressure, drawing liquid up.
  - **Venturi tube:** measures flow rate using pressure difference.

### Adds for [5]

- **Limitations of Bernoulli.**
  - Real fluids have viscosity (energy lost to internal friction).
  - Compressible fluids (gases at high speeds) require modifications.
  - Steady flow assumption may not hold for turbulent flow.
- **Why streamline flow vs turbulent flow matters.**
  - Streamline (laminar): Bernoulli applies; smooth, predictable.
  - Turbulent: chaotic; requires more complex analysis.
  - Reynolds number distinguishes regimes.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Pressure at depth

A diver descends to 30 m. Find pressure at that depth (atmospheric pressure 101,325 Pa).
- P = P₀ + ρgh = 101,325 + 1000·9.8·30 = 101,325 + 294,000 = 395,325 Pa ≈ 4 atm.

### Example 2 [3] — Buoyancy

A 2 kg block (density 800 kg/m³) is placed in water. Does it sink or float?
- Volume of block: 2/800 = 0.0025 m³.
- If fully submerged, displaces 0.0025 m³ of water.
- Buoyant force = 1000·0.0025·9.8 = 24.5 N.
- Weight = 2·9.8 = 19.6 N.
- Buoyant > weight → FLOATS.

### Example 3 [4] — Hydraulic press

A hydraulic press has piston areas of 0.01 m² and 0.10 m². If 100 N is applied to small piston, find force on large piston.
- F₂ = F₁·(A₂/A₁) = 100·(0.10/0.01) = 1000 N.
- 10x mechanical advantage.

### Example 4 [4] — Continuity equation

Water flows at 5 m/s through a 0.05 m² cross-section pipe, then enters a 0.01 m² section. Find new velocity.
- A₁v₁ = A₂v₂.
- 0.05·5 = 0.01·v₂ → v₂ = 25 m/s.

### Example 5 [4][5] — Bernoulli's equation

Water flows at 4 m/s through a horizontal pipe (1 m elevation). Pressure at this point: 200 kPa. The pipe rises to 5 m elevation, where velocity is 2 m/s. Find pressure at higher point.

- Bernoulli: P₁ + (1/2)ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + (1/2)ρv₂² + ρgh₂.
- 200,000 + (1/2)(1000)(16) + (1000)(9.8)(1) = P₂ + (1/2)(1000)(4) + (1000)(9.8)(5).
- 200,000 + 8000 + 9800 = P₂ + 2000 + 49,000.
- 217,800 = P₂ + 51,000.
- P₂ = 166,800 Pa.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Wrong pressure formula.** P = ρgh (gauge pressure above atmospheric). P_total = P_atm + ρgh.
2. **Wrong buoyancy formula.** F_b = ρ_fluid · V_displaced · g (weight of DISPLACED fluid).
3. **Forgetting V_displaced changes for floating object.** Only submerged portion counts.
4. **Wrong direction of buoyant force.** Always upward.
5. **Confusing pressure and force.** P = F/A. Higher pressure can result from less area, even with same force.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For pressure problems:**
- Apply P = P₀ + ρgh.
- Convert units consistently.

**For buoyancy:**
- Calculate F_b = ρ·V·g.
- Compare to weight to predict floating/sinking.

**For Bernoulli:**
- Apply at two points.
- Sum pressure, KE/volume, PE/volume.
