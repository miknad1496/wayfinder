# AP African American Studies — Unit 3: The Practice of Freedom — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~25% of the AP African American Studies exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** emancipation; Reconstruction; Jim Crow; Great Migration; Harlem Renaissance; Civil Rights Movement; Black Power; Black feminism; education and economic struggles.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Major historical periods after slavery; key figures and movements; cultural achievements.

## Big Ideas

1. **Emancipation (1863-1865)** ended legal slavery; Reconstruction's promise undone by Jim Crow.
2. **Jim Crow** (1877-1965) imposed segregation, disenfranchisement, terror.
3. **Great Migration** (~1910-1970) relocated millions of Black Americans North/West.
4. **Harlem Renaissance, Civil Rights, Black Power, Black feminism** — major movements.
5. **Constant struggle for full freedom** alongside cultural flourishing.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Emancipation:**
  - **Emancipation Proclamation (Jan 1, 1863):** freed enslaved in Confederacy.
  - **13th Amendment (1865):** abolished slavery.
  - **14th Amendment (1868):** birthright citizenship; equal protection.
  - **15th Amendment (1870):** Black male suffrage.
- **Reconstruction (1865-1877):**
  - **Freedmen's Bureau:** aid for newly freed.
  - **Black political participation:** Black congressmen, state legislators.
  - **Hiram Revels, Blanche K. Bruce** (US Senators).
  - **HBCUs** founded.
  - **Black churches, schools, communities** built.
- **End of Reconstruction:**
  - **Compromise of 1877:** federal troops withdrawn.
  - **White supremacist redemption** governments.
  - **KKK terror.**
- **Jim Crow Era (1877-1965):**
  - **Segregation:** "separate but equal" (Plessy v. Ferguson, 1896).
  - **Disenfranchisement:** poll taxes, literacy tests, grandfather clauses, white primaries.
  - **Lynching:** ~4,000+ documented (1877-1950).
  - **Sharecropping:** debt peonage.
- **Great Migration (~1910-1970):**
  - **~6 million Black people** moved from rural South to urban North/West.
  - **Drivers:** escape Jim Crow; northern industrial jobs; WWI/WWII labor demand.
  - **Created Black communities** in Chicago, Detroit, NYC, Philadelphia, LA.
- **Harlem Renaissance (1920s):**
  - **Cultural flourishing** in Harlem (NYC).
  - **Writers:** Langston Hughes, Zora Neale Hurston, Countee Cullen, Claude McKay.
  - **Music:** Duke Ellington, Louis Armstrong, Bessie Smith.
  - **Visual art:** Aaron Douglas.
  - **W.E.B. Du Bois, Alain Locke** intellectual leaders.
- **Civil Rights Movement (1954-1968):**
  - **Brown v. Board (1954):** overturned Plessy.
  - **Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955-1956):** Rosa Parks; MLK rises.
  - **Little Rock (1957):** integration enforcement.
  - **Greensboro sit-ins (1960):** SNCC formed.
  - **Freedom Rides (1961).**
  - **March on Washington (1963):** "I Have a Dream."
  - **Civil Rights Act (1964):** outlawed segregation in public.
  - **Voting Rights Act (1965).**
  - **MLK assassinated (1968).**
- **Black Power era:**
  - **Malcolm X:** Nation of Islam; assassinated 1965.
  - **Stokely Carmichael:** "Black Power" slogan (1966).
  - **Black Panthers (1966):** Huey Newton, Bobby Seale; community programs + armed self-defense.
  - **AIM (American Indian Movement)** allied.
- **Black feminism:**
  - **Combahee River Collective** (1977 statement).
  - **bell hooks, Audre Lorde, Angela Davis, Patricia Hill Collins.**
  - **Intersectionality** (Kimberlé Crenshaw, 1989).

### Adds for [4]

- **Reconstruction's interracial coalitions:**
  - **Black voters and white Republicans** elected progressive state governments.
  - **Constitutional reforms:** public education, social services.
- **Why Plessy v. Ferguson mattered:**
  - **"Separate but equal"** legitimized segregation.
  - **Justice Harlan's lone dissent:** "Constitution is color-blind."
- **MLK vs Malcolm X:**
  - **MLK:** nonviolent civil disobedience; integration; appealed to American conscience.
  - **Malcolm X:** "by any means necessary"; Black nationalism (early); evolved late in life.
  - **Both essential** to broader movement.
- **Why Harlem Renaissance mattered:**
  - **Asserted Black cultural sophistication.**
  - **"New Negro" identity.**
  - **Cultural foundation** for later civil rights and Black Power.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Civil Rights Movement gains incomplete:**
  - **Legal segregation ended** but de facto continues.
  - **Wealth gap, education gap, criminal justice disparities** persist.
- **Why intersectionality concept important:**
  - **Black women** face race AND gender oppression.
  - **Single-axis analysis insufficient.**
- **Why "freedom" still contested:**
  - **Modern movements:** Black Lives Matter (2013+), criminal justice reform.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Reconstruction amendments

What did the 13th, 14th, 15th Amendments do?
- **13th (1865):** abolished slavery.
- **14th (1868):** birthright citizenship; equal protection; due process.
- **15th (1870):** Black male suffrage.

### Example 2 [3] — Civil rights milestones

Identify Civil Rights Movement milestones.
- **Brown v. Board (1954):** ended legal school segregation.
- **Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955-1956):** Rosa Parks; MLK.
- **March on Washington (1963):** "I Have a Dream."
- **Civil Rights Act (1964):** ended segregation in public.
- **Voting Rights Act (1965):** dismantled disenfranchisement.

### Example 3 [4] — Great Migration

What was the Great Migration?
- **~6 million Black Americans** moved from rural South to urban North/West, ~1910-1970.
- **Push:** Jim Crow, sharecropping, lynching.
- **Pull:** northern industrial jobs (WWI, WWII boomed demand).
- **Created Black communities:** Chicago South Side, Harlem, Detroit, Watts.
- **Cultural flourishing:** blues, jazz, gospel migrated.

### Example 4 [4] — Black Power

What did "Black Power" represent?
- **Slogan from Stokely Carmichael (1966).**
- **Emphasized:** Black self-determination, pride, political/economic power.
- **Black Panthers (1966):** community programs (free breakfast, health clinics), armed self-defense, monitored police.
- **Cultural:** "Black is Beautiful"; afros; African names.
- **Critique of nonviolent integrationism;** complementary not necessarily opposed.

### Example 5 [5] — Intersectionality

What is intersectionality?
- **Coined by Kimberlé Crenshaw (1989).**
- **Recognition** that people experience overlapping forms of oppression (race + gender + class + sexuality).
- **Black women** face anti-Black racism AND sexism — not additive but interactive.
- **Earlier roots:** Combahee River Collective (1977), Sojourner Truth ("Ain't I a Woman?").
- **Major framework** in modern social movements.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Reconstruction's failures undid much of its promise** — but achievements were real.
2. **Plessy v. Ferguson date 1896,** not 1865.
3. **Brown v. Board overturned Plessy** (1954).
4. **Great Migration timing:** ~1910-1970, two waves.
5. **Black Power complemented, didn't replace, civil rights.**
6. **Intersectionality** is analytical framework, not just buzzword.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For periods:** identify dates, key events, key figures.
**For movements:** identify leaders, strategies, achievements, limits.
**For cultural questions:** identify Harlem Renaissance figures and works.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Reconstruction (1865-1877) brought the 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments, the Freedmen's Bureau, Black political participation (Hiram Revels, Blanche K. Bruce in US Senate), and the founding of HBCUs — but ended with the Compromise of 1877 and white supremacist 'redemption' that ushered in Jim Crow."
2. "Jim Crow (1877-1965) imposed legal segregation (codified by Plessy v. Ferguson, 1896), disenfranchisement through poll taxes, literacy tests, and grandfather clauses, and racial terror including ~4,000+ documented lynchings between 1877 and 1950."
3. "The Great Migration (~1910-1970) relocated approximately 6 million Black Americans from the rural South to urban centers in the North and West, transforming American demographics, politics, and culture, and seeding centers like Chicago, Detroit, Harlem, and Los Angeles."
4. "The Civil Rights Movement (1954-1968) — driven by Black activists, churches, students, and organizations (NAACP, SNCC, SCLC, CORE) — produced Brown v. Board (1954), the Civil Rights Act (1964), and the Voting Rights Act (1965)."
5. "Black feminism, articulated in the Combahee River Collective Statement (1977) and theorized through intersectionality (Kimberlé Crenshaw, 1989), recognizes that Black women experience interlocking systems of oppression that single-axis analyses (race-only or gender-only) cannot capture."
