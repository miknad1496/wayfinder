# AP African American Studies — Unit 4: Movements and Debates — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~25% of the AP African American Studies exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** post-Civil Rights era; Black Lives Matter; mass incarceration; Black political achievements (Obama); Afrofuturism; Black diaspora globally; ongoing debates (reparations, education, identity); contemporary cultural production.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Modern movements; ongoing debates; contemporary cultural figures and works.

## Big Ideas

1. **Post-Civil Rights era** brought political gains AND new struggles (mass incarceration).
2. **Mass incarceration** disproportionately affects Black Americans; "New Jim Crow."
3. **Black Lives Matter** (2013+) responds to police violence, broader racial justice.
4. **Black diaspora** stretches across Americas, Europe, Africa.
5. **Cultural production** continues — hip-hop, film, literature, Afrofuturism.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Post-1968 era:**
  - **Slowed federal civil rights enforcement.**
  - **Affirmative action** debates (Bakke 1978; recent cases).
  - **Continued de facto segregation** in schools, housing.
  - **Black political achievements:** Black mayors of major cities (Detroit, Atlanta, LA); Congressional Black Caucus.
- **Mass incarceration:**
  - **War on Drugs** (1980s+); "tough on crime."
  - **Sentencing disparities** (crack vs powder cocaine).
  - **Prison population exploded** — US has highest incarceration rate globally.
  - **Black Americans incarcerated** at ~5x white rate.
  - **Michelle Alexander's *The New Jim Crow*** (2010) prominent analysis.
- **Black political milestones:**
  - **Shirley Chisholm:** first Black woman in Congress (1968); ran for president (1972).
  - **Barbara Jordan:** powerful Watergate-era congresswoman.
  - **Jesse Jackson:** ran for president (1984, 1988).
  - **Barack Obama:** first Black US president (2009-2017).
  - **Kamala Harris:** first Black woman VP (2021); presidential candidate (2024).
- **Black Lives Matter (2013+):**
  - **Founded:** Alicia Garza, Patrisse Cullors, Opal Tometi after Trayvon Martin's killer acquittal.
  - **Police violence:** Michael Brown (Ferguson 2014), Eric Garner, Tamir Rice, Sandra Bland, George Floyd (2020).
  - **2020 protests** following Floyd's murder were largest US protests in history (estimates).
- **Afrofuturism:**
  - **Cultural movement** combining science fiction, fantasy, African/African American history.
  - **Octavia Butler, Samuel Delany** (literature).
  - **Sun Ra, George Clinton, Janelle Monáe** (music).
  - **Black Panther** film (2018) brought to mainstream.
- **Hip-hop:**
  - **Born in Bronx (1973):** DJ Kool Herc.
  - **Pioneers:** Grandmaster Flash, Run DMC.
  - **Golden Age:** Public Enemy, NWA, Tribe Called Quest, Wu-Tang.
  - **Major figures:** Tupac, Biggie, Jay-Z, Kanye, Kendrick Lamar, Beyoncé.
- **Black diaspora globally:**
  - **Caribbean:** Jamaica, Haiti, Trinidad, etc.
  - **Brazil:** largest Black population outside Africa.
  - **UK, France, Netherlands:** post-colonial Black communities.
- **Ongoing debates:**
  - **Reparations:** for slavery and Jim Crow harms (HR 40, federal study bill).
  - **Affirmative action** (recent SCOTUS ruling 2023 ended in college admissions).
  - **Critical Race Theory** debates in education.
  - **Police reform.**

### Adds for [4]

- **The New Jim Crow argument:**
  - **Michelle Alexander's thesis:** mass incarceration functions as new system of racial caste.
  - **Felons disenfranchised, unable to access housing, jobs, food stamps.**
  - **"Color-blind" criminal justice has racially disparate outcomes.**
- **Critical Race Theory:**
  - **Academic legal framework** (Derrick Bell, Kimberlé Crenshaw, Patricia Williams).
  - **Examines how race is socially constructed** and embedded in law.
  - **Recently politicized** in K-12 education debates.
- **Reparations:**
  - **HR 40** would establish federal commission to study (introduced annually since 1989, John Conyers).
  - **Local efforts:** Evanston IL (2021), California study commission.
  - **Coates's "The Case for Reparations" (2014, *The Atlantic*).**

### Adds for [5]

- **Why post-Civil Rights "second freedom struggle":**
  - **Legal victories** didn't translate to full equality.
  - **Wealth gap, education gap, criminal justice gap** persist.
- **Why hip-hop matters culturally:**
  - **Most influential American music genre globally** by 21st century.
  - **Vehicle for Black voice, social commentary, art.**
- **Why diaspora consciousness:**
  - **Connects African Americans to global Black struggles.**
  - **Pan-Africanism** thread from Garvey to today.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Black political milestones

Identify three Black political "firsts."
- **Shirley Chisholm:** first Black woman in Congress (1968).
- **Thurgood Marshall:** first Black Supreme Court Justice (1967).
- **Barack Obama:** first Black US president (2009).
- **Kamala Harris:** first Black woman VP (2021).

### Example 2 [3] — Black Lives Matter

When and why did Black Lives Matter emerge?
- **Founded 2013** by Alicia Garza, Patrisse Cullors, Opal Tometi.
- **Trigger:** acquittal of George Zimmerman (killed Trayvon Martin).
- **Subsequent catalysts:** Michael Brown (Ferguson 2014), Eric Garner, Tamir Rice, others.
- **Peak mobilization:** summer 2020 after George Floyd's murder; ~15-26 million Americans participated.

### Example 3 [4] — Mass incarceration

Why is mass incarceration considered a racial justice issue?
- **Black Americans incarcerated** at ~5x white rate.
- **Crack/powder cocaine sentencing disparity** (~100:1, reduced to 18:1 in 2010).
- **Felony disenfranchisement** removes voting rights.
- **War on Drugs targeted** Black communities.
- **Michelle Alexander's *The New Jim Crow*** (2010) frames as new racial caste system.

### Example 4 [4] — Afrofuturism

What is Afrofuturism?
- **Cultural-aesthetic movement** combining sci-fi, fantasy, African/African American history.
- **Reimagines future** through Black perspectives.
- **Examples:**
  - **Literature:** Octavia Butler (*Kindred*, *Parable* series), Samuel Delany.
  - **Music:** Sun Ra, George Clinton/Parliament-Funkadelic, Janelle Monáe.
  - **Film:** *Black Panther* (2018) brought to mainstream.
  - **Visual art:** Jean-Michel Basquiat, others.

### Example 5 [5] — Reparations debate

What's the case for and against reparations?
- **For:**
  - Compensate descendants for slavery (~250 years), Jim Crow (~100 years), ongoing harm.
  - Address persistent wealth gap.
  - **Coates's "The Case for Reparations"** (2014).
- **Against:**
  - Logistical complexity.
  - "Already paid through welfare/affirmative action."
  - Generational distance.
- **Status:** federal HR 40 unenacted; local efforts (Evanston, California study).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Civil Rights Movement didn't end racism** — legal victories partial.
2. **Mass incarceration started in 1980s,** linked to War on Drugs.
3. **BLM founded 2013,** before George Floyd.
4. **Afrofuturism predates Black Panther film** by decades.
5. **Hip-hop born 1973** in the Bronx, not later.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For modern movement questions:** identify origin, key figures, demands, achievements.
**For ongoing debates:** identify positions and stakes.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The post-Civil Rights era brought political achievements (Shirley Chisholm, Thurgood Marshall, Barack Obama, Kamala Harris) alongside new struggles, particularly mass incarceration linked to the War on Drugs and 'tough on crime' policies."
2. "Mass incarceration disproportionately affects Black Americans, who are incarcerated at approximately 5x the white rate. Michelle Alexander's *The New Jim Crow* (2010) argues this functions as a new system of racial caste through felony disenfranchisement and post-incarceration exclusion."
3. "Black Lives Matter, founded in 2013 by Alicia Garza, Patrisse Cullors, and Opal Tometi after Trayvon Martin's killer was acquitted, mobilized national protests in response to police killings — culminating in 2020's mass protests after George Floyd's murder."
4. "Afrofuturism is a cultural-aesthetic movement combining science fiction, fantasy, and African/African American history — represented in literature (Octavia Butler), music (Sun Ra, Janelle Monáe), and film (*Black Panther*, 2018)."
5. "The reparations debate centers on whether and how the US should compensate Black Americans for slavery, Jim Crow, and ongoing systemic harms. Federal HR 40 (introduced annually since 1989) would establish a study commission; local efforts in Evanston, IL (2021) and California have begun implementation."
