## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~50% of AP Latin (with Caesar)
- **Required passages:** Selected passages from *Aeneid* Books 1, 2, 4, 6 (and book selections per CED).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Translation, sight-reading, analysis FRQ all draw from Vergil.

## Big Ideas

1. **Vergil's *Aeneid*** (~29-19 BCE) is Rome's national epic.
2. **Aeneas** = Trojan hero who founds Roman lineage; embodies Roman virtues (pietas).
3. **Augustan** ideology: Aeneas's mission parallels Augustus's establishment of empire.
4. **Pietas** (duty to gods, country, family) drives Aeneas; conflicts with personal desire.
5. **Tragic dimensions:** Dido's suicide, Aeneas's losses, "tears of things" (lacrimae rerum).

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Vergil (Publius Vergilius Maro, 70-19 BCE):**
  - **Composed *Aeneid*** under Augustus's patronage.
  - **12 books;** ~10,000 lines.
  - **Modeled on Homer:** Books 1-6 echo *Odyssey* (wandering); Books 7-12 echo *Iliad* (war).
  - **Died before completing;** ordered manuscript burned but Augustus preserved.
- **Plot summary:**
  - **Book 1:** Aeneas storms to Carthage; Venus reveals fate.
  - **Book 2:** Aeneas tells of Troy's fall (Trojan Horse, etc.).
  - **Book 3:** Aeneas's wanderings.
  - **Book 4:** Aeneas and Dido love affair; he abandons her at gods' command; she suicides.
  - **Book 6:** Aeneas descends to underworld; meets father Anchises; sees future Romans (including Augustus).
  - **Books 7-12:** war in Italy; Aeneas defeats Turnus.
- **Key themes:**
  - **Pietas** = duty (to gods, country, family).
  - **Furor** = irrational passion (often Dido, Turnus, Juno).
  - **Fate (fatum)** vs free will.
  - **Cost of empire:** sacrifices required.
- **Famous lines:**
  - **"Arma virumque cano"** (1.1) "I sing of arms and the man."
  - **"Dum conderet urbem"** ("until he could found the city").
  - **"Sunt lacrimae rerum"** (1.462) "There are tears in things."
- **Aeneas's character:**
  - **Pius Aeneas** (devoted to duty).
  - **Suffers loss** (wife Creusa, father Anchises, lover Dido).
  - **Sometimes ambivalent** about mission.

### Adds for [4]

- **Augustan context:**
  - **Augustus = first emperor (27 BCE).**
  - **Vergil's epic** legitimates Augustus's rule by genealogy (Augustus claimed descent from Aeneas via Iulus/Julius).
  - **Pax Romana** beginning.
- **Dactylic hexameter:**
  - **Six metrical feet** per line; each foot dactyl (—∪∪) or spondee (— —).
  - **Standard epic meter.**
- **Stylistic features:**
  - **Similes** (extended comparisons).
  - **Foreshadowing.**
  - **Allusion** to Homer.
  - **Ekphrasis** (description of art, e.g., shield of Aeneas in Book 8).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why *Aeneid* tragic:**
  - **Aeneas suffers** to fulfill duty.
  - **Dido's death** undeserved (innocent victim of his mission).
  - **Final scene:** Aeneas kills Turnus in rage, complicating "Roman virtue."
- **Modern critical readings:**
  - **Two voices** (Adam Parry): triumphal AND tragic.
  - **Cost of empire** as central theme.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify Aeneas

Who is Aeneas?
- **Trojan prince** (son of Anchises and Venus).
- **Survives Troy's fall** (Book 2).
- **Wanders Mediterranean.**
- **Founds Roman lineage** by settling in Italy.
- **Embodies pietas.**

### Example 2 [4] — Pietas

Define pietas with examples.
- **Pietas:** dutiful devotion to gods, country, family.
- **Examples in Aeneid:**
  - **Aeneas carries father Anchises** out of burning Troy (Book 2).
  - **Aeneas leaves Dido** at gods' command (Book 4).
  - **Aeneas visits Anchises** in underworld (Book 6).
- **Defining Roman virtue.**

### Example 3 [5] — Tragic dimensions

Why is *Aeneid* both triumphal and tragic?
- **Triumphal:** Aeneas fulfills divine mission; founds Roman lineage; Augustus's ideology.
- **Tragic:** Dido's suicide; loss of Creusa, Anchises; Aeneas's emotional cost.
- **Final scene:** Aeneas kills supplicant Turnus in rage (echoing Achilles's killing of Hector in Iliad).
- **Adam Parry's "two voices"** — public ideological + private tragic.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Vergil ≠ Virgil's contemporary** — same person, anglicized name.
2. **Aeneid in 12 books**, modeled on Homer.
3. **Pietas ≠ piety** in narrow religious sense; broader civic/familial duty.
4. **Aeneas's killing of Turnus** at end is morally complex.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For translation:** know required passages thoroughly.
**For analysis:** address pietas, fate, Augustan ideology, tragic elements.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Vergil's *Aeneid* (~29-19 BCE), composed under Augustan patronage, presents Aeneas as the embodiment of *pietas* — dutiful devotion to gods, country, and family — whose mission to found the Roman lineage parallels and legitimates Augustus's establishment of empire."
2. "In Book 4, Aeneas's abandonment of Dido at the gods' command exemplifies the tension between *pietas* (duty to fate) and personal desire; her subsequent suicide casts a tragic shadow over the founding mission."
3. "The famous line 'sunt lacrimae rerum et mentem mortalia tangunt' (1.462) — 'there are tears in things and mortal matters touch the mind' — captures the *Aeneid*'s tragic undercurrent even amid its triumphalist narrative."
