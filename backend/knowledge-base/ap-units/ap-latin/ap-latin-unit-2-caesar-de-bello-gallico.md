# AP Latin — Unit 2: Caesar's De Bello Gallico — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** ~50% of AP Latin (with Vergil)
- **Required passages:** Selected passages from Books 1, 4, 5, 6 of *De Bello Gallico*.

## Big Ideas

1. **Julius Caesar's *De Bello Gallico*** = personal account of Gallic Wars (58-51 BCE).
2. **Written in third person** ("Caesar did this") — pseudo-objectivity.
3. **Propaganda + literature:** justifies Caesar's conquests; portrays him favorably.
4. **Ethnographic descriptions** of Gauls, Germans, Britons.
5. **Direct, lucid Latin prose** — model of classical style.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Julius Caesar (100-44 BCE):**
  - **Roman general, statesman.**
  - **Conquered Gaul** (modern France/Belgium/etc., 58-51 BCE).
  - **Crossed Rubicon (49 BCE)** to start civil war.
  - **Dictator perpetuo;** assassinated Ides of March 44 BCE.
- ***De Bello Gallico*:**
  - **7 books by Caesar** + 8th by Hirtius (lieutenant).
  - **Records Gallic Wars 58-51 BCE.**
  - **Caesar wrote** likely during/after campaigns, distributed in Rome.
  - **Strategic political document** as well as historical record.
- **Famous opening:**
  - **"Gallia est omnis divisa in partes tres"** ("All Gaul is divided into three parts").
- **Plot outline:**
  - **Book 1:** war with Helvetii, Ariovistus.
  - **Book 4:** German campaigns, first British invasion.
  - **Book 5:** second British invasion, Ambiorix's revolt.
  - **Book 6:** descriptions of Gauls, Germans, druids.
  - **Book 7:** Vercingetorix's revolt, siege of Alesia.
- **Key themes:**
  - **Caesar's leadership** (always brave, decisive).
  - **Roman military discipline.**
  - **Barbarian "otherness"** (Gauls, Germans).
  - **Justification** for conquest.
- **Famous figures:**
  - **Vercingetorix:** Gallic chieftain who united Gauls; defeated at Alesia (52 BCE); paraded in Caesar's triumph; executed.
  - **Ariovistus:** German king Caesar defeated.
  - **Ambiorix:** Gallic chieftain who massacred Roman legion; escaped capture.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why Caesar wrote in 3rd person:**
  - **Appears objective.**
  - **Distances Caesar-author from Caesar-actor.**
  - **Subtle propaganda.**
- **Ethnographic descriptions (Book 6):**
  - **Gauls:** druids, religion, social structure.
  - **Germans:** (according to Caesar) more primitive than Gauls.
  - **Distinguishes** Rhine as boundary.
  - **Some accuracy + much stereotyping.**
- **Caesar's Latin style:**
  - **Direct, clear.**
  - **Subordinate clauses skillfully managed.**
  - **Model for Latin learners.**

### Adds for [5]

- **Why *Bellum Gallicum* matters as propaganda:**
  - **Self-promotion** while away from Rome.
  - **Justifies conquest** to Senate, public.
  - **Sets up civil war** by building Caesar's reputation, army.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify Caesar

Who was Julius Caesar?
- **Roman general (100-44 BCE).**
- **Conquered Gaul** (58-51 BCE).
- **Crossed Rubicon to start civil war (49 BCE).**
- **Dictator perpetuo;** assassinated Ides of March 44 BCE.
- **His legacy:** ended Republic; nephew Octavian became Augustus, first emperor.

### Example 2 [4] — Vercingetorix

Who was Vercingetorix?
- **Gallic chieftain (Arverni tribe).**
- **United Gauls against Rome (52 BCE).**
- **Caesar besieged at Alesia;** double siege (Caesar surrounded Vercingetorix; then circle of Gauls outside surrounded Caesar's army; Caesar won both battles).
- **Surrendered to Caesar.**
- **Paraded in Caesar's triumph in Rome (46 BCE);** executed.
- **Modern French national hero.**

### Example 3 [5] — Caesar's third person

Why does Caesar write in third person ("Caesar marched..." not "I marched...")?
- **Creates appearance of objective historical record.**
- **Distances Caesar-author from Caesar-actor.**
- **Reader may forget** they're reading Caesar's self-presentation.
- **Subtle propaganda technique.**
- **Distinguishes from memoirs;** seems more authoritative.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Caesar's account is propaganda + history,** not neutral.
2. **Vercingetorix at Alesia** = climax (Book 7).
3. **Caesar wrote 7 books;** Hirtius added 8th.
4. **Crossed Rubicon 49 BCE,** not during Gallic Wars.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For translation:** know required passages.
**For analysis:** address propaganda, ethnographic stereotyping, military strategy.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Caesar's *De Bello Gallico* (58-51 BCE) records the Gallic Wars in third person — 'Caesar marched...' rather than 'I marched...' — creating appearance of objective historical record while serving as political propaganda for his Roman audience."
2. "The famous opening 'Gallia est omnis divisa in partes tres' ('All Gaul is divided into three parts') begins Caesar's account of conquering territory roughly modern France, Belgium, and adjacent regions."
3. "The siege of Alesia (52 BCE), in which Caesar defeated Vercingetorix's united Gallic revolt through a double siege (besieging Vercingetorix while himself surrounded by relief Gauls), marks the climax of the Gallic Wars and Caesar's military genius."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master required passages. Master Caesar's third-person technique. Master Vercingetorix and Alesia. Master ethnographic descriptions of Gauls/Germans.*
