## Big Ideas

1. **Family structures vary** across the Spanish-speaking world.
2. **Customs and values** include religion, traditions, food, gender roles.
3. **Education systems** differ (public/private, university structure).
4. **Communities and citizenship** themes include immigration, identity, civic engagement.
5. **Spanish-speaking world** is huge: 21+ countries, 500+ million native speakers.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Vocabulary domain (key terms):**
  - **La familia:** padres, hijos, abuelos, tíos, primos, sobrinos, cuñados, suegros.
  - **Familias modernas:** padrastro/madrastra (step-parents), hermanastros (step-siblings), padres divorciados, familias monoparentales (single-parent), familias homoparentales (same-sex), padrinos (godparents).
  - **La comunidad:** vecinos, vecindario, barrio, ciudadano/a, ciudadanía, voluntariado, participación cívica.
  - **La educación:** escuela primaria, secundaria, preparatoria/bachillerato, universidad, beca (scholarship), maestro/profesor.
  - **Costumbres y valores:** tradición, religión, herencia, identidad, generación.
- **Cultural products, practices, perspectives (3 P's):**
  - **Products:** food (paella, mole, asado), music (flamenco, mariachi, tango), clothing (ropa típica).
  - **Practices:** family meals, quinceañera (15th birthday), Día de los Muertos, Three Kings Day (Reyes Magos).
  - **Perspectives:** importance of family, respect for elders (mayores), religious influence (Catholicism), regional pride.
- **Common contrasts:**
  - **Multi-generational households common** in many Hispanic cultures vs nuclear in US.
  - **Compadrazgo:** godparent relationships extend family.
  - **Quinceañera:** marks transition to womanhood (15th birthday).

### Adds for [4]

- **Education systems:**
  - **Spain:** ESO (Educación Secundaria Obligatoria), Bachillerato, university (Selectividad entrance exam).
  - **Mexico:** primaria → secundaria → preparatoria → universidad.
  - **Latin American universities** often free or low cost (UNAM Mexico, UBA Argentina).
- **Citizenship and immigration:**
  - **Hispanic communities in US** = ~63 million; largest minority.
  - **Immigration debates:** Mexican border, Cuban exile, Central American refugees.
  - **Dual nationality** common (Spain, Mexico, Argentina allow).

### Adds for [5]

- **Diversity within "Hispanic" or "Latino":**
  - **National origin** matters: Mexican-American ≠ Cuban-American ≠ Puerto Rican.
  - **Indigenous, mestizo, Afro-Latino, criollo** identities.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Vocabulary

Translate to Spanish:
(a) godparent.
(b) neighborhood.
(c) high school (in Mexico).

- (a) **padrino/madrina.**
- (b) **vecindario** (or barrio).
- (c) **preparatoria** (also bachillerato).

### Example 2 [4] — Cultural

What is a quinceañera?
- **Celebration of girl's 15th birthday** marking transition to womanhood.
- **Common in Mexico, US Mexican-American communities, broader Latin America.**
- **Includes religious mass + party** (vals, padrinos, vestido).
- **Comparable to** sweet sixteen but earlier and more religiously rooted.

### Example 3 [5] — Cross-cultural

Compare family structure norms in Hispanic cultures vs typical US.
- **Hispanic:** multi-generational households common; strong extended family ties; godparent system (compadrazgo) extends "family."
- **US (typical):** more nuclear family; greater geographic dispersion; less reliance on extended family.
- **Trends:** Hispanic patterns persist but evolve with urbanization, migration.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **"Hispanic"/"Latino" not monolithic.** Many distinct cultures.
2. **Quinceañera at 15** (NOT 16).
3. **Padrinos** is plural (godparents); padrino (m), madrina (f).
4. **"Familia" includes extended family** more than English "family" often implies.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For all themes:** identify cultural products, practices, perspectives. Cite authentic sources.
**For interpersonal speaking:** demonstrate cultural sensitivity.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Las familias hispanas tradicionalmente valoran las relaciones extendidas; abuelos, tíos y primos a menudo viven cerca o forman parte de la vida cotidiana, a diferencia del modelo nuclear más común en EE.UU."
2. "La quinceañera celebra la transición de niña a mujer al cumplir 15 años; combina una misa religiosa con una fiesta tradicional, donde la quinceañera baila el vals con su padre o padrino."
3. "El compadrazgo extiende la familia más allá de los lazos de sangre; los padrinos asumen responsabilidades formales hacia los ahijados, fortaleciendo la red comunitaria."

*Master family vocabulary. Master quinceañera, padrinos. Recognize diversity within Hispanic/Latino label. Practice with authentic sources from various countries.*
