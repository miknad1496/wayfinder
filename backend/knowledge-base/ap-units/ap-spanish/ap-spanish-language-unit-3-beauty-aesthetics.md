# AP Spanish Language and Culture — Unit 3: La Belleza y la Estética — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Theme:** Beauty and Aesthetics — Beauty Concepts, Architecture, Visual and Performing Arts, Fashion, Literature

## Big Ideas

1. **Cultural concepts of beauty** vary across Spanish-speaking world.
2. **Hispanic visual arts** include Spanish masters (Velázquez, Goya, Picasso) and Latin American (Diego Rivera, Frida Kahlo).
3. **Architecture:** Moorish (Alhambra), colonial, modern (Gaudí).
4. **Music:** flamenco, mariachi, tango, salsa, reggaeton.
5. **Literature:** Cervantes, Lorca, Borges, García Márquez, Allende, Cortázar.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Vocabulary:**
  - **Las artes visuales:** pintor, escultor, museo, exposición, pintura, escultura, dibujo, retrato, paisaje, naturaleza muerta.
  - **Música:** cantante, compositor, instrumento, ritmo, melodía, género (flamenco, salsa, tango, reggaeton, mariachi).
  - **Arquitectura:** edificio, monumento, catedral, palacio, plaza, estilo (gótico, barroco, modernista).
  - **Literatura:** novela, cuento, poesía, autor, narrador, personaje.
  - **La moda:** estilo, tendencia, ropa, diseñador.
- **Major artists:**
  - **Spain:** Velázquez (Las Meninas), Goya (3 de mayo), Picasso (Guernica), Dalí (Persistencia de la memoria), Miró.
  - **Mexico:** Diego Rivera (muralism), Frida Kahlo, José Clemente Orozco, David Alfaro Siqueiros.
  - **Other Latin America:** Botero (Colombia, voluminous figures), Wifredo Lam (Cuba).
- **Architecture:**
  - **Alhambra (Granada, Spain):** Moorish.
  - **Sagrada Familia (Barcelona):** Gaudí.
  - **Colonial cathedrals** across Latin America.
- **Music genres:**
  - **Flamenco** (Spain, Andalusia): guitarra, cante, baile.
  - **Tango** (Argentina/Uruguay).
  - **Mariachi** (Mexico).
  - **Salsa** (Cuba/Puerto Rico/NYC).
  - **Reggaeton** (Puerto Rico, global).
  - **Bachata, merengue** (Dominican Republic).
- **Literature:**
  - **Don Quijote** (Cervantes, 1605/1615): foundational Spanish novel.
  - **Federico García Lorca** (Spain): poet/playwright.
  - **Boom latinoamericano** (~1960s-70s): García Márquez, Cortázar, Vargas Llosa, Fuentes.
  - **Magical realism:** García Márquez (*Cien Años de Soledad*), Allende (*Casa de los Espíritus*).

### Adds for [4]

- **Mexican muralism (Rivera, Orozco, Siqueiros):**
  - **Public art** depicting workers, indigenous peoples, history.
  - **Post-Revolution (1920s-1930s).**
- **Magical realism:**
  - **Fantastical elements treated as ordinary.**
  - **García Márquez's Macondo** in *Cien Años de Soledad*.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Spanish-language literature shaped global culture:**
  - **Cervantes' Don Quijote** = first modern novel.
  - **Latin American Boom** introduced magical realism globally.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify

For each, identify country and field:
(a) Picasso.
(b) Frida Kahlo.
(c) Gaudí.

- (a) **Spain; painting (Cubism).**
- (b) **Mexico; painting.**
- (c) **Spain (Catalonia); architecture.**

### Example 2 [4] — Music genres

Match genre to origin:
(a) Flamenco.
(b) Tango.
(c) Mariachi.
(d) Salsa.

- (a) **Spain (Andalusia).**
- (b) **Argentina/Uruguay.**
- (c) **Mexico.**
- (d) **Cuba/Puerto Rico/NYC.**

### Example 3 [5] — Magical realism

What is magical realism?
- **Genre treating fantastical elements as ordinary** (no surprise, no explanation).
- **Latin American Boom (1960s-70s)** popularized.
- **García Márquez's *Cien Años de Soledad*** classic example.
- **Reflects** Latin American reality where myth, history, dream blur.
- **Influenced global literature.**

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Picasso = Spanish** (born Málaga; spent career mostly in France).
2. **Frida Kahlo's husband Diego Rivera** — both major Mexican artists.
3. **Gaudí = Catalan modernist;** Sagrada Familia still being completed.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "El muralismo mexicano (Rivera, Orozco, Siqueiros) en los años 1920-1930 utilizaba el arte público para representar a los trabajadores, los pueblos indígenas y la historia revolucionaria, llevando el arte a las masas."
2. "El realismo mágico, perfeccionado por Gabriel García Márquez en *Cien Años de Soledad*, presenta lo fantástico como cotidiano sin sorpresa ni explicación, reflejando una realidad latinoamericana donde mito, historia y sueño se entrelazan."
3. "Don Quijote de la Mancha (Cervantes, 1605/1615) se considera la primera novela moderna; su sátira de los libros de caballerías y su exploración de la locura noble inauguraron una tradición que sigue influyendo."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Theme

*Master key artists (Picasso, Kahlo, Rivera, Goya). Master music genres by origin. Master magical realism. Master Don Quijote. Architecture: Alhambra, Sagrada Familia.*
