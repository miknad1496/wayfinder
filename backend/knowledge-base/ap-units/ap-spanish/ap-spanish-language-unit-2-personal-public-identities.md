# AP Spanish Language and Culture — Unit 2: Las Identidades Personales y Públicas — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Theme:** Personal and Public Identities — Self-Identity, National and Ethnic Identities, Beliefs, Heroes and Historical Figures
- **Where this theme appears on the exam:** Identity-themed sources across exam.

## Big Ideas

1. **Identity** is layered: personal, family, ethnic, national, religious, professional.
2. **Spanish-speaking world's identity diversity:** indigenous, mestizo, Afro-Latino, criollo, immigrant.
3. **Heroes and historical figures** — Bolívar, San Martín, José Martí, Frida Kahlo, García Márquez, Neruda.
4. **Religion** influences identity (Catholicism dominant; Indigenous religions, evangelical growth).
5. **Stereotypes** about Hispanic identity often inaccurate.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Vocabulary:**
  - **La identidad:** identidad personal, nacional, étnica, religiosa, profesional, sexual, generacional.
  - **Etnicidad:** indígena, mestizo, afrodescendiente, criollo, inmigrante.
  - **Creencias:** religión, fe, espiritualidad, cosmovisión.
  - **Personajes históricos:** héroes, líderes, libertadores, escritores, artistas.
- **Major historical figures across Spanish-speaking world:**
  - **Simón Bolívar** (Venezuela): liberator of much of South America.
  - **José de San Martín** (Argentina): liberator of Argentina, Chile, Peru.
  - **José Martí** (Cuba): poet, revolutionary, hero of Cuban independence.
  - **Eva Perón** (Argentina): political icon.
  - **Frida Kahlo** (Mexico): painter; cultural icon.
  - **Gabriel García Márquez** (Colombia): magic realism, Nobel laureate.
  - **Pablo Neruda** (Chile): poet, Nobel laureate.
  - **Octavio Paz** (Mexico): poet, essayist, Nobel laureate.
  - **Isabel Allende** (Chile): novelist.
  - **Cervantes** (Spain): *Don Quixote*.
- **Cultural identity issues:**
  - **Mestizaje:** mixing of European and indigenous (and African).
  - **Indigenismo:** valuing indigenous heritage.
  - **Identity in diaspora:** Hispanic-Americans, balancing cultures.

### Adds for [4]

- **Religious diversity:**
  - **Catholic majority** but evangelical Protestantism growing.
  - **Indigenous spiritualities** persist (Andean, Maya, etc.).
  - **Syncretism:** Santería (Cuba), Vodou (Haiti), Candomblé (Brazil).
- **National identity formation:**
  - **Independence struggles** (early 1800s) shaped identities.
  - **Symbols:** flags, national anthems, founding figures.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why "Latin American" identity contested:**
  - **Imposed by 19th-century French intellectuals** (to differentiate from Anglo-Saxon America).
  - **Many prefer specific national** or ethnic identities.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify figure

For each, identify country and contribution:
(a) Simón Bolívar.
(b) Gabriel García Márquez.
(c) Frida Kahlo.

- (a) **Venezuela; liberator of much of South America (early 1800s).**
- (b) **Colombia; Nobel laureate, magic realism (Cien Años de Soledad).**
- (c) **Mexico; painter; cultural icon.**

### Example 2 [4] — Identity layers

Describe layers of identity for a Mexican-American teenager.
- **Personal identity** (individual).
- **Family identity** (Mexican heritage).
- **Ethnic identity** (Latino/Mexican-American).
- **National identity** (American or dual).
- **Religious identity** (often Catholic).
- **Generational identity** (1st, 2nd, 3rd-gen immigrant).
- **Code-switching** between English and Spanish common.

### Example 3 [5] — Mestizaje

What is mestizaje and why is it culturally significant in Latin America?
- **Mestizaje** = mixing of European, indigenous, and African ancestries.
- **Reflects colonial history** of Spanish encounter with Americas.
- **Most Latin Americans have mixed ancestry.**
- **National identity often celebrates** mestizaje (Mexico's "raza cósmica" — Vasconcelos).
- **But masks** persistent inequalities by ethnicity.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Latin American identities ≠ Spanish identity** — distinct histories.
2. **Mestizaje's celebration** can mask real inequalities.
3. **Religious diversity growing** beyond traditional Catholic majority.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Las identidades en el mundo hispanohablante son múltiples y superpuestas: personal, familiar, étnica (indígena, mestizo, afrodescendiente, criollo), nacional, religiosa, generacional."
2. "El mestizaje — la mezcla de ascendencias europea, indígena y africana — define a gran parte de Latinoamérica; refleja la historia colonial y subyace a las identidades nacionales modernas, aunque a veces oscurece desigualdades persistentes."
3. "Figuras históricas como Simón Bolívar (libertador), José Martí (poeta-revolucionario), y Frida Kahlo (pintora) encarnan identidades nacionales y continentales que continúan moldeando el sentido de pertenencia hispanoamericano."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Theme

*Master mestizaje. Master key historical/cultural figures (Bolívar, García Márquez, Kahlo, Neruda). Master layered identity. Recognize religious diversity.*
