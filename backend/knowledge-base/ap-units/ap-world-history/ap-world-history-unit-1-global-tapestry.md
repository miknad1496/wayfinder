- **Exam weight:** 8–10% of the AP World History exam
- **Era:** 1200–1450 (paired with Unit 2 — both cover the period BEFORE European maritime expansion)
- **Sub-topics covered:**
  - 1.1 Developments in East Asia
  - 1.2 Developments in Dar al-Islam
  - 1.3 Developments in South and Southeast Asia
  - 1.4 State Building in the Americas
  - 1.5 State Building in Africa
  - 1.6 Developments in Europe

- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Unit 1 establishes the world BEFORE Western dominance — sophisticated civilizations across Asia, Africa, the Americas, and Europe. Frequent FRQ topics: comparing East Asian dynasties (Song China especially); Dar al-Islam's spread; Mali Empire and Mansa Musa; Byzantine Empire; medieval European feudalism. The period is foundational for understanding why Western dominance was NOT inevitable.

## Big Ideas

1. **Multiple sophisticated civilizations existed before European dominance.** Song/Yuan/Ming China, the Islamic Caliphates, Indian sultanates, West African empires (Mali), Mesoamerican (Aztec) and Andean (Inca) empires, Byzantine and medieval European kingdoms — each had complex governance, vibrant trade, and advanced technologies.
2. **East Asia was the most economically developed region.** Song China especially: paper money, gunpowder, compass, printing, advanced agriculture (champa rice), urbanization. China was central to Afro-Eurasian trade.
3. **Dar al-Islam stretched from Spain to India** — a vast commercial and cultural network. Islamic scholars preserved and advanced Greek, Indian, and Chinese knowledge.
4. **Africa had sophisticated states.** Mali Empire wealth (Mansa Musa's hajj 1324), Great Zimbabwe (gold and trade), Ethiopian Christian kingdom, Swahili coast trading cities.
5. **The Americas had complex societies.** Mexica (Aztec) Empire centered at Tenochtitlán, Inca Empire extending across Andes, Mississippian mound builders in North America.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **East Asia — Song China (960–1279):**
  - **Confucian-bureaucratic state:** civil service exams selected officials based on Confucian classics.
  - **Foot binding** intensified during this period.
  - **Economic innovations:** paper money, banking, urbanization (Hangzhou with ~1 million people).
  - **Agricultural revolution:** champa rice (early-ripening, drought-resistant); allowed multiple harvests per year.
  - **Technologies:** gunpowder (initially fireworks, then military), compass, movable type printing, advanced shipbuilding.
- **Yuan Dynasty (1279–1368):** Mongol-ruled China. Marco Polo visited.
- **Ming Dynasty (1368–1644):** restored ethnic Han rule. **Zheng He's voyages (1405–1433):** seven naval expeditions reaching East Africa. Demonstrated Chinese maritime capability before Europe began similar voyages.
- **Korea, Japan, Vietnam:** influenced by China but maintained distinct identities.
- **Dar al-Islam (Islamic World):**
  - **Abbasid Caliphate** (Baghdad) declined in this period; political fragmentation.
  - **Seljuk Turks** ruled much of Middle East.
  - **Mamluk Sultanate** in Egypt (1250–1517) — slave soldiers became rulers.
  - **Delhi Sultanate** (1206–1526) — Islamic rule of much of South Asia.
  - **Ibn Battuta** (1304–1369): Moroccan scholar who traveled the Islamic world for 30 years; wrote influential travel account.
  - **Islamic scholarship preserved and advanced** Greek philosophy, Indian mathematics, Chinese inventions.
- **South and Southeast Asia:**
  - **Vijayanagara Empire** (1336–1646) in southern India: Hindu state countering Muslim northern empires.
  - **Khmer Empire** (Angkor Wat, Cambodia): originally Hindu, then Buddhist.
  - **Srivijaya** (maritime trading kingdom in Indonesia).
  - **Trade between India and SE Asia** brought Indian religion and culture (Hindu and Buddhist).
- **Africa:**
  - **Mali Empire** (1230s–1670s): West Africa.
    - **Mansa Musa** (r. 1312–1337): wealthiest person of his era. **Hajj of 1324** to Mecca made Mali famous.
    - Capital Timbuktu became major center of Islamic scholarship.
    - Built on gold and salt trans-Saharan trade.
  - **Great Zimbabwe** (1100s-1450s): southern African kingdom; massive stone city; gold and ivory trade.
  - **Ethiopia:** Christian kingdom that survived expansion of Islam; Coptic Orthodox traditions.
  - **Swahili Coast:** trading cities (Kilwa, Mombasa, Zanzibar) along East African coast; mixture of Bantu and Arab cultures; participated in Indian Ocean trade.
- **The Americas:**
  - **Aztec (Mexica) Empire** (1428–1521): central Mexico.
    - Capital **Tenochtitlán** (modern Mexico City): population ~200,000+, massive markets, complex religious ceremonies.
    - Tribute system from conquered peoples.
    - Religion: Huitzilopochtli, ritual sacrifice.
  - **Inca Empire** (1400s–1530s): Andes (modern Peru).
    - Capital Cuzco. Population 12+ million.
    - Sophisticated road network (~25,000 miles).
    - **Quipu** (knotted strings) for record-keeping (no writing system).
    - Mit'a labor system.
  - **Mississippian culture** in eastern North America: large settlements (Cahokia near St. Louis), corn agriculture, mound building.
  - **Pueblo peoples** in southwestern North America: adobe villages.
- **Europe (Medieval):**
  - **Feudalism:** lord-vassal relationships with land grants in exchange for military service.
  - **Manorialism:** economic system tying peasants (serfs) to land.
  - **Catholic Church** powerful: pope, monasteries, cathedrals.
  - **Holy Roman Empire** in central Europe (loose collection of German principalities).
  - **Crusades** (1095–1291): Christian military expeditions to Holy Land. Stimulated trade, exposed Europeans to Islamic learning.
  - **Black Death** (1347–1351): bubonic plague. Killed ~30-60% of European population. Massive social, economic, and religious consequences.
  - **Hundred Years' War** (1337–1453): England vs France.
- **Byzantine Empire** (continued from earlier eras): Constantinople. Eastern Orthodox Christianity. Continued until 1453 (Constantinople fell to Ottomans).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why Song China was so advanced.**
  - **Government investment in infrastructure** (canals, roads).
  - **Civil service exams created meritocratic bureaucracy** (theoretically).
  - **Innovations in agriculture** dramatically increased food supply.
  - **Population reached 100 million by 1100** (compared to Europe's much smaller numbers).
  - **Many technologies** (printing, gunpowder, compass) preceded European versions by centuries.
- **Why Confucianism shaped Song China.**
  - **Examination system** based on Confucian classics produced literate bureaucrats.
  - **Hierarchical social order:** ruler-subject, parent-child, husband-wife.
  - **Filial piety** central virtue.
  - **Neo-Confucianism** revived under Song: synthesized with Buddhist and Daoist ideas.
- **Why Mansa Musa's hajj mattered.** His journey to Mecca (1324) showcased Mali's wealth — he gave away so much gold in Cairo that it depressed gold prices for years. Made Mali famous in Islamic world; placed on European maps for the first time. Symbolized integration of West Africa into the broader Islamic and global economy.
- **Why Tenochtitlán was a remarkable city.**
  - Built on islands in Lake Texcoco.
  - Connected to mainland by causeways.
  - Engineered chinampas (floating gardens) for agriculture.
  - Major markets (Tlatelolco) attracting tens of thousands of buyers daily.
  - Complex social hierarchy and religious ceremonies.
  - Population estimated 200,000+ — larger than any European city of the time.
- **Why feudalism is best understood as a SET of contracts.**
  - Lord granted land to vassal in exchange for military service.
  - Serfs were tied to land, owed labor and dues to lords.
  - Decentralized political authority — kings often weak.
  - Catholic Church played role of unifying European culture across political fragmentation.
- **The Black Death's transformative effects.**
  - **Demographic catastrophe:** 30-60% population loss.
  - **Economic effects:** labor shortage → higher wages for survivors; weakened serfdom.
  - **Religious effects:** questioned Church's authority; contributed to later Reformation.
  - **Cultural effects:** preoccupation with death (memento mori in art).
  - Set stage for Renaissance and early modern transformations.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why understanding the pre-1450 world is essential.** Without it, the Western dominance from 1500 onward looks inevitable. In 1400, China was the world's leading economy; the Islamic world was a major commercial and intellectual center; Africa had sophisticated empires. The shift to European dominance was not inevitable — it depended on specific historical processes that began in this period.
- **Comparative frameworks for AP World.**
  - Always think "how does this region compare to others at the same time?"
  - **Universal Evidence Bank** (per brain file): build a list of comparable examples across regions.
- **The "agricultural revolution" of Song China.** Champa rice (introduced from Vietnam) dramatically increased food production, supporting urban growth and economic development. China's agricultural productivity in 1000 CE exceeded most of Europe well into the modern era.
- **The Crusades' unintended consequences.** While militarily failures, the Crusades:
  - Increased Mediterranean trade.
  - Exposed Europeans to advanced Islamic civilization.
  - Reintroduced Greek philosophy preserved by Muslim scholars.
  - Spurred Italian city-states (Venice, Genoa) as commercial powers.
  - Indirectly contributed to Renaissance.
- **Why Indian Ocean trade is essential to understanding pre-1500 globalization.** This network connected China, Southeast Asia, India, the Middle East, and East Africa. Goods, religions (Islam, Buddhism), and ideas flowed. Swahili Coast cities, Indian merchants, Arab traders, and Chinese ships all participated. This was the original "world economy" — long before Europeans entered it.
- **Why labor systems matter for understanding states.** Mit'a (Inca), tribute systems (Aztec), serfdom (medieval Europe), enslavement of various peoples — different societies had different ways of mobilizing labor for the state and elite. These systems profoundly shaped social structure.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — DBQ-style identification

A document from c. 1300 describes a major trading city with mosques, markets, and a diverse population including Africans, Arabs, and Indians.

(a) Where might this city be?

- **Likely:** the Swahili Coast (Mombasa, Kilwa, Zanzibar) — these cities were major Indian Ocean trading hubs, Islamicized, with diverse populations.

### Example 2 [3] — Comparing pre-1450 societies

Compare Song China and Mali Empire in two ways.

- **Both:** sophisticated states with elaborate trade networks; both had specific commodities (China: silk, porcelain; Mali: gold, salt); both connected to broader trade networks.
- **Different:** Song China was Confucian/Buddhist with civil service bureaucracy; Mali was Islamic with monarchy. Song China had highly developed cities; Mali based wealth on trans-Saharan trade. Different scale: Song China's population dwarfed Mali's.

### Example 3 [4] — Mansa Musa's hajj

Why was Mansa Musa's hajj of 1324 historically significant?

- **Demonstrated Mali's enormous wealth** — gold Mansa Musa gave away in Cairo depressed gold prices for years.
- **Established Mali on the world map** — European maps subsequently included Mali as a known wealthy kingdom.
- **Strengthened Islamic ties** — Mali identified as part of the Islamic world, not just an exotic distant kingdom.
- **Brought scholars and architects to Mali** — contributed to building of Timbuktu's mosques and university.

### Example 4 [4] — Comparative analysis essay setup

Compare governmental and social structures of Aztec and Inca Empires.

- **Both:** centralized empires with sophisticated administration; large populations; tribute extraction from subject peoples.
- **Aztec (Tenochtitlán):**
  - Tribute system from conquered peoples.
  - Religion central — Huitzilopochtli, ritual sacrifice.
  - Capital on Lake Texcoco; chinampas agriculture.
  - Society hierarchical: emperor → nobles → commoners → slaves.
- **Inca (Cuzco):**
  - Mit'a labor system: rotating labor obligation from subject peoples.
  - **No writing system** but sophisticated quipu record-keeping.
  - Massive road network connecting empire.
  - Intensive agricultural terracing.
  - Society hierarchical: Sapa Inca → nobles → ayllu (commoners).

### Example 5 [5] — Sophistication move on pre-1450 civilizations

Common assumption: European exploration in 1400s opened up an unknown world. Sophisticated framing: by 1400, Afro-Eurasian trade networks already connected most of the OLD World — China, India, Africa, Europe, the Middle East — through Indian Ocean trade and the Silk Roads. Europeans were entering an existing world economy, not creating one. The "Age of Exploration" is more accurately understood as Europeans CONNECTING to a pre-existing global system AND bringing the Americas into it for the first time. The connection of the Americas was the truly novel development.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Treating European medieval feudalism as "advanced."** China, the Islamic world, and even some African states had more sophisticated political and economic systems in 1300.
2. **Assuming Mongol rule was unique.** Yuan, Ilkhanate, Golden Horde — Mongol khanates ruled large parts of Asia for over a century, integrating regions and facilitating trade.
3. **Misnaming Aztec capital.** Tenochtitlán (modern Mexico City).
4. **Confusing Aztec and Inca.** Aztec: Mexico; tribute system; Tenochtitlán. Inca: Andes (Peru); mit'a labor; Cuzco.
5. **Missing Mali's importance.** Mansa Musa's hajj (1324) is one of the most important events for understanding pre-Columbian Africa.
6. **Treating Song China as "ancient."** Song China (960-1279) was MEDIEVAL — equivalent in time to European High Middle Ages. China's economic and technological lead was contemporary, not ancient.
7. **Wrong dates for the Black Death.** 1347-1351 (peak); continued in waves.
8. **Confusing Crusades with Hundred Years' War.** Crusades (1095-1291): Christian military expeditions to Holy Land. Hundred Years' War (1337-1453): England vs France in Europe.
9. **Treating Indian Ocean trade as new in 1500.** It existed for centuries before European arrival.
10. **Confusing Mali with Songhai.** Mali (1230s-1670s, Mansa Musa). Songhai (1430s-1591, Sunni Ali). Both West African empires; sequential.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For comparative essays:**
- Compare regions on multiple axes (political, economic, cultural, religious).
- Use specific evidence (specific rulers, specific cities, specific institutions).
- Acknowledge similarities AND differences.

**For DBQ-style sourcing (HIPP):**
- **Historical situation:** what era and region?
- **Intended audience:** who was the document for?
- **Point of view:** who wrote it?
- **Purpose:** what was the author trying to do?

**For descriptive responses:**
- Use specific names (Mansa Musa, Ibn Battuta, Tenochtitlán).
- Cite specific dates and events.
- Connect specific examples to broader patterns.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Song China (960-1279) led the world in technology and economic development, with innovations including paper money, gunpowder, compass, and printing — many of which Europe would adopt centuries later."
2. "The civil service examinations of Confucian China created a meritocratic bureaucracy. Officials selected based on knowledge of Confucian classics rather than birth — though wealthy families dominated the system in practice."
3. "Mansa Musa's pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 demonstrated Mali's enormous wealth — his gifts of gold depressed Cairo's gold prices for years. The journey placed Mali on European maps and integrated West Africa into the broader Islamic world."
4. "Tenochtitlán, capital of the Aztec Empire, was one of the largest cities in the world c. 1500 — population estimated at 200,000+, with engineered floating gardens (chinampas), causeways connecting island to mainland, and massive markets."
5. "The Black Death (1347-1351) killed approximately 30-60% of Europe's population, fundamentally transforming European society — labor shortage weakened serfdom, the Catholic Church's authority was questioned, and the demographic shock contributed to long-term economic and cultural changes."
6. "Indian Ocean trade networks connected East Africa, the Middle East, India, Southeast Asia, and China through Muslim merchant networks. Goods, religions (Islam, Buddhism), and ideas flowed across this network long before European arrival."
7. "Mit'a, the labor system of the Inca Empire, required subject peoples to provide rotating labor for the state — used to build roads, agricultural terraces, monumental architecture, and military service. This was distinct from the tribute system of the Aztecs, which extracted goods and services from conquered peoples."
