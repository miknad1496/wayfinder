## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 8–10% of the AP World History exam
- **Era:** 1900-Present (paired with Units 8 and 9)
- **Sub-topics covered:**
  - 7.1 Shifting Power After 1900
  - 7.2 Causes of World War I
  - 7.3 Conducting World War I
  - 7.4 Economy in the Interwar Period
  - 7.5 Unresolved Tensions After World War I
  - 7.6 Causes of World War II
  - 7.7 Conducting World War II
  - 7.8 Mass Atrocities After 1900
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Unit 7 covers WWI, the interwar period, and WWII. The MAIN causes (Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism), total war, the Great Depression, fascism, and the Holocaust are all common topics. Comparative essays on the world wars and on totalitarian regimes are common.

## Big Ideas

1. **The world wars (1914-1918, 1939-1945) reshaped global power.** Europe's dominance ended; US and USSR emerged as superpowers; colonial empires weakened.
2. **WWI's main causes (MAIN): Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism.** Sparked by assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (June 1914).
3. **The Russian Revolution (1917) created the world's first communist state.** Led by Vladimir Lenin and the Bolsheviks. Eventually became Soviet Union (1922).
4. **The Great Depression (1929-1930s) destabilized global politics.** Enabled rise of fascism (Italy, Germany, Japan) and led to authoritarian governments worldwide.
5. **WWII (1939-1945) was the deadliest conflict in history (60-80 million dead).** Holocaust killed 6 million Jews; atomic bombings ended Pacific War; established US-USSR superpower era.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Pre-WWI tensions:**
  - **MAIN causes:**
    - **Militarism:** arms race especially between Britain and Germany.
    - **Alliances:** Triple Entente (Britain, France, Russia) vs Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy).
    - **Imperialism:** competition for colonies.
    - **Nationalism:** ethnic tensions especially in Balkans.
  - **Trigger:** **assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand** (June 28, 1914) in Sarajevo by Serbian nationalist.
  - **Cascade:** Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia → alliance system pulled in major powers.
- **WWI (1914-1918):**
  - **Allies (mostly Western):** Britain, France, Russia (until 1917), USA (joined 1917), Italy.
  - **Central Powers:** Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria.
  - **Western Front:** trench warfare (France, Belgium). Stalemate, immense casualties.
  - **Eastern Front:** more mobile; Russia eventually withdrew (1918) after revolution.
  - **Total war:** mobilization of entire societies (industrial production, propaganda, women in workforce).
  - **Russia withdraws (Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, March 1918)** after Bolshevik Revolution.
  - **USA enters (April 1917)** after German submarine warfare and Zimmerman Telegram.
  - **Armistice November 11, 1918.**
  - **Casualties:** ~17 million dead.
- **Treaty of Versailles (1919):**
  - **War guilt clause:** Germany blamed.
  - **Reparations:** massive payments demanded.
  - **Territorial losses:** Germany lost colonies, parts of European territory.
  - **Military restrictions** on Germany.
  - **League of Nations** created (US never joined).
  - **German resentment** sowed seeds of WWII.
- **Russian Revolution (1917):**
  - **February Revolution (March 1917):** Tsar abdicated; Provisional Government formed.
  - **October Revolution (November 1917):** Bolsheviks under Lenin overthrew Provisional Government.
  - **Civil War (1918-1922):** Reds (Bolsheviks) vs Whites (anti-Bolsheviks). Reds won.
  - **USSR established (1922).**
  - **Lenin died 1924; Stalin took control by 1928.**
  - **First socialist state in history.**
- **Interwar Period:**
  - **Great Depression (1929 onward):** US stock market crash → global economic collapse.
  - **Mass unemployment, deflation, social unrest.**
  - **Authoritarian governments emerged in many countries.**
- **Rise of Fascism:**
  - **Italy:** Benito Mussolini and Fascist Party (1922).
  - **Germany:** Adolf Hitler and Nazi Party (1933) — exploited resentment over Treaty of Versailles, Great Depression, anti-Semitism.
  - **Japan:** militarist government dominated by 1930s.
  - **Spain:** Francisco Franco's nationalists won Spanish Civil War (1936-1939).
- **Stalinist USSR:**
  - **Stalin (1928 onward):** ruthless dictator.
  - **Five-Year Plans:** rapid industrialization.
  - **Collectivization of agriculture:** caused millions of deaths (Ukrainian famine especially).
  - **Great Purge (1936-1938):** Stalin eliminated political rivals, military officers, intellectuals.
  - **20+ million Soviet citizens died** under Stalin's policies.
- **WWII (1939-1945):**
  - **Causes:** Treaty of Versailles resentment, Great Depression, fascist ideologies, failure of appeasement.
  - **Axis Powers:** Germany, Italy, Japan.
  - **Allied Powers:** Britain, France, USA (after Pearl Harbor 1941), USSR (after German invasion 1941), China.
  - **European theater:**
    - Germany invades Poland (Sept 1939) — war begins.
    - Germany conquers most of Western Europe (1940).
    - Battle of Britain (1940-41).
    - Germany invades USSR (June 1941).
    - D-Day Allied invasion of Normandy (June 1944).
    - Germany surrenders (May 1945).
  - **Pacific theater:**
    - Japanese expansion in China (since 1937).
    - Pearl Harbor attack on US (Dec 7, 1941).
    - Naval battles in Pacific.
    - Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki (August 1945).
    - Japan surrenders (Aug-Sept 1945).
  - **Total casualties:** 60-80 million.
  - **Holocaust:** 6 million Jews murdered by Nazis.
- **Other 20th century atrocities:**
  - **Armenian Genocide (1915):** Ottoman Empire killed 1-1.5 million Armenians.
  - **Holocaust (1941-1945):** 6 million Jews; also Roma, disabled, others.
  - **Soviet purges and famines:** millions died.
  - **Japanese atrocities** (Nanjing Massacre 1937, etc.).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why WWI's MAIN causes mattered.**
  - **Militarism:** arms races created mobilized societies prepared for war.
  - **Alliances:** rigid alliance system meant local conflict could escalate quickly.
  - **Imperialism:** colonial competition created multiple flashpoints.
  - **Nationalism:** ethnic minorities seeking independence; great powers competing for prestige.
- **Trench warfare on Western Front:**
  - Stalemate from 1914 to 1917-1918.
  - **New weapons:** machine guns, poison gas, tanks (introduced 1916), airplanes.
  - **Massive casualties** for minimal territorial gains.
  - **Battle of the Somme (1916):** ~1 million casualties combined.
- **Total war characteristics:**
  - Entire civilian population mobilized.
  - Government took control of economy.
  - Women entered workforce (sometimes for first time).
  - Propaganda campaigns.
  - Civilian targets sometimes attacked (zeppelin raids, submarine warfare).
- **Russian Revolution context:**
  - Russia had been losing WWI badly.
  - Tsar Nicholas II's incompetence.
  - Food shortages, unrest in cities.
  - February Revolution overthrew Tsar.
  - Bolsheviks promised "Peace, Land, Bread."
  - Lenin's leadership crucial.
- **Treaty of Versailles consequences:**
  - **War Guilt Clause** (Article 231): Germany solely blamed for war.
  - **Reparations:** massive financial burden.
  - **Military restrictions:** small army, no air force, no submarines.
  - **Territorial losses:** colonies, Alsace-Lorraine, Polish Corridor.
  - **German bitterness** fueled rise of Nazism in next two decades.
- **Great Depression's role:**
  - **US stock market crash** (October 1929) triggered cascading global collapse.
  - **Bank failures, unemployment, deflation.**
  - **Discredited liberal democracy** in many countries.
  - **Enabled fascist movements** — promised national revival, scapegoated minorities.
- **Why fascism appealed:**
  - Promised national strength after WWI defeat (Germany).
  - Promised economic recovery during Depression.
  - Scapegoated Jews, communists, others.
  - Strong leadership cult (Hitler, Mussolini).
  - Anti-democratic, anti-communist.
- **Holocaust:**
  - Nazi systematic murder of European Jews.
  - **6 million Jews** killed.
  - **Concentration camps** (Auschwitz, etc.).
  - Also targeted: Roma, disabled, gay people, political dissidents, Soviet POWs.
  - **Final Solution:** systematic genocide planned at Wannsee Conference (1942).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why WWI mattered globally.**
  - Ended European dominance (US and USSR emerged as superpowers).
  - Toppled four empires (German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, Ottoman).
  - Created new nations (Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Poland, etc.).
  - Set stage for WWII through Treaty of Versailles.
  - Created League of Nations (failed predecessor of UN).
- **Russian Revolution's global impact.**
  - First successful socialist revolution.
  - Inspired communist movements worldwide.
  - Cold War origins (US-USSR rivalry).
  - Eventually led to Chinese Revolution, decolonization movements.
- **Why appeasement failed.**
  - Britain and France appeased Hitler in 1930s (Munich 1938 — Czechoslovakia).
  - Believed concessions would prevent war.
  - **Encouraged Hitler** to seek more territory.
  - Lesson learned: aggressive dictators must be confronted, not appeased.
- **Holocaust's significance:**
  - Industrial-scale murder.
  - Demonstrated capacity for evil in modern industrial society.
  - Foundational for postwar human rights framework.
  - Created Israel (1948).
  - "Never again" became guiding principle.
- **Atomic bombings:**
  - Ended Pacific War (Japan surrendered after Hiroshima and Nagasaki).
  - Demonstrated US technological/military power.
  - Began nuclear age.
  - Set template for Cold War nuclear standoff.
- **Why both world wars are connected.** Treaty of Versailles → German resentment → Hitler → WWII. Also: failure of appeasement, Great Depression's destabilization, ideological conflicts (fascism vs democracy vs communism).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying WWI

What were the MAIN causes of WWI?

- **Militarism:** arms race between major powers.
- **Alliances:** Triple Entente vs Triple Alliance.
- **Imperialism:** colonial competition.
- **Nationalism:** ethnic tensions especially in Balkans.
- **Trigger:** assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (June 1914).

### Example 2 [3] — Russian Revolution

How did the Russian Revolution unfold?

- **February Revolution (March 1917):** Tsar Nicholas II abdicated.
- **Provisional Government** formed.
- **October Revolution (November 1917):** Bolsheviks under Lenin overthrew Provisional Government.
- **Civil War (1918-1922):** Reds vs Whites; Reds won.
- **USSR established (1922).**
- **Lenin died 1924; Stalin came to power by 1928.**

### Example 3 [4] — Treaty of Versailles

How did Treaty of Versailles contribute to WWII?

- **War guilt clause** blamed Germany.
- **Massive reparations** burdened German economy.
- **Territorial losses** humiliated Germany.
- **Military restrictions** limited German power.
- **German resentment** fueled Hitler's rise.
- **Combined with Great Depression**, created conditions for Nazi success.

### Example 4 [4] — Comparing fascism and communism

Compare fascism (Germany, Italy) and communism (USSR).

- **Both:** authoritarian, single-party rule, ideology, suppression of dissent.
- **Different goals:**
  - Fascism: nationalism, racism, traditional hierarchy preserved.
  - Communism: classless society, internationalism (in theory).
- **Different victims:**
  - Fascism: Jews, ethnic minorities, leftists.
  - Communism (Stalinist): kulaks (peasants), political rivals, ethnic minorities (Tatars, etc.).
- **Both** killed millions and produced totalitarian states.

### Example 5 [5] — Sophistication on WWII

Common framing: WWII was a struggle between democracy and totalitarianism. Sophisticated framing: WWII was a complex conflict involving democratic powers (US, UK), communist totalitarianism (USSR, briefly allied with democracies against fascism), and fascist totalitarianism (Germany, Italy, Japan). The democratic-totalitarian binary obscures that USSR was a totalitarian ally; that colonial empires (UK, France) were not democratic to colonized subjects; and that war crimes were committed by all sides. Postwar moral clarity (good vs evil) emerged largely from the unique horror of the Holocaust.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Misnaming WWI causes.** MAIN: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism.
2. **Wrong dates for WWI.** 1914-1918.
3. **Confusing Tsar Nicholas II and Stalin.** Tsar Nicholas II overthrown in 1917. Stalin came to power 1928.
4. **Forgetting that Russia withdrew from WWI early.** Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 1918).
5. **Treating WWII as inevitable.** Could have been prevented with different decisions in 1930s (resistance to Hitler).
6. **Wrong dates for Holocaust.** 1941-1945 (peak), though anti-Semitism began earlier.
7. **Confusing WWII military theaters.** European theater AND Pacific theater were distinct.
8. **Underestimating Soviet role in WWII.** USSR fought brutal Eastern Front against Germany; suffered 26+ million casualties.

- Address consequences (political, economic, social).
- Compare with each other.

7. "Both world wars reshaped global power: ended European dominance, toppled four empires (German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, Ottoman) in WWI, and elevated US and USSR as superpowers in WWII. The end of European colonialism began with WWII's weakening of European powers."
