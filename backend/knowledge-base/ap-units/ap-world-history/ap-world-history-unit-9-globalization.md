## Unit at a Glance

- **Era:** Late 20th-21st centuries
- **Sub-topics covered:**

  - 9.6 Globalized Culture After 1900
  - 9.7 Resistance to Globalization After 1900
  - 9.8 Institutions Developing in a Globalized World

- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Unit 9 covers the post-Cold War world and contemporary globalization. Common topics: economic globalization (NAFTA, WTO, multinational corporations), environmental issues (climate change), technological change (internet, mobile phones), responses to globalization (anti-globalization movements, religious fundamentalism).

## Big Ideas

1. **Late 20th-21st centuries saw unprecedented globalization.** Communication (internet, mobile phones), economic (multinational corporations, free trade agreements), cultural (Hollywood, K-pop, social media).
2. **Climate change emerged as global crisis.** Industrial economy depending on fossil fuels causing global warming. International agreements (Paris Accord 2015) seeking responses.
3. **Religious and cultural fundamentalism arose as response.** Iranian Islamic Revolution (1979), Hindu nationalism (India), Christian fundamentalism (US), Islamic movements globally.
4. **Economic globalization created winners and losers.** Some countries prospered (China's rise); some experienced stagnation; income inequality rose globally.
5. **International institutions tried to manage global problems.** UN, WTO, World Bank, IMF, NGOs. Mixed effectiveness.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Technology revolution:**
  - **Computers and internet (1990s+):** transformed communication, commerce, information.
  - **Mobile phones:** by 2010s, ~80% of world population.
  - **Social media:** Facebook, Twitter (now X), TikTok shaping politics, culture.
  - **Globalization of communication:** instant worldwide connectivity.
- **Economic globalization:**
  - **Multinational corporations** (Apple, Google, Walmart, Toyota, etc.) with global supply chains.
  - **Free trade agreements:** **NAFTA (1994):** US, Canada, Mexico. **WTO (1995):** global trade rules.
  - **Outsourcing and offshoring:** manufacturing moved to lower-cost countries (China, Mexico, Vietnam).
  - **Rise of China:** became world's second-largest economy by 2010s.
  - **2008 Financial Crisis:** triggered global recession; revealed global financial interconnection.
- **Climate change:**
  - **Scientific consensus** since 1990s on human-caused warming.
  - **Greenhouse gases** (CO₂, methane) trapping heat.
  - **Effects:** rising temperatures, sea level rise, extreme weather, biodiversity loss.
  - **Major polluters:** US, China, India, Russia.
  - **International agreements:** **Kyoto Protocol (1997):** US never ratified. **Paris Climate Accord (2015):** voluntary national commitments.
- **Religious and Cultural Movements:**
  - **Iranian Islamic Revolution (1979):** overthrew US-backed Shah; established Islamic Republic.
  - **Islamic fundamentalism:** Wahhabism (Saudi Arabia), Muslim Brotherhood (various), al-Qaeda, ISIS.
  - **Hindu nationalism (India):** BJP/RSS movements.
  - **Christian fundamentalism (US):** evangelical movement.
  - **Religious revival** as response to globalization, secularism.
- **Conflicts:**
  - **Israeli-Palestinian conflict:** ongoing since 1948.
  - **Persian Gulf War (1990-91):** US-led coalition expelled Iraq from Kuwait.
  - **9/11 attacks (2001):** al-Qaeda terror attacks on US; ~3,000 killed.
  - **War on Terror:** US invasion of Afghanistan (2001-2021), Iraq (2003-2011).
  - **Arab Spring (2010-2012):** popular uprisings across Middle East and North Africa. Mixed outcomes — some democratic transitions; some civil wars (Syria, Yemen, Libya).
  - **Russia's invasion of Ukraine (2022):** ongoing war, major international crisis.
- **Health and disease:**
  - **HIV/AIDS pandemic** (1980s-present): killed 40+ million globally.
  - **COVID-19 pandemic (2020-):** killed 7+ million; transformed global economy and politics.
  - **Antibiotic resistance** rising threat.
  - **Childhood vaccinations** dramatically reduced many diseases.
- **International Institutions:**
  - **United Nations (1945):** primary international body.
  - **International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Bank (1944):** Bretton Woods institutions.
  - **WTO (1995):** trade rules.
  - **NGOs:** Médecins Sans Frontières, Amnesty International, etc.
  - **G20 (1999):** major economies for global economic coordination.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why globalization matters.**
  - **Economic interconnection:** national economies deeply linked.
  - **Cultural exchange:** spread of Hollywood, McDonald's, K-pop, Bollywood, etc.
  - **Disease spread:** COVID-19 demonstrated global vulnerability.
  - **Climate change:** truly global problem requiring global cooperation.
  - **Income inequality:** globalization benefited some (Chinese workers, US tech) but not others (US manufacturing workers).
- **Why China rose so dramatically:**
  - **Deng Xiaoping's reforms** (1980s onward): "socialism with Chinese characteristics" — market reforms.
  - **Special Economic Zones** attracted foreign investment.
  - **Manufacturing exports** drove rapid growth.
  - **2001 WTO membership** integrated China into global economy.
  - **By 2010s:** world's 2nd largest economy.
- **Climate change politics:**
  - **Scientific consensus:** human-caused warming; need for emissions reductions.
  - **Political resistance:** vested interests in fossil fuels; concerns about economic costs.
  - **Developing vs developed countries:** different responsibilities and capabilities.
  - **Paris Climate Accord (2015):** voluntary national commitments. US withdrew under Trump (2017), rejoined under Biden.
- **Why religious fundamentalism arose:**
  - **Reaction to secularism, modernization.**
  - **Anti-Western backlash** in some Muslim countries.
  - **Identity preservation** in face of cultural globalization.
  - **Political mobilization** vehicle.
- **9/11 and War on Terror:**
  - Al-Qaeda attacks on US (September 11, 2001) killed ~3,000.
  - **US invasion of Afghanistan (2001):** overthrew Taliban; 20-year war until 2021 withdrawal.
  - **US invasion of Iraq (2003):** ostensibly over WMDs (none found); long occupation.
  - **Wars cost trillions of dollars; killed 100,000s of civilians; created refugee crises.**
- **Arab Spring (2010-2012):**
  - **Tunisia (December 2010):** vendor's self-immolation triggered protests.
  - Spread across Middle East/North Africa.
  - **Outcomes:**
    - Tunisia: democratic transition.
    - Egypt: Mubarak overthrown; subsequent military coup.
    - Libya: NATO intervention; Gaddafi overthrown; ongoing civil war.
    - Syria: catastrophic civil war ongoing.
    - **Mixed/disappointing outcomes overall.**

### Adds for [5]

- **Globalization's defining tensions.**
  - **Cultural homogenization vs cultural diversity preservation.**
  - **Economic integration vs national sovereignty.**
  - **Free trade vs protectionism.**
  - **Global solutions (climate, pandemic) vs national interests.**
- **The "end of history" thesis (Francis Fukuyama, 1992).** Argued that liberal democracy + market capitalism had triumphed and would be the final form of human government. Subsequent events (rise of authoritarianism, China's success without democracy, populist movements) complicated this thesis.
- **Why income inequality rose globally.**
  - **Capital became more mobile** than labor; capital extracts more rent.
  - **Technological change** rewards skilled workers more.
  - **Tax cuts** favored wealthy in many countries.
  - **Decline of unions** reduced wage pressure.
  - **Rich countries:** top 1% gains far outpaced rest.
- **Climate change's intergenerational nature.** Current actions affect future generations. Political systems struggle with such long time horizons.
- **The unfinished story.** This unit covers ongoing developments. Future historians will judge.

How did China rise as global economic power?
- **Deng Xiaoping's reforms (1980s+):** "socialism with Chinese characteristics" — market reforms within communist political system.
- **Special Economic Zones** attracted foreign investment.
- **Manufacturing exports** drove rapid growth.
- **2001 WTO membership** integrated China into global trade.

### Example 4 [4] — Religious fundamentalism

Why did religious fundamentalism rise globally in late 20th century?
- **Reaction to secularism** and modernization.
- **Identity preservation** against globalization.
- **Political mobilization** vehicle.
- **Examples:** Islamic Revolution (Iran 1979), Islamic militant movements (al-Qaeda, ISIS), Hindu nationalism (BJP in India), Christian fundamentalism (US evangelical movement).

### Example 5 [5] — Sophistication on contemporary issues

Common framing: globalization has been beneficial overall. Sophisticated framing: globalization has had complex, uneven impacts. WINNERS include: Chinese workers (lifted from poverty), US tech industry, multinational corporations, urban professionals globally. LOSERS include: many manufacturing workers in developed countries, environmental systems, traditional cultural communities. Income inequality has risen globally. Climate change disproportionately affects poorer countries. Backlash against globalization (Brexit, Trump, populism) reflects these dislocations. The story is not one of unmitigated progress but of complex transformation with both gains and losses.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Overstating consensus on globalization's benefits.** Significant losers and ongoing controversy.
2. **Treating "global" as universal.** Globalization affected different regions differently.
3. **Underestimating climate change's significance.** Among defining issues of 21st century.
4. **Wrong dates for major events.** 9/11: 2001. NAFTA: 1994. Iranian Revolution: 1979.
5. **Forgetting Arab Spring outcomes were mixed.** Tunisia: success. Most others: failure or worse.
6. **Treating COVID as ended phenomenon.** Effects continuing.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For globalization essays:**
- Address economic, technological, cultural, environmental dimensions.
- Note both benefits and costs.
- Address regional variations.

**For climate change essays:**
- Scientific consensus + political challenges.
- International agreements + national policies.
- Equity issues (developing vs developed countries).

**For DBQ-style sourcing:**
- HIPP for documents.
- Note multiple perspectives (corporations, governments, activists, ordinary people).
