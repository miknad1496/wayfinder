## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–15% of the AP World History exam
- **Era:** 1450-1750 (paired with Unit 4)
- **Sub-topics covered:**
  - 3.1 Empires Expand
  - 3.2 Empires: Administration
  - 3.3 Empires: Belief Systems
  - 3.4 Comparison in Land-Based Empires
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Unit 3 covers the great LAND-BASED EMPIRES of the early modern period: the Ottomans (Middle East/Mediterranean), Safavids (Persia), Mughals (India), Russia, Ming/Qing (China), and to some extent Habsburg Spain. Comparative essays on imperial administration are common. The "gunpowder empires" framework (Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal, Mughal) is essential.

## Big Ideas

1. **The "Gunpowder Empires" — Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal — used new military technology to build vast empires.** Cannons, muskets, and centralized professional armies enabled conquest of larger territories than ever before.
2. **Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal each represent ISLAMIC empires with distinct character.** Ottoman Sunni; Safavid Shia; Mughal Sunni ruling Hindu majority. Religious differences shaped politics.
3. **Russia under the Romanovs expanded dramatically.** From Muscovy to vast Eurasian empire including Siberia. Westernization under Peter the Great.
4. **Ming/Qing China continued and modified earlier patterns.** Ming restored Han rule (1368) after Mongol Yuan; Qing (1644) was a Manchu (non-Han) dynasty that adapted Chinese administration.
5. **All these empires faced challenges.** Religious diversity required management; succession disputes; bureaucratic corruption; ethnic tensions. Yet they were the dominant world powers in this era.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **The "Gunpowder Empires":** Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal. All three used cannons, muskets, and centralized professional armies. All three Islamic but with different sects/relationships to other religions.
- **Ottoman Empire (c. 1300-1922):**
  - **Origin:** Anatolia.
  - **Conquest of Constantinople (1453):** ended Byzantine Empire.
  - **Suleyman the Magnificent (r. 1520-1566):** peak of Ottoman power. Conquered Belgrade, Hungary; nearly besieged Vienna (1529).
  - **Devshirme system:** Ottoman recruitment of Christian boys from Balkans for the elite Janissary military and bureaucratic service. Forcibly converted to Islam.
  - **Religious tolerance** (relatively): Christians and Jews paid jizya (poll tax) but practiced their religions. Millet system organized non-Muslim communities.
  - **Decline beginning late 1600s:** military defeats, internal corruption.
- **Safavid Empire (1501-1722):**
  - **Origin:** Persia (modern Iran).
  - **Shah Ismail I** founded; established **Twelver Shia Islam** as state religion (distinguishing from Sunni Ottomans).
  - **Frequent wars** with Sunni Ottomans over religious and territorial disputes.
  - **Shah Abbas the Great (r. 1588-1629):** peak. Capital Isfahan became magnificent. Reformed military with European-style firearms.
  - **Decline early 1700s.**
- **Mughal Empire (1526-1857):**
  - **Founded:** Babur (descendant of Tamerlane and Genghis Khan), defeated Sultan of Delhi.
  - **Akbar the Great (r. 1556-1605):** religious tolerance — abolished jizya tax on Hindus, intermarried with Hindu Rajputs, established "Divine Faith" syncretism. Centralized administration.
  - **Shah Jahan (r. 1628-1658):** built Taj Mahal as tomb for wife Mumtaz Mahal.
  - **Aurangzeb (r. 1658-1707):** strict Sunni Islam, reimposed jizya, persecuted Hindus and Sikhs, aggressively expanded empire. Empire weakened by his policies.
  - **After Aurangzeb:** rapid decline. British East India Company became dominant.
- **Russia under Romanovs:**
  - **Romanov dynasty** began 1613 (after "Time of Troubles").
  - **Ivan the Terrible (r. 1547-1584):** strengthened autocracy; established serfdom.
  - **Peter the Great (r. 1682-1725):** Westernized Russia. Built navy, established St. Petersburg as new capital, modernized army, forced nobles to adopt European customs (shaved beards, Western dress).
  - **Catherine the Great (r. 1762-1796):** expanded Russia significantly (Polish partitions, conquests on Black Sea).
  - **Russia's enduring features:** Russian Orthodox Christianity, autocratic monarchy, serfdom (until 1861).
- **Ming Dynasty (1368-1644, China):**
  - **Founded** by Hongwu Emperor (overthrew Yuan/Mongol rule).
  - **Yongle Emperor:** moved capital to Beijing (built Forbidden City). Sent Zheng He on naval expeditions (1405-1433). Reaffirmed Confucian governance.
  - **Withdrawal from maritime exploration** after Zheng He: focused on internal development, agricultural production. Did not pursue overseas trade or empire.
  - **Decline late 1500s:** taxation problems, corruption, peasant rebellions.
- **Qing Dynasty (1644-1912, China):**
  - **Manchus** (from Manchuria, north of Great Wall) conquered Ming.
  - **Adapted Chinese administration:** kept Confucian bureaucracy, civil service exams, Han Chinese institutions. But maintained Manchu cultural identity.
  - **Kangxi (r. 1661-1722) and Qianlong (r. 1735-1796):** peak Qing power.
  - **Expanded territory:** absorbed Mongolia, Tibet, Xinjiang (modern western China).

### Adds for [4]

- **The "Gunpowder Empires" thesis.** Marshall Hodgson coined this term to describe Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal — three Islamic empires that used gunpowder weapons to build large empires in the early modern period. The term is somewhat contested (Russia and Qing also used gunpowder; Mughal control over key military technologies was less than Ottomans), but the framework helps compare these empires.
- **Religious management in empires (key comparison topic):**
  - **Ottoman:** Sunni state, but Christian and Jewish communities managed through millet system. Jizya tax. Devshirme recruited Christians for elite service.
  - **Safavid:** Shia state, Sunni population persecuted. Caused conflict with Sunni Ottomans.
  - **Mughal:** Sunni rulers governing Hindu majority. Akbar tolerant; Aurangzeb intolerant. Religious conflict became chronic.
  - **Russia:** Orthodox state, persecuted Old Believers (schismatics). Tolerated some Muslim subjects (Tatars).
  - **Ming/Qing:** primarily secular Confucian government. Tolerated Buddhism, Daoism. Catholic missionaries (Matteo Ricci) sometimes welcomed.
- **Why Akbar's tolerance mattered.** Mughal Empire ruled vast Hindu majority. Akbar's tolerance (abolishing jizya, intermarrying with Rajputs, syncretic religion) helped consolidate Mughal rule. Aurangzeb's later intolerance contributed to Mughal decline as Hindu (Maratha), Sikh, and other groups rebelled.
- **Why Peter the Great's Westernization mattered.** Pre-Peter, Russia was somewhat isolated, technologically behind Western Europe. Peter modernized military, built navy, established St. Petersburg as westward-facing capital. Adopted Western technologies, customs, institutions while maintaining autocracy. Set template for "modernization without democratization" later imitated by other countries.
- **Why Ming withdrawal from maritime exploration mattered.** Ming had naval capability (Zheng He's voyages) exceeding any contemporary European fleet. Decision to halt exploration around 1433 — reasons debated (cost, internal political shift toward Confucian frugality, lack of perceived benefit). Centuries later, this is often cited as a missed opportunity that allowed European maritime dominance.
- **Why Qing expansion mattered.** The Qing more than doubled the territorial size of China — adding Mongolia, Tibet, Xinjiang. Modern China's territorial extent largely reflects Qing-era conquests.

### Adds for [5]

- **The comparative framework for empires.** AP World rewards comparing across empires:
  - **Religion-state relations.**
  - **Bureaucratic structures.**
  - **Military organization.**
  - **Treatment of subject peoples.**
  - **Trade and commercial policies.**
- **Why empires faced succession problems.** No clear primogeniture (Mughal, Ottoman). Civil wars between brothers common. Devshirme recruitment somewhat solved Ottoman succession (limited royal princes, but other instabilities arose).
- **The "Western dominance" question.** In 1750, Asian empires (Mughal, Qing) were still wealthy and populous. European dominance was NOT yet established. Why did the balance shift in the 1800s?
  - Industrial Revolution (Unit 5).
  - Indigenous decline (Mughal collapse, Qing internal problems).
  - European competitive state system encouraging innovation.
  - This is a major historiographical question.
- **Why "land-based empires" framing matters.** Distinguishes from emerging European MARITIME empires (Unit 4). Land-based empires had different challenges (managing vast territories, ethnic diversity, agricultural economies); maritime empires had different opportunities (overseas conquest, trade, slavery).
- **Why all three gunpowder empires declined by 1750.**
  - **Ottoman:** military stagnation, increasing European technological advantage, internal corruption.
  - **Safavid:** sectarian conflict, weak rulers, Afghan invasion (1722).
  - **Mughal:** Aurangzeb's intolerance, religious war, British EIC ascendancy.
  - All faced challenges from rising European powers, economic shifts, and internal tensions.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying empires

For each: name the empire and a key feature.
(a) Ruled Persia, established Shia Islam as state religion.
(b) Built Taj Mahal; Akbar tolerant; Aurangzeb intolerant.
(c) Conquered Constantinople in 1453; used devshirme system.
(d) Manchu dynasty; expanded into Tibet, Xinjiang, Mongolia.

- **(a) Safavid Empire** — Persia, Shia Islam.
- **(b) Mughal Empire** — India, Taj Mahal under Shah Jahan.
- **(c) Ottoman Empire** — devshirme recruitment of Christian boys.
- **(d) Qing Dynasty** — China, Manchu rulers.

### Example 2 [4] — Comparative essay setup

Compare religious policies of Ottoman and Mughal Empires.

- **Ottoman (Sunni):** Christian and Jewish communities tolerated through millet system. Pay jizya. Children of conquered Christian families recruited to imperial service (devshirme).
- **Mughal (Sunni ruling Hindu majority):**
  - Akbar (r. 1556-1605): tolerance, abolished jizya, married Hindu princesses.
  - Aurangzeb (r. 1658-1707): reversed tolerance, reimposed jizya, persecuted Hindus and Sikhs.
- **Comparison:** both Sunni Islamic states managing religiously diverse populations. Ottoman more institutionally tolerant (millet system); Mughal varied dramatically by ruler. Both faced challenges of multi-religious empires.

### Example 3 [4] — Peter the Great's Westernization

How did Peter the Great change Russia?

- **Built modern navy** (Russia hadn't had significant maritime presence).
- **Established St. Petersburg** as new capital, facing west toward Europe.
- **Reformed military** along European lines.
- **Forced cultural changes** on nobility (shaved beards, Western dress).
- **Encouraged Western technology and education.**
- **Maintained autocracy** — modernization was top-down, not democratic.
- **Set precedent** for other rulers seeking to "catch up" to West (Japan's Meiji, Turkey's Atatürk, etc.).

### Example 4 [4] — Ming withdrawal from maritime exploration

Why might the Ming Dynasty have stopped Zheng He's voyages around 1433?

- **Cost:** voyages were enormously expensive, especially for a state focused on internal development.
- **Confucian frugality:** factions emphasizing internal infrastructure (canals, agriculture) opposed the expensive overseas voyages.
- **No perceived benefit:** Chinese had everything they needed; foreign goods provided minimal advantage.
- **Mongol threat:** military attention shifted to defending against Mongols.
- **Internal political shift:** new emperors had different priorities.
- **Historical irony:** decision is often cited as a missed opportunity. Within a century, European fleets would dominate seas China had once explored.

### Example 5 [5] — Sophistication on land-based empires

The pre-1750 world was dominated by LAND-BASED empires (Ottoman, Mughal, Qing) — not yet by European maritime empires. In 1700, Mughal India had GDP comparable to Europe; Qing China was the world's largest economy. Western dominance came later, driven by Industrial Revolution. Sophisticated framing: in 1500, Western Europeans were peripheral to the world economy; in 1900 they dominated it. Understanding this transition (the "great divergence") requires understanding both why land-based empires declined AND why Europe rose — neither of which was inevitable.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing the three Islamic gunpowder empires.** Ottoman (Sunni, Anatolia/Mediterranean). Safavid (Shia, Persia). Mughal (Sunni, India).
2. **Confusing Akbar and Aurangzeb (both Mughal).** Akbar: tolerant. Aurangzeb: intolerant. Different policies and consequences.
3. **Misattributing devshirme.** Ottoman system, not Mughal or Safavid.
4. **Wrong dates for Ottoman conquest of Constantinople.** 1453.
5. **Treating Russia as Asian.** Russia spans both continents but was European in identity (Christianity, language family). Peter pushed even more Westward orientation.
6. **Confusing Ming and Qing.** Ming (1368-1644): Han Chinese restored after Mongols. Qing (1644-1912): Manchu dynasty.
7. **Underestimating Asian empires in 1700.** Mughal India, Qing China, Ottoman empire were major world powers. European dominance came later.
8. **Treating Zheng He's voyages as exploration like Columbus.** Zheng He was on diplomatic/tribute missions — his voyages were one-time displays of Chinese might, not establishing trade routes for ongoing commerce.
9. **Confusing Suleyman and Saladin.** Saladin: 12th-century Muslim leader during Crusades. Suleyman the Magnificent: 16th-century Ottoman ruler.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For comparative empire essays:**
- Compare on multiple axes (administration, religion, military, economy).
- Use specific names (Akbar, Suleyman, Peter the Great, Kangxi).
- Acknowledge both similarities and differences.

**For DBQ-style sourcing:**
- HIPP for documents.
- Note perspective: rulers vs subjects vs foreign observers.

**For change/continuity essays:**
- Identify what stayed the same (e.g., Confucian governance in China).
- Identify what changed (e.g., Manchu vs Han ruling dynasty).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The 'Gunpowder Empires' — Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal — used new military technology (cannons, muskets) and centralized professional armies to build vast empires across the Islamic world. Each represented a distinct branch of Islam (Ottoman Sunni, Safavid Shia, Mughal Sunni ruling Hindu majority)."
2. "The Ottoman devshirme system recruited Christian boys from the Balkans, converted them to Islam, and trained them for elite military (Janissaries) and bureaucratic service. This created loyal, talented servants of the sultan who owed their position not to noble birth but to imperial favor."
3. "Akbar's policy of religious tolerance — abolishing the jizya tax on Hindus, intermarrying with Hindu Rajputs, establishing the syncretic 'Divine Faith' — helped consolidate Mughal rule over a vast Hindu majority. Aurangzeb's later reversal of these policies (reimposing jizya, persecuting Hindus and Sikhs) contributed to Mughal decline."
4. "Peter the Great's Westernization of Russia transformed the empire's military, government, and culture along European lines while maintaining autocratic rule. He established St. Petersburg as a new westward-facing capital, modernized the navy, and forced nobles to adopt European customs."
5. "Despite the Ming's earlier maritime capability — Zheng He's seven voyages (1405-1433) reached East Africa — China withdrew from oceanic exploration around 1433, focusing on internal development and defense against Mongol threats. This decision is often cited as a missed opportunity, as European maritime dominance arose in subsequent centuries."
6. "The Qing Dynasty (1644-1912) was a Manchu dynasty that conquered the Ming and adapted Chinese administration. It maintained Confucian bureaucracy and civil service exams while preserving Manchu cultural identity. Kangxi and Qianlong's reigns saw vast territorial expansion into Mongolia, Tibet, and Xinjiang — establishing modern China's territorial extent."
7. "In 1700, the Mughal, Qing, and Ottoman empires were major world powers with sophisticated economies. European maritime empires were peripheral by comparison. The shift to European dominance occurred through the Industrial Revolution and indigenous empire decline in subsequent centuries — a process not yet complete in our period."
