## Big Ideas

1. **The Enlightenment provided intellectual foundation for modern political revolutions.** Locke, Voltaire, Rousseau, Montesquieu — natural rights, social contract, consent of governed.
2. **The Atlantic Revolutions transformed politics globally.** American Revolution (1776), French Revolution (1789), Haitian Revolution (1791-1804), Latin American Revolutions (1810-1820s) — each inspired and extended Enlightenment principles.
3. **The Industrial Revolution started in Britain and spread globally.** Coal, steam, factories, railroads transformed production and society.
4. **Industrial capitalism produced both prosperity and exploitation.** Working-class poverty and reform movements (labor unions, socialism).
5. **Nationalism emerged as a powerful force.** Italian and German unifications (1860s-1870s); national independence movements in Latin America, Greece, Belgium.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **The Enlightenment (c. 1700-1800):**
  - **John Locke:** natural rights (life, liberty, property); social contract; consent of governed.
  - **Voltaire:** religious tolerance, freedom of speech.
  - **Montesquieu:** separation of powers (executive, legislative, judicial).
  - **Jean-Jacques Rousseau:** "general will," democratic republicanism.
  - **Adam Smith:** *Wealth of Nations* (1776) — laissez-faire, invisible hand of market.
- **American Revolution (1776):**
  - Thirteen British colonies declared independence (1776).
  - **Declaration of Independence:** Lockean natural rights, listed grievances against King George III.
  - **Defeated British** (with French help) by 1781.
  - **Constitution (1787)** established federal republic with separation of powers, bill of rights.
- **French Revolution (1789-1799):**
  - **Causes:** financial crisis, social inequality (Three Estates: Clergy, Nobility, Commoners), Enlightenment ideas, American Revolution example.
  - **Phases:**
    - Constitutional Monarchy (1789-1791): Declaration of Rights of Man, abolition of feudalism.
    - Republic (1792-1799): execution of Louis XVI (1793), Reign of Terror (Jacobins).
    - Napoleonic era (1799-1815): military expansion, civil code.
  - **Consequences:** spread of revolutionary ideas across Europe.
- **Haitian Revolution (1791-1804):**
  - **Slave revolt** in French colony of Saint-Domingue.
  - **Toussaint L'Ouverture** led most of revolution; later imprisoned by French.
  - **Jean-Jacques Dessalines** declared independence (1804) — first Latin American republic, first Black-led republic in Americas.
  - **Significance:** only successful slave revolt in modern history; inspired enslaved peoples across Americas.
- **Latin American Independence (1810-1820s):**
  - **Simón Bolívar:** liberated Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia.
  - **José de San Martín:** liberated Argentina, Chile, Peru.
  - **Mexican Independence (1821):** Miguel Hidalgo led initial revolt (1810).
  - **Causes:** Creole resentment of Peninsulares, Enlightenment ideas, Napoleonic occupation of Spain (1808).
- **Italian and German Unification (1860s-1871):**
  - **Italy:** Camillo di Cavour (Piedmont diplomat) and Giuseppe Garibaldi (popular leader). Unified by 1871.
  - **Germany:** Otto von Bismarck (Prussian chancellor) used wars (Danish, Austrian, French) to unite German states under Prussian leadership. German Empire proclaimed 1871.
- **The Industrial Revolution (begins c. 1760 in Britain):**
  - **Why Britain first?**
    - Coal and iron resources.
    - Agricultural productivity (enclosure movement).
    - Stable government.
    - Growing colonial markets.
    - Capital from Atlantic slave trade.
    - Scientific tradition.
  - **Key inventions:**
    - **James Watt's steam engine** (1769) — efficient, multipurpose.
    - **Spinning jenny, water frame, power loom** — textile manufacturing.
    - **Railroad** (1830s onward) — transformed transportation.
  - **Factory system:** workers gathered in factories using machines.
  - **Urbanization:** people moved to cities for factory work.
- **Spread of Industrialization:**
  - **Belgium, France, Germany, USA:** industrialized by mid-1800s.
  - **Russia, Japan:** state-led industrialization later.
  - **Other regions** (Latin America, India, China): largely de-industrialized as European factories outcompeted local production.
- **Social consequences:**
  - **Working-class poverty** in industrial cities.
  - **Child labor** widespread.
  - **Long hours** (12+ hour workdays common).
  - **Environmental pollution.**
  - **Public health crises** (cholera, etc.).
- **Reactions to industrial capitalism:**
  - **Labor unions** organizing for worker rights.
  - **Socialism:** various forms.
    - **Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels:** *Communist Manifesto* (1848), *Capital* (1867). Class struggle, eventual proletarian revolution.
  - **Reform movements:** Factory Acts (limiting child labor, hours), public health.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why the Enlightenment was revolutionary.** Challenged divine right of kings, religious authority, and rigid social hierarchy. Argued REASON could understand and improve human society.
- **Why French Revolution was so violent.**
  - Multiple competing factions (Girondins, Jacobins).
  - Foreign invasions threatened revolution.
  - Internal counter-revolutionary movements.
  - Robespierre's Reign of Terror (1793-1794) executed thousands.
  - Eventually led to Napoleon's authoritarian rule.
- **Haitian Revolution's significance.**
  - Only successful slave revolt in modern history.
  - First independent Black-led republic.
  - Inspired enslaved peoples across Americas.
  - Frightened slave-owning powers (US South, Brazil).
  - Demonstrated that Enlightenment ideals could be applied universally — including to Africans.
- **Why Latin American Revolutions came after Napoleonic occupation:**
  - Napoleon's invasion of Spain (1808) created political vacuum in colonies.
  - Creole elites used opportunity to declare independence.
  - Liberation movements led by Bolívar, San Martín, Hidalgo.
  - Result: independent republics, but social structures (large estates, racial hierarchies) often persisted.
- **Why Britain industrialized first:**
  - **Geography:** coal and iron resources.
  - **Agriculture:** enclosure movement created surplus labor; high agricultural productivity.
  - **Politics:** stable government, property rights.
  - **Economy:** capital from Atlantic slave trade and colonial commerce.
  - **Markets:** colonial empire provided raw materials and markets for goods.
  - **Science and engineering:** tradition of practical innovation.
- **Industrialization spread:**
  - **Belgium** (early 1800s): first to industrialize after Britain.
  - **France:** industrialized more slowly than Britain or Germany.
  - **Germany** (after unification 1871): rapid heavy industry growth.
  - **USA** (especially after Civil War): rapid industrialization, surpassed Britain by 1900.
  - **Russia and Japan:** state-led industrialization in late 1800s. Russia under Witte; Japan under Meiji.
- **Marxism explained:**
  - **History as class struggle** between owners and workers.
  - **Bourgeoisie** (capitalist class) exploits **proletariat** (working class).
  - **Inevitable proletarian revolution** would overthrow capitalism.
  - **Communism:** classless society.
  - Significant influence on labor movements; basis for later Russian and Chinese revolutions.
- **Other reactions to industrial capitalism:**
  - **Anarchism:** government and capital are oppressive; advocate stateless society.
  - **Christian socialism:** economic justice through religious teachings.
  - **Utopian socialism:** Robert Owen, Charles Fourier — planned communities.
  - **Liberalism:** classical (laissez-faire) and modern (regulated capitalism).
  - **Conservatism:** preserve traditional institutions against revolutionary change.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Atlantic Revolutions are connected.** Each inspired the next:
  - American Revolution → demonstrated colonial independence.
  - French Revolution → spread Enlightenment ideals across Europe.
  - Haitian Revolution → applied revolutionary ideals to slavery.
  - Latin American Revolutions → continued the pattern in different colonies.
- **The "Atlantic World" framing.** Sees these revolutions as connected events in a single Atlantic system, not separate national stories. Goods, ideas, and revolutionaries traveled across the Atlantic.
- **Why Haitian Revolution challenged conventional narratives.** It demonstrated that:
  - Enlightenment principles applied universally (not just to whites).
  - Africans were not naturally suited to slavery.
  - Slave societies were inherently unstable.
  - Many "civilized" Europeans found Haitian Revolution threatening — confirming racial hierarchies they sought to maintain.
- **The "Industrial Revolution" debate.** Some historians argue:
  - **It was less revolutionary than depicted** (more gradual transition).
  - **Multiple revolutions** in different industries at different times.
  - **Geographic specificity** matters (Britain different from continental Europe; non-European industrialization different from European).
- **Why industrialization mattered globally.** It created the FIRST modern economic divide between industrialized and non-industrialized countries. This divide:
  - Enabled European imperialism (Unit 6).
  - Created enduring inequalities still visible today.
  - Shaped modern political ideologies (capitalism, socialism, communism).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying revolutions

For each: identify and date.
(a) First successful slave revolt in modern history.
(b) Liberated Argentina, Chile, Peru.
(c) Resulted in execution of Louis XVI.
(d) Declaration of Independence based on Lockean natural rights.

- **(a) Haitian Revolution (1791-1804).**
- **(b) José de San Martín** in Latin American Independence movements.
- **(c) French Revolution (1789-1799).**
- **(d) American Revolution (1776).**

### Example 2 [3] — Industrial Revolution causes

Why did the Industrial Revolution start in Britain?

- **Coal and iron resources** for industrial production.
- **Agricultural productivity** and enclosure movement creating surplus labor.
- **Stable government** and property rights.
- **Atlantic slave trade and colonial commerce** providing capital.
- **Colonial markets** for goods, raw materials.
- **Scientific and engineering tradition.**

### Example 3 [4] — Comparing Atlantic Revolutions

Compare American and Haitian Revolutions.

- **Both:** rejected European colonial rule; declared independence.
- **American:** mostly white propertied colonists; Lockean natural rights; preserved slavery.
- **Haitian:** enslaved Africans; demanded full equality and emancipation; first successful slave revolt; first Black-led republic.
- **Significance:** Haitian Revolution applied revolutionary ideals universally; demonstrated Enlightenment principles could include Africans — challenging white supremacy.

### Example 4 [4] — Marx and labor movements

How did Marx's ideas shape responses to industrial capitalism?

- **Communist Manifesto (1848)** identified history as class struggle.
- **Capitalist class (bourgeoisie)** exploits **working class (proletariat)**.
- **Inevitable proletarian revolution** would overthrow capitalism.
- **Influenced labor movements:** organizing for worker rights, eventual revolutionary movements.
- **Practical results:** factory laws limiting child labor; public health reforms; eventually socialist parties; in 20th century, Russian and Chinese revolutions inspired by Marxism.

### Example 5 [5] — Sophistication on Industrial Revolution

Common framing: the Industrial Revolution was a sudden transformation. Sophisticated framing: industrialization was a gradual, regionally-specific process. Britain industrialized 1760-1830; Belgium and France followed; Germany after 1871; USA after Civil War. The "great divergence" between industrialized and non-industrialized countries created the modern global economic order — including the political-economic ideologies (capitalism, socialism) that shape contemporary politics. Industrialization's transformative effects unfolded over generations, not decades.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing Enlightenment thinkers.** Locke (natural rights). Voltaire (tolerance). Montesquieu (separation of powers). Rousseau (general will). Smith (laissez-faire economics).
2. **Wrong dates for French Revolution.** 1789-1799 (long form), or 1789-1815 (with Napoleonic era).
3. **Underestimating Haitian Revolution.** Often overlooked but among the most significant.
4. **Treating Latin American Revolutions as monolithic.** Different leaders, regions, outcomes.
5. **Misnaming Industrial Revolution start date.** Usually c. 1760-1830 in Britain.
6. **Forgetting non-British industrialization.** Belgium, USA, Germany, Japan all industrialized.
7. **Treating Marx as proven theory.** Marx's predictions had mixed accuracy; the historical impact (in inspiring movements) was huge regardless.
8. **Confusing Bolívar and San Martín.** Both Latin American liberators. Bolívar: northern South America (Venezuela, Colombia, Peru). San Martín: southern (Argentina, Chile, Peru).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The Enlightenment provided the intellectual foundation for the Atlantic Revolutions. John Locke's theory of natural rights and consent of the governed, Montesquieu's separation of powers, and Rousseau's social contract all influenced revolutionary documents."
2. "The Haitian Revolution (1791-1804) — led initially by Toussaint L'Ouverture and completed by Jean-Jacques Dessalines — was the only successful slave revolt in modern history. Haiti became the first independent Black-led republic, demonstrating the universal applicability of Enlightenment principles."
3. "The Industrial Revolution began in Britain c. 1760 due to coal and iron resources, agricultural productivity from the enclosure movement, stable government, capital from Atlantic slave trade and colonial commerce, and a tradition of practical innovation."
4. "Industrial capitalism produced both unprecedented prosperity and significant exploitation: child labor, 12+ hour workdays, urban poverty, and environmental degradation. Reactions included labor unions, socialist movements (Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels), and reformist legislation (Factory Acts)."
5. "Karl Marx's Communist Manifesto (1848) and Capital (1867) identified history as class struggle and predicted inevitable proletarian revolution against capitalist exploitation. Marxist ideas influenced labor movements and inspired later revolutions in Russia (1917) and China (1949)."
6. "Italian unification (1859-1871) was achieved through the diplomacy of Camillo di Cavour and the popular leadership of Giuseppe Garibaldi. German unification (1864-1871) was orchestrated by Otto von Bismarck through wars against Denmark, Austria, and France, culminating in the German Empire (1871)."
7. "The 'Atlantic Revolutions' — American (1776), French (1789), Haitian (1791-1804), and Latin American (1810-1820s) — were connected events that inspired and extended each other, applying Enlightenment principles in different contexts. Together, they transformed politics from divine-right monarchies to constitutional governments."
