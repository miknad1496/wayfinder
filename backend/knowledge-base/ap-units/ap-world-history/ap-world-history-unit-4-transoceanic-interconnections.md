## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–15% of the AP World History exam
- **Era:** 1450-1750 (paired with Unit 3)
- **Sub-topics covered:**
  - 4.1 Technological Innovations from 1450 to 1750
  - 4.2 Exploration: Causes and Events from 1450 to 1750
  - 4.3 Columbian Exchange
  - 4.4 Maritime Empires Established
  - 4.5 Maritime Empires Maintained and Developed
  - 4.6 Internal and External Challenges to State Power
  - 4.7 Changing Social Hierarchies from 1450 to 1750
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Unit 4 covers EUROPEAN MARITIME EXPANSION — the "Age of Exploration" and its consequences. The COLUMBIAN EXCHANGE is a perennial topic. Maritime empires (Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, English) and their impacts on Africa, Americas, and Asia are central. The Atlantic slave trade is essential.

## Big Ideas

1. **European maritime expansion (1450 onward) integrated the Americas with the OLD World.** Pre-1492, Afro-Eurasia was integrated; Americas isolated. After 1492, single global system began forming.
2. **The Columbian Exchange transformed both hemispheres.** Crops, animals, diseases moved both ways. Devastating Native populations (smallpox); fueling European population growth (potato).
3. **Maritime empires were different from land-based.** Smaller territorial control but massive overseas trade and colonization.
4. **The Atlantic slave trade integrated Africa into a global capitalist economy on tragic terms.** ~12 million Africans forcibly transported to Americas. Effects on Africa, Americas, Europe profound.
5. **Silver and global commerce created the first global economy.** Spanish silver from Americas flowed to China through Manila galleons. Global commerce intensified.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Technological enabling factors for European exploration:**
  - **Caravel** (Portuguese ship combining lateen and square sails) — could sail against winds.
  - **Magnetic compass** (originally Chinese, transmitted via Islamic world).
  - **Astrolabe** for celestial navigation.
  - **Improvements in cartography.**
  - **Joint-stock companies** for financing voyages.
- **Portuguese exploration (started 1400s):**
  - **Henry the Navigator** sponsored exploration of African coast.
  - **Bartolomeu Dias** rounded Cape of Good Hope (1488).
  - **Vasco da Gama** reached India (1498) — first all-water route from Europe to Asia.
  - **Pedro Cabral** reached Brazil (1500).
  - Established trading posts (factories) along African and Asian coasts.
- **Spanish exploration:**
  - **Christopher Columbus** (1492-1504): four voyages, "discovered" Caribbean for Europeans. Convinced he reached Asia.
  - **Treaty of Tordesillas (1494):** divided New World between Spain (west) and Portugal (east). Why Brazil is Portuguese; rest of Latin America is Spanish.
  - **Hernán Cortés** conquered Aztec Empire (1519-1521).
  - **Francisco Pizarro** conquered Inca Empire (1532-1533).
- **Other European exploration:**
  - **Dutch:** colonized Indonesia, parts of South Africa, briefly Brazil and New York (New Amsterdam → New York 1664).
  - **English:** colonized eastern North America (Jamestown 1607, Plymouth 1620), India (East India Company), Caribbean.
  - **French:** Canada, Louisiana, Caribbean.
- **The Columbian Exchange:**
  - **From Old to New World:** smallpox (and other diseases), wheat, sugar cane, rice, horses, cattle, pigs, sheep.
  - **From New to Old World:** corn, potatoes, tomatoes, cacao, tobacco, beans.
  - **Demographic disaster:** disease killed an estimated 80-95% of Native populations. The single greatest demographic catastrophe in history.
  - **European population growth** in subsequent centuries fueled by New World crops (especially potato).
- **Spanish colonial system in Americas:**
  - **Encomienda:** Spanish granted right to demand labor and tribute from Native peoples.
  - **Casta system:** racial hierarchy based on Spanish, Native, African mixing. Peninsulares > Creoles > Mestizos > Mulattoes > Native Americans > Africans.
  - **Silver mining** (Potosí, Bolivia; Zacatecas, Mexico) — primary economic activity.
  - **Mit'a labor** continued from Inca period for silver mines.
  - **Hacienda system:** large estates worked by Native or African labor.
- **Atlantic Slave Trade:**
  - **Approximately 12 million Africans** forcibly transported to Americas (1500-1800s).
  - **Mortality on Middle Passage:** ~10-15% died crossing Atlantic.
  - **Triangular Trade:** European manufactured goods to Africa → Africans to Americas → American sugar, tobacco, cotton to Europe.
  - **African societies:** complex impacts. Some West African states (like Dahomey) became militarily powerful through slave-raiding. Others depopulated.
  - **Plantation slavery in Americas:** sugar (Caribbean, Brazil), tobacco (Virginia), rice (South Carolina), cotton (after 1800).
- **Silver and global trade:**
  - **Spanish silver from Americas** dominated global commerce.
  - **Manila galleons** (1565-1815): Spanish ships connecting Acapulco (Mexico) to Manila (Philippines), exchanging silver for Chinese silk and porcelain.
  - **Silver flowed to China** because China demanded silver as payment for goods.
  - **Some historians argue:** China was the primary global economic power, with Spanish silver fueling its economy.
- **Joint-stock companies:**
  - **Dutch East India Company (VOC, 1602)** and **British East India Company (BEIC, 1600)**: pooled investment for risky overseas ventures.
  - Could maintain own armies, conduct diplomacy, govern territories.
  - **Foreshadowed modern corporations.**

### Adds for [4]

- **Why Europeans sought new sea routes.**
  - **Bypass Italian/Muslim middlemen** in Mediterranean spice trade.
  - **Religious motivations** (Christianize new peoples, find legendary Prester John).
  - **Wealth from Asian trade** (spices, silk, porcelain).
  - **Reconquista finished** in Spain (1492) — military energies redirected.
  - **Renaissance curiosity** about distant lands.
- **Why disease was so catastrophic for Native Americans.**
  - Old World had centuries of exposure to diseases (cattle, pigs gave humans many pathogens) — built partial immunity.
  - New World had few large domesticated animals — fewer pathogens, less immunity.
  - Smallpox, measles, influenza, typhus all devastating.
  - Estimated 80-95% Native population died from disease in century after contact.
- **The Encomienda system:**
  - Spanish granted right to demand tribute and labor from Native peoples.
  - In exchange, expected to "Christianize" them.
  - In practice: brutal exploitation, contributed to Native death rates.
  - **Bartolomé de las Casas** (Dominican priest) advocated against Encomienda's abuses, helped end it (replaced with Repartimiento, then wage labor as Native population collapsed).
- **The "Triangular Trade":**
  - **Leg 1:** Europe to Africa — manufactured goods (textiles, weapons, alcohol).
  - **Leg 2:** Africa to Americas — enslaved Africans (Middle Passage).
  - **Leg 3:** Americas to Europe — sugar, tobacco, cotton, silver.
  - **Variations:** New England rum to Africa, etc.
- **Why slavery focused on Africa.**
  - Native American populations decimated by disease.
  - European indentured servitude provided some labor but inadequate.
  - West African states' political organization made enslaving Africans through warfare possible.
  - African slavery existed before European arrival but was transformed and intensified.
  - European demand for plantation labor (sugar especially) created enormous market.
- **Casta system in Spanish America:**
  - Hierarchy based on European/Native/African mixing.
  - **Peninsulares** (Spanish-born): highest.
  - **Creoles** (Spanish parents, born in Americas): second.
  - **Mestizos** (Spanish + Native): middle.
  - **Mulattoes** (Spanish + African): lower.
  - **Native Americans** and **Africans**: lowest.
  - Tensions between Peninsulares and Creoles fueled later Latin American independence movements.
- **Resistance to colonialism:**
  - **Pueblo Revolt (1680, New Mexico):** Native uprising successfully drove out Spanish for 12 years.
  - **Maroons:** escaped slaves forming free communities.
  - **Various Native rebellions** in Spanish Americas.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why the Columbian Exchange was BIDIRECTIONAL.** Europeans brought devastation (disease) but also took (corn, potatoes, tobacco). Both hemispheres transformed. The "Atlantic World" framing emphasizes mutual exchange, not one-way "Europe affected Americas."
- **Why slavery's racialization happened in this period.** Slavery existed throughout history but was usually based on captivity, war, or status (not race). Atlantic slavery developed RACIAL bases — partly because:
  - Africans were the available labor source.
  - Religious/cultural distinctions justified differential treatment.
  - Increasing legal codification (slave codes) hardened racial categories.
  - Result: "race" became a meaningful category in ways it hadn't been historically.
- **The "Great Divergence" question.** When did Europe become richer than Asia? Some scholars (Andre Gunder Frank, Kenneth Pomeranz) argue it was relatively LATE (early 1800s). In 1750, China was still likely the world's largest economy. The Industrial Revolution (Unit 5) is the key turning point.
- **Manila galleon as world-historical event.** From 1565 to 1815, Spanish ships sailed annually between Mexico and Philippines, carrying American silver for Chinese silk. This trans-Pacific trade integrated all major economies for the first time. Foreshadowed later globalization.
- **Why the 1492 voyage matters.** Beyond bringing Americas into world trade:
  - Created the first truly global economy.
  - Began European dominance.
  - Triggered the demographic catastrophe of Native peoples.
  - Initiated transatlantic slave trade.
  - Set the stage for industrial revolution and modern world.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying explorers

For each: identify the explorer.
(a) First European to round Cape of Good Hope (1488).
(b) Reached India (1498) via all-water route.
(c) "Discovered" Americas for Europeans (1492).
(d) Conquered Aztec Empire (1519-1521).
(e) Conquered Inca Empire (1532-1533).

- **(a) Bartolomeu Dias** (Portuguese).
- **(b) Vasco da Gama** (Portuguese).
- **(c) Christopher Columbus** (sponsored by Spain).
- **(d) Hernán Cortés** (Spanish).
- **(e) Francisco Pizarro** (Spanish).

### Example 2 [3] — Columbian Exchange

What were major consequences?

- **Demographic disaster:** Native American populations dropped 80-95% due to disease (smallpox, measles, etc.).
- **European population growth:** New World crops (potato, corn) supported expansion.
- **Slavery:** demographic collapse of Native labor → Atlantic slave trade.
- **Crops:** sugar, coffee, chocolate became global commodities.
- **Animals:** horses transformed Native American Plains cultures (e.g., buffalo hunting).

### Example 3 [4] — Encomienda

Explain the Encomienda system.

- Spanish granted **encomenderos** the right to demand tribute and labor from Native peoples.
- In exchange, encomenderos were to "Christianize" Natives.
- In practice: brutal exploitation; contributed to Native population decline.
- **Bartolomé de las Casas** advocated against abuses, helped end Encomienda.
- Replaced with Repartimiento, then wage labor (as Native population collapsed and African slavery expanded).

### Example 4 [4] — Atlantic Slave Trade

How did the Atlantic slave trade work and what were its consequences?

- **Triangular Trade:** Europe → Africa (manufactured goods) → Americas (enslaved Africans) → Europe (raw materials, sugar, tobacco, cotton).
- **Middle Passage:** brutal Atlantic crossing — 10-15% mortality.
- **Total:** ~12 million Africans transported (1500-1800s).
- **Consequences:**
  - **Africa:** depopulated some regions; some states grew powerful through slave-raiding.
  - **Americas:** plantation economies (sugar, tobacco, cotton, rice) built on slave labor.
  - **Europe:** profits enriched merchants and economies.
  - **Long-term:** racial categories hardened; African diaspora created.

### Example 5 [5] — Sophistication on the Columbian Exchange

Common framing: Europeans brought disease and conquered Americas. Sophisticated framing: the Columbian Exchange was BIDIRECTIONAL — Europeans brought disease and animals to Americas, but also took crops (potato, corn, tomato) that fueled European population growth and economic transformation. Both hemispheres were dramatically changed. The "Atlantic World" framework emphasizes mutual exchange, not one-way European action. The exchange was simultaneously catastrophic (Native population collapse) and economically transformative (creating the first global economy).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Treating Columbus as discoverer of America.** Native Americans had been there for ~15,000 years. Norse Vikings reached Newfoundland c. 1000 CE. Columbus connected Americas to broader world after 1492.
2. **Confusing Spanish and Portuguese empires.** Spanish: most of Latin America (Mexico, Central/South America except Brazil). Portuguese: Brazil, parts of Africa, India (Goa), Macau.
3. **Underestimating Native population decline.** 80-95% from disease — single greatest demographic catastrophe in history.
4. **Confusing types of slavery.** Pre-Columbian African slavery existed but was different from racialized chattel slavery that emerged in Atlantic system.
5. **Wrong direction of Columbian Exchange.** Both ways. Not just Europeans bringing things to Americas.
6. **Treating slave trade as primarily transatlantic.** Africans had been enslaved within Africa and into Mediterranean for centuries. Atlantic slave trade was new in scale and racial dimension.
7. **Underestimating Asian role in global trade.** Spanish silver flowed to China — China remained major economic power.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Columbian Exchange essays:**
- Acknowledge bidirectional nature.
- Discuss specific exchanges (smallpox, potato, corn, horses).
- Address demographic, economic, cultural consequences.

**For maritime empire essays:**
- Distinguish Spanish (territorial conquest) from Portuguese (trading post empire) from Dutch/English (mixed model).
- Address relationships with Native peoples and Africans.

**For Atlantic slave trade essays:**
- Use specific numbers (12 million, 10-15% Middle Passage mortality).
- Address consequences for Africa, Americas, Europe.

**For DBQ-style sourcing:**
- HIPP for documents.
- Distinguish European sources from indigenous sources from African sources.
