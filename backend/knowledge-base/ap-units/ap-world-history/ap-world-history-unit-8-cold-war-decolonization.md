## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 8–10% of the AP World History exam
- **Era:** 1945-1991 mostly
- **Sub-topics covered:**
  - 8.1 Setting the Stage for the Cold War and Decolonization
  - 8.2 The Cold War
  - 8.3 Effects of the Cold War
  - 8.4 Spread of Communism After 1900
  - 8.5 Decolonization After 1900
  - 8.6 Newly Independent States
  - 8.7 Global Resistance to Established Power Structures After 1900
  - 8.8 End of the Cold War
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Cold War (1945-1991) + decolonization in Asia and Africa. Comparative essays on independence movements (India, Vietnam, Algeria, Ghana, etc.) common. Cuban Missile Crisis, Vietnam War, end of Soviet Union are perennial topics.

## Big Ideas

1. **The Cold War (1945-1991) divided the world into US-led capitalist bloc and Soviet-led communist bloc.** Proxy wars, nuclear arms race, ideological competition.
2. **Decolonization (1945-1980s) ended European empires.** Most African and Asian countries achieved independence. Struggles often violent.
3. **Newly independent states faced economic and political challenges.** Postcolonial economies often dependent on former colonial powers; political instability common.
4. **Communism spread beyond USSR.** China (1949), Vietnam, Cuba, Eastern European satellites — created network of socialist states.
5. **The Soviet Union collapsed (1991) ending Cold War.** Reagan-era Western pressure + Gorbachev's reforms + popular movements. End of bipolar world.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Cold War origins (1945-1949):**
  - WWII alliance of US-USSR-UK collapsed after war.
  - Soviet expansion into Eastern Europe.
  - Yalta Conference (Feb 1945) — postwar division.
  - Iron Curtain dropped across Europe (Churchill speech 1946).
  - **Truman Doctrine (1947):** US containment policy — support free peoples against communist expansion.
  - **Marshall Plan (1948):** US economic aid to rebuild Western Europe (and prevent communism).
  - **Berlin Blockade (1948-1949):** Soviet blockade; US-UK Berlin Airlift response.
  - **NATO (1949):** Western military alliance.
  - **Warsaw Pact (1955):** Soviet military alliance.
- **Communist takeover in China (1949):**
  - **Mao Zedong** led Communist Party of China (CCP).
  - Civil War with Nationalists (Kuomintang) under Chiang Kai-shek.
  - Mao victorious; established People's Republic of China (PRC).
  - Chiang fled to Taiwan.
  - China + USSR became major communist powers.
  - **Sino-Soviet Split (1950s-1960s):** rift over ideology and leadership.
- **Korean War (1950-1953):**
  - North Korea invaded South Korea.
  - UN forces (mostly US) defended South.
  - China entered when US neared Chinese border.
  - Stalemate at 38th parallel; armistice 1953.
- **Vietnam War (~1955-1975):**
  - Ho Chi Minh led communist Vietnam (formerly French colony).
  - North Vietnam (communist) vs South Vietnam (US-backed).
  - Massive US military involvement.
  - **Tet Offensive (1968):** turning point against US.
  - **US withdrawal (1973);** Saigon fell to communists (1975).
  - Vietnam reunified under communist government.
- **Cuban Revolution (1959):**
  - **Fidel Castro** overthrew US-backed dictator Batista.
  - Cuba aligned with USSR.
  - **Bay of Pigs Invasion (1961):** failed US-backed attempt to overthrow Castro.
  - **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962):** USSR placed nuclear missiles in Cuba; US imposed naval blockade. Resolved through diplomacy. Closest the world came to nuclear war.
- **Other Cold War conflicts:**
  - **Berlin Wall (built 1961, fell 1989).**
  - **Soviet invasions:** Hungary (1956), Czechoslovakia (1968).
  - **Soviet-Afghan War (1979-1989):** Soviet invasion; eventual withdrawal.
- **Decolonization in Asia:**
  - **India (1947):** independence from Britain.
    - **Mohandas Gandhi:** nonviolent resistance.
    - **Jawaharlal Nehru:** first prime minister.
    - **Partition** between Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan caused massive violence and displacement.
  - **Vietnam (1954, then 1975):** French defeated; later US war.
  - **Indonesia (1949):** independence from Netherlands.
  - **Many other Asian colonies** independent in 1940s-1950s.
- **Decolonization in Africa:**
  - **Most African colonies independent in 1950s-1960s.**
  - **Ghana (1957):** Kwame Nkrumah, first sub-Saharan African colony to gain independence.
  - **Kenya:** Mau Mau Rebellion (1952-1960) against British rule.
  - **Algeria (1962):** independence from France after brutal war.
  - **Apartheid South Africa:** institutionalized racial segregation 1948-1994. Nelson Mandela's African National Congress.
- **Decolonization in Middle East:**
  - **Israel (1948):** Jewish state established; immediate Arab-Israeli wars.
  - **Egypt:** independence 1922 (formally), Nasser's Suez Crisis (1956).
  - **Iran:** 1953 CIA-backed coup against Mossadegh; Shah ruled until 1979 Islamic Revolution.
- **Newly Independent States — Challenges:**
  - **Postcolonial economies dependent** on former colonial powers (commodities, trade).
  - **Political instability:** military coups, civil wars common.
  - **Cold War interference:** US and USSR backed competing factions.
  - **Ethnic tensions:** colonial borders often artificial.
- **Spread of Communism:**
  - **USSR-led bloc:** Eastern European satellites.
  - **China:** People's Republic established 1949.
  - **Vietnam:** unified under communism 1975.
  - **Cuba:** 1959 onward.
  - **Other:** various African and Latin American socialist movements.
- **End of Cold War:**
  - **Mikhail Gorbachev** Soviet leader (1985).
  - **Glasnost** (openness) and **Perestroika** (restructuring): attempted to reform USSR.
  - **Eastern European communist regimes collapsed (1989):** Berlin Wall fell November 9, 1989.
  - **Soviet Union dissolved (December 1991).**
  - **Reagan's military buildup** (1980s) often credited with pressuring Soviets.

### Adds for [4]

- **Containment policy:**
  - **George Kennan's "X" article (1947)** outlined containment doctrine.
  - **Goal:** prevent further Soviet expansion.
  - **Methods:** military alliances (NATO), economic aid (Marshall Plan), proxy wars, intelligence operations.
- **Why China became communist:**
  - **Long Civil War (1927-1949)** with interruption for WWII.
  - **Mao's appeal to peasants** with land reform promises.
  - **Nationalist (Kuomintang) corruption** and weakness.
  - **Massive social unrest** in war-torn China.
- **Mao's policies in China:**
  - **Great Leap Forward (1958-1962):** rapid industrialization, communal farming. Caused Great Chinese Famine — 30+ million deaths.
  - **Cultural Revolution (1966-1976):** mass political mobilization. Persecuted intellectuals and party officials. Massive social disruption.
  - **Mao died 1976; Deng Xiaoping took power**, began economic reforms ("socialism with Chinese characteristics").
- **Vietnam War causes:**
  - French colony Vietnam after WWII.
  - Ho Chi Minh declared independence (1945); fought French (1946-1954) — defeated French at Dien Bien Phu.
  - **Geneva Accords (1954)** divided Vietnam at 17th parallel.
  - North communist; South US-backed.
  - US fought to prevent communist takeover (domino theory).
  - **Tet Offensive (1968):** massive North Vietnamese attack; US/South Vietnam repelled militarily but undermined US public support.
  - **US withdrawal (1973);** Saigon fell (1975).
- **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962):**
  - USSR placed nuclear missiles in Cuba (response to US missiles in Turkey).
  - US discovered missiles; imposed naval blockade.
  - 13-day standoff; closest to nuclear war.
  - **Resolution:** USSR removed missiles; US secretly removed missiles from Turkey.
- **Indian Independence:**
  - **Gandhi's Indian National Congress** led nonviolent resistance.
  - **Salt March (1930):** symbolic protest of British salt monopoly.
  - **WWII weakened Britain;** independence inevitable.
  - **Partition (1947):** India (Hindu majority) and Pakistan (Muslim majority).
  - **Massive violence** during partition: 1+ million dead, 10+ million displaced.
  - **Gandhi assassinated (1948)** by Hindu extremist for advocating tolerance.
  - **Nehru** first prime minister.
- **Apartheid in South Africa:**
  - **Institutionalized racial segregation (1948-1994).**
  - **African National Congress (ANC)** opposition.
  - **Nelson Mandela** imprisoned (1962-1990).
  - **International sanctions** (1980s) pressured regime.
  - **Apartheid ended (1994);** Mandela elected president.
- **Why Soviet Union collapsed:**
  - **Economic stagnation** (1970s-1980s).
  - **Reagan's military buildup** strained Soviet defense spending.
  - **Soviet-Afghan War (1979-1989)** drained resources.
  - **Gorbachev's reforms** (Glasnost, Perestroika) intended to revitalize but instead accelerated collapse.
  - **Eastern European populations** rejected communist regimes (1989).
  - **Soviet republics declared independence** (1991).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why "Cold War" was cold.**
  - Both US and USSR had nuclear weapons.
  - Direct conflict would mean mutually assured destruction (MAD).
  - Used proxies (Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan, Angola, Nicaragua).
  - **Conflict was real and intense** even if rarely direct combat between US and Soviet forces.
- **Decolonization's complexity.**
  - **Successful decolonization** required postcolonial state-building.
  - **Many new states** struggled with: artificial colonial borders, ethnic conflict, economic dependence, weak institutions.
  - **Some former colonies** prospered (South Korea, Singapore); many remained poor.
  - **Continuing inequalities** (often called "neocolonialism") persisted.
- **The "Third World" framing:**
  - **First World:** US-led capitalist bloc.
  - **Second World:** Soviet-led communist bloc.
  - **Third World:** non-aligned countries (India, Egypt, Yugoslavia, etc.).
  - **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM):** founded 1955 (Bandung Conference) — sought independence from both superpowers.
- **Postcolonial neocolonialism.** Even after formal independence, former colonies often economically dependent on former colonial powers (commodity exports, foreign capital). This persisted as a pattern.
- **Why Mao's policies caused such death.**
  - Great Leap Forward's communal agriculture failed; reports of failure suppressed; famine killed ~30 million.
  - Cultural Revolution's mass political mobilization devastated education, culture, professional classes.
  - Mao's authority enabled vast policy disasters.

### Example 3 [4] — Cuban Missile Crisis analysis

Explain the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962).
- USSR placed nuclear missiles in Cuba.
- US discovered missiles; imposed naval blockade.
- 13-day standoff — world's closest approach to nuclear war.
- **Resolution:** USSR removed missiles; US secretly agreed to remove missiles from Turkey.
- **Significance:** demonstrated nuclear danger; led to direct hotline between US-USSR; led to nuclear arms control negotiations.

### Example 4 [4] — Decolonization comparison

Compare decolonization in India and Algeria.

- **India (1947):**
  - Largely nonviolent (Gandhi, Nehru).
  - British weakened by WWII.
  - Independence achieved through political pressure.
  - Bloody partition between India and Pakistan.

- **Algeria (1962):**
  - Violent struggle against France.
  - **Algerian War (1954-1962):** ~1 million Algerian dead.
  - French settlers (pieds-noirs) significant population — fought to keep Algeria French.
  - French Fourth Republic collapsed; de Gaulle came to power and granted independence.

- **Comparison:** both former European colonies achieving independence. India peaceful; Algeria violent. Both had significant complications (partition violence; French settler conflict).

### Example 5 [5] — Sophistication on Cold War

Common framing: Cold War was a struggle between democracy and totalitarianism. Sophisticated framing: Cold War was a complex bipolar conflict with:
- USSR genuinely totalitarian under Stalin and beyond.
- US allies often included undemocratic regimes (Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, various dictators).
- "Free World" rhetoric obscured US support of authoritarian regimes that opposed communism.
- **Real democracy** in the world was confined to a relatively small number of countries (Western Europe, North America, Japan, India, etc.).
- Cold War was REAL ideological conflict but its rhetoric often outpaced reality.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Wrong dates for Cold War.** Roughly 1945-1991.
2. **Confusing Korean and Vietnam Wars.** Korean (1950-1953); Vietnam (~1955-1975).
3. **Treating decolonization as monolithic.** Highly variable — from peaceful (India) to extremely violent (Algeria).
4. **Forgetting Mao's Great Famine.** 30+ million deaths from Great Leap Forward (1958-1962).
5. **Confusing causes of Cuban Missile Crisis.** USSR placed missiles RESPONDING to US missiles in Turkey.
6. **Wrong dates for Berlin Wall.** Built 1961; fell 1989.
7. **Treating Cold War as unidirectional.** Both US and USSR pursued aggressive policies.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

- Address ideological, military, economic dimensions.
- Compare specific conflicts.
- Note proxy wars in Asia, Latin America, Africa.

**For decolonization essays:**

1. "The Cold War (1945-1991) divided the world into US-led capitalist bloc and Soviet-led communist bloc. Containment policy (Truman Doctrine 1947), economic aid (Marshall Plan 1948), and military alliances (NATO 1949) shaped Western strategy."
2. "The Communist Party of China under Mao Zedong won the Chinese Civil War, establishing the People's Republic of China (1949). China became a major communist power, though the Sino-Soviet Split (1950s-1960s) divided the communist movement."
3. "The Cuban Missile Crisis (October 1962) brought the world closer to nuclear war than any other event. USSR placed nuclear missiles in Cuba; US imposed naval blockade. Resolved through diplomacy: USSR removed missiles; US secretly removed missiles from Turkey."
4. "Decolonization (1945-1980s) ended European colonial empires. Independence movements ranged from nonviolent (India under Gandhi) to violent (Algeria's war against France 1954-1962). Most African colonies achieved independence in 1950s-1960s."

6. "Mao's Great Leap Forward (1958-1962) attempted rapid industrialization through communal farming. Disastrous failure caused Great Chinese Famine — 30+ million deaths. Cultural Revolution (1966-1976) further disrupted Chinese society and economy."
7. "The Soviet Union collapsed (December 1991) ending the Cold War. Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms (Glasnost, Perestroika), Reagan-era military buildup, Eastern European popular movements (1989), and Soviet republics' independence movements combined to bring down the USSR."
