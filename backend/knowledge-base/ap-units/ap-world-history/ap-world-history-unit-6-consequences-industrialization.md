  - 6.4 Global Economic Development from 1750 to 1900

  - 6.6 Causes of Migration from 1750 to 1900

- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Unit 6 covers IMPERIALISM and its consequences. The "Scramble for Africa" (1880s-1900s), British rule of India, Opium Wars in China, Meiji Japan's modernization — all common topics. The "civilizing mission" rhetoric and Social Darwinism rationalized European dominance.

## Big Ideas

1. **Industrial dominance enabled European imperial expansion.** Industrial economies needed raw materials and markets; industrial military technology (Maxim guns, ironclad ships) overwhelmed non-industrial powers.
2. **The "New Imperialism" of 1880s-1900s saw most of Africa and much of Asia colonized.** "Scramble for Africa," British India, French Indochina, Dutch Indonesia.
3. **Imperialism was justified through racist ideologies.** "White Man's Burden" (Kipling), Social Darwinism, "civilizing mission."
4. **Indigenous resistance varied widely.** Some succeeded (Ethiopia at Battle of Adwa 1896); some defeated (most). Modernization responses (Meiji Japan).
5. **Mass migration accompanied industrialization.** ~50 million Europeans moved overseas (1840-1914). Forced migration of South Asian and Chinese laborers under indentured systems.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Industrial-era Imperialism (c. 1750-1900):**
  - **Earlier "Old Imperialism"** (1500-1750): Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, English colonies in Americas, trade outposts in Africa/Asia.
  - **"New Imperialism" (1870s-1914):** Europeans claimed most of Africa, much of Asia.
- **Rationales for Imperialism:**
  - **Economic:** raw materials, markets, investment opportunities.
  - **Strategic:** naval bases, control over trade routes.
  - **Religious:** missionary work, "civilizing mission."
  - **Nationalist:** national prestige, competition with rivals.
  - **Ideological:** Social Darwinism, racial hierarchy, Kipling's "White Man's Burden."
- **Scramble for Africa (1880s-1900s):**
  - **Berlin Conference (1884-1885):** European powers divided Africa among themselves WITHOUT African participation. Established rules: effective occupation required, free trade in Niger and Congo basins.
  - **By 1914:** virtually all of Africa colonized (only Ethiopia and Liberia remained independent).
  - **Major colonial powers:** Britain, France, Germany, Belgium, Portugal, Italy, Spain.
- **British Empire in India:**
  - **British East India Company** dominated India by mid-1800s.
  - **Sepoy Mutiny / Indian Rebellion of 1857:** Indian soldiers in EIC army revolted; brutally suppressed.
  - **British government took direct control** (1858) — "Raj."
  - **Queen Victoria** declared "Empress of India" (1877).
  - **Indian National Congress** founded (1885) — early independence movement.
- **China and Imperialism:**
  - **Opium Wars (1839-1842, 1856-1860):** Britain forced China to legalize opium import. Treaty of Nanjing (1842) opened ports, ceded Hong Kong, set tariffs.
  - **Spheres of influence:** European powers, Japan carved out exclusive economic zones.
  - **Taiping Rebellion (1850-1864):** internal rebellion against Qing; killed 20-30 million.
  - **Boxer Rebellion (1900):** anti-foreign uprising; suppressed by international force.
  - **Qing weakening continued** until republican revolution (1911-1912).
- **Japan and Modernization (Meiji Restoration 1868):**
  - **Tokugawa shogunate** had ruled Japan since 1603 (closed-door policy).
  - **Commodore Perry's "Black Ships" (1853-1854):** US naval mission forced Japan to open to trade.
  - **Meiji Restoration (1868):** revived imperial rule, modernized rapidly.
  - **Meiji reforms:** Western technology adoption, industrialization, modern military, Constitution (1889), universal education.
  - **Japan emerged as colonial power:** defeated China (Sino-Japanese War 1894-95) and Russia (Russo-Japanese War 1904-05).
- **Indigenous Resistance:**
  - **Ethiopia** at **Battle of Adwa (1896):** defeated Italian invasion. Maintained independence.
  - **Boxer Rebellion** in China (1900): anti-foreign uprising, ultimately suppressed.
  - **Indian Rebellion of 1857:** failed but expanded resistance traditions.
  - **Zulu War** (1879): Zulu defeated British at Isandlwana, lost final war.
  - **Mahdist War** in Sudan (1881-1898): Mahdi revolt, eventually crushed.
- **Economic Imperialism:**
  - Even where formally independent, many countries economically dominated by European/American capital.
  - **Latin America:** US economic dominance after independence.
  - **China:** "spheres of influence" economically controlled by foreigners.
  - **Egypt:** British control of Suez Canal (after 1869).
- **Mass Migration:**
  - **European migration to Americas:** ~50 million Europeans (1840-1914) — Irish (escaping famine), Italians, Eastern Europeans, Germans.
  - **Indian indentured servants:** ~1.5 million sent to Caribbean, Africa, Pacific colonies.
  - **Chinese coolie trade:** Chinese laborers to Americas, Southeast Asia, Australia.
  - **Push factors:** poverty, famine, political/religious persecution.
  - **Pull factors:** economic opportunity, gold rushes, available land.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why "New Imperialism" was different from earlier:**
  - **Industrial era technology:** railroads, steamships, machine guns, telegraph.
  - **Direct colonial rule** (rather than just trade outposts).
  - **Ideological justifications** (Social Darwinism, racial hierarchy).
  - **Competition between European powers** drove the rapid colonial scramble.
- **The "Scramble for Africa" mechanics:**
  - **Berlin Conference (1884-1885):** Bismarck-organized meeting of European powers.
  - **Established rules:** effective occupation needed for territory claim, free trade in Niger and Congo basins.
  - **No African representatives** — Africa carved up among Europeans.
  - **By 1914:** Ethiopia and Liberia were the only African countries not under European control.
- **British India details:**
  - **East India Company** ruled large parts of India from 1700s.
  - **Indian Rebellion of 1857** (also called Sepoy Mutiny): grievances included use of cartridges greased with cow and pig fat (offensive to Hindus and Muslims), British cultural insensitivity, peasant grievances. Brutal suppression.
  - **British Crown took direct control** (1858) — the "Raj."
  - **Indian National Congress (1885):** initially moderate; later led independence movement under Gandhi (Unit 7-8).
- **Opium Wars:**
  - **Background:** Britain's tea trade with China caused massive trade deficits — Britain wanted Chinese silver. Britain illegally exported opium from India to China to balance trade.
  - **Chinese government tried to ban opium.**
  - **First Opium War (1839-1842):** Britain victorious. Treaty of Nanjing: ceded Hong Kong, opened ports, fixed tariffs, extraterritoriality.
  - **Second Opium War (1856-1860):** further humiliations.
  - **Started "Century of Humiliation"** in Chinese historical memory.
- **Meiji Japan's transformation:**
  - **Pre-Meiji:** Tokugawa shogunate, closed-door policy.
  - **Commodore Perry's mission (1853-54)** ended isolation.
  - **Meiji Restoration (1868):** ended shogunate, restored emperor as symbolic head while modernizing oligarchs ruled.
  - **Reforms:** abolished feudalism, modernized army (universal conscription), built railroads, established public education, adopted Western legal codes.
  - **Outcome:** Japan industrialized rapidly, defeated China (1895) and Russia (1905). Became imperial power itself.
- **Why Ethiopia survived:**
  - **Menelik II** modernized Ethiopian army with European weapons.
  - **Battle of Adwa (1896):** Ethiopia defeated Italian invasion.
  - **Italian humiliation** kept Ethiopia independent (until Italian invasion in 1935 under Mussolini).
  - **Demonstrated** that European victory wasn't inevitable.
- **Migration patterns:**
  - **European to Americas:** mostly free (though pushed by poverty/famine/persecution).
  - **Indian indentured:** to British colonies (Caribbean, South Africa, Fiji, Malaya).
  - **Chinese coolie trade:** to Americas (gold mines, railroads, plantations), Southeast Asia, Australia.
  - **African slavery's end:** British abolition (1807-1834), US (1865), Brazil (1888) ended the trade.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why imperialism mattered economically.**
  - **Raw materials** (rubber, oil, minerals, palm oil, etc.) for European industries.
  - **Markets** for European manufactured goods.
  - **Investment opportunities** for European capital.
  - **Labor exploitation** in mines, plantations.
- **Why imperialism's ideological justifications mattered.**
  - **Justified European brutality** as "civilizing."
  - **Hardened racial categories** in Europe and abroad.
  - **Created lasting psychological/cultural impacts** in colonized societies.
  - **Legacies persist** in modern global racial hierarchies.
- **Why some societies modernized successfully (Japan) while others didn't (China):**
  - **Japan** had centralized state, smaller territory, willing leadership. Meiji oligarchs concentrated power, made strategic choices.
  - **China** had vast territory, decentralized state, conservative elite resistant to change. Self-Strengthening Movement was limited and incomplete.
  - **External pressures** different: China faced multiple imperial powers; Japan was less central to global trade.
- **The "developing/developed" world divide.** Industrialization created economic divide between industrialized (Europe, North America, Japan) and non-industrialized (most of the rest). Imperialism reinforced this. Postcolonial economic inequalities largely originate in this period.
- **Why migration matters globally.**
  - **Created new diasporas** (Italian, Irish in Americas; Indian and Chinese across British/colonial world).
  - **Cultural mixing.**
  - **Economic transformation** of receiving regions (Americas, Australia).
  - **Demographic transformation** of sending regions.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying imperial events

For each: identify the event.
(a) European powers divide Africa without African participation (1884-1885).
(b) British force China to legalize opium imports (1839-1842).
(c) Indian soldiers revolt against British East India Company (1857).
(d) Japan's modernization period beginning 1868.

- **(a) Berlin Conference.**
- **(b) First Opium War.**
- **(c) Sepoy Mutiny / Indian Rebellion of 1857.**
- **(d) Meiji Restoration.**

### Example 2 [3] — Rationales for imperialism

What rationales did Europeans use to justify imperialism?
- **Economic:** raw materials, markets.
- **Strategic:** naval bases.
- **Religious:** missionary work, "civilizing mission."
- **Nationalist:** national prestige, rivalry.
- **Ideological:** Social Darwinism, "White Man's Burden," racial hierarchy.

### Example 3 [4] — Comparing China and Japan

Compare China's and Japan's responses to Western pressure in the 1800s.
- **Both faced Western military pressure** (Britain in China, US in Japan).
- **China:** lost wars (Opium Wars, etc.); forced to make concessions. Self-Strengthening Movement attempted but limited modernization. Continued decline.
- **Japan:** Meiji Restoration (1868) led rapid modernization. Industrialized, built modern military. Eventually became imperial power itself.
- **Key differences:** Japan's centralized state, smaller territory, willing leadership enabled coordinated reform. China's vast territory, decentralized state, conservative elite resistance prevented similar transformation.

### Example 4 [4] — Berlin Conference analysis

Explain the Berlin Conference's role in the "Scramble for Africa."
- **Context:** by 1880s, European powers competing for African territory.
- **Bismarck convened conference** (1884-1885).
- **Established rules:** effective occupation required for claims; free trade in Niger and Congo basins.
- **No African representation** — African peoples and rulers excluded from decisions.
- **Result:** Europeans rapidly carved up Africa over next two decades.
- **By 1914:** Ethiopia and Liberia were only African nations not under European control.

### Example 5 [5] — Sophistication on imperialism's economic effects

Common framing: imperialism was driven by greed; its consequences were primarily oppression. Sophisticated framing: imperialism had multiple motivations (economic, strategic, ideological) and complex consequences. While European economies benefited (raw materials, markets), colonial subjects experienced varied outcomes — exploitation often, but also infrastructure development, education, exposure to global ideas. The legacies are deeply contested. Modern global inequality has roots in this period, but so do modern political, educational, and economic institutions in former colonies. Postcolonial scholarship emphasizes the complicated, often contradictory nature of imperialism's impacts.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Treating Old and New Imperialism the same.** Old (1500-1750): trade outposts, some settlement. New (1870s-1914): direct colonial rule, ideological justification.
2. **Wrong dates for Berlin Conference.** 1884-1885.
3. **Confusing Indian Rebellion of 1857 (mid-19th c.) with Indian Independence (1947).**
4. **Underestimating Ethiopian resistance.** Battle of Adwa (1896) is among the most significant African resistance events.
5. **Treating Meiji Japan as Western-style democracy.** It modernized institutionally but maintained centralized authority and emperor symbolism.
6. **Confusing Opium Wars (China) with other 1800s conflicts.** Opium Wars: 1839-1842 and 1856-1860.
7. **Forgetting Latin America's economic imperialism.** Even after independence, Latin America was economically dominated by US/Europe.
