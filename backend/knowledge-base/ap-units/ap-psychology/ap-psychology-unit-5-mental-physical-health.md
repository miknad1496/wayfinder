## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 15–25% of the AP Psychology exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** stress; psychological disorders (anxiety, mood, schizophrenia, dissociative, personality, neurodevelopmental); treatment of disorders (psychotherapy, biomedical).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** DSM disorders are perennial. Treatment approaches (psychodynamic, behavioral, cognitive, humanistic, biomedical) common. Stress and health connections frequent.

## Big Ideas

1. **Stress affects both mental and physical health.** Chronic stress weakens immune system, increases disease risk.
2. **Psychological disorders are diagnosed using DSM (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual).** Specific criteria for each disorder.
3. **Disorders span multiple categories:** anxiety, mood, schizophrenia, dissociative, personality, neurodevelopmental, eating, etc.
4. **Treatments include psychotherapy AND biomedical (drugs).** Often combined.
5. **Common therapies:** cognitive-behavioral (CBT), psychodynamic, humanistic, biological (medication).

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Stress:**
  - **Selye's General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS):** alarm → resistance → exhaustion.
  - **Stressors:** events causing stress.
  - **Health effects:** chronic stress → suppressed immunity, cardiovascular issues.
- **Coping strategies:**
  - **Problem-focused coping:** addressing the source.
  - **Emotion-focused coping:** managing emotional response.
- **DSM-5:** Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders. Clinical criteria for diagnosing.
- **Anxiety disorders:**
  - **Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD):** chronic worry.
  - **Panic Disorder:** sudden intense fear attacks.
  - **Phobias:** specific irrational fears.
  - **Social Anxiety Disorder.**
- **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD):** intrusive thoughts (obsessions) and repetitive behaviors (compulsions).
- **Mood disorders:**
  - **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** persistent sadness, loss of interest.
  - **Bipolar Disorder:** alternating depression and mania.
- **Schizophrenia:** disturbances in thought, perception, emotion.
  - **Positive symptoms:** hallucinations, delusions (added experiences).
  - **Negative symptoms:** flat affect, social withdrawal (subtracted experiences).
- **Dissociative disorders:**
  - **Dissociative Identity Disorder (formerly multiple personality):** different identities.
  - **Dissociative Amnesia:** memory loss.
- **Personality disorders:**
  - **Antisocial Personality Disorder:** disregard for others' rights.
  - **Borderline Personality Disorder:** unstable relationships, emotions, self-image.
  - **Narcissistic Personality Disorder.**
- **Neurodevelopmental disorders:**
  - **Autism Spectrum Disorder.**
  - **ADHD (Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder).**
- **Eating disorders:**
  - **Anorexia Nervosa:** restriction; intense fear of weight gain.
  - **Bulimia Nervosa:** binge-purge cycles.
- **Treatment approaches:**
  - **Psychodynamic:** explore unconscious, childhood experiences.
  - **Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT):** identify and change irrational thoughts and behaviors. Most empirically supported for many disorders.
  - **Humanistic:** Rogers's client-centered therapy. Unconditional positive regard.
  - **Behavioral:** classical/operant conditioning techniques. Systematic desensitization for phobias.
  - **Biomedical:** medication. Antidepressants (SSRIs), antipsychotics, anti-anxiety drugs (benzodiazepines).
- **Common medications:**
  - **SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors):** depression, anxiety. Examples: Prozac, Zoloft.
  - **Antipsychotics:** schizophrenia. Block dopamine receptors.
  - **Benzodiazepines:** anxiety. Enhance GABA. Risk of dependence.
  - **Mood stabilizers:** bipolar disorder. Lithium classic example.

### Adds for [4]

- **Diathesis-stress model:** disorders result from genetic predisposition + environmental stress.
- **Stigma:** negative attitudes toward mental illness; barrier to treatment.
- **Hierarchy of effective treatment** (per current research):
  - Combined therapy + medication often most effective for many disorders.
  - CBT among most empirically supported individual therapies.
- **Important contributors:**
  - **Aaron Beck:** founded cognitive therapy.
  - **Carl Rogers:** humanistic psychology.
- **Cognitive triad** (Beck): negative thoughts about self, world, future.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why CBT is widely used.**
  - Empirically supported for many disorders.
  - Time-limited (typically 12-20 sessions).
  - Teaches transferable skills.
  - Can be combined with medication.
- **Why understanding disorders matters.**
  - Reducing stigma.
  - Promoting evidence-based treatment.
  - Recognizing variations in human experience.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying disorders

For each scenario, identify the disorder:
(a) Person experiences sudden intense fear with rapid heartbeat, shortness of breath; not preceded by danger.
(b) Person has uncontrollable repetitive thoughts and feels compelled to wash hands repeatedly.
(c) Person alternates between depression and elevated mood/energy.

- (a) **Panic Disorder.**
- (b) **OCD (Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder).**
- (c) **Bipolar Disorder.**

### Example 2 [3] — Treatment approaches

For each scenario, suggest appropriate therapy:
(a) Person with depression and irrational thoughts.
(b) Person with phobia.
(c) Person with schizophrenia.

- (a) **CBT** (cognitive-behavioral) and possibly SSRIs.
- (b) **Behavioral therapy** (systematic desensitization) and possibly CBT.
- (c) **Antipsychotic medication** plus psychotherapy.

### Example 3 [4] — Schizophrenia symptoms

Distinguish positive and negative symptoms of schizophrenia.
- **Positive (added experiences):** hallucinations (false perceptions), delusions (false beliefs), disorganized thinking.
- **Negative (subtracted experiences):** flat affect, social withdrawal, alogia (poverty of speech).

### Example 4 [4] — Diathesis-stress model

How might depression develop?
- **Diathesis:** genetic predisposition.
- **Stress:** life event (loss, trauma).
- **Combined:** genetic vulnerability + environmental trigger leads to disorder.

### Example 5 [4][5] — Treatment effectiveness

For depression, what's the most empirically supported treatment?
- **CBT alone** is highly effective.
- **SSRIs alone** also effective.
- **Combined CBT + medication** often most effective.
- Note: not all medications work for all individuals; finding the right treatment can take trials.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing anxiety and depression.** Anxiety = excessive worry/fear. Depression = persistent sadness.
2. **Wrong schizophrenia symptoms.** Positive (added: hallucinations, delusions). Negative (subtracted: flat affect, withdrawal).
3. **Confusing OCD and OCPD.** OCD: anxiety disorder. OCPD: personality disorder (perfectionism, control).
4. **Confusing therapies.** CBT (cognitive + behavioral). Psychodynamic (unconscious). Humanistic (positive regard). Behavioral (conditioning).
5. **Treating SSRIs as cure-all.** Help many; don't help all; finding right medication often requires trials.
6. **Wrong DSM categorizations.** Different disorders fall in different categories.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For disorder identification:**
- Identify symptoms.
- Match to DSM-5 criteria.

**For treatment questions:**
- Identify approach (cognitive, behavioral, etc.).
- Address effectiveness.

**For stress questions:**
- Apply Selye's GAS framework.
- Connect to physical and mental health.
