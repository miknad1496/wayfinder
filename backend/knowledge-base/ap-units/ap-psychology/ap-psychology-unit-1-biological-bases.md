## Big Ideas

1. **Behavior has biological roots.** Genes, hormones, brain structures, neurotransmitters all shape psychological functioning.
2. **Neurons communicate via electrochemical signals** — action potentials traveling along axons, neurotransmitters bridging synapses.
3. **Different brain regions perform specialized functions** while working as integrated networks.
4. **The brain is plastic and adaptive** — capable of restructuring through experience and learning.
5. **Sensation translates physical stimuli into neural information.** Each sense follows specific transduction processes.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Genetics:** DNA codes for biological traits. Combination of nature (genes) and nurture (environment) shapes behavior.
- **Twin studies and adoption studies:** isolate genetic from environmental factors. Identical twins have identical DNA; fraternal twins share ~50%.
- **Nervous system divisions:**
  - **Central Nervous System (CNS):** brain + spinal cord.
  - **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS):**
    - **Somatic** (voluntary): controls skeletal muscles.
    - **Autonomic** (involuntary): controls organs and glands.
      - **Sympathetic** (fight or flight): arousal, accelerates heart rate, dilates pupils.
      - **Parasympathetic** (rest and digest): calming, slows heart rate, contracts pupils.
- **Neurons:** cells that transmit information.
  - **Dendrites:** receive signals.
  - **Cell body (soma):** processes signals.
  - **Axon:** sends signals.
  - **Myelin sheath:** insulates axon, speeds transmission.
  - **Terminal buttons:** release neurotransmitters.
- **Action potential:** electrical signal traveling along axon.
  - **All-or-nothing** principle: either fires or doesn't (no partial signal).
  - **Threshold:** minimum stimulation required.
  - **Refractory period:** brief recovery period after firing.
- **Neurotransmitters** (chemical messengers in synapse):
  - **Dopamine:** pleasure, motor control, reward. Excess linked to schizophrenia; deficit to Parkinson's.
  - **Serotonin:** mood, sleep, appetite. Deficit linked to depression.
  - **Norepinephrine:** arousal, alertness.
  - **GABA:** inhibitory; reduces neural activity. Deficit linked to anxiety, seizures.
  - **Glutamate:** excitatory; major neurotransmitter.
  - **Acetylcholine:** muscle action, memory. Deficit linked to Alzheimer's.
  - **Endorphins:** natural painkillers; pleasure response.
- **Drug effects on neurotransmitters:**
  - **Agonists:** mimic or boost neurotransmitter action.
  - **Antagonists:** block neurotransmitter action.
- **Brain regions (major structures):**
  - **Brainstem:** medulla (heartbeat, breathing), pons (sleep, arousal).
  - **Cerebellum:** balance, coordination.
  - **Limbic system:** emotion, memory. Includes:
    - **Hippocampus:** memory formation.
    - **Amygdala:** emotion (especially fear, aggression).
    - **Hypothalamus:** hunger, thirst, body temperature, hormones.
  - **Thalamus:** sensory relay station (except smell).
  - **Cerebral cortex** (outer layer): higher functions. Lobes:
    - **Frontal:** planning, decision-making, motor control. Prefrontal cortex: personality, judgment.
    - **Parietal:** somatosensory (touch, body position).
    - **Temporal:** auditory processing, language comprehension.
    - **Occipital:** visual processing.
- **Brain hemispheres:**
  - **Corpus callosum:** connects two hemispheres.
  - **Left hemisphere:** language (Broca's area for production, Wernicke's for comprehension).
  - **Right hemisphere:** spatial reasoning, face recognition, music.
- **Brain imaging tools:**
  - **EEG (electroencephalogram):** measures electrical brain activity.
  - **fMRI (functional MRI):** measures blood flow, real-time brain activity.
  - **PET scan:** measures metabolic activity.
- **Sleep stages:**
  - **NREM stages 1-3:** progressively deeper sleep.
  - **REM (Rapid Eye Movement):** dreaming; brain very active; muscles paralyzed.
  - **Sleep cycles:** ~90 minutes.
- **Sensation transduction:** converting physical stimuli into neural signals.
- **Vision:**
  - Light enters cornea, passes through pupil/iris, lens focuses on retina.
  - **Rods** (peripheral, dim light, no color).
  - **Cones** (central, bright light, color).
  - **Optic nerve** carries signal to brain.

### Adds for [4]

- **Famous neurotransmitter associations:**
  - Dopamine: reward, motor control. Parkinson's (deficit), schizophrenia (excess).
  - Serotonin: mood. Depression (deficit). SSRIs treat depression.
  - GABA: inhibitory. Anxiety, seizures (deficit). Alcohol affects GABA.
  - Acetylcholine: memory. Alzheimer's (deficit).
  - Endorphins: natural opioids. Heroin/morphine mimic.
- **Endocrine system** (hormones):
  - **Pituitary gland:** "master gland." Influences growth, milk production.
  - **Adrenal glands:** stress response (cortisol, adrenaline).
  - **Thyroid:** metabolism.
  - **Pancreas:** insulin (blood sugar).
- **Brain plasticity:**
  - **Neuroplasticity:** brain's ability to change throughout life.
  - **Critical periods:** windows when learning is especially effective.
  - **Recovery from injury:** brain can sometimes reroute functions.
- **Sleep deprivation effects:**
  - Cognitive impairment, mood disturbance, weakened immune system.
  - Long-term: linked to obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular issues.
- **Sleep disorders:**
  - **Insomnia:** difficulty falling/staying asleep.
  - **Narcolepsy:** sudden sleep attacks.
  - **Sleep apnea:** breathing interruptions during sleep.
  - **Night terrors:** episodes of fear during deep sleep.
- **Sensation vs perception:**
  - **Sensation:** detection of physical stimuli (eyes, ears, skin).
  - **Perception:** brain's interpretation of sensations.
- **Sensory adaptation:** decreased response to constant stimulus.
- **Perception:**
  - **Bottom-up processing:** start with sensation, build perception.
  - **Top-down processing:** use experience and context to interpret sensation.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why dopamine theory of schizophrenia matters.** First-generation antipsychotics block dopamine receptors. Treatment effectiveness suggests dopamine dysregulation is involved.
- **Why serotonin and depression: SSRIs work for some.** Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors keep more serotonin in synapse. Treatment effectiveness (modest) supports serotonin's role.
- **Brain plasticity research.** Neuroplasticity is more extensive than once thought. Adult brain capable of significant reorganization. Implications for stroke recovery, learning new skills.
- **Sensation-perception distinction.** Critical for understanding how brain CONSTRUCTS reality. Same physical input can be perceived differently depending on context, attention, expectations.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying neurotransmitter

A patient is diagnosed with Parkinson's disease, which causes motor control problems. Which neurotransmitter is most affected?
- **Dopamine.** Parkinson's involves deficit of dopamine in motor control areas.

### Example 2 [3] — Brain region function

A patient has damage to which brain region if they have difficulty:
(a) Forming new memories?
(b) Producing speech?
(c) Understanding speech?
(d) Maintaining balance?

- (a) Hippocampus.
- (b) Broca's area (frontal lobe, left hemisphere).
- (c) Wernicke's area (temporal lobe, left hemisphere).
- (d) Cerebellum.

### Example 3 [3] — Nervous system divisions

A person hears a loud bang. Their heart races, pupils dilate. Which nervous system is activated?
- **Sympathetic nervous system** (fight or flight response).

### Example 4 [4] — Sleep stages

What happens in REM sleep?
- Rapid eye movements; brain very active (similar to waking); body muscles paralyzed; vivid dreams typically occur in REM.

### Example 5 [4][5] — Brain imaging applications

For each scenario, identify the most appropriate brain imaging tool:
(a) Wanting to see real-time brain activity during a task.
(b) Needing detailed structural images of brain anatomy.
(c) Measuring electrical activity during sleep stages.

- (a) **fMRI** (functional MRI) — measures blood flow, real-time activity.
- (b) **MRI** — detailed structural images.
- (c) **EEG** (electroencephalogram) — measures electrical brain activity.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing sympathetic and parasympathetic.** Sympathetic = arousal (fight/flight). Parasympathetic = calming (rest/digest).
2. **Wrong neurotransmitter associations.** Dopamine (reward/motor). Serotonin (mood). GABA (inhibitory). Acetylcholine (memory).
3. **Confusing CNS and PNS.** CNS = brain + spinal cord. PNS = everything else.
4. **Mixing brain hemispheres.** Left hemisphere typically processes right side of body and language. Right hemisphere processes left side and spatial.
5. **Confusing sensation and perception.** Sensation = receiving stimuli. Perception = interpreting them.
6. **Wrong sleep stage descriptions.** REM has high brain activity, paralyzed body, vivid dreams.
7. **Misordering neuron parts.** Dendrites (receive) → soma (process) → axon (transmit) → terminal buttons (release neurotransmitters).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For brain region identification:**
- Match function to region.
- Use specific anatomical names.

**For neurotransmitter problems:**
- Identify symptoms.
- Connect to relevant neurotransmitter.

**For AAQ (Article Analysis Question):**
- Identify research method, hypothesis, results.
- Connect to broader psychological concepts.

**For EBQ (Evidence-Based Question):**
- Use evidence from sources.
- Construct argument.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The autonomic nervous system has two divisions: sympathetic (fight or flight, arousal) and parasympathetic (rest and digest, calming)."
2. "Neurons communicate via action potentials (electrical signals) along axons and neurotransmitters (chemical signals) at synapses."
3. "Dopamine is associated with pleasure, motor control, and reward. Parkinson's disease involves dopamine deficit; some symptoms of schizophrenia involve dopamine excess."
4. "REM sleep is characterized by rapid eye movements, high brain activity, body paralysis, and vivid dreams."
5. "The brain demonstrates plasticity throughout life — the ability to change in response to experience, learning, and injury."
