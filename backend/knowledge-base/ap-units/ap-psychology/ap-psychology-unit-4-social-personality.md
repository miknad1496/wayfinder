## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 15–25% of the AP Psychology exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** social psychology (attribution, attitudes, conformity, obedience, group dynamics, prejudice); personality theories (Freud, humanistic, trait, social-cognitive); famous studies (Milgram, Zimbardo, Asch).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Famous social psychology experiments (Milgram, Zimbardo, Asch, Festinger) referenced constantly. Personality theories and tests common. Big Five personality trait theory frequently tested.

## Big Ideas

1. **Behavior is heavily influenced by social context.** People often act differently in groups vs alone.
2. **Attribution theory** explains how we interpret others' behavior — and our biases.
3. **Conformity, obedience, and group influence** are powerful — often more than individuals realize.
4. **Personality** has multiple theoretical perspectives (psychodynamic, humanistic, trait, social-cognitive).
5. **The Big Five personality traits** (OCEAN) is the dominant modern model.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Social psychology:** how individuals are influenced by others.
- **Attribution theory:**
  - **Internal (dispositional) attribution:** behavior caused by person's traits.
  - **External (situational) attribution:** behavior caused by circumstances.
  - **Fundamental attribution error:** tendency to overattribute others' behavior to internal traits, while attributing our own behavior to circumstances.
- **Cognitive dissonance (Festinger):** discomfort from holding contradictory beliefs/behaviors. People reduce dissonance by changing attitudes.
- **Attitudes:** evaluative beliefs about people, objects, ideas.
  - **Foot-in-the-door:** small request first → larger request more likely.
  - **Door-in-the-face:** large request first → smaller request more likely.
- **Conformity (Asch):** Asch's line experiments showed people conform to group judgments even when clearly wrong.
  - **Normative influence:** conform to fit in.
  - **Informational influence:** conform because we believe others are right.
- **Obedience (Milgram):** Milgram's experiments showed people obey authority even when asked to harm others.
  - About 65% of subjects went to maximum shock level.
  - Demonstrated power of situational pressure.
- **Group dynamics:**
  - **Group polarization:** group discussion strengthens preexisting tendencies.
  - **Groupthink (Janis):** group consensus suppresses dissent. Leads to bad decisions (e.g., Bay of Pigs).
  - **Bystander effect:** people less likely to help when others present (diffusion of responsibility).
- **Stanford Prison Experiment (Zimbardo, 1971):**
  - College students role-played guards and prisoners.
  - Behavior became extreme; experiment ended early.
  - Demonstrated power of social roles. (Some critique of methodology and ethical concerns.)
- **Prejudice and discrimination:**
  - **Prejudice:** attitudes toward groups.
  - **Discrimination:** behaviors against groups.
  - **Stereotypes:** generalizations about groups.
  - **In-group bias:** favoring one's own group.
- **Personality theories:**
  - **Psychoanalytic (Freud):** unconscious drives, id-ego-superego, defense mechanisms, psychosexual stages.
  - **Humanistic (Maslow, Rogers):** focus on positive growth. Self-actualization. Maslow's hierarchy of needs.
  - **Trait theory:** consistent characteristics across situations.
    - **Big Five (OCEAN):** Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.
  - **Social-cognitive (Bandura):** reciprocal interaction of person, behavior, and environment. Self-efficacy.

### Adds for [4]

- **Maslow's hierarchy of needs:** physiological → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualization.
- **Freud's id-ego-superego:**
  - **Id:** unconscious instincts, pleasure principle.
  - **Ego:** conscious reality testing.
  - **Superego:** moral standards, conscience.
  - **Defense mechanisms:** unconscious strategies to reduce anxiety (denial, projection, displacement, etc.).
- **Self-fulfilling prophecy:** expectations influence behavior, which influences outcomes.
- **Just-world hypothesis:** people deserve what happens to them. Can blame victims.
- **Social loafing:** people put less effort in groups than alone.
- **Deindividuation:** loss of self-awareness in groups, can lead to extreme behavior.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Milgram's experiment was so disturbing.**
  - Vast majority obeyed despite distress.
  - Suggests ordinary people can do harm under authority.
  - Implications for understanding atrocities (e.g., Holocaust).
- **Why Zimbardo's prison experiment is contested.**
  - Recent reviews found Zimbardo encouraged "guard" behavior.
  - Methodological and ethical issues acknowledged.
  - Still influential as illustration of role power, but with caveats.
- **Why Big Five is dominant model.**
  - Empirically validated across cultures.
  - Better predictor than older trait theories.
  - More parsimonious than longer trait lists.
- **Critiques of Freud.** Most scientific psychology rejects much of Freudian theory due to lack of empirical support, but Freudian concepts persist culturally.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Famous studies

For each, identify the famous study/researcher:
(a) Milgram.
(b) Asch.
(c) Zimbardo.

- (a) **Milgram's obedience experiments** — about 65% delivered maximum shocks under authority.
- (b) **Asch's conformity experiments** — line judgments; people conformed to group even when clearly wrong.
- (c) **Stanford Prison Experiment** — students role-played guards/prisoners; behavior became extreme.

### Example 2 [3] — Attribution

A driver cuts you off. You think "What a jerk!"
- **Fundamental attribution error.** You attribute behavior to driver's character (internal/dispositional) while ignoring situational factors (maybe they're rushing to a hospital).

### Example 3 [3] — Group dynamics

A team of intelligent people make a poor decision because no one wants to disagree.
- **Groupthink** (Janis). Group consensus suppresses dissenting views.

### Example 4 [4] — Personality theories

Maslow's hierarchy of needs:
- Bottom: **physiological** (food, water, sleep).
- Then: **safety** (security, stability).
- Then: **love/belonging** (relationships).
- Then: **esteem** (recognition, achievement).
- Top: **self-actualization** (realizing one's potential).

### Example 5 [4][5] — Big Five (OCEAN)

What does each letter in OCEAN stand for?
- **O:** Openness (to experience).
- **C:** Conscientiousness.
- **E:** Extraversion.
- **A:** Agreeableness.
- **N:** Neuroticism.
- The Big Five is the dominant modern personality framework, validated across cultures.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing attribution types.** Internal/dispositional (traits). External/situational (circumstances).
2. **Wrong famous experiment associations.** Milgram (obedience). Asch (conformity). Zimbardo (Stanford Prison). Festinger (cognitive dissonance).
3. **Confusing Maslow's order.** Physiological → safety → love → esteem → self-actualization.
4. **Wrong Freud terminology.** Id (instincts), Ego (reality), Superego (morality).
5. **Big Five letter mistakes.** OCEAN (or CANOE).
6. **Confusing groupthink and group polarization.** Groupthink: consensus suppresses dissent. Polarization: group strengthens existing tendencies.

3. "Asch's conformity experiments showed people will conform to group judgments even when those judgments are clearly wrong, demonstrating both normative (fit in) and informational (others might be right) influence."

5. "The Big Five (OCEAN) personality traits are: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism."
