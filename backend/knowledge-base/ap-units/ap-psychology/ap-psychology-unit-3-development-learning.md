## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 15–25% of the AP Psychology exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** developmental psychology (lifespan); attachment; Piaget's cognitive development; Erikson's psychosocial stages; Vygotsky; classical conditioning (Pavlov); operant conditioning (Skinner); observational learning (Bandura); social-cognitive learning.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Major theorists (Piaget, Erikson, Vygotsky, Pavlov, Skinner, Bandura) referenced constantly. Conditioning principles applied to scenarios. Famous studies (Bobo doll experiment, etc.) common.

## Big Ideas

1. **Development is lifelong.** Multiple theorists (Piaget for cognitive, Erikson for psychosocial) describe stages.
2. **Attachment shapes early development.** Secure attachment promotes healthy development.
3. **Classical conditioning** (Pavlov): learning by association. Neutral stimulus + unconditioned stimulus → conditioned response.
4. **Operant conditioning** (Skinner): learning by consequences. Reinforcement increases behavior; punishment decreases.
5. **Observational learning** (Bandura): we learn by watching others.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Developmental psychology:** study of how people change throughout life.
- **Attachment:**
  - **Harlow's monkey studies:** baby monkeys preferred soft surrogate over food-giving wire surrogate → contact comfort matters.
  - **Ainsworth's Strange Situation:**
    - **Secure attachment:** baby explores when caregiver present; distressed when caregiver leaves; comforted on return.
    - **Anxious-resistant:** distressed when caregiver leaves; ambivalent on return.
    - **Anxious-avoidant:** little distress when caregiver leaves; ignores caregiver on return.
- **Piaget's cognitive development stages:**
  - **Sensorimotor (0-2):** explore through senses. Object permanence develops (~8 months).
  - **Preoperational (2-7):** symbolic thought, language. Egocentrism. No conservation.
  - **Concrete operational (7-11):** logical thought about concrete events. Conservation acquired.
  - **Formal operational (12+):** abstract reasoning, hypothetical thinking.
- **Vygotsky's sociocultural theory:**
  - Cognitive development through social interaction.
  - **Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD):** range of tasks too difficult alone but achievable with help.
  - **Scaffolding:** support provided to help learner master tasks.
- **Erikson's psychosocial stages (8 stages):**
  - Each stage has a CRISIS to resolve.
  - **Trust vs Mistrust** (infancy).
  - **Autonomy vs Shame and Doubt** (toddler).
  - **Initiative vs Guilt** (preschool).
  - **Industry vs Inferiority** (school age).
  - **Identity vs Role Confusion** (adolescence).
  - **Intimacy vs Isolation** (young adult).
  - **Generativity vs Stagnation** (middle adult).
  - **Integrity vs Despair** (older adult).
- **Classical conditioning (Pavlov):** learning by association.
  - **Unconditioned stimulus (UCS):** naturally triggers response (food).
  - **Unconditioned response (UCR):** natural reaction (salivation).
  - **Neutral stimulus** (NS): no natural response (bell).
  - **Conditioned stimulus (CS):** the previously neutral stimulus after pairing.
  - **Conditioned response (CR):** learned response (salivation to bell).
- **Other classical conditioning concepts:**
  - **Acquisition:** initial learning.
  - **Extinction:** weakening of CR when CS presented without UCS.
  - **Spontaneous recovery:** weak return of CR after rest period.
  - **Generalization:** similar stimuli trigger CR (dog → other animals).
  - **Discrimination:** learning to differentiate similar stimuli.
- **Operant conditioning (Skinner):** learning by consequences.
  - **Reinforcement:** INCREASES behavior.
    - **Positive reinforcement:** ADD desirable consequence (treat for sitting).
    - **Negative reinforcement:** REMOVE aversive consequence (turn off alarm by getting up).
  - **Punishment:** DECREASES behavior.
    - **Positive punishment:** ADD aversive consequence (spanking).
    - **Negative punishment:** REMOVE desirable consequence (timeout).
  - **Reinforcement schedules:**
    - **Fixed Ratio (FR):** every nth response. Produces high response rate.
    - **Variable Ratio (VR):** unpredictable number of responses. Strongest, most resistant to extinction (gambling).
    - **Fixed Interval (FI):** first response after fixed time.
    - **Variable Interval (VI):** first response after variable time. Steady response.
- **Observational learning (Bandura):**
  - Learning by watching others.
  - **Bobo doll experiment** (1961): children who watched adult attack Bobo doll were more likely to attack themselves.
  - **Modeling:** demonstration of behavior.
  - **Vicarious reinforcement:** observing others being reinforced increases own behavior.

### Adds for [4]

- **Piaget's stage of concrete operational** is when CONSERVATION is acquired:
  - Conservation: understanding that quantity remains the same despite changes in shape.
  - Pre-conservation child thinks tall thin glass holds more water than short fat glass even after seeing same water poured.
- **Critical periods:** times when brain is especially sensitive to certain types of learning. Examples: language acquisition (early childhood); attachment (infancy).
- **Theory of mind:** understanding that others have different beliefs/perspectives. Develops around age 4.
- **Adolescence:** period of identity formation (Erikson's Identity vs Role Confusion). Marcia's identity statuses: identity diffusion, foreclosure, moratorium, achievement.
- **Aging:** crystallized intelligence (knowledge) often increases; fluid intelligence (problem-solving) typically declines.
- **Operant conditioning examples:**
  - **Positive reinforcement:** dog gets treat for sitting → sits more.
  - **Negative reinforcement:** turn off alarm clock by getting up → get up faster next time.
  - **Positive punishment:** child smacked for hitting → hits less.
  - **Negative punishment:** child loses TV time for hitting → hits less.
- **Watson's Little Albert experiment:** classical conditioning with infant; showed fear could be conditioned. Ethically problematic.
- **Bandura's later work:** social-cognitive theory emphasizes COGNITIVE processes in learning (not just direct conditioning).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why developmental stages matter.**
  - Predict capabilities at different ages.
  - Inform educational practices.
  - Guide therapy approaches.
- **Vygotsky vs Piaget:**
  - Both stage theorists but emphasize different mechanisms.
  - Piaget: development driven by individual interaction with environment.
  - Vygotsky: development primarily through SOCIAL interaction.
- **Why operant conditioning is so powerful.**
  - Used in animal training, education, therapy.
  - Shapes behavior through controlled consequences.
- **Why Bandura's research expanded learning theory.**
  - Showed learning can occur without direct reinforcement.
  - Watching others (modeling) is enough.
  - Implications for media violence, advertising, social influence.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Piaget's stages

A 5-year-old believes that a tall, thin glass contains more water than a short, fat glass even when he sees the water poured between them. Which Piaget stage?
- **Preoperational** (2-7). Lacks conservation.

### Example 2 [3] — Classical conditioning

A dog learns to salivate at the sound of a bell because the bell has been paired with food. Identify the components.
- **UCS:** food.
- **UCR:** salivation (natural to food).
- **NS (initially):** bell.
- **CS (after conditioning):** bell.
- **CR:** salivation (to bell).

### Example 3 [3] — Operant conditioning

For each: identify reinforcement or punishment type:
(a) Child sits quietly during a meeting; gets a sticker.
(b) Teen breaks curfew; loses use of phone.
(c) Putting on seatbelt makes annoying alarm stop.
(d) Child bites sibling; gets a stern talking-to.

- (a) **Positive reinforcement** (added desirable consequence).
- (b) **Negative punishment** (removed desirable thing).
- (c) **Negative reinforcement** (removed aversive stimulus).
- (d) **Positive punishment** (added aversive consequence).

### Example 4 [4] — Bandura's Bobo doll

Bandura's Bobo doll experiment showed:
- Children watched adult attacking Bobo doll.
- When given access to Bobo, they imitated aggression.
- **Demonstrated:** observational learning (modeling). Children learn behaviors by watching others.

### Example 5 [4][5] — Reinforcement schedules

Why are gambling addictions so resistant to extinction?
- Gambling reinforces on **variable ratio schedule** (unpredictable wins).
- VR schedules produce strongest, most consistent behavior.
- Most resistant to extinction (people keep playing even after losing many times).
- This is why slot machines are addictive — random rewards.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing classical and operant conditioning.** Classical = association (Pavlov, dogs). Operant = consequences (Skinner, rats).
2. **Confusing types of operant conditioning:**
   - Positive REINFORCEMENT = adding desirable.
   - Negative REINFORCEMENT = removing aversive.
   - Positive PUNISHMENT = adding aversive.
   - Negative PUNISHMENT = removing desirable.
3. **Confusing UCS and CS.** Unconditioned (natural). Conditioned (learned).
4. **Wrong order of Piaget stages.** Sensorimotor (0-2) → Preoperational (2-7) → Concrete operational (7-11) → Formal operational (12+).
5. **Confusing Erikson's stages.** Each has distinct crisis.
6. **Wrong reinforcement schedule effects.** Variable ratio = strongest, most resistant.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For developmental questions:**
- Identify theorist (Piaget, Erikson, Vygotsky).
- Match age/stage.

**For conditioning questions:**
- Distinguish classical from operant.
- Identify components (UCS, CS, etc. for classical; reinforcement type for operant).

**For learning theory:**
- Identify mechanism (association, consequence, observation).
