## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 14–18% of the AP Comparative Government exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** legislative, executive, judicial branches across 6 countries; bureaucracy; head of state vs head of government; checks on power.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Comparing institutional structures across 6 countries. Strong/weak executives. Independent vs constrained judiciaries.

## Big Ideas

1. **Three branches** vary in power across countries.
2. **UK:** PM dominant; House of Commons; House of Lords (limited).
3. **Russia:** strong president; weak Duma; nominal court system.
4. **China:** CCP General Secretary; National People's Congress (rubber stamp); courts party-controlled.
5. **Mexico, Iran, Nigeria** each have unique configurations.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **United Kingdom:**
  - **Executive:** Prime Minister (head of government); Monarch (head of state, ceremonial).
  - **Legislative:** Parliament — House of Commons (elected, 650 MPs, FPTP) + House of Lords (appointed, life peers).
  - **Judiciary:** UK Supreme Court (since 2009); judicial review limited (no power to strike down acts of Parliament — parliamentary sovereignty).
  - **Cabinet:** PM + ministers from Commons.
- **Russia:**
  - **Executive:** President (head of state, dominant — Putin); Prime Minister (head of government, weak).
  - **Legislative:** Federal Assembly = State Duma (lower, elected) + Federation Council (upper).
  - **Judiciary:** Constitutional Court (formal); subordinate to executive in practice.
- **China:**
  - **Executive:** President (Xi Jinping) is also CCP General Secretary; Premier heads State Council.
  - **Legislative:** National People's Congress (~3,000 members, rubber stamps).
  - **Judiciary:** Supreme People's Court; CCP-controlled.
  - **Real power:** **Politburo Standing Committee** (~7 members).
- **Mexico:**
  - **Executive:** President (single 6-year term, no re-election).
  - **Legislative:** bicameral Congress — Senate + Chamber of Deputies.
  - **Judiciary:** Supreme Court of Justice; growing independence.
- **Iran:**
  - **Supreme Leader (Khamenei):** ultimate authority.
  - **President:** elected; runs day-to-day government.
  - **Majles:** parliament; can be dissolved by Guardian Council.
  - **Guardian Council:** vets candidates and laws.
  - **Assembly of Experts:** elects Supreme Leader.
- **Nigeria:**
  - **Executive:** President (since 1999 democracy).
  - **Legislative:** National Assembly = Senate + House of Representatives.
  - **Judiciary:** Supreme Court; growing role.

### Adds for [4]

- **Head of state vs head of government:**
  - **HoS:** ceremonial (Monarch UK; President Iran with Supreme Leader holding real power).
  - **HoG:** runs government (PM UK; President US).
  - **Combined:** US president, Mexican president.
- **Strong executives:**
  - Russia (Putin), China (Xi), Iran (Supreme Leader).
- **Weak/checked executives:**
  - UK PM (subject to Commons no-confidence), Mexican president (term limit, divided government).
- **Bicameralism:**
  - **Strong:** US-style (both chambers powerful).
  - **Weak:** UK (Lords largely advisory).
- **Bureaucracies:**
  - Often large in authoritarian states.
  - Can be source of patronage.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why institutional design matters:**
  - Constrains/enables leaders.
  - Federal systems disperse power.
  - Independent judiciaries protect rights.
- **Russia and China move toward** more authoritarian institutional arrangements (term limit removal — Russia 2020 amendment, China 2018).

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identify head of state

For each country, identify head of state and head of government:
(a) UK.
(b) Russia.
(c) Mexico.
(d) Iran.

- (a) **HoS:** Monarch (ceremonial). **HoG:** Prime Minister.
- (b) **HoS and HoG (de facto):** President.
- (c) **HoS and HoG:** President.
- (d) **HoS:** Supreme Leader (real power). President = head of government but Supreme Leader trumps.

### Example 2 [3] — UK Parliament

Compare House of Commons and House of Lords.
- **Commons:** elected (FPTP); 650 MPs; powerful (legislation, PM accountable to).
- **Lords:** appointed (life peers); ~800 members; revising chamber; can delay but not block (Parliament Acts).

### Example 3 [4] — Iran's institutions

How do elected and unelected institutions interact in Iran?
- **Supreme Leader (unelected):** controls military, judiciary, key policy.
- **President (elected):** runs day-to-day government.
- **Majles (elected):** legislates, but laws vetted by Guardian Council.
- **Guardian Council (12 members, half appointed by Supreme Leader, half by Chief Justice):** vets candidates AND legislation.
- **Result:** elections occur but within tight constraints.

### Example 4 [4] — Strong vs weak executives

Compare strong (Russia) and weak (Mexico) executives.
- **Russian President:** dominates legislature; controls media; long tenure (Putin since 1999, intermittently).
- **Mexican President:** single 6-year term, no reelection; faces divided Congress; institutional checks.

### Example 5 [5] — Chinese power

Where does real power lie in China?
- **CCP, not state institutions.**
- **Politburo Standing Committee** (~7 members) makes key decisions.
- **General Secretary of CCP** (Xi Jinping) is paramount leader.
- **State institutions** (President, NPC) are formally separate but CCP-controlled.
- **Xi Jinping:** General Secretary + President + Chairman of Central Military Commission.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **UK has no judicial review** of parliamentary acts (parliamentary sovereignty).
2. **Iran's Supreme Leader** trumps elected president.
3. **China's CCP** is real power; state institutions formally separate.
4. **Russia's "elected"** institutions exist but heavily managed.
5. **Mexico's "no reelection"** historic principle — president cannot run again.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For institutional comparison:** identify executive, legislative, judicial structures.
**For power analysis:** identify formal vs informal authority.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The UK is a parliamentary democracy: the Prime Minister (head of government) is drawn from the majority party in the House of Commons; the Monarch is the ceremonial head of state. Parliamentary sovereignty means courts cannot strike down acts of Parliament."
2. "Russia's President (Putin) dominates a semi-presidential system, with a weak Prime Minister and a State Duma controlled by United Russia. Constitutional amendments in 2020 enabled Putin to potentially serve until 2036."
3. "China's real power resides in the Chinese Communist Party, particularly the Politburo Standing Committee (~7 members). State institutions (President, National People's Congress) are formally separate but CCP-controlled. Xi Jinping holds CCP General Secretary, President, and Central Military Commission chair simultaneously."
4. "Iran's hybrid theocracy combines elected institutions (President, Majles) with unelected religious authority (Supreme Leader, Guardian Council). The Guardian Council vets candidates and legislation; the Supreme Leader holds ultimate authority."
5. "Mexican presidents serve a single non-renewable six-year term (sexenio), a deliberate constraint on executive power inherited from the 1917 Constitution after Porfirio Díaz's long dictatorship."
