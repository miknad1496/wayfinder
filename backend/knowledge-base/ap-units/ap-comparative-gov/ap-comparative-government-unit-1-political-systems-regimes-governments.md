## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 18–22% of the AP Comparative Government exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** state, nation, regime, government distinction; democratic vs authoritarian regimes; legitimacy; sovereignty; six AP comparative countries (UK, Russia, China, Mexico, Iran, Nigeria).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Foundation. Six countries appear throughout; this unit introduces them. Regime classifications.

## Big Ideas

1. **State, nation, regime, government** are distinct concepts.
2. **Six AP comparative countries:** UK, Russia, China, Mexico, Iran, Nigeria.
3. **Regime types:** democratic (UK, Mexico, Nigeria) vs authoritarian (Russia, China, Iran).
4. **Legitimacy** sources differ: traditional, charismatic, rational-legal (Weber).
5. **Sovereignty** = ultimate authority over territory.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Core concepts:**
  - **State:** politically organized territory with sovereignty.
  - **Nation:** group with shared culture, identity.
  - **Regime:** rules and norms of how power is exercised.
  - **Government:** specific people and institutions in power at a given time.
- **Sovereignty:** ultimate authority over territory. Internal (within) + external (recognized).
- **Six AP Comparative countries:**
  - **United Kingdom:** parliamentary democracy; constitutional monarchy.
  - **Russian Federation:** semi-presidential; competitive authoritarian.
  - **People's Republic of China:** one-party communist authoritarian.
  - **Mexico:** federal presidential republic; democracy (PRI dominance ended 2000).
  - **Iran:** theocratic; Supreme Leader + elected president.
  - **Nigeria:** federal presidential republic; emerging democracy.
- **Regime classifications:**
  - **Liberal democracy:** free elections + civil liberties + rule of law (UK, Mexico, Nigeria).
  - **Illiberal/competitive authoritarian:** elections occur but unfair (Russia today).
  - **Authoritarian:** no real political competition (China, Iran).
  - **Theocratic:** religious authority dominates (Iran).
- **Legitimacy (Max Weber's three types):**
  - **Traditional:** legitimacy through custom, hereditary (monarchies).
  - **Charismatic:** through leader's personal qualities (Mao, Khomeini).
  - **Rational-legal:** through laws, procedures (modern democracies).
- **Government structures:**
  - **Parliamentary:** executive (PM) drawn from legislature; fused powers.
  - **Presidential:** separate executive elected; separate from legislature.
  - **Semi-presidential:** president + PM share executive power (Russia, France).
- **Federal vs unitary:**
  - **Federal:** power shared between national and regional (Russia, Mexico, Nigeria).
  - **Unitary:** power concentrated in central (UK, China, Iran).
- **Constitutional vs unconstitutional:** UK has unwritten/uncodified constitution.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why state ≠ nation ≠ regime ≠ government:**
  - State persists; governments come and go.
  - Regime defines how government operates.
  - Nation cultural, can span states or none.
- **Hybrid regimes:** combine democratic and authoritarian features.
- **Comparative method:** look at similarities/differences across countries.
- **Country quick takes:**
  - **UK:** parliamentary, unitary (with devolved Scotland/Wales/NI), constitutional monarchy, FPTP elections.
  - **Russia:** semi-presidential, federal, electoral authoritarian, dominant United Russia party.
  - **China:** one-party (CCP) authoritarian, unitary, Standing Committee of Politburo.
  - **Mexico:** presidential, federal, democracy after 2000 PRI loss; Morena dominant since 2018.
  - **Iran:** theocracy with elected institutions; Supreme Leader has final authority; Guardian Council vets candidates.
  - **Nigeria:** presidential, federal, multi-ethnic; transitioned from military rule 1999.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why regime types vary:** historical legacy, culture, economic structure.
- **Why sovereignty contested today:** globalization, supranational organizations, secession movements.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Concepts

Distinguish state, nation, regime, government with example.
- **State:** France (sovereign territory).
- **Nation:** Catalans (cultural group within Spain).
- **Regime:** UK's parliamentary democracy (rules of how power exercised).
- **Government:** specific PM and Cabinet currently in office.

### Example 2 [3] — Country regime types

For each, identify regime type:
(a) UK.
(b) China.
(c) Iran.
(d) Russia.

- (a) **Liberal democracy** (parliamentary).
- (b) **Authoritarian** (one-party communist).
- (c) **Theocracy** (with elected institutions).
- (d) **Competitive/electoral authoritarian.**

### Example 3 [4] — Parliamentary vs presidential

Compare UK and Mexico systems.
- **UK:** parliamentary; PM chosen from majority party in Commons; fused powers; can be removed by no-confidence vote.
- **Mexico:** presidential; president directly elected; fixed term; separate from legislature; cannot be easily removed.

### Example 4 [4] — Legitimacy types

For each, identify Weber's legitimacy type:
(a) Queen Elizabeth as British monarch.
(b) Khomeini's leadership of Iran's Islamic Revolution.
(c) Modern democratic prime minister.

- (a) **Traditional.**
- (b) **Charismatic.**
- (c) **Rational-legal.**

### Example 5 [5] — Hybrid regimes

How is Russia a competitive authoritarian regime?
- **Elections held** (formal democracy).
- **But unfair:** opposition harassed, media controlled, electoral fraud.
- **Power concentrated** in Putin and inner circle.
- **Hybrid:** some democratic features (elections); some authoritarian (suppression).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing state and government.** State persists; governments change.
2. **Confusing UK and US systems.** UK parliamentary; US presidential.
3. **Wrong regime classification.** Russia: competitive authoritarian, not full democracy.
4. **Iran's complexity:** theocracy + elected president + Supreme Leader trumps.
5. **Federal vs unitary by country.** Russia, Mexico, Nigeria federal; UK, China, Iran unitary.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For comparison questions:** identify regime types; address similarities/differences.
**For concept questions:** use precise terminology.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Distinguish state (sovereign territory), nation (cultural group), regime (rules of how power is exercised), and government (specific people in office). The state persists while governments change; regimes shape how those governments operate."
2. "The six AP Comparative countries divide into liberal democracies (UK, Mexico, Nigeria), competitive/electoral authoritarian (Russia), one-party authoritarian (China), and theocracy with elected institutions (Iran)."
3. "Weber's three sources of legitimacy: traditional (custom, hereditary), charismatic (leader's personal qualities), and rational-legal (laws and procedures). Modern democracies primarily rely on rational-legal."
4. "The UK is parliamentary (PM drawn from legislative majority, fused powers), unitary (with devolved regional assemblies in Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland), and a constitutional monarchy with an uncodified constitution."
5. "Iran's hybrid theocracy combines elected institutions (President, Majles) with unelected religious authority (Supreme Leader, Guardian Council); the Supreme Leader holds ultimate authority over military, judiciary, and key policy."
