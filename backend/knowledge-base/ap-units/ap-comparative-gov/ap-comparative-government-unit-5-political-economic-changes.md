# AP Comparative Government and Politics — Unit 5: Political and Economic Changes and Development — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 16–20% of the AP Comparative Government exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** democratization; reform/revolution; economic policies (command, mixed, market); globalization impacts; sustainability and development goals; how regimes respond to change.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Trajectories of 6 countries. China's market reforms. Russia's authoritarian consolidation. Iran's resistance. Nigeria's development challenges.

## Big Ideas

1. **Democratization** = transition to democracy (Mexico, Nigeria recently).
2. **Reform vs revolution:** gradual change vs rupture.
3. **Economic systems:** command (Soviet, North Korea), mixed (most), market (US, UK).
4. **Globalization:** integration into global economy; affects all 6 countries differently.
5. **Sustainability** and inequality challenges across regimes.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Democratization:**
  - **Transition** from authoritarian to democratic.
  - **Mexico:** post-2000 transition.
  - **Nigeria:** post-1999 transition from military rule.
  - **Russia briefly democratized 1991-1999** then re-authoritarianized.
- **Authoritarian consolidation:**
  - **Russia under Putin** (2000-present).
  - **China's renewed centralization under Xi.**
- **Economic systems:**
  - **Command:** state plans entire economy (Soviet historically; modern China retains some).
  - **Mixed:** state and market roles (most countries today).
  - **Market:** mostly private (US, UK).
- **China's economic reform:**
  - **Deng Xiaoping's reforms (1978+):** "Reform and Opening Up."
  - **Special Economic Zones** (Shenzhen first).
  - **Township and Village Enterprises.**
  - **State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs)** still dominant.
  - **Result:** lifted ~800 million from poverty; second-largest economy.
- **Russia's economic transition:**
  - **Yeltsin "shock therapy" 1990s:** chaotic; oligarchs emerged.
  - **Putin recentralized:** state controls energy sector (Gazprom, Rosneft).
  - **Sanctions** since Ukraine invasion 2022 hurting economy.
- **Mexican economy:**
  - **Import-substitution industrialization** before 1980s.
  - **NAFTA (1994):** opened to US/Canada trade.
  - **USMCA replaced NAFTA (2020).**
- **Nigerian economy:**
  - **Oil-dependent** (resource curse).
  - **Niger Delta militancy.**
  - **Diversification efforts.**
- **Iranian economy:**
  - **Sanctions** (US since 1979 hostage crisis; intensified post-nuclear deal collapse).
  - **State + private (bazaaris) + religious foundations (bonyads).**
- **UK economy:**
  - **Mixed market** with strong financial sector.
  - **Brexit (2020):** left EU; trade implications.
  - **NHS** large public health system.
- **Globalization:** trade, capital, information flows.
  - **Affects sovereignty.**
  - **Creates winners and losers.**
- **Sustainable development goals (SDGs):** 17 UN goals (2015-2030).

### Adds for [4]

- **China's "Socialism with Chinese characteristics":**
  - **CCP retains political monopoly** while embracing market.
  - **Mixed economy:** SOEs + private + foreign-invested.
- **Resource curse (Nigeria):**
  - **Oil revenue** can entrench corruption.
  - **Dutch disease:** other industries decline.
  - **Conflict over oil regions** (Niger Delta militancy).
- **Iran's complex economy:**
  - **Bonyads** (religious foundations) control significant assets, tax-exempt.
  - **IRGC** (Revolutionary Guard) involved in many sectors.
  - **Sanctions** drive parallel/black markets.
- **Mexico's drug war (since 2006):** affects governance and economy.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why China can have market economy AND CCP monopoly:**
  - Economic liberalization without political liberalization.
  - Bargain: prosperity for political loyalty.
  - **Tested by current slowdown and zero-COVID legacy.**
- **Why democratization difficult to sustain:**
  - Russia shows democracy can backslide.
  - Requires institutions, civil society, economic foundations.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Transition stories

For each, identify regime change:
(a) Mexico 2000.
(b) Nigeria 1999.
(c) Iran 1979.

- (a) **Democratization:** PRI lost first presidential election; transition to multi-party democracy.
- (b) **Democratization:** transition from military rule.
- (c) **Iranian Revolution:** overthrew Shah; established Islamic Republic.

### Example 2 [3] — Economic systems

For each country, identify economic system:
(a) China.
(b) UK.
(c) Iran.

- (a) **Mixed (state-led with growing market);** "socialism with Chinese characteristics."
- (b) **Mixed market** (welfare state + private sector).
- (c) **Mixed (state + private + religious foundations);** sanctions-affected.

### Example 3 [4] — China's reform

How did China transform its economy after 1978?
- **Deng Xiaoping's "Reform and Opening Up."**
- **Decollectivization** of agriculture.
- **Special Economic Zones** for foreign investment.
- **Township and Village Enterprises** in rural areas.
- **State-Owned Enterprises** retained but reformed.
- **Result:** ~800 million lifted from poverty; second-largest economy by GDP.
- **Political:** CCP retained monopoly; no political liberalization.

### Example 4 [4] — Russia's trajectory

Trace Russia's political-economic trajectory 1991-present.
- **1991:** USSR collapsed; Yeltsin's Russia emerged.
- **1990s:** chaotic transition; "shock therapy"; oligarchs emerged; democracy uneven.
- **2000+:** Putin recentralized; state captured energy sector.
- **2008-2024:** further authoritarian consolidation.
- **2014:** Crimea annexation, Western sanctions begin.
- **2022:** Ukraine invasion, severe sanctions, partial mobilization, repression intensified.

### Example 5 [5] — Nigeria's resource curse

How does oil affect Nigeria's politics and economy?
- **Oil ~80% of exports, ~50% of government revenue.**
- **Concentrates wealth** in those controlling oil rents.
- **Corruption** facilitated by oil revenues.
- **Niger Delta** environmental damage; MEND militancy.
- **Other sectors underdeveloped** (Dutch disease).
- **Vulnerable to oil price swings.**

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Mexico's transition** dated to 2000, not earlier reforms.
2. **China's economy** is mixed but politically still CCP-monopoly.
3. **Russia's democracy** existed briefly post-1991; eroded under Putin.
4. **Iran has private sector** alongside state and bonyads.
5. **Nigeria's oil wealth** ≠ broad development.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For change questions:** identify direction (democratization vs authoritarianization).
**For economic questions:** identify system (command/mixed/market).
**For globalization:** identify impacts (winners/losers).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Mexico democratized in 2000 when Vicente Fox (PAN) defeated PRI's candidate, ending 71 years of one-party rule. Nigeria democratized in 1999 after decades of intermittent military rule."
2. "China's economic reforms under Deng Xiaoping (1978+) — 'Reform and Opening Up,' Special Economic Zones, decollectivization, state-owned enterprise reform — produced extraordinary growth (~800 million lifted from poverty) without political liberalization. The CCP retained monopoly."
3. "Russia's post-Soviet trajectory illustrates democratic backsliding: brief democratization (1991-1999) gave way to authoritarian consolidation under Putin (2000+), with state capture of energy (Gazprom, Rosneft), media control, and crackdown on opposition (Navalny)."
4. "Nigeria's oil-dependent economy illustrates the 'resource curse': oil generates ~80% of exports and ~50% of government revenue, concentrating wealth among controlling elites, fostering corruption, fueling Niger Delta conflict, and crowding out other sectors (Dutch disease)."
5. "Iran's mixed economy combines state enterprises, private firms, and religious foundations (bonyads); ongoing US sanctions since 1979 (intensified after 2018 nuclear deal withdrawal) limit growth and drive parallel markets."
