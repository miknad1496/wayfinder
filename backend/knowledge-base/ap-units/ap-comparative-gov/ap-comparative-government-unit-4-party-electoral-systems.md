## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 13–17% of the AP Comparative Government exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** electoral systems (FPTP, PR, mixed); party systems (one-party, two-party, multi-party); interest groups; civil society organizations; social movements.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** FPTP vs PR consequences. Major parties in 6 countries. Interest groups across regimes.

## Big Ideas

1. **FPTP** (UK, Mexico) tends to two-party; **PR** tends to multi-party.
2. **Russia, China:** dominant party (United Russia, CCP).
3. **Iran:** factions within constraints (no full opposition parties).
4. **Mexico, Nigeria, UK:** multi-party with major contenders.
5. **Interest groups** vary by regime — pluralist (UK), corporatist (Mexico historically), state-controlled (China).

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Electoral systems:**
  - **First-Past-The-Post (FPTP):** plurality wins; single-member districts; tends to 2-party (Duverger's Law).
  - **Proportional Representation (PR):** seats proportional to vote share; multi-member districts; tends to multi-party.
  - **Mixed (MMP):** combines (Russia: half FPTP, half PR; Mexico: similar).
- **Party systems:**
  - **One-party:** China (CCP).
  - **Dominant party (de facto):** Russia (United Russia).
  - **Two-party tendency:** UK (Labour vs Conservative; though SNP and Liberal Democrats matter).
  - **Multi-party:** Mexico, Nigeria.
  - **Restricted multi-party:** Iran (within ideological limits).
- **Major parties by country:**
  - **UK:** Conservative (Tory), Labour, Liberal Democrats, SNP (Scottish National Party), Reform UK.
  - **Russia:** United Russia (dominant), Communist Party (CPRF), LDPR, A Just Russia.
  - **China:** Chinese Communist Party (CCP) only ruling party; 8 minor "democratic parties" subordinate.
  - **Mexico:** Morena (currently dominant — AMLO/Sheinbaum), PAN, PRI (formerly hegemonic), MC.
  - **Iran:** factions — Reformists, Principlists/Conservatives, Hardliners. Not Western-style parties.
  - **Nigeria:** APC, PDP main parties; multi-party.
- **Interest groups:**
  - **Pluralist:** many groups compete (UK, US).
  - **Corporatist:** state-recognized peak associations (historically Mexico under PRI).
  - **State-controlled:** "transmission belts" of party (China).
- **Social movements:**
  - **Iran's Green Movement** (2009 election protests).
  - **2022 Mahsa Amini** protests in Iran.
  - **Russian opposition** under Navalny.
  - **Nigerian #EndSARS** anti-police-brutality protests (2020).
  - **Hong Kong** democracy protests (2014, 2019-2020).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why FPTP → 2-party (Duverger's Law):**
  - Voters strategic; don't want to "waste" vote on third party.
  - Winners take all; small parties get nothing.
- **Why PR → multi-party:**
  - Small parties get seats proportional to vote share.
  - Lower threshold for representation.
- **Mexican 2000 transition:**
  - **PRI ruled 1929-2000;** Vicente Fox (PAN) won 2000 election.
  - **Marked transition** from hegemonic to competitive party system.
  - **AMLO/Morena** dominant since 2018.
- **Russia under Putin:**
  - **United Russia** is "party of power."
  - **Other parties exist** but contained.
  - **Real opposition** (Navalny, Yashin) repressed.
  - **2024 election** Putin won with claimed 87%.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why electoral systems matter:**
  - **Shape party landscape.**
  - **Influence representation** of minorities.
  - **Reform attempts** (UK 2011 referendum failed; Mexico mixed system).
- **Why parties matter even in authoritarian states:**
  - Vehicle for selecting/training leaders.
  - Channel patronage.
  - CCP integrated into state apparatus.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Electoral systems

For each country, identify electoral system:
(a) UK House of Commons.
(b) Iran Majles.
(c) Mexico Chamber of Deputies.

- (a) **First-Past-The-Post** (single-member districts, plurality).
- (b) **Mixed/runoff in single-member districts;** within constraints set by Guardian Council.
- (c) **Mixed (parallel):** 300 FPTP seats + 200 PR seats (total 500).

### Example 2 [3] — Major parties

Identify dominant party in each:
(a) Russia.
(b) China.
(c) Mexico (recent).
(d) UK (recent).

- (a) **United Russia.**
- (b) **CCP (only).**
- (c) **Morena (since 2018).**
- (d) varies; **Conservative** 2010-2024; **Labour** post-2024.

### Example 3 [4] — Duverger's Law

Why does FPTP tend to two-party system?
- **Strategic voting:** voters don't waste vote on uncompetitive party.
- **Winner-take-all** punishes small parties.
- **UK:** despite Liberal Democrats and SNP existing, Conservative-Labour dominate.
- **Exception:** regional parties (SNP) can win in their region.

### Example 4 [4] — Mexico transition

How did Mexico transition from PRI to multi-party?
- **PRI dominance 1929-2000:** elections held but rigged.
- **1990s reforms:** independent electoral commission (IFE).
- **2000:** Vicente Fox (PAN) won presidency.
- **Subsequent elections** competitive.
- **2018:** AMLO (Morena) won; new dominant party.

### Example 5 [5] — Interest groups by regime

Compare interest group systems in UK, China, Mexico (historically).
- **UK pluralist:** many groups compete; lobby government; some access (CBI = business; TUC = unions).
- **China state-controlled:** ACFTU (unions) is CCP transmission belt; independent organizing illegal.
- **Mexico corporatist (under PRI):** PRI organized peak associations (CTM unions, CNOP for popular sector); state-society relationship managed by party.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **FPTP doesn't always produce 2-party** — UK has SNP, Lib Dems.
2. **Russia has multiple parties** but United Russia dominant.
3. **China formally has 9 parties** but CCP only ruling one.
4. **Iran factions ≠ Western parties.**
5. **PR thresholds** vary; common 5% to filter out tiny parties.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For electoral systems:** identify type; predict party system effects.
**For interest groups:** identify pluralist/corporatist/state-controlled.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Duverger's Law: First-Past-The-Post electoral systems tend to produce two-party systems because strategic voters avoid 'wasting' votes on third parties; PR systems produce multi-party systems by translating vote share into seat share."
2. "United Russia operates as Russia's 'party of power' under Putin; while other parties exist (CPRF, LDPR), real opposition (Navalny) faces imprisonment, exile, or death. Russia's 2024 election claimed 87% for Putin amid eliminated opposition."
3. "Mexico's PRI ruled 1929-2000 in a hegemonic party system; the 2000 election of Vicente Fox (PAN) marked transition to a competitive multi-party system. Morena (AMLO) became dominant in 2018."
4. "China's CCP is the only ruling party in a one-party state. Eight 'minor democratic parties' exist but are CCP-affiliated and politically subordinate."
5. "Interest group systems range from pluralist (UK — many competing groups) to corporatist (historic Mexican PRI — peak associations integrated with state) to state-controlled (China — ACFTU and other 'transmission belts' for CCP)."
