## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–17% of the AP Human Geography exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Industrial Revolution diffusion; economic sectors (primary/secondary/tertiary/quaternary/quinary); measures of development (GDP, GNI, HDI); Rostow's stages; Wallerstein's world systems; sustainable development; women in development.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** HDI and development measures tested constantly. Rostow's stages and Wallerstein's world systems frequent. Sectors of economy. Globalization patterns.

## Big Ideas

1. **Industrial Revolution started in Britain (~1750s)** then spread globally.
2. **Economic sectors:** primary (extraction), secondary (manufacturing), tertiary (services), quaternary (knowledge), quinary (decision-making).
3. **Multiple measures of development:** GDP, GNI, HDI, GII.
4. **Theories of development:** Rostow (stages), Wallerstein (world systems).
5. **Sustainable development** balances economic, social, environmental needs.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Industrial Revolution diffusion:**
  - **Britain (1750s+).**
  - **Spread to Belgium, France, Germany** (early 1800s).
  - **US** (mid-1800s).
  - **Japan** (late 1800s, Meiji Restoration).
  - **Russia/USSR** (early 1900s, Stalin's Five-Year Plans).
  - **NICs (Newly Industrialized Countries):** South Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, Hong Kong (Asian Tigers).
  - **China** (since 1980s reforms).
  - **India, Brazil, Vietnam** more recent.
- **Economic sectors:**
  - **Primary:** extraction of natural resources (farming, mining, fishing, forestry).
  - **Secondary:** manufacturing (factories, processing).
  - **Tertiary:** services (retail, transportation, banking, healthcare, education).
  - **Quaternary:** knowledge work (research, IT, finance, consulting).
  - **Quinary:** decision-making (top executives, government leaders).
- **Sectoral shift with development:**
  - **Pre-industrial:** mostly primary.
  - **Industrial:** shift to secondary (manufacturing).
  - **Post-industrial:** shift to tertiary, quaternary (services, knowledge).
  - **US, Western Europe** are post-industrial (services dominate).
- **Measures of development:**
  - **GDP (Gross Domestic Product):** total economic output. Per capita = average wealth.
  - **GNI (Gross National Income):** GDP + income from abroad.
  - **PPP (Purchasing Power Parity):** adjusts for cost of living.
  - **HDI (Human Development Index):** life expectancy + education + income (0-1 scale).
  - **GII (Gender Inequality Index):** maternal mortality + adolescent birth + parliamentary representation + education + labor.
  - **Gini coefficient:** income inequality (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality).
- **Rostow's Stages of Economic Growth (1960):**
  - **Stage 1 — Traditional society:** subsistence; little technology.
  - **Stage 2 — Preconditions for takeoff:** modern infrastructure begins.
  - **Stage 3 — Takeoff:** rapid industrialization in key sectors.
  - **Stage 4 — Drive to maturity:** industrialization spreads; technology improves.
  - **Stage 5 — High mass consumption:** advanced consumer society.
- **Wallerstein's World Systems Theory (1974):**
  - **Core:** developed, industrial economies (US, Western Europe, Japan).
  - **Periphery:** developing, dependent on core (much of Africa, Latin America).
  - **Semi-periphery:** intermediate (Brazil, China, Mexico, NICs).
  - **Core extracts wealth from periphery.**
  - **Critique of Rostow:** Wallerstein argues periphery can't simply follow core's path.
- **Globalization:**
  - **Multinational corporations** (MNCs).
  - **Free trade** (WTO, NAFTA, EU).
  - **Outsourcing and offshoring** of production.
  - **Global supply chains.**
  - **Tariff reduction** (post-WWII pattern).
- **Trade theories:**
  - **Comparative advantage (Ricardo):** countries should specialize in what they produce relatively most efficiently.
  - **Free trade vs protectionism** debate.
- **Industrial location:**
  - **Weber's Least Cost Theory:** locate where transport + labor + agglomeration costs minimized.
  - **Bulk-reducing industry:** locate near raw materials (steel near iron/coal).
  - **Bulk-gaining industry:** locate near markets (beverages near consumers).
  - **Footloose industry:** can locate anywhere (high-tech).
- **Sustainable development:**
  - **Brundtland Report (1987):** "meets needs of present without compromising future."
  - **Triple bottom line:** economic, social, environmental.
  - **UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs, 2015):** 17 goals.
- **Women in development:**
  - **Microfinance** (Grameen Bank, Muhammad Yunus): small loans to poor women.
  - **Women's education** correlates with development.
  - **GII** measures gender inequality.
  - **Gender gap** persists in labor, politics, education globally.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why NICs succeeded:**
  - **Export-oriented industrialization** (vs. import substitution).
  - **State coordination** with private sector.
  - **Investment in education.**
  - **US Cold War alliance** (Korea, Taiwan).
- **HDI calculation:**
  - **Life expectancy** at birth.
  - **Education** (mean and expected years of schooling).
  - **GNI per capita** (adjusted for PPP).
  - **Composite score** 0-1; higher = more developed.
  - **Top:** Norway, Switzerland, Iceland (~0.96).
  - **Bottom:** Niger, Chad, South Sudan (<0.45).
- **Wallerstein vs Rostow:**
  - **Rostow:** stages model; assumes all countries can develop following same path.
  - **Wallerstein:** structural model; argues core-periphery system constrains periphery development.
  - **Dependency theory** related to Wallerstein.
- **Outsourcing/offshoring:**
  - Manufacturing moved to lower-wage countries (China, Mexico, Vietnam).
  - Services outsourced (call centers in India).
  - Benefits consumers (cheaper goods); costs workers in core (job loss).
- **Special economic zones (SEZs):**
  - Reduced regulation/tax to attract foreign investment.
  - **China's SEZs** (Shenzhen) drove industrialization.
  - **Maquiladoras** in Mexico along US border.
- **Fossil fuel transition:**
  - Climate change requires shift from fossil fuels.
  - **Renewable energy** (solar, wind) growing.
  - **Geographic implications:** new resource geography (lithium, rare earths).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why measures of development matter.**
  - GDP per capita misses inequality.
  - HDI captures broader well-being.
  - GII captures gender disparity.
  - Multiple measures needed for complete picture.
- **Why globalization is contested.**
  - **Pro:** efficient resource allocation; consumer benefits; lifted billions out of poverty (especially China).
  - **Con:** job loss in core; inequality; environmental damage; cultural homogenization; dependence on global supply chains (COVID disruption).
- **Sustainability tension.**
  - Developing world wants to industrialize (raises emissions).
  - Developed world should reduce emissions but historic emitter.
  - **Climate justice debate.**

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Economic sectors

For each job, identify sector:
(a) Coal miner.
(b) Auto factory worker.
(c) Schoolteacher.
(d) Software developer.
(e) Government leader.

- (a) **Primary.**
- (b) **Secondary.**
- (c) **Tertiary.**
- (d) **Quaternary.**
- (e) **Quinary.**

### Example 2 [3] — Development measures

What does HDI measure?
- **Life expectancy** at birth.
- **Education** (mean and expected years of schooling).
- **GNI per capita** (PPP-adjusted).
- **Composite 0-1 score.**
- Captures broader development than GDP alone.

### Example 3 [4] — Rostow vs Wallerstein

Compare Rostow's and Wallerstein's models.
- **Rostow (1960):** 5 stages from traditional to mass consumption. Assumes all countries can follow same path.
- **Wallerstein (1974):** core-periphery world system. Periphery is structurally exploited by core; can't simply follow core's path.
- **Rostow optimistic** about development; **Wallerstein pessimistic** without structural change.

### Example 4 [4] — Industrial location

Why is steel near iron and coal? Why are beverages near markets?
- **Steel:** **bulk-reducing** — heavy raw materials become lighter product. Locate near raw materials to reduce transport.
- **Beverages:** **bulk-gaining** — light raw materials (concentrate) become heavy product (water added). Locate near markets to reduce transport of finished product.

### Example 5 [5] — NICs

What enabled the Asian Tigers (South Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, Hong Kong) to industrialize?
- **Export-oriented industrialization** (target world markets).
- **State coordination** with private sector (chaebols in Korea).
- **Heavy investment in education.**
- **US Cold War economic support** (Korea, Taiwan).
- **Discipline and savings rates.**
- **Result:** all moved from poor to developed within 1-2 generations.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing economic sectors.** Primary (extraction), secondary (manufacturing), tertiary (services), quaternary (knowledge), quinary (decision).
2. **Confusing GDP and GNI.** GDP: produced within country. GNI: includes income from abroad.
3. **Wrong HDI components.** Life expectancy + education + income (per capita PPP).
4. **Confusing Rostow stages.** Stage 3 takeoff is key transition.
5. **Wrong world systems categories.** Core (developed), periphery (dependent), semi-periphery (intermediate).
6. **Confusing Weber bulk-reducing/bulk-gaining.** Bulk-reducing: near raw materials. Bulk-gaining: near markets.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For development questions:**
- Identify measure (GDP, HDI, GII).
- Note what measure captures and misses.

**For theory questions:**
- Identify Rostow or Wallerstein.
- Address strengths and limits.

**For globalization questions:**
- Address economic dimensions (trade, MNCs, outsourcing).
- Address effects on different groups.

1. "Economic sectors progress with development: from primary (extraction) to secondary (manufacturing) to tertiary (services) to quaternary (knowledge work) — culminating in post-industrial economies dominated by services."
2. "Multiple measures capture development: GDP per capita (economic output), HDI (life expectancy + education + income), GII (gender inequality), and Gini coefficient (income inequality) — each revealing different dimensions."
3. "Rostow's Five Stages of Economic Growth (1960) describe a linear development path from traditional society through takeoff to high mass consumption — but Wallerstein's World Systems Theory (1974) argues that core-periphery structural relationships constrain peripheral countries' development."
4. "Industrial location follows Weber's Least Cost Theory: bulk-reducing industries (steel) locate near raw materials; bulk-gaining industries (beverages) locate near markets; footloose industries (high-tech) can locate anywhere."
5. "The Newly Industrialized Countries — Asian Tigers (South Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, Hong Kong) followed by China and others — succeeded through export-oriented industrialization, state-business coordination, education investment, and rapid sectoral transformation."
