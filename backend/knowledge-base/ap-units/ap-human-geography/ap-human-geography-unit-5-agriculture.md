## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–17% of the AP Human Geography exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** agricultural origins (hearths); types of agriculture (subsistence, commercial); intensive vs extensive; agricultural revolutions (Neolithic, Second, Third/Green); von Thünen model; rural settlement patterns; food security; sustainability.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Agricultural revolutions tested constantly. Von Thünen model essential. Subsistence vs commercial distinction. Green Revolution impacts.

## Big Ideas

1. **Agriculture began in multiple hearths** during Neolithic Revolution (~10,000 BCE).
2. **Three agricultural revolutions** shaped human history.
3. **Subsistence vs commercial agriculture** is fundamental distinction.
4. **Von Thünen's model** explains land use around urban markets.
5. **Modern challenges:** food security, sustainability, climate change.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Agricultural origins:**
  - **Neolithic (First) Agricultural Revolution (~10,000 BCE):** transition from hunting-gathering to farming.
  - **Multiple hearths** of independent agricultural origin:
    - **Fertile Crescent** (Mesopotamia): wheat, barley, sheep.
    - **East Asia (China):** rice, millet, soybeans, pigs.
    - **South Asia (India):** rice, wheat, cotton.
    - **Mesoamerica:** maize (corn), beans, squash, turkey.
    - **Andes (Peru):** potatoes, quinoa, llamas.
    - **Sub-Saharan Africa:** sorghum, yams.
- **Three Agricultural Revolutions:**
  - **First (Neolithic, ~10,000 BCE):** domestication; settled agriculture begins.
  - **Second (1700s-1800s):** improvements in farming (crop rotation, enclosure, mechanization).
  - **Third (Green Revolution, ~1940s-1970s):** high-yield seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, irrigation.
- **Types of agriculture:**
  - **Subsistence:** food for own family/community; little surplus.
    - **Shifting cultivation (slash-and-burn):** clear forest, farm few years, move on. Tropics (Amazon, Central Africa, SE Asia).
    - **Pastoral nomadism:** moving herds. Dry climates (Sahara, Central Asia).
    - **Intensive subsistence (with rice):** Asian river valleys; very high labor input per land.
    - **Intensive subsistence (without rice):** wheat, vegetables.
  - **Commercial:** food for sale.
    - **Mixed crop and livestock:** US Corn Belt.
    - **Dairy farming:** US Northeast, Northern Europe.
    - **Grain farming:** US Great Plains.
    - **Mediterranean agriculture:** olives, grapes, fruits. Mediterranean climate areas.
    - **Plantation agriculture:** large estate, monoculture cash crop. Tropics (sugar, coffee, bananas).
    - **Livestock ranching:** large-scale cattle/sheep. Western US, Argentina, Australia.
    - **Truck farming:** vegetables/fruits for distant markets. Specialization (e.g., Salinas Valley CA = "salad bowl").
- **Intensive vs extensive:**
  - **Intensive:** high labor and capital input per unit land. Small plots. Asian rice; truck farming; dairy.
  - **Extensive:** low input per unit land; large plots. Ranching; shifting cultivation; grain farming.
- **Von Thünen Model (1826):**
  - **Concentric rings** around central market city.
  - **Ring 1:** dairy, market gardening (perishable, high-value).
  - **Ring 2:** forest (heavy, expensive to transport — historically).
  - **Ring 3:** crops (grains, root crops; less perishable).
  - **Ring 4:** livestock ranching (extensive, low-value per area).
  - **Logic:** products with highest transportation cost relative to value located closest.
  - **Modern modifications:** transportation tech (refrigeration, trucking) reduced friction; model still useful for general principles.
- **Green Revolution (1940s-1970s):**
  - **Norman Borlaug** developed high-yield wheat; Nobel Peace Prize.
  - **High-yield varieties (HYVs)** of wheat, rice, corn.
  - **Heavy fertilizer, pesticide, irrigation** use.
  - **Mexico, India, Pakistan, Philippines** dramatically increased yields.
  - **Saved possibly a billion lives.**
  - **Critics:** environmental costs (soil, water, biodiversity); favored large farmers; created dependency on inputs.
- **Rural settlement patterns:**
  - **Clustered (nucleated):** houses grouped together; common in Europe, Asia.
  - **Dispersed (isolated):** individual farmsteads spread out; common in US (after Homestead Act).
  - **Linear:** along roads/rivers.
- **Land survey systems:**
  - **Township and range (US Public Land Survey):** rectangular grid; square miles.
  - **Metes and bounds:** physical features used; common in colonial Eastern US, England.
  - **Long lots:** narrow strips perpendicular to river; French Canada, Louisiana.
- **Modern food issues:**
  - **Food security:** reliable access to adequate food.
  - **Food deserts:** urban areas lacking fresh food access.
  - **Organic agriculture:** without synthetic chemicals.
  - **GMOs (genetically modified organisms):** controversial; widely used in US.
  - **Sustainable agriculture:** maintains productivity long-term.
  - **Agribusiness:** corporate, vertically integrated agriculture.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why Fertile Crescent first:**
  - **Climate** (Mediterranean — wet winter, dry summer).
  - **Diversity of wild plants and animals** suited for domestication (wheat, barley, sheep, goats, cattle).
  - **River valleys** (Tigris, Euphrates) enabled irrigation.
- **Bid-rent theory:**
  - Land closer to market commands higher rent.
  - Higher-value uses outbid lower-value uses for central locations.
  - Underpins von Thünen model and urban land use.
- **Green Revolution impacts:**
  - **Yields multiplied** (Mexican wheat tripled; Indian wheat doubled).
  - **Famine averted** in Asia.
  - **Environmental costs:** soil depletion; water depletion (aquifers); pesticide pollution; loss of biodiversity (monoculture).
  - **Social costs:** large farmers benefited more; small farmers couldn't afford inputs; rural inequality grew.
- **Why agribusiness consolidation:**
  - Economies of scale.
  - Government policy (subsidies favoring large producers).
  - Vertical integration (seed → farm → processing → distribution).
- **Plantation agriculture:**
  - Originated with European colonialism.
  - Often single cash crop (sugar, coffee, tea, bananas, rubber).
  - Originally relied on slave/coerced labor.
  - Today: large estates with low-wage labor.
  - Examples: Caribbean sugar; Brazilian coffee; Central American bananas.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Neolithic Revolution mattered.**
  - Enabled population growth, settlement, civilization.
  - Created social hierarchy, surplus, specialization.
  - Transformed human relationship to land.
- **Why Green Revolution mattered.**
  - Averted predicted famines.
  - Made South Asia food-secure.
  - But created environmental and social problems.
  - Limited application in Africa (different crops, less infrastructure).
- **Modern food challenges.**
  - **Climate change** disrupting agriculture.
  - **Water scarcity** especially Aquifer depletion (Ogallala).
  - **Soil degradation.**
  - **Biodiversity loss** (commercial monocultures).
  - **Urbanization** consuming farmland.
  - **Need for sustainable practices:** crop rotation, cover crops, integrated pest management.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Agricultural hearths

For each crop, identify likely hearth:
(a) Wheat.
(b) Maize (corn).
(c) Rice.
(d) Potato.

- (a) **Fertile Crescent** (Mesopotamia).
- (b) **Mesoamerica.**
- (c) **East Asia/China** (or South Asia).
- (d) **Andes** (Peru).

### Example 2 [3] — Agriculture types

For each, identify type:
(a) Amazon farmer clears forest, farms 3 years, moves on.
(b) US Great Plains wheat farm.
(c) US Corn Belt mixed corn-soybean-hog farm.
(d) Bedouin moves goats across desert.

- (a) **Shifting cultivation** (subsistence).
- (b) **Commercial grain farming** (extensive).
- (c) **Mixed crop and livestock** (commercial).
- (d) **Pastoral nomadism** (subsistence).

### Example 3 [4] — Von Thünen model

Why does dairy farming locate near cities (in von Thünen)?
- **Milk is perishable** and expensive to transport (especially before refrigeration).
- **High value relative to weight**, so high transportation cost reduces profit if far from market.
- **Logic:** products most affected by transport cost go in inner ring.

### Example 4 [4] — Green Revolution impacts

What did the Green Revolution accomplish? What were its costs?
- **Accomplished:** dramatic yield increases (wheat tripled in Mexico; rice yields multiplied in Asia); famine averted; saved possibly a billion lives.
- **Environmental costs:** soil depletion; aquifer depletion; pesticide pollution; biodiversity loss.
- **Social costs:** benefited large farmers more; rural inequality grew.

### Example 5 [5] — Sustainable agriculture

What practices make agriculture more sustainable?
- **Crop rotation** maintains soil fertility.
- **Cover crops** prevent erosion, fix nitrogen.
- **Integrated pest management:** biological controls + targeted chemicals.
- **Drip irrigation** reduces water use.
- **Reduced tillage** (no-till) preserves soil.
- **Organic methods** reduce synthetic input.
- **Agroecology** mimics natural systems.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Wrong Neolithic date.** ~**10,000 BCE** (about 12,000 years ago).
2. **Confusing intensive and extensive.** Intensive: high input/land (rice, dairy). Extensive: low input/land (ranching, grain).
3. **Confusing subsistence types.** Shifting (slash-burn), pastoral nomadism, intensive subsistence rice/non-rice.
4. **Forgetting plantation origin.** **Colonial origin**; cash crop; originally slave labor.
5. **Wrong Green Revolution leader.** **Norman Borlaug** (Nobel Peace Prize 1970).
6. **Wrong von Thünen rings.** Innermost: perishables (dairy, market garden). Outermost: extensive (livestock).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For agriculture type questions:**
- Identify subsistence or commercial.
- Identify intensive or extensive.
- Connect to climate, geography, market.

**For von Thünen questions:**
- Identify ring and product.
- Justify with transport cost / value logic.

**For Green Revolution questions:**
- Address yields, technology, regions.
- Address benefits AND costs.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The Neolithic Agricultural Revolution (~10,000 BCE) transitioned humans from hunting-gathering to settled farming, with multiple independent hearths in the Fertile Crescent (wheat, barley), East Asia (rice), Mesoamerica (maize), and the Andes (potatoes)."
2. "Subsistence agriculture (shifting cultivation, pastoral nomadism, intensive rice farming) produces food for the producer's family or community. Commercial agriculture (mixed crop-livestock, plantations, ranching, truck farming) produces for sale in markets."
3. "Von Thünen's model (1826) explains agricultural land use as concentric rings around a market city: inner rings hold perishable, high-value products (dairy, market gardening); outer rings hold extensive, low-value uses (livestock ranching). Transportation costs relative to product value drive the pattern."
4. "The Green Revolution (1940s-1970s), led by Norman Borlaug, used high-yield varieties, synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, and irrigation to dramatically increase yields in Mexico, India, and Asia — averting famine but causing environmental costs (soil depletion, aquifer loss, biodiversity loss) and social costs (favoring large farmers)."
5. "Modern food systems face climate change, water scarcity, soil degradation, biodiversity loss, and urbanization-driven loss of farmland — driving interest in sustainable practices: crop rotation, cover crops, integrated pest management, and agroecology."
