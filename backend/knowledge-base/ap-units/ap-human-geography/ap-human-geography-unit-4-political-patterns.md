## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–17% of the AP Human Geography exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** state, nation, nation-state; types of boundaries; political organization (federal/unitary); supranational organizations (EU, UN, NATO); centripetal vs centrifugal forces; gerrymandering; devolution.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** State/nation/nation-state distinction. Boundary types (geometric, physical, cultural). Centripetal vs centrifugal forces. Devolution and supranational organizations. Gerrymandering types.

## Big Ideas

1. **State, nation, nation-state are distinct concepts** often confused.
2. **Boundaries** delimit states; can be geometric, physical, cultural; create conflict.
3. **Political organization** ranges from unitary to federal, with consequences.
4. **Supranational organizations** (EU, UN) increase cooperation but limit sovereignty.
5. **Centripetal forces** unite states; **centrifugal forces** divide them.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Core political geography concepts:**
  - **State:** politically organized territory with sovereignty, government, recognized borders. (E.g., France, Japan.)
  - **Nation:** group with shared culture, history, often language/religion. (E.g., Kurds, Catalans, French.)
  - **Nation-state:** state whose population is largely one nation. (E.g., Japan, Iceland — relatively rare in pure form.)
  - **Multinational state:** state with multiple nations. (E.g., Russia, Canada, US, India.)
  - **Stateless nation:** nation without state. (E.g., Kurds, Palestinians, historically Jews before 1948.)
- **Sovereignty:** ultimate authority over territory.
- **Boundaries (borders):**
  - **Geometric/superimposed:** straight lines (US-Canada 49th parallel; many African borders from colonial era).
  - **Physical/natural:** rivers, mountains (Rio Grande US-Mexico).
  - **Cultural/ethnographic:** based on cultural divides.
  - **Antecedent:** existed before settlement.
  - **Subsequent:** drawn after settlement (often follows cultural lines).
  - **Superimposed:** drawn over existing patterns by outsiders (colonial Africa).
  - **Relict:** former boundary still visible (Berlin Wall remnants).
- **State shapes:**
  - **Compact:** roughly equal in all directions (Poland, Uruguay).
  - **Elongated:** long and narrow (Chile, Italy, Vietnam).
  - **Prorupted:** compact with extension (Thailand, Namibia).
  - **Fragmented:** in multiple pieces (Indonesia, Philippines, US).
  - **Perforated:** surrounds another state (South Africa surrounds Lesotho).
  - **Enclave:** state surrounded by another (Lesotho, Vatican).
  - **Exclave:** part of state separated from main territory (Alaska, Kaliningrad).
- **Political organization:**
  - **Unitary state:** strong central government (France, Japan).
  - **Federal state:** power shared between central and regional (US, Germany, Canada, India).
  - **Confederation:** loose association of states.
- **Supranational organizations:**
  - **United Nations (1945):** global forum; 193 member states.
  - **European Union:** economic and political integration.
  - **NATO:** military alliance.
  - **OPEC:** oil-producing nations.
  - **ASEAN, African Union, Mercosur:** regional.
  - **Trade-off:** cooperation gains; sovereignty losses.
- **Forces shaping states:**
  - **Centripetal forces:** unite (national identity, common language/religion, strong institutions, infrastructure, external threat).
  - **Centrifugal forces:** divide (ethnic/religious divisions, economic inequality, weak institutions, separatist movements).
- **Devolution:**
  - Transfer of power from central government to regions.
  - **Examples:** UK to Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland; Spain to Catalonia, Basque; Belgium between Flemish and Walloon.
  - **Can lead to independence movements** (Catalonia, Quebec, Scotland).
- **Electoral geography:**
  - **Reapportionment:** redistribution of seats among states (US: every 10 years, after census).
  - **Redistricting:** redrawing district boundaries.
  - **Gerrymandering:** drawing districts to favor party/group.
    - **Packing:** concentrate opposing voters in few districts.
    - **Cracking:** spread opposing voters across many districts (dilute their power).
- **Geopolitics:**
  - **Heartland Theory (Mackinder):** controlling Eurasian core = world dominance.
  - **Rimland Theory (Spykman):** controlling coastal periphery > controlling heartland.
  - **Used to justify Cold War containment policy.**
- **Imperialism and colonialism:**
  - **Colonialism:** direct rule of one country by another.
  - **Imperialism:** broader; includes economic/political domination.
  - **European colonies** carved up Africa, Asia, Americas.
  - **Decolonization** post-WWII; many post-colonial conflicts from arbitrary borders.
  - **Neocolonialism:** continued economic/cultural domination after political independence.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why nation-states are rare:**
  - Most states are multinational (Russia ~190 ethnic groups; Nigeria 250+).
  - **Pure nation-states:** Japan, Iceland, Korea (mostly).
- **Stateless nations:**
  - **Kurds:** ~30 million; spread across Turkey, Iraq, Iran, Syria.
  - **Palestinians:** seek state.
  - **Tibetans:** under Chinese rule.
  - **Catalans, Basques:** within Spain.
  - **Kashmiris:** divided India/Pakistan.
- **Why African borders are problematic:**
  - **Berlin Conference (1884-85)** drew borders without regard to ethnic/cultural patterns.
  - **Superimposed borders** divided ethnic groups, combined hostile groups.
  - **Source of post-independence conflict** (Rwanda, Sudan, etc.).
- **EU as supranational:**
  - **Free movement** of people, goods, capital.
  - **Common currency** (Euro) for many members.
  - **EU institutions** (Parliament, Commission, Court).
  - **Brexit (2020):** UK left, citing sovereignty concerns.
  - Trade-off: cooperation vs national sovereignty.
- **Centripetal/centrifugal in practice:**
  - **Centripetal — US:** national identity, common language, strong federal institutions, shared mythology.
  - **Centrifugal — US:** racial/ethnic divisions, regional differences, polarization.
  - **Centripetal — Germany after WWII:** Constitution, prosperity, EU membership.
  - **Centrifugal — former Yugoslavia:** ethnic/religious divisions led to breakup (1990s).
- **Devolution outcomes:**
  - **Some succeed:** Scotland gained Parliament (1999); Catalonia has autonomy.
  - **Some lead to independence:** South Sudan from Sudan (2011); Eritrea from Ethiopia (1993); USSR republics (1991).
  - **Some create ongoing tension:** Quebec separatism (1980, 1995 referendums failed).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why nation-state ideal matters.**
  - Modern political legitimacy often rooted in nation-state model.
  - Reality is messier — most states multinational.
  - Tension between state sovereignty and nation self-determination drives many conflicts.
- **Supranational vs sovereignty tension.**
  - EU benefits: economic integration, prevent war, larger market.
  - EU costs: democratic deficit, loss of monetary policy (Eurozone), regulatory burden.
  - Brexit shows the tension's political force.
- **Geopolitics today.**
  - Russia's invasions (Crimea 2014, Ukraine 2022) revive geopolitical competition.
  - China's rise reshapes global power.
  - **Multipolar world** emerging.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — State vs nation

For each, identify state, nation, nation-state, or stateless nation:
(a) Japan.
(b) Kurds.
(c) Russia.
(d) Catalans.

- (a) **Nation-state** (largely homogeneous; one nation, one state).
- (b) **Stateless nation** (lack their own state).
- (c) **Multinational state** (many nations within one state).
- (d) **Nation** within multinational state (Spain).

### Example 2 [3] — Boundary types

For each, identify boundary type:
(a) US-Canada 49th parallel.
(b) Rio Grande between US and Mexico.
(c) Most African borders.

- (a) **Geometric/superimposed.**
- (b) **Physical/natural.**
- (c) **Superimposed** (colonial; ignored ethnic patterns).

### Example 3 [4] — Centripetal/centrifugal forces

Identify centripetal and centrifugal forces in former Yugoslavia.
- **Centripetal (under Tito):** common Yugoslav identity, communist ideology, federal structure, suppression of ethnic conflict.
- **Centrifugal:** **deep ethnic/religious divisions** (Croats, Serbs, Bosniaks, Albanians; Catholic, Orthodox, Muslim), **economic disparities**, **weak central state after Tito's death (1980)**.
- **Result:** Yugoslavia broke up (1991-2001); ethnic war.

### Example 4 [4] — Gerrymandering

What's the difference between packing and cracking?
- **Packing:** concentrate opposing voters in **few districts** so they "waste" their majority.
- **Cracking:** **spread** opposing voters across **many districts** so they're minority in each.
- **Both reduce opposition's representation.**

### Example 5 [5] — Devolution

Trace devolution in the United Kingdom.
- **Pre-1997:** highly centralized.
- **1997-1999:** Scottish Parliament, Welsh Assembly, Northern Ireland Assembly created.
- **2014 Scottish independence referendum:** No (55-45).
- **2016 Brexit referendum:** Scotland voted to remain (62%) — fueled new independence sentiment.
- **Devolution as compromise:** maintain UK while granting regional autonomy.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing state and nation.** State: political entity with sovereignty. Nation: cultural group.
2. **Misidentifying nation-states.** Most countries are multinational. Pure nation-states (Japan, Iceland, Korea) are rare.
3. **Wrong state shape.** Compact (Uruguay), elongated (Chile), fragmented (Indonesia), perforated (South Africa around Lesotho), prorupted (Thailand).
4. **Confusing enclave/exclave.** Enclave: state surrounded by another. Exclave: part of state separated from main territory.
5. **Confusing unitary/federal.** Unitary: strong central (France). Federal: power shared (US, Germany).
6. **Mixing up gerrymandering types.** Packing concentrates; cracking dilutes.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For state/nation questions:**
- Distinguish concepts.
- Identify multinational/stateless examples.

**For boundary questions:**
- Identify type (geometric/physical/cultural; antecedent/subsequent/superimposed/relict).
- Address conflicts arising from boundaries.

**For centripetal/centrifugal questions:**
- List specific forces.
- Connect to outcomes (stability/breakup).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "A STATE is a politically organized territory with sovereignty (e.g., France); a NATION is a cultural group sharing identity (e.g., Kurds); a NATION-STATE is a state whose population is largely one nation (rare; e.g., Japan, Iceland)."
2. "Boundaries are categorized as geometric (e.g., US-Canada 49th parallel), physical (e.g., Rio Grande), or cultural; and as antecedent, subsequent, superimposed (e.g., colonial Africa from Berlin Conference 1884-85), or relict (e.g., former Berlin Wall)."
3. "Centripetal forces unite a state — common language, national identity, strong institutions, external threat. Centrifugal forces divide it — ethnic/religious divisions, economic inequality, separatist movements."
4. "Devolution transfers power from the central government to regional units. The UK devolved power to Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland; Spain devolved to Catalonia and the Basque Country."
5. "Gerrymandering manipulates district boundaries to favor a party. PACKING concentrates opposing voters in few districts; CRACKING spreads them across many districts to dilute their power."
