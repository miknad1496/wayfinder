## Big Ideas

1. **Urbanization** is one of defining trends of modern era; >55% of world urban.
2. **Cities organized in hierarchies** by size and function (Christaller, rank-size).
3. **Urban models** describe internal city structure (Burgess, Hoyt, Harris-Ullman).
4. **Cities in different regions** have different patterns (Latin America, Africa, Asia).
5. **Urban challenges:** sprawl, gentrification, slums, sustainability.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Urbanization basics:**
  - **Urbanization:** increase in % of population living in cities.
  - **Urban revolution:** ~5,500 BCE first cities (Mesopotamia, Egypt, Indus, China).
  - **Industrial Revolution** drove modern urbanization.
  - **Today:** ~57% of world urban (>80% in developed countries).
- **Types of cities by size/function:**
  - **Megacity:** >10 million population (Tokyo, Delhi, Shanghai, Sao Paulo, Mexico City, Cairo, Mumbai).
  - **Metacity:** >20 million.
  - **World city (global city):** centers of global economy (NYC, London, Tokyo, Shanghai, Hong Kong, Singapore).
- **Urban hierarchies:**
  - **Rank-size rule:** the nth largest city ~ 1/n the size of the largest. (US, Brazil approximate this.)
  - **Primate city:** disproportionately large; dominates country (Paris in France; Bangkok in Thailand; Mexico City; Lagos in Nigeria).
- **Christaller's Central Place Theory (1933):**
  - Cities provide goods/services to surrounding areas.
  - **Central place:** market center.
  - **Range:** maximum distance consumers travel for good.
  - **Threshold:** minimum population needed to support service.
  - **Hexagonal market areas** (most efficient packing).
  - **Hierarchy:** small towns provide low-order goods (groceries); larger cities provide high-order goods (specialty stores).
- **Urban models (North American):**
  - **Concentric Zone Model (Burgess, 1925):**
    - **Ring 1:** CBD (Central Business District).
    - **Ring 2:** transition zone (industry, slums).
    - **Ring 3:** working-class housing.
    - **Ring 4:** middle-class housing.
    - **Ring 5:** suburbs/commuter zone.
    - Based on Chicago.
  - **Sector Model (Hoyt, 1939):**
    - Wedges/sectors radiating from CBD along transportation lines.
    - High-rent sectors extend outward along desirable corridors.
    - Better captures transportation influence.
  - **Multiple Nuclei Model (Harris-Ullman, 1945):**
    - City has multiple centers (CBD + outlying business districts, industrial parks, residential nodes).
    - Better captures modern decentralized city.
- **City models in other regions:**
  - **Latin American (Griffin-Ford):**
    - **CBD with commercial spine** extending outward.
    - **Elite residential along spine.**
    - **Disamenity zones** (slums) on periphery.
  - **African city (de Blij):**
    - Three CBDs (colonial, traditional, market).
    - Ethnic neighborhoods.
    - Squatter settlements on outskirts.
  - **Southeast Asian city (McGee):**
    - **Port** is focal point (no traditional CBD).
    - Mixed land uses.
    - Suburban squatter areas.
- **Urban patterns in developed vs developing world:**
  - **Developed:** suburbanization; sprawl; central city decline then renewal; gentrification.
  - **Developing:** rapid urbanization; squatter settlements/slums; primate cities; informal economy.
- **Suburbanization and sprawl:**
  - **Suburbanization:** movement to suburbs (post-WWII US).
  - **Sprawl:** low-density development outward; consumes farmland; car-dependent.
  - **Edge cities:** suburban centers with offices, shopping (Tysons Corner VA).
- **Urban challenges:**
  - **Slums/squatter settlements:** unplanned, unauthorized housing (favelas in Brazil; barrios; townships in South Africa).
  - **Gentrification:** wealthy people move into poor neighborhoods, displace original residents.
  - **Food deserts.**
  - **Urban heat islands.**
  - **Inadequate infrastructure** in rapidly growing cities.
- **Smart growth and new urbanism:**
  - **Smart growth:** dense, walkable, transit-oriented development.
  - **New Urbanism:** architectural movement; mixed-use, walkable neighborhoods.
  - **Transit-oriented development.**
  - **Greenbelts:** undeveloped buffers around cities (London).

### Adds for [4]

- **Why primate cities form:**
  - **Colonial heritage:** colonial capital concentrated power, wealth.
  - **Economic concentration:** primate city attracts investment, talent.
  - **Political centralization.**
  - **Network effects:** big cities attract more people.
- **Christaller assumptions:**
  - **Flat, uniform terrain.**
  - **Even population distribution.**
  - **Rational consumers** travel to nearest center.
  - **Transportation costs uniform.**
  - **Real-world departures** (mountains, rivers, etc.) modify pattern.
- **Why Burgess model based on Chicago:**
  - **Industrial-era city** with rail/streetcar transportation.
  - **Walkable scale.**
  - **Less applicable** to car-dependent modern cities.
- **Latin American city features:**
  - **Colonial heritage** with central plaza (Spanish grid).
  - **Elite live near center** (unlike US).
  - **Slums on periphery** (favelas, barrios).
- **Gentrification dynamics:**
  - **Drivers:** declining urban housing prices + young professionals seeking urban living + investor capital.
  - **Effects:** rising rents; displacement of original residents; cultural change; new businesses.
  - **Cities affected:** SF, Brooklyn, Portland, Denver, Berlin.
- **Megacity challenges:**
  - **Lagos, Mumbai, Mexico City, Delhi:** rapid growth strains infrastructure.
  - **Slums** house large populations.
  - **Traffic, pollution, water scarcity.**
  - **Informal economy** dominant.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why urbanization matters.**
  - Defining trend of modern era.
  - >50% of world now urban (since ~2007).
  - Cities drive economic productivity.
  - Concentrate environmental impact.
  - Centers of innovation, culture, politics.
- **Why urban models matter.**
  - Help analyze and plan cities.
  - Reveal patterns of inequality, segregation.
  - Different regions follow different patterns.
- **Sustainable urbanism.**
  - Density reduces per-capita resource use.
  - Walkability, transit reduce emissions.
  - Mixed-use reduces commute.
  - Smart growth, new urbanism address sprawl.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Urban hierarchy

For each, identify rank-size or primate:
(a) US: NYC ~8.4M, LA ~4M, Chicago ~2.7M.
(b) France: Paris ~12M (metro), Marseille ~1.6M.

- (a) **Approximates rank-size** (LA close to NYC/2; Chicago close to NYC/3).
- (b) **Primate city** (Paris dominates; ~7x larger than next).

### Example 2 [3] — Urban models

For each, match to Burgess, Hoyt, or Harris-Ullman:
(a) Concentric rings around CBD.
(b) Wedges along transportation lines.
(c) Multiple business districts.

- (a) **Burgess (concentric).**
- (b) **Hoyt (sector).**
- (c) **Harris-Ullman (multiple nuclei).**

### Example 3 [4] — Christaller

Why are coffee shops more common than law firms in small towns?
- **Coffee shop has low threshold** (small population needed to support).
- **Coffee shop has low range** (people don't travel far).
- **Law firm has high threshold** (needs large population).
- **Law firm has high range** (people travel further for it).
- **Small towns have low-order services**; large cities have high-order.

### Example 4 [4] — Latin American city

Compare Latin American and US city patterns.
- **US (Burgess):** poor near center; wealthy in suburbs.
- **Latin American (Griffin-Ford):** **wealthy near center** along commercial spine; **slums on periphery** (favelas).
- **Different colonial heritage** explains.
- **Modern global flows** modify both.

### Example 5 [5] — Gentrification

What is gentrification and what are its effects?
- **Definition:** wealthy/educated people move into poor neighborhood, increasing property values.
- **Drivers:** declining urban prices; young professionals; investor capital; preference for urban living.
- **Effects:**
  - **Rising rents** displace original residents.
  - **Cultural change** (new businesses, cuisine).
  - **Improved infrastructure.**
  - **Loss of community.**
  - **Examples:** Williamsburg Brooklyn, Mission SF, Logan Square Chicago.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing urban models.** Burgess (rings, 1925), Hoyt (sectors, 1939), Harris-Ullman (multiple nuclei, 1945).
2. **Confusing rank-size and primate.** Rank-size: predictable hierarchy. Primate: one dominant.
3. **Confusing Christaller's range and threshold.** Range: max distance traveled. Threshold: minimum population.
4. **Wrong Latin American pattern.** Wealthy near center (commercial spine); slums on periphery.
5. **Confusing megacity definitions.** Megacity: >10 million. Metacity: >20 million.
6. **Wrong urbanization percentage.** ~57% world urban today.
