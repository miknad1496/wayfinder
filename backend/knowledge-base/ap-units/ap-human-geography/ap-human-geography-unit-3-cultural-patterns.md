## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 12–17% of the AP Human Geography exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** culture (folk vs popular); cultural landscape; language families and diffusion; religion (universalizing vs ethnic); ethnicity; cultural diffusion; globalization's cultural impacts.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Folk vs popular culture distinction. Universalizing vs ethnic religions. Major language families (Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan, etc.). Religious distributions and origins.

## Big Ideas

1. **Culture** = beliefs, practices, language, religion shared by a group.
2. **Folk vs popular culture** distinction is foundational.
3. **Languages** organized into families; spread through migration and conquest.
4. **Religions** divide into universalizing (seek converts) and ethnic (tied to people).
5. **Cultural landscapes** reflect cultural values.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Core culture concepts:**
  - **Culture:** shared beliefs, practices, values, language, religion of a group.
  - **Cultural landscape (Carl Sauer):** how culture shapes the visible environment.
  - **Material culture:** physical objects (clothing, buildings, tools).
  - **Non-material culture:** ideas, beliefs, practices.
- **Folk vs popular culture:**
  - **Folk culture:**
    - **Small, homogeneous groups** in isolated areas.
    - Slowly changing.
    - Tied to physical environment (clothing, food, architecture reflect local conditions).
    - Diffuses through **relocation**.
    - Examples: Amish, Indigenous cultures, traditional folk music.
  - **Popular culture:**
    - **Large, heterogeneous groups** across many places.
    - Rapidly changing.
    - Less tied to local environment.
    - Diffuses through **hierarchical and contagious expansion** (mass media, internet).
    - Examples: pop music, fast food, jeans, soccer.
- **Cultural homogenization vs heterogeneity:**
  - **Globalization** spreads popular culture (homogenization).
  - **Local cultures** persist or hybridize.
  - **Cultural convergence** vs **divergence.**
- **Language:**
  - **Language families** — broadest grouping; share common ancestor.
    - **Indo-European:** largest by speakers (English, Spanish, Hindi, Russian, etc.).
    - **Sino-Tibetan:** Chinese (Mandarin largest single language).
    - **Afro-Asiatic:** Arabic, Hebrew.
    - **Niger-Congo:** Sub-Saharan African.
    - **Austronesian:** Indonesian, Tagalog, Maori.
    - **Dravidian:** South India.
    - **Japanese, Korean** language isolates.
  - **Language branches** subgroups within families.
  - **Lingua franca:** common second language for trade/communication (English globally).
  - **Pidgin:** simplified hybrid for trade.
  - **Creole:** pidgin that becomes native (Haitian Creole).
  - **Dialects:** regional variations of language.
  - **Isogloss:** boundary between dialect features.
  - **Toponym:** place name; reveals cultural history.
- **Religion:**
  - **Universalizing religions:** seek to convert all; spread widely.
    - **Christianity:** ~2.4 billion. Origin: Israel/Palestine, ~1st century CE.
    - **Islam:** ~1.9 billion. Origin: Mecca, ~622 CE (Muhammad).
    - **Buddhism:** ~500 million. Origin: India, ~6th century BCE (Siddhartha Gautama).
    - **Sikhism:** ~25 million. Origin: Punjab, ~1500 CE (Guru Nanak).
  - **Ethnic religions:** tied to specific group; rarely seek converts.
    - **Hinduism:** ~1.2 billion. Origin: India.
    - **Judaism:** ~15 million. Origin: Levant.
    - **Shinto:** Japan.
    - **Confucianism, Daoism:** China (sometimes considered philosophies).
    - **Indigenous/animist** religions.
  - **Christian branches:** Catholicism (largest), Protestantism, Eastern Orthodoxy.
  - **Muslim branches:** Sunni (~85%), Shia (~15%, Iran, Iraq).
  - **Buddhist branches:** Mahayana, Theravada, Vajrayana.
- **Religious diffusion:**
  - **Christianity:** spread by missionaries, conquest (Roman Empire, colonial era).
  - **Islam:** spread by trade, conquest (caliphates).
  - **Buddhism:** spread by missionaries, trade routes.
- **Sacred spaces:**
  - **Mecca** (Islam): pilgrimage destination.
  - **Jerusalem:** sacred to Jews, Christians, Muslims.
  - **Vatican** (Catholicism).
  - **Varanasi** (Hinduism).
  - **Bodh Gaya** (Buddhism).
- **Religion and landscape:**
  - **Churches, mosques, temples** mark cultural landscape.
  - **Cemeteries** reveal religious traditions.
  - **Religious land use** (e.g., kosher/halal districts).
- **Ethnicity:**
  - Identity tied to shared ancestry, history, sometimes religion/language.
  - Different from race (more biological/phenotypic categorization).
  - **Ethnic enclaves** (Chinatowns, Little Italys).
  - **Ethnic conflict** (Yugoslavia 1990s, Rwanda 1994).
- **Cultural diffusion (review from Unit 1):**
  - Religion, language, food spread via diffusion processes.
  - **Lingua franca** = common second language for communication (English today).

### Adds for [4]

- **Folk culture preservation:**
  - Globalization threatens many folk cultures.
  - Preservation efforts (UNESCO heritage, indigenous rights).
  - Local resistance to homogenization.
- **Sacred sites and conflict:**
  - **Jerusalem:** contested by 3 religions.
  - **Ayodhya** (India): Hindu-Muslim conflict over site.
  - **Israel-Palestine** conflict has religious dimensions.
- **Language and identity:**
  - Language ties to nationalism (French in Quebec, Catalan in Spain, Welsh in Wales).
  - **Language preservation** efforts (Welsh, Irish, indigenous languages).
  - **Language extinction:** ~half of world's ~7,000 languages may disappear this century.
- **Religious geography:**
  - **Christianity:** Europe, Americas, Sub-Saharan Africa, Australia, Philippines.
  - **Islam:** Middle East, North Africa, Pakistan, Indonesia (largest Muslim country), Bangladesh.
  - **Hinduism:** India, Nepal.
  - **Buddhism:** East/Southeast Asia.
  - **Judaism:** Israel + diaspora (US, Europe).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why culture matters geographically.**
  - Culture shapes landscape, settlement, economy.
  - Cultural identity sources of conflict (Yugoslavia, Sri Lanka, Israel-Palestine).
  - Cultural tolerance/intolerance shapes societies.
- **Language extinction.**
  - Globalization, dominant languages displacing minority languages.
  - Loss of linguistic diversity = loss of cultural knowledge.
- **Religion in modern world.**
  - Sacred sites contested.
  - Religion shapes political movements (Iran's Islamic Revolution 1979; American Christian Right; Hindu nationalism in India).
  - Secularization in some regions (Western Europe); religious revival in others.

For each, identify folk or popular culture:
(a) Amish quilting traditions.
(b) Wearing jeans.
(c) Pop music.
(d) Traditional Hopi ceremonies.

- (a) **Folk.**
- (b) **Popular.**
- (c) **Popular.**
- (d) **Folk.**

(a) English, Spanish, Hindi.
(b) Mandarin Chinese.
(c) Arabic.
(d) Tagalog.

- (b) **Sino-Tibetan.**

### Example 3 [3] — Universalizing vs ethnic

For each, identify universalizing or ethnic:
(a) Christianity.
(b) Hinduism.
(c) Islam.
(d) Judaism.
(e) Buddhism.

- (a) **Universalizing.**
- (b) **Ethnic.**
- (c) **Universalizing.**
- (d) **Ethnic.**
- (e) **Universalizing.**

### Example 4 [4] — Religious diffusion

How did Islam spread?
- Origin: **Mecca, ~622 CE** (Muhammad).
- **Conquest:** Arab caliphates spread Islam across Middle East, North Africa, into Spain.
- **Trade:** Islam spread along trade routes (Indian Ocean, Sahara, Silk Roads).
- **Today:** ~1.9 billion Muslims; **Indonesia largest Muslim country.**

### Example 5 [5] — Cultural landscape

How does religion shape cultural landscape?
- **Religious buildings:** churches, mosques, temples define cityscapes.
- **Cemeteries:** burial customs reveal beliefs.
- **Sacred natural sites** preserved.
- **Land use:** religious districts; kosher/halal markets.
- **Toponyms:** place names (Mt. Olympus, Saint Louis).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing folk and popular culture.** Folk: small, slow, local. Popular: large, fast, widespread.
2. **Wrong religion classifications.** Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, Sikhism = universalizing. Hinduism, Judaism, Shinto = ethnic.
3. **Wrong religion origins.** Christianity (1st cent. CE, Israel/Palestine). Islam (622 CE, Arabia). Buddhism (6th cent. BCE, India). Hinduism (no single founder).
4. **Wrong Buddhist origin.** **India** (not China/Japan; spread there later).
5. **Wrong largest Muslim country.** **Indonesia** (~270M, more than Middle East combined).
6. **Confusing pidgin and creole.** Pidgin: simplified trade language. Creole: pidgin become native.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For folk/popular questions:**
- Identify which type.
- Address diffusion process.

**For religion questions:**
- Identify universalizing or ethnic.
- Identify origin and diffusion.

**For language questions:**
- Identify family and major examples.
- Address spread.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Folk culture characterizes small, homogeneous groups in isolated areas, changing slowly and tied to local environments — diffusing primarily through relocation. Popular culture characterizes large, heterogeneous groups across many places, changing rapidly through hierarchical and contagious expansion via mass media."
2. "Universalizing religions (Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, Sikhism) seek converts and spread widely. Ethnic religions (Hinduism, Judaism, Shinto) are tied to specific groups and rarely proselytize."
3. "Christianity originated in 1st-century Palestine and spread through Roman Empire and colonialism. Islam originated in 7th-century Arabia and spread through caliphate conquests and trade. Buddhism originated in 6th-century BCE India and spread through missionaries and trade routes."
4. "The Indo-European language family includes the largest number of speakers globally — English, Spanish, Hindi, Russian, Portuguese, Bengali, French, German. Sino-Tibetan (Mandarin Chinese) is second."
5. "Cultural landscapes — Carl Sauer's concept — reflect cultural values: religious buildings, cemeteries, settlement patterns, agricultural fields all show how culture shapes the visible environment."
