## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 8–10% of the AP Human Geography exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** geographic concepts (place, space, scale, region); maps and projections; geographic data; spatial analysis; human-environment interaction.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Foundational; vocabulary used throughout. Map projections (Mercator, Robinson, Goode's, etc.). Scale (local/regional/global). Region types (formal, functional, perceptual). GIS, GPS, remote sensing.

## Big Ideas

1. **Geography studies spatial patterns** — where things are and why they're there.
2. **Maps are tools but distort reality** — every projection trades off some accuracy.
3. **Scale matters** — patterns visible at one scale may not appear at another.
4. **Regions** are mental constructs we use to organize space.
5. **Geographers use multiple data tools** — GIS, GPS, remote sensing, census, fieldwork.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Core geographic concepts:**
  - **Place:** specific location with unique characteristics.
  - **Space:** extent in which things exist.
  - **Location:** where something is.
    - **Absolute location:** lat/long coordinates.
    - **Relative location:** location relative to other places.
  - **Site:** physical characteristics of a place.
  - **Situation:** location relative to surroundings.
  - **Distance:**
    - **Absolute distance:** measurable (miles, km).
    - **Relative distance:** time, cost, perception.
- **Spatial concepts:**
  - **Pattern:** geometric arrangement of objects in space.
  - **Distribution:** arrangement of features in space.
    - **Density:** quantity per unit area.
    - **Concentration:** clustered vs dispersed.
    - **Pattern:** linear, geometric, random.
  - **Diffusion:** spread of phenomena across space.
    - **Relocation diffusion:** movement of people carries idea.
    - **Expansion diffusion:**
      - **Contagious:** spreads broadly (e.g., disease).
      - **Hierarchical:** spreads through hierarchy (e.g., fashion from cities).
      - **Stimulus:** core idea spreads, adapted (e.g., McDonalds menu localized).
- **Scale:**
  - **Map scale:** ratio of map distance to real distance.
  - **Scale of analysis:** level at which phenomenon studied.
    - **Local, regional, national, global.**
  - **Patterns differ across scales.**
- **Maps:**
  - **Projections:** flat representation of curved Earth — every projection distorts something.
    - **Mercator:** preserves direction; distorts area (Greenland huge).
    - **Robinson:** balanced; minor distortions in all directions.
    - **Goode's homolosine:** preserves area; "interrupted" appearance.
    - **Peters:** preserves area; distorts shape.
  - **Map types:**
    - **Reference maps:** show locations (political, physical).
    - **Thematic maps:** show data themes.
      - **Choropleth:** shaded by quantity (income by county).
      - **Isoline (isarithmic):** lines of equal value (elevation, temp).
      - **Dot density:** dots represent quantities.
      - **Graduated/proportional symbol:** symbol size shows quantity.
      - **Cartogram:** size distorted to represent variable (population, GDP).
- **Regions:**
  - **Formal (uniform) region:** shared characteristic (e.g., French-speaking).
  - **Functional (nodal) region:** organized around node (e.g., metro area).
  - **Perceptual (vernacular) region:** based on perception (e.g., "the South").
- **Geographic data:**
  - **GIS (Geographic Information Systems):** layered digital mapping/analysis.
  - **GPS (Global Positioning System):** satellite location.
  - **Remote sensing:** data from satellites, aircraft (imagery, etc.).
  - **Census:** population data.
  - **Fieldwork:** direct observation.
- **Human-environment interaction:**
  - **Environmental determinism:** environment determines culture/development. (Largely rejected.)
  - **Possibilism:** environment sets limits but humans choose response. (More accepted.)
  - **Sustainability:** meeting present needs without compromising future.

### Adds for [4]

- **Map projections trade-offs:**
  - **Conformal:** preserves shape; distorts area (Mercator).
  - **Equal-area:** preserves area; distorts shape (Peters, Goode's).
  - **Compromise:** balanced (Robinson).
  - **Cannot preserve everything** — physical impossibility.
- **Diffusion examples:**
  - **Relocation:** Spanish carried language to Americas.
  - **Contagious expansion:** TikTok spread, virus spread.
  - **Hierarchical expansion:** McDonald's spread city → suburb.
  - **Stimulus expansion:** ice hockey spread (idea adopted, modified).
- **Scale of analysis matters:**
  - National-scale data hides regional differences.
  - Local-scale shows neighborhood patterns missed at national scale.
  - Always specify scale when analyzing.
- **GIS layers:**
  - Multiple layers (population, transportation, water, soil) overlaid.
  - Allows complex spatial analysis.
  - Used for urban planning, environmental analysis, public health, etc.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why geographic thinking matters.**
  - Spatial perspective unique to geography.
  - Helps understand interconnections.
  - Foundation for analyzing migration, development, urbanization, environment.
- **Map biases.**
  - **Eurocentrism:** Mercator centers Europe; makes northern continents look bigger.
  - **Choice of projection** carries political/cultural implications.
  - **Critical geography:** examine power in mapmaking.
- **GIS revolution.**
  - Transformed geography from descriptive to analytical.
  - Used in business (Walmart site selection), public health (COVID tracking), military, environmental science.
  - Big data + GIS = powerful spatial analysis.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Region types

For each, identify the region type:
(a) "The Bible Belt" (informal area where evangelical Christianity is strong).
(b) New York City metro area (commuting/economic ties).
(c) French-speaking Quebec.

- (a) **Perceptual (vernacular).**
- (b) **Functional (nodal).**
- (c) **Formal (uniform).**

### Example 2 [3] — Diffusion types

For each, identify the diffusion type:
(a) Spanish language spread to Americas via colonization.
(b) Virus spread person-to-person.
(c) Sushi spread from urban centers to small towns.
(d) Yoga spread, but Western practice differs from original.

- (a) **Relocation diffusion.**
- (b) **Contagious expansion diffusion.**
- (c) **Hierarchical expansion diffusion.**
- (d) **Stimulus expansion diffusion.**

### Example 3 [4] — Map projections

For each task, suggest appropriate projection:
(a) Comparing land area of countries.
(b) Marine navigation.
(c) General-purpose world map.

- (a) **Equal-area** (Peters or Goode's).
- (b) **Mercator** (preserves direction).
- (c) **Robinson** (balanced).

### Example 4 [4] — Scale of analysis

A national poverty map shows uniform poverty across a county. What might local-scale analysis reveal?
- **Within-county variation:** specific neighborhoods may have much higher or lower poverty than county average.
- **Hidden patterns** at smaller scales.
- **Importance of scale:** national patterns mask local realities.

### Example 5 [5] — GIS application

How might GIS be used in retail site selection?
- Layer demographic data (income, age, household size).
- Layer competitor locations.
- Layer transportation networks.
- Layer existing customer locations.
- **Identify optimal new store sites.**
- Walmart famously pioneered this approach.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing absolute and relative location.** Absolute: lat/long. Relative: in relation to other places.
2. **Confusing site and situation.** Site: physical features of place itself. Situation: location relative to surroundings.
3. **Confusing region types.** Formal (shared trait), functional (around node), perceptual (mental).
4. **Missing diffusion subtypes.** Expansion has THREE: contagious, hierarchical, stimulus.
5. **Confusing density and concentration.** Density: count per area. Concentration: clustered vs dispersed.
6. **Forgetting Mercator distorts area.** Greenland looks bigger than it is.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For map analysis questions:**
- Identify projection and what it preserves/distorts.
- Identify map type (choropleth, isoline, etc.).

**For region questions:**
- Identify type (formal, functional, perceptual).
- Justify with characteristics.

**For diffusion questions:**
- Identify specific subtype.
- Provide example.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Geographers analyze spatial patterns through concepts of location (absolute and relative), site (physical features), situation (location relative to surroundings), and scale of analysis (local to global)."
2. "Map projections all involve distortion: Mercator preserves direction (useful for navigation) but distorts area; Peters preserves area but distorts shape; Robinson balances distortions for general use."
3. "Diffusion takes multiple forms: relocation (people carry the trait), contagious expansion (spreads broadly), hierarchical expansion (through ranked structure), and stimulus expansion (core idea spreads, adapted locally)."
4. "Regions are categorized as formal (shared trait, e.g., language), functional (organized around a node, e.g., metro area), or perceptual (based on perception, e.g., 'the Midwest')."
5. "Modern geography uses GIS (layered spatial analysis), GPS (satellite positioning), and remote sensing (satellite/aircraft imagery) to analyze patterns invisible to traditional cartography."
