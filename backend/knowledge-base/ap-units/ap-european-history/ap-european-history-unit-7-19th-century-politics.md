## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP European History exam
- **Period:** c. 1815–1914
- **Sub-topics covered:** Romanticism; Realism; Revolutions of 1830 and 1848; nationalism; unification of Italy (1861) and Germany (1871); imperialism; cultural and intellectual developments (Darwin, Freud, Nietzsche).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Italian unification (Cavour, Garibaldi) and German unification (Bismarck) tested constantly. Revolutions of 1848 ("springtime of peoples") common. Imperialism (Scramble for Africa, Berlin Conference 1884-85) frequent. Darwin and Freud as cultural shifts.

## Big Ideas

1. **19th century shaped by nationalism, liberalism, conservatism, socialism.**
2. **Revolutions of 1848** ("springtime of peoples") swept Europe but mostly failed.
3. **Italian and German unification** (1860s-1871) reshaped European map.
4. **New imperialism** (1870-1914): "Scramble for Africa"; European powers carved up Africa and Asia.
5. **Cultural shifts:** Romanticism → Realism; Darwin's evolution; Freud's psychoanalysis; Nietzsche's critique.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Cultural movements:**
  - **Romanticism (early-mid 1800s):**
    - Reaction against Enlightenment rationalism.
    - **Emotion, individualism, nature, imagination.**
    - **Beethoven** (music), **Wordsworth, Byron, Shelley** (poetry), **Goethe** (literature), **Caspar David Friedrich** (painting).
  - **Realism (mid-late 1800s):**
    - Depicted ordinary life and social conditions.
    - **Dickens, Flaubert, Tolstoy, Dostoevsky** (literature).
- **Revolutions of 1830:**
  - **France:** July Revolution; overthrew Charles X; replaced with **Louis-Philippe** (constitutional monarch).
  - **Belgium:** independence from Netherlands.
- **Revolutions of 1848 ("Springtime of Peoples"):**
  - Spread across Europe (France, Germany, Italy, Austria, Hungary).
  - **Liberal and nationalist demands.**
  - **France:** Louis-Philippe overthrown; Second Republic; Louis-Napoleon (Napoleon III) elected president.
  - **Germany:** **Frankfurt Assembly** tried to unify Germany; failed.
  - **Italy:** revolts in various states; suppressed.
  - **Austria-Hungary:** Metternich resigned; Hungary briefly independent under Kossuth; suppressed by Russia.
  - **Most revolutions failed** by 1849.
- **Italian Unification (Risorgimento):**
  - **Giuseppe Mazzini:** "Young Italy"; nationalist ideology.
  - **Count Camillo di Cavour:** Prime Minister of Piedmont-Sardinia; **realpolitik**; allied with France.
  - **Giuseppe Garibaldi:** Red Shirts; conquered Sicily and southern Italy (1860).
  - **Kingdom of Italy** proclaimed (1861) under King Victor Emmanuel II.
  - **Rome** added (1870).
- **German Unification:**
  - **Otto von Bismarck:** Prussian Chancellor; "blood and iron"; **realpolitik**.
  - **Three wars** of unification:
    - **Danish War** (1864): vs Denmark; gained Schleswig-Holstein.
    - **Austro-Prussian War** (1866): defeated Austria; excluded from German affairs.
    - **Franco-Prussian War** (1870-1871): defeated France; **German Empire** proclaimed at Versailles (January 1871) under Kaiser Wilhelm I.
- **New Imperialism (~1870-1914):**
  - European powers carved up Africa and Asia.
  - **Berlin Conference (1884-1885):** divided Africa among European powers.
  - **Justifications:** economic (markets, resources); strategic; "civilizing mission" (paternalistic, racist); social Darwinism.
  - **British Empire:** largest; "sun never sets."
  - **France:** Africa, Indochina.
  - **Germany, Belgium, Italy, Portugal, Spain** also colonies.
  - **King Leopold II's Congo Free State** brutal exploitation.
- **Late 19th-century intellectual shifts:**
  - **Charles Darwin (1809-1882):** *On the Origin of Species* (1859). **Evolution by natural selection.** Challenged biblical creation.
  - **Social Darwinism:** misapplied Darwin to society; "survival of the fittest" justified inequality, imperialism, racism. (Herbert Spencer.)
  - **Sigmund Freud (1856-1939):** psychoanalysis; unconscious mind; *The Interpretation of Dreams* (1900).
  - **Friedrich Nietzsche (1844-1900):** critique of Christianity and rationalism; "God is dead"; will to power.
- **Late 19th-century politics:**
  - **France:** Third Republic (after 1870 fall of Napoleon III).
  - **Britain:** continued Reform Bills; Liberal vs Conservative parties; rise of Labour Party.
  - **Germany:** unified empire under Bismarck; **Kulturkampf** vs Catholics; **anti-socialist laws**.
  - **Russia:** **Alexander II** emancipated serfs (1861); assassinated 1881; subsequent reaction.
  - **Austria-Hungary:** **Compromise of 1867 (Ausgleich):** dual monarchy.
- **Women's movements:**
  - **Suffragists:** demand vote for women.
  - **Suffragettes** in Britain (Pankhursts, militant tactics).
  - **First-wave feminism** broadly.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why Revolutions of 1848 failed:**
  - **Divisions** among revolutionaries (liberals vs radicals; nationalist conflicts).
  - **Conservative recovery** by armies (Russia helped Austria).
  - **Lack of working-class support** in some places.
  - **Bourgeois fear** of radical revolution (especially after June Days in France).
- **Realpolitik:**
  - Practical politics based on interests, not ideology.
  - **Bismarck** epitome.
  - Justified strategic use of war and diplomacy for state goals.
- **Bismarck's diplomacy after unification:**
  - **Three Emperors' League** (Germany, Austria, Russia).
  - **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria, Italy, 1882).
  - Goal: isolate France; prevent two-front war.
  - System unraveled after Bismarck's dismissal (1890) by Wilhelm II.
- **Causes of New Imperialism:**
  - **Industrial economies** needed raw materials and markets.
  - **National prestige.**
  - **Strategic competition** among European powers.
  - **"Civilizing mission"** ideology.
  - **Social Darwinism.**
  - **Improvements** (steamships, machine guns, quinine for malaria) made deeper penetration possible.
- **Effects of imperialism:**
  - Massive transfer of resources from Africa/Asia to Europe.
  - Brutal exploitation and violence (Congo, Algeria, India).
  - Disrupted traditional societies.
  - Created colonial elites.
  - Sowed seeds of 20th-century anti-colonial movements.
- **Intellectual impact of Darwin:**
  - Challenged literal biblical interpretation.
  - Provoked religious controversy (Scopes-style debates).
  - Misapplied as Social Darwinism (Spencer).
  - Influenced 20th-century eugenics movements.
- **Freud's significance:**
  - Unconscious mind drives behavior.
  - Childhood experiences shape adult personality.
  - Repression and defense mechanisms.
  - Influence on psychology, literature, art.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Italian/German unification mattered.**
  - Created two new great powers in middle of Europe.
  - **Disrupted balance of power** established at Vienna (1815).
  - **Germany** especially became dominant continental power, setting up tensions with Britain and France.
  - Set stage for World War I.
- **Why Imperialism mattered.**
  - Massive transfer of wealth and resources to Europe.
  - Created modern global economy.
  - Built racial and civilizational hierarchies that persist.
  - Sowed seeds of 20th-century decolonization struggles.
- **Why intellectual shifts mattered.**
  - Darwin: biology and origins of humanity.
  - Freud: human psychology.
  - Nietzsche: meaning, morality.
  - Together: undermined certainties of 19th century; foundation of modernist 20th century.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying figures

For each, identify the figure and contribution:
(a) Italian unification — Prime Minister of Piedmont; realpolitik.
(b) Italian unification — Red Shirts; conquered south.
(c) German unification — "blood and iron"; three wars.

- (a) **Cavour.**
- (b) **Garibaldi.**
- (c) **Bismarck.**

### Example 2 [3] — Revolutions of 1848

What happened in 1848?
- Revolutions across Europe (France, Germany, Italy, Austria, Hungary).
- Demands for **liberal constitutions** and **national unification**.
- France: Louis-Philippe overthrown; Second Republic.
- Germany: Frankfurt Assembly tried unification; failed.
- Italy/Austria: revolts suppressed.
- **Most revolutions failed** by 1849; conservative restoration.

### Example 3 [4] — German unification

How did Bismarck unify Germany?
- **Realpolitik** strategy.
- **Three wars:**
  - **Danish War (1864):** gained Schleswig-Holstein.
  - **Austro-Prussian War (1866):** excluded Austria from Germany.
  - **Franco-Prussian War (1870-1871):** defeated France; Alsace-Lorraine taken.
- **German Empire proclaimed** at Versailles (January 1871) under Kaiser Wilhelm I.
- **Significance:** new dominant continental power; shifted European balance.

### Example 4 [4] — Causes of New Imperialism

What caused European imperialism (~1870-1914)?
- **Economic:** industrial economies need raw materials, markets.
- **Strategic:** great-power competition; naval bases.
- **Ideological:** "civilizing mission"; social Darwinism; nationalism.
- **Technology:** steamships, machine guns, quinine made conquest feasible.
- **Berlin Conference (1884-1885)** divided Africa among European powers.

### Example 5 [5] — Intellectual shifts

How did Darwin, Freud, and Nietzsche shift 19th-century thought?
- **Darwin:** evolution by natural selection (*Origin of Species*, 1859); challenged biblical creation; misapplied as Social Darwinism.
- **Freud:** unconscious mind drives behavior (*Interpretation of Dreams*, 1900); childhood shapes personality.
- **Nietzsche:** critique of Christianity and rationalism; "God is dead"; will to power.
- **Combined:** undermined Victorian certainties; foundation of modernist 20th century.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing 1830 and 1848 revolutions.** 1830: France (July Revolution) and Belgium. 1848: widespread; mostly failed.
2. **Confusing Cavour, Mazzini, Garibaldi.** Mazzini: ideologue ("Young Italy"). Cavour: politician (Piedmont). Garibaldi: military (Red Shirts).
3. **Wrong Bismarck dates.** Three wars: 1864, 1866, 1870-71. Empire proclaimed January 1871.
4. **Confusing Berlin Conference and Berlin Congress.** **Berlin Conference (1884-85):** divided Africa. **Congress of Berlin (1878):** Balkans after Russo-Turkish War.
5. **Misattributing Social Darwinism.** Darwin himself didn't apply it to society. **Herbert Spencer** popularized "survival of the fittest."
6. **Wrong Darwin date.** *On the Origin of Species* — **1859**.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For unification essays:**
- Identify key figures (Cavour, Garibaldi for Italy; Bismarck for Germany).
- Address realpolitik strategy.
- Address timeline of wars and proclamations.

**For imperialism essays:**
- Address multiple causes (economic, strategic, ideological, technological).
- Address effects on Africa/Asia AND Europe.
- Address Berlin Conference (1884-85).

**For intellectual essays:**
- Address Darwin, Freud, Nietzsche specifically.
- Address impact on broader culture.

4. "The 'New Imperialism' (c. 1870-1914) was driven by industrial demand for raw materials, great-power competition, the 'civilizing mission' ideology, and Social Darwinism. The Berlin Conference (1884-1885) divided Africa among European powers."
5. "Darwin's *On the Origin of Species* (1859), Freud's *Interpretation of Dreams* (1900), and Nietzsche's critique of Christianity collectively undermined Victorian certainties about religion, rationality, and human nature, laying the foundation for the modernist culture of the early 20th century."
