# AP European History — Unit 8: 20th-Century Global Conflicts (c. 1914–1945) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP European History exam
- **Period:** c. 1914–1945
- **Sub-topics covered:** World War I (causes, course, consequences); Russian Revolution (1917); interwar period (Versailles Treaty, Great Depression); rise of totalitarianism (Stalin, Mussolini, Hitler); World War II (course, Holocaust, consequences).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** WWI causes (MAIN: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism) tested constantly. Treaty of Versailles (1919) and its failures. Russian Revolution (Bolsheviks, Lenin, Stalin). Nazi rise. Holocaust. WWII course and consequences.

## Big Ideas

1. **WWI (1914-1918) shattered 19th-century European order.** Caused by MAIN factors + assassination spark.
2. **Russian Revolution (1917)** brought first communist state. Bolsheviks → Lenin → Stalin's totalitarianism.
3. **Versailles Treaty (1919)** humiliated Germany; sowed seeds of WWII.
4. **Great Depression (1929+)** destabilized Europe; opened door to fascism.
5. **WWII (1939-1945)** + **Holocaust** caused unprecedented destruction; ended European global dominance; began Cold War.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Causes of WWI ("MAIN"):**
  - **Militarism:** arms race, especially Anglo-German naval rivalry.
  - **Alliances:** **Triple Entente** (France, Russia, Britain) vs **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy).
  - **Imperialism:** competition for colonies and prestige.
  - **Nationalism:** especially in Balkans ("powder keg of Europe").
  - **Spark:** assassination of **Archduke Franz Ferdinand** (heir to Austrian throne) in **Sarajevo** by **Gavrilo Princip** (Serbian nationalist), **June 28, 1914**.
- **Course of WWI:**
  - **August 1914:** war declared; Germany invades Belgium (Schlieffen Plan).
  - **Western Front:** trench warfare; stalemate; battles of **Verdun, Somme, Marne**.
  - **Eastern Front:** more mobile; Russia struggles.
  - **New technologies:** machine guns, poison gas, tanks, airplanes, submarines.
  - **US enters** (April 1917) after **Lusitania (1915)**, **Zimmermann Telegram (1917)**.
  - **Russia exits** (March 1918, **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk**) due to revolution.
  - **Armistice:** **November 11, 1918**.
- **Treaty of Versailles (1919):**
  - Germany blamed (**War Guilt Clause, Article 231**).
  - **Reparations** (~$33 billion).
  - **Territorial losses:** Alsace-Lorraine to France; lost colonies; Polish Corridor.
  - **Military restrictions:** army limited to 100,000; no air force; demilitarized Rhineland.
  - **League of Nations** created (Wilson's idea).
  - **US Senate rejected treaty**; US never joined League.
  - **German resentment** sowed seeds of WWII.
- **Russian Revolution (1917):**
  - **Long-term causes:** autocracy, peasant misery, industrial workers' grievances, defeat in Russo-Japanese War (1905), 1905 Revolution.
  - **WWI catastrophe** for Russia: massive casualties, food shortages, government collapse.
  - **February Revolution (March 1917):** Tsar **Nicholas II** abdicated; Provisional Government.
  - **October Revolution (November 1917):** **Bolsheviks** under **Vladimir Lenin** seized power.
  - **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk** (March 1918): Russia exits WWI; cedes vast territory.
  - **Russian Civil War** (1918-1921): Reds vs Whites; Bolsheviks win.
  - **USSR formed** (1922).
- **Lenin and Stalin:**
  - **Lenin:** Bolshevik leader; communist state; **War Communism** then **NEP (New Economic Policy, 1921)**.
  - **Lenin died 1924; Stalin won power struggle** vs Trotsky.
  - **Joseph Stalin (1878-1953):**
    - **Five-Year Plans** (1928+): rapid industrialization.
    - **Forced collectivization** of agriculture.
    - **Kulaks** persecuted; **Ukraine famine (Holodomor, 1932-1933)**: millions died.
    - **Great Purges** (1936-1938): show trials; ~1 million executed.
    - **Totalitarian dictatorship.**
- **Interwar period:**
  - **1920s:** brief recovery; **Roaring Twenties**; Weimar Republic in Germany.
  - **Great Depression (1929+):** US stock market crash spread globally; mass unemployment.
  - **Failure of democratic governments** in many countries.
  - **Rise of fascism** as response to economic crisis.
- **Mussolini and Italian Fascism:**
  - **Benito Mussolini** founded **Fascist Party** (1919).
  - **March on Rome (1922):** Mussolini named Prime Minister.
  - Established **dictatorship**: outlawed opposition, controlled press.
  - **Corporatism**: state-controlled economy.
  - Invaded **Ethiopia (1935-1936).**
- **Hitler and Nazi Germany:**
  - **Adolf Hitler (1889-1945):** Austrian-born; WWI veteran; embittered by Versailles.
  - **Nazi Party** (NSDAP) — National Socialist German Workers' Party.
  - **Beer Hall Putsch (1923):** failed coup; jailed; wrote ***Mein Kampf***.
  - **Nazis exploited Depression**; gained votes.
  - **Hitler appointed Chancellor (January 30, 1933)**.
  - **Reichstag Fire (February 1933):** used to suspend civil liberties.
  - **Enabling Act (March 1933):** dictatorial power.
  - **One-party state** by mid-1933.
  - **Nuremberg Laws (1935):** stripped Jews of citizenship.
  - **Kristallnacht (November 1938):** Nazi-organized violence vs Jews.
  - **Rearmament; remilitarization of Rhineland (1936); annexation of Austria (1938); Sudetenland (1938); Czechoslovakia (1939).**
- **Appeasement:**
  - **Munich Conference (September 1938):** Britain, France allowed Germany to take Sudetenland.
  - **Neville Chamberlain:** "peace for our time."
  - **Appeasement failed**: Hitler invaded rest of Czechoslovakia (March 1939).
- **WWII (1939-1945):**
  - **Nazi-Soviet Pact (August 1939):** non-aggression; secret protocols divided Eastern Europe.
  - **Germany invades Poland (September 1, 1939)**: WWII begins.
  - **Blitzkrieg:** Poland (1939), Denmark/Norway, Low Countries, France (1940). France fell June 1940.
  - **Battle of Britain (1940):** RAF defeated Luftwaffe.
  - **Operation Barbarossa (June 1941):** Germany invades USSR; broke Nazi-Soviet Pact.
  - **Pearl Harbor (December 7, 1941):** Japan attacks; US enters war.
  - **Stalingrad (1942-1943):** turning point on Eastern Front.
  - **D-Day (June 6, 1944):** Allied invasion of Normandy.
  - **Soviet advance from East; Allied advance from West.**
  - **Battle of the Bulge (December 1944).**
  - **Hitler suicide (April 30, 1945)**; Berlin falls.
  - **VE Day (May 8, 1945)**: Europe.
  - **VJ Day (August 15, 1945)**: after atomic bombs (Hiroshima August 6, Nagasaki August 9).
- **Holocaust:**
  - Nazi systematic murder of **6 million Jews** + millions of Roma, disabled, Soviet POWs, political opponents.
  - **Concentration camps** then **death camps** (Auschwitz, Treblinka, Sobibor, etc.).
  - **Wannsee Conference (January 1942):** "Final Solution" coordinated.
  - Holocaust scale unprecedented.
- **WWII consequences:**
  - **~70-85 million dead globally** (military + civilian).
  - **Europe devastated.**
  - **US and USSR emerged as superpowers.**
  - **United Nations founded (1945).**
  - **Cold War began.**
  - **Decolonization accelerated.**

### Adds for [4]

- **Why WWI happened:**
  - System of **interlocking alliances** turned regional crisis into world war.
  - **War plans** (especially German Schlieffen Plan) prioritized mobilization speed over diplomacy.
  - **Imperial competition** generated tensions.
  - **Nationalism** in Balkans created tinderbox.
  - **Assassination** triggered cascade.
- **Why Versailles failed:**
  - **Too harsh** to reconcile Germany; not harsh enough to prevent revival.
  - **Reparations** wrecked German economy.
  - **War guilt clause** humiliated Germany.
  - **US absent** from League.
  - **Self-determination applied selectively** (not to colonies).
- **Why Bolsheviks won:**
  - Provisional Government continued unpopular war.
  - **Lenin** offered **"Peace, Land, Bread."**
  - Bolsheviks well-organized; ruthless.
  - **Trotsky** organized Red Army.
  - Whites disorganized; foreign intervention limited.
- **Stalin's industrialization:**
  - **Five-Year Plans** transformed USSR into industrial power.
  - **Cost:** mass suffering; ~10 million deaths from collectivization, famine, purges.
  - **Strategic outcome:** USSR survived Nazi invasion (1941+) due to industrial base.
- **Why fascism appealed:**
  - **Economic crisis** (Depression) discredited liberal capitalism.
  - **Communist threat** scared middle class and elites.
  - **Versailles humiliation** in Germany created resentment.
  - **Nationalist appeal** to greatness.
  - **Strong leader cult** offered certainty.
- **Why appeasement failed:**
  - Misjudged Hitler's intentions.
  - Believed Versailles was unfair; some German revisions reasonable.
  - Wanted to avoid another war.
  - **Sold out Czechs at Munich.**
  - Encouraged Hitler's aggression rather than deterring it.
- **Holocaust mechanism:**
  - **Nuremberg Laws (1935):** legal exclusion of Jews.
  - **Kristallnacht (1938):** violent escalation.
  - **Ghettos** in occupied Poland.
  - **Einsatzgruppen** mass shootings (Babi Yar, etc.).
  - **Wannsee Conference (1942):** "Final Solution" coordinated.
  - **Death camps** for industrial murder (especially Auschwitz).

### Adds for [5]

- **Why WWI mattered.**
  - Ended European dominance.
  - Brought down four empires (German, Austrian, Russian, Ottoman).
  - Created new states in Eastern Europe.
  - Sowed seeds of WWII.
  - Russian Revolution → first communist state.
- **Why Russian Revolution mattered.**
  - First successful Marxist revolution.
  - Created USSR; shaped 20th century.
  - Inspired/aided communist movements worldwide.
  - Created Cold War antagonism with West.
- **Why Holocaust matters.**
  - Industrial-scale genocide unprecedented.
  - Foundation of modern human rights law (Nuremberg Trials, UN Declaration).
  - "Holocaust uniqueness" debate continues.
  - Israel founded (1948) partly in response.
- **Why WWII mattered.**
  - Unprecedented destruction (~70-85 million dead).
  - Ended European global dominance.
  - Created superpowers (US, USSR).
  - Set up Cold War.
  - Accelerated decolonization.
  - UN, IMF, World Bank, modern international order.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Causes of WWI

What does "MAIN" stand for as causes of WWI?
- **M**ilitarism (arms race).
- **A**lliances (Triple Entente vs Triple Alliance).
- **I**mperialism (colonial competition).
- **N**ationalism (especially Balkans).
- **Spark:** assassination of **Archduke Franz Ferdinand** in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip (June 28, 1914).

### Example 2 [3] — Russian Revolution

Sequence the Russian Revolution.
- **February 1917:** Tsar Nicholas II abdicated; Provisional Government formed.
- **October 1917:** Bolsheviks under Lenin seized power.
- **March 1918:** Treaty of Brest-Litovsk; Russia exits WWI.
- **1918-1921:** Russian Civil War; Bolsheviks (Reds) defeat Whites.
- **1922:** USSR formed.

### Example 3 [4] — Why Hitler rose

How did Hitler come to power?
- **Versailles humiliation** generated resentment.
- **Great Depression (1929+)** destabilized Weimar Republic.
- **Nazi propaganda** appealed to multiple groups.
- **Conservative elites** thought they could control Hitler.
- **Hitler appointed Chancellor (January 30, 1933).**
- **Reichstag Fire (February)** used to suspend civil liberties.
- **Enabling Act (March)** gave dictatorial power.
- **One-party state** by mid-1933.

### Example 4 [4] — Holocaust mechanism

Trace the escalation of Nazi anti-Jewish policy.
- **1933:** boycott of Jewish businesses.
- **1935:** Nuremberg Laws stripped citizenship.
- **1938:** Kristallnacht (organized violence).
- **1939+:** ghettos in occupied Poland.
- **1941:** Einsatzgruppen mass shootings in USSR.
- **January 1942:** Wannsee Conference coordinated "Final Solution."
- **Death camps** (especially Auschwitz) for industrial murder.
- **~6 million Jews killed** + millions of others.

### Example 5 [5] — WWII consequences

What were the major consequences of WWII?
- **~70-85 million dead globally.**
- **Europe devastated** physically and economically.
- **US and USSR** emerged as superpowers.
- **Cold War** began (Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, Berlin Blockade).
- **United Nations** founded (1945).
- **Decolonization** accelerated.
- **Holocaust** transformed concepts of human rights.
- **End of European global dominance** that began ~1500.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Wrong WWI dates.** Started **August 1914**; ended **November 11, 1918**.
2. **Confusing alliances.** **Triple Entente:** France, Russia, Britain. **Triple Alliance:** Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy.
3. **Wrong Russian Revolution dates.** February (March) and October (November) 1917 (Old Style/New Style calendar).
4. **Confusing Lenin and Stalin.** Lenin: 1917 revolution; led until death 1924. Stalin: power struggle 1924-1928; ruled 1928-1953.
5. **Wrong Hitler appointment.** Chancellor **January 30, 1933** (not seized power; legally appointed).
6. **Wrong WWII start.** Germany invades Poland **September 1, 1939**.
7. **Wrong D-Day date.** **June 6, 1944.**

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For WWI causation essays:**
- Use MAIN framework.
- Address spark (assassination) AND structural causes.
- Address how alliance system spread war.

**For Russian Revolution essays:**
- Distinguish February and October revolutions.
- Address Bolshevik appeal ("Peace, Land, Bread").
- Address Civil War.

**For totalitarianism essays:**
- Compare/contrast Mussolini, Hitler, Stalin.
- Address economic crisis as enabling.
- Address ideology and methods of control.

**For WWII essays:**
- Address appeasement failures.
- Address Holocaust.
- Address consequences (superpowers, UN, decolonization).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "World War I (1914-1918) erupted from converging structural causes — militarism, alliances, imperialism, and nationalism — triggered by the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo (June 28, 1914). The interlocking alliance system turned a regional Balkan crisis into global war."
2. "The Russian Revolution unfolded in two phases in 1917: the February Revolution that overthrew Tsar Nicholas II and the October Revolution in which Lenin's Bolsheviks seized power, promising 'Peace, Land, Bread.' The resulting USSR became the first Marxist state."
3. "The Treaty of Versailles (1919) imposed war guilt, $33 billion in reparations, territorial losses, and military restrictions on Germany — humiliations that, combined with the Great Depression, opened the door to Hitler's rise."
4. "Hitler exploited Depression-era discontent and Versailles resentment to come to power legally — appointed Chancellor January 30, 1933 — then used the Reichstag Fire and Enabling Act to establish a one-party totalitarian state by mid-1933."
5. "The Holocaust — the Nazi systematic murder of approximately six million Jews and millions of Roma, disabled, Soviet POWs, and political opponents — escalated from the Nuremberg Laws (1935) and Kristallnacht (1938) to the Wannsee Conference (January 1942) and industrial-scale killing in death camps."
