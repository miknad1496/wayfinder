- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Unit 1 establishes foundations of early modern Europe. Italian Renaissance (Florence, Medici, humanism), Northern Renaissance (Erasmus), Age of Exploration, and the Commercial Revolution are perennial topics.

## Big Ideas

1. **The Italian Renaissance (~1300-1600) was a cultural rebirth centered on classical learning, humanism, and individualism.** Florence, Venice, Rome were major centers.
2. **Printing press (Gutenberg, 1450s) revolutionized communication.** Enabled spread of ideas, eventually Reformation.
3. **New Monarchies consolidated power** — Spain (Ferdinand and Isabella), France (Louis XI), England (Henry VII).
4. **Age of Exploration (1450s onward) integrated Europe into global trade.** Spanish and Portuguese pioneered.
5. **Atlantic Slave Trade** integrated Europe, Africa, and Americas in tragic system.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Italian Renaissance:**
  - **Renaissance** = "rebirth." Revival of classical (Greek/Roman) learning.
  - **Florence:** epicenter under Medici family.
  - **Patronage:** wealthy supporters (Medici, popes) funded artists and scholars.
  - **Humanism:** focus on human potential, classical learning, individual achievement.
  - **Petrarch:** "father of humanism." Latin and Greek classics.
  - **Major artists:** Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, Raphael.
  - **Major works:** Mona Lisa, Sistine Chapel, David, School of Athens.
  - **Architectural innovation:** Brunelleschi's dome (Florence Cathedral).
- **Civic humanism:** humanism applied to civic life, public service.
- **Political philosophy:**
  - **Machiavelli (1469-1527):** *The Prince* (1513). "Ends justify means" pragmatic political philosophy.
- **Northern Renaissance:**
  - Spread north of Italy (Netherlands, Germany, England, France) by 1500.
  - **Christian humanism:** focus on early Christian sources, ethics.
  - **Erasmus (1466-1536):** Dutch humanist. *In Praise of Folly* satirized Church.
  - **Thomas More (1478-1535):** *Utopia* (1516).
  - **Northern art:** Albrecht Dürer (Germany), Jan van Eyck (Netherlands).
- **Printing Press (Gutenberg, ~1450s):**
  - Movable type printing.
  - First book: Gutenberg Bible.
  - **Effects:** mass production of books; literacy expansion; spread of ideas; eventually Reformation.
- **New Monarchies (centralized power):**
  - **Spain:** Ferdinand and Isabella (married 1469); united Aragon and Castile; expelled Jews and Moors (1492); funded Columbus.
  - **France:** Louis XI ("Spider King," r. 1461-1483); centralized administration.
  - **England:** Henry VII (Tudor dynasty after Wars of the Roses); restored royal authority.
- **Age of Exploration:**
  - **Why explore:** spices, gold, glory, religion (Christianizing), bypass Italian/Muslim middlemen.
  - **Technology enabling:** caravel ship, magnetic compass, astrolabe, gunpowder.
  - **Portuguese:** **Henry the Navigator** sponsored exploration of African coast.
  - **Bartolomeu Dias** rounded Cape of Good Hope (1488).
  - **Vasco da Gama** reached India (1498).
  - **Pedro Cabral** reached Brazil (1500).
- **Spanish exploration:**
  - **Christopher Columbus** (1492): four voyages to Americas (still believing he reached Asia).
  - **Treaty of Tordesillas (1494):** divided New World between Spain and Portugal.
  - **Hernán Cortés** conquered Aztec Empire (1519-1521).
  - **Francisco Pizarro** conquered Inca Empire (1532-1533).
- **Other European exploration:**
  - **Dutch:** colonies in Indonesia, Caribbean, briefly Brazil.
  - **English:** founded Jamestown (1607).
  - **French:** Canada, Louisiana.
- **Columbian Exchange:**
  - **From Old to New World:** smallpox (devastating Native populations), wheat, sugar, horses, cattle.
  - **From New to Old World:** corn, potatoes, tomatoes, cacao, tobacco.
  - **Demographic catastrophe:** ~80-95% of Native populations died from disease.
- **Atlantic Slave Trade:**
  - ~12 million Africans transported to Americas (1500-1800s).
  - **Triangular Trade:** European goods to Africa → enslaved Africans to Americas → American sugar, tobacco to Europe.
- **Commercial Revolution (1500s-1700s):**
  - Massive expansion of trade, banking, capitalism.
  - **Joint-stock companies:** Dutch East India Company (1602), British East India Company (1600). Pooled investment for risky ventures.
  - **Mercantilism:** economic theory that nations grow rich by accumulating gold/silver, exporting more than importing.
  - **Inflation:** "Price Revolution" — Spanish silver from Americas caused European inflation.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why Italian Renaissance flourished in Italy.**
  - Italian city-states' commercial wealth.
  - Trade with Byzantine Empire preserved classical knowledge.
  - Wealthy patrons (Medici especially) funded culture.
  - Italian peninsula was crossroads of Mediterranean trade.
- **Italian Renaissance characteristics:**
  - **Individualism:** focus on individual genius, achievement.
  - **Secularism:** focus on this world, not just afterlife.
  - **Classicism:** revival of Greek and Roman culture.
  - **Realism in art:** linear perspective, anatomy, naturalistic depiction.
- **Major Renaissance artistic innovations:**
  - **Linear perspective** (Brunelleschi).
  - **Chiaroscuro** (light/shadow contrast — Leonardo, Caravaggio).
  - **Sfumato** (smoky transitions — Leonardo).
  - **Anatomical accuracy** (Michelangelo's David).
- **Northern vs Italian Renaissance:**
  - Italian: more secular, classical focus.
  - Northern: Christian humanism, religious reform agenda.
- **Why printing press changed Europe.**
  - Books cheaper, more accessible.
  - Literacy increased.
  - Local languages flourished (vernacular books).
  - Religious texts available to laypeople (foreshadowed Reformation).
  - Scientific knowledge spread.
- **Causes of European exploration:**
  - **Economic:** spices, gold, markets.
  - **Religious:** Christianizing, finding mythical Prester John.
  - **Political:** national prestige.
  - **Technological:** caravel, compass, astrolabe.
  - **Reconquista finished** (1492 in Spain) freed military energies.
- **Why Spanish silver caused European inflation.**
  - Massive influx of silver from Americas.
  - Increased money supply faster than economic production.
  - Prices rose dramatically.
  - "Price Revolution" (1500s-1600s).
- **Mercantilism details:**
  - Goal: positive balance of trade (export more than import).
  - Colonies served as sources of raw materials AND markets for finished goods.
  - Government regulated economy.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why understand the Renaissance for Europe.** It's the foundation of modern Western culture: art, science, individualism, secularism. Many ideas we associate with "Western civilization" originate here.
- **Machiavelli's enduring influence.** *The Prince* still studied in political science. Pragmatic, realistic political analysis (vs idealistic) shaped modern political thought.
- **Why printing was revolutionary.** Sometimes compared to internet — democratized access to information; enabled new movements (Reformation), new fields (modern science), new forms of community (national vernacular literatures).
- **Spanish gold/silver paradox.** Despite vast American wealth, Spain's economy declined relative to other European powers in 1600s. Reasons: dependence on imports, expulsion of productive minorities (Jews, Moors), heavy religious wars, inflation.
- **The "first globalization" thesis.** Some historians argue Atlantic trade integration after 1500 was the first true globalization. Foundations of modern capitalism, slavery, racial categories, global commerce all date from this period.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Renaissance figures

For each: identify the Renaissance figure.
(a) Florentine artist who painted Mona Lisa.
(b) "Father of humanism."
(c) Author of *The Prince*.
(d) Dutch Christian humanist who wrote *In Praise of Folly*.

- (a) **Leonardo da Vinci.**
- (b) **Petrarch.**
- (c) **Niccolò Machiavelli.**
- (d) **Desiderius Erasmus.**

### Example 2 [3] — Printing press effects

How did Gutenberg's printing press change Europe?
- **Mass production of books** at lower cost.
- **Increased literacy** and access to information.
- **Spread of ideas** including religious reform (later Reformation).
- **Vernacular languages** flourished in print.
- **Standardized texts** spread Renaissance learning.
- Some historians argue it was as transformative as the internet today.

### Example 3 [4] — Causes of exploration

Why did Europeans begin maritime exploration in the 1400s?
- **Economic:** desire for Asian spices, gold, new markets.
- **Religious:** spread Christianity, find legendary Prester John.
- **Political:** national prestige, competition.
- **Technological:** caravel, compass, astrolabe.
- **Bypass Italian/Muslim middlemen** in Mediterranean spice trade.

### Example 4 [4] — Columbian Exchange

What were major consequences of the Columbian Exchange?
- **Demographic disaster:** disease killed 80-95% of Native populations.
- **European population growth:** New World crops (potato, corn) supported expansion.
- **Atlantic slave trade:** Native labor shortage led to African enslavement.
- **New global economy:** integrated Old and New Worlds.
- **Global commodities:** sugar, coffee, chocolate became worldwide trade goods.

### Example 5 [5] — Italian vs Northern Renaissance

Compare Italian and Northern Renaissances.
- **Both:** revival of classical learning, humanism, art, printing.
- **Italian (1300s-1500s):** more SECULAR, focused on classical Greek/Roman texts. Florence, Venice, Rome centers. Famous artists (da Vinci, Michelangelo, Raphael).
- **Northern (1400s-1500s):** more religious. Christian humanism. Focused on early Christian sources for ethical reform. Erasmus, Thomas More, Dürer, van Eyck.
- **Significance of Northern:** set stage for Reformation by criticizing church corruption and emphasizing scriptural study.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing Italian and Northern Renaissances.** Italian: secular, classical. Northern: Christian, religious reform agenda.
2. **Wrong dates for Gutenberg.** ~1450s. First major printed book was Gutenberg Bible.
3. **Wrong attributions for exploration.** Portuguese: African coast, India route. Spanish: Americas. Dutch: Indonesia. English: North America.
4. **Confusing Treaty of Tordesillas.** 1494. Divided New World between Spain (west) and Portugal (east). Why Brazil is Portuguese.
5. **Wrong Renaissance period.** Italian Renaissance: ~1300-1600. Northern: ~1450-1600.
6. **Forgetting Reconquista significance.** 1492: completed Reconquista freed Spain to fund Columbus.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Renaissance essays:**
- Distinguish Italian and Northern.
- Address artistic and intellectual contributions.
- Note social/political context (city-states, patronage).

**For exploration essays:**
- Address multiple causes (economic, religious, political, technological).
- Compare different European powers.

**For DBQ-style sourcing:**
- HIPP for documents.
- Note perspectives (rulers, merchants, religious figures, indigenous peoples).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The Italian Renaissance (c. 1300-1600), centered in Florence and other city-states, revived classical learning and emphasized individualism, secularism, and humanism. Wealthy patrons (especially the Medici) funded artists and scholars."
2. "The Gutenberg printing press (~1450s) enabled mass production of books, dramatically expanding literacy and accelerating the spread of ideas — eventually contributing to the Protestant Reformation by making religious texts widely available."
3. "European maritime exploration was driven by multiple factors: economic (spices, gold, markets), religious (Christianizing), political (national prestige), and technological (caravel, compass, astrolabe). Reconquista's completion in 1492 freed Spanish military energies."
4. "The Columbian Exchange transformed both hemispheres. Old World pathogens (smallpox especially) killed 80-95% of Native populations; New World crops (potato, corn) fueled European population growth in subsequent centuries."
5. "Mercantilism was the dominant economic theory of early modern Europe: nations grew rich by accumulating gold/silver, exporting more than importing, and using colonies as sources of raw materials and markets for finished goods."
