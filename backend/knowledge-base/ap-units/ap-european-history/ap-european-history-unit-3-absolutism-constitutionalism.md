# AP European History — Unit 3: Absolutism and Constitutionalism (c. 1648–1815) — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this unit. Use to ground custom teaching responses + on-demand teaching guide generation.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP European History exam
- **Period:** c. 1648-1815
- **Sub-topics covered:** absolutism (Louis XIV, Peter the Great); constitutionalism (England — Stuart kings, Civil War, Glorious Revolution); economic and demographic changes; Dutch Golden Age.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Louis XIV (the "Sun King") epitomizes absolutism. Glorious Revolution (1688) marks rise of constitutional monarchy in England. Peter the Great's Westernization of Russia. Comparing absolutism vs constitutionalism is common essay topic.

## Big Ideas

1. **Absolutism: monarchs claimed divine right to absolute power.** Louis XIV ("L'état, c'est moi") epitomized.
2. **Constitutionalism: monarchs limited by laws/parliament.** England developed via Civil War and Glorious Revolution.
3. **France under Louis XIV (r. 1643-1715) was Europe's dominant power.** Versailles symbol of royal power.
4. **England's Glorious Revolution (1688) established constitutional monarchy and Bill of Rights (1689).**
5. **Peter the Great westernized Russia.** Built St. Petersburg as new capital.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Absolutism:**
  - **Divine right of kings:** monarchs ruled by God's authority.
  - **Centralized power** in monarch.
  - **No representative bodies** with real power.
  - **Standing armies** loyal to monarch.
  - **Bureaucracy** to administer state.
- **Louis XIV of France (r. 1643-1715):**
  - **"L'état, c'est moi"** ("I am the state").
  - **Sun King.**
  - **Versailles:** elaborate palace; subordinated nobility through court life.
  - **Cardinal Mazarin** advised young Louis.
  - **Jean-Baptiste Colbert** finance minister; mercantilist economics.
  - **Edict of Fontainebleau (1685):** revoked Edict of Nantes; persecuted Huguenots.
  - **Wars:** spent vast resources on wars (war of Spanish Succession 1701-1714 etc.).
  - **Death (1715):** left France in financial ruin despite glory.
- **Other absolutists:**
  - **Frederick William the Great Elector** of Brandenburg-Prussia.
  - **Frederick the Great** (Prussia, r. 1740-1786): enlightened absolutist.
  - **Maria Theresa** of Austria (r. 1740-1780).
  - **Joseph II of Austria** (r. 1780-1790): enlightened absolutist; abolished serfdom.
  - **Catherine the Great of Russia** (r. 1762-1796): expanded territory; corresponded with philosophes.
- **Peter the Great of Russia (r. 1682-1725):**
  - **Westernized Russia.**
  - Built modern navy and army.
  - Founded **St. Petersburg** (1703) as new capital, "window to the West."
  - Forced nobles to adopt Western dress, customs.
  - **Table of Ranks:** civil/military service positions based on merit.
  - **Strengthened serfdom** (paradox of modernization).
- **English Civil War and Glorious Revolution:**
  - **Stuart kings** (after Elizabeth I died 1603) clashed with Parliament.
  - **James I (r. 1603-1625):** divine right ideology.
  - **Charles I (r. 1625-1649):** clashed with Parliament; executed (1649).
  - **English Civil War (1642-1649):** Parliamentarians (Roundheads) vs Royalists (Cavaliers).
  - **Oliver Cromwell:** led Parliamentarians to victory; ruled as Lord Protector.
  - **Restoration (1660):** Charles II returned to throne.
  - **James II (r. 1685-1688):** Catholic king; tried to restore Catholicism.
  - **Glorious Revolution (1688):** William and Mary invited from Netherlands; James II fled.
  - **Bill of Rights (1689):** limited royal power; established constitutional monarchy.
- **English political philosophers:**
  - **Thomas Hobbes (1588-1679):** *Leviathan* (1651). State of nature is "nasty, brutish, short"; need strong sovereign for security.
  - **John Locke (1632-1704):** *Two Treatises of Government* (1690). Natural rights (life, liberty, property); social contract; consent of governed.
- **Dutch Republic (Golden Age, 1600s):**
  - Wealthy through trade.
  - **Religious tolerance** (relatively).
  - Center of finance, art (Rembrandt, Vermeer), philosophy (Spinoza).
  - **Dutch East India Company** (VOC, 1602): joint-stock company; first multinational corporation.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why Louis XIV centered Versailles strategy.**
  - Required nobility to attend court.
  - Reduced their independent power.
  - Spectacle reinforced royal authority.
  - Drained nobles' resources.
- **Mercantilism under Colbert.**
  - State-directed economy.
  - Protective tariffs.
  - Subsidies for export industries.
  - Goal: positive balance of trade.
- **Peter the Great's reforms in detail:**
  - Modernized army and navy.
  - Built St. Petersburg as Western-facing capital.
  - Forced nobles to adopt Western dress, shave beards.
  - Promoted Western technology, education.
  - Strengthened autocracy AND serfdom (paradox).
- **English Civil War causes:**
  - Religious tensions (Anglican Charles I vs Puritan Parliament).
  - Royal financial demands.
  - Charles I's autocratic style.
  - Dissolved Parliament repeatedly.
- **Glorious Revolution's significance:**
  - Established CONSTITUTIONAL MONARCHY.
  - Parliament became dominant.
  - Bill of Rights (1689) limited royal power.
  - Set precedent for modern parliamentary democracy.
- **Hobbes vs Locke:**
  - Hobbes: pessimistic about human nature; need absolute sovereign for security.
  - Locke: more optimistic; natural rights; right to revolution if government violates contract.
  - Locke heavily influenced American Revolution and modern liberal democracy.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why Louis XIV's reign mattered.**
  - Set template for European absolutism.
  - French culture (language, fashion, art) dominated Europe.
  - Versailles became model for other royal palaces.
  - But left France in financial ruin (eventually contributing to French Revolution).
- **Why Glorious Revolution mattered.**
  - "Bloodless" transition (relatively).
  - Established parliamentary supremacy.
  - Foundation of modern constitutional government.
- **Why Hobbes and Locke matter.**
  - Both shaped political thought.
  - Hobbes: justification for strong central government.
  - Locke: basis for natural rights theory; influenced American Founders, Enlightenment.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Identifying rulers

For each: identify the absolutist monarch.
(a) "Sun King" of France; built Versailles.
(b) Westernized Russia; founded St. Petersburg.
(c) Enlightened despot of Prussia; "first servant of state."

- (a) **Louis XIV.**
- (b) **Peter the Great.**
- (c) **Frederick the Great** (Frederick II of Prussia).

### Example 2 [3] — Versailles strategy

How did Louis XIV use Versailles to consolidate power?
- Required nobility to attend court at Versailles.
- Subordinated them through elaborate court rituals.
- Reduced their independent regional power.
- Drained their financial resources.
- Made them dependent on royal favor.

### Example 3 [4] — Glorious Revolution

What happened in the Glorious Revolution (1688) and why was it significant?
- **William and Mary** invited from Netherlands.
- **James II fled** without major military conflict.
- **Bill of Rights (1689)** limited royal power.
- **Established constitutional monarchy.**
- **Significance:** parliament became dominant; foundation of modern parliamentary democracy.

### Example 4 [4] — Hobbes vs Locke

Compare Hobbes and Locke.
- **Both:** social contract theorists.
- **Hobbes** (*Leviathan*): pessimistic. State of nature is "nasty, brutish, short." Need strong absolute sovereign for security. Justified absolutism.
- **Locke** (*Two Treatises*): more optimistic. Natural rights (life, liberty, property). Government legitimate only with consent of governed. Right to revolution if violated. Justified limited government.

### Example 5 [5] — Comparing absolutism and constitutionalism

Compare French absolutism (Louis XIV) and English constitutionalism (post-Glorious Revolution).
- **Both:** strong central governments.
- **France:** absolute monarch (Louis XIV); divine right; subordinated nobility; centralized administration.
- **England:** constitutional monarchy (William and Mary onwards); Parliament dominant; Bill of Rights; no divine right.
- **Significance:** different paths to modern state. France's absolutism eventually fell to revolution (1789); England's constitutional monarchy evolved into modern parliamentary democracy.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Wrong dates for Louis XIV.** Reign 1643-1715 (long reign — 72 years).
2. **Confusing English Civil War and Glorious Revolution.** Civil War (1642-1649): Parliament vs King; Charles I executed. Glorious Revolution (1688): William and Mary; bloodless.
3. **Misnaming Bill of Rights.** English Bill of Rights (1689). DIFFERENT from American Bill of Rights (1791).
4. **Wrong Peter the Great dates.** Reign 1682-1725.
5. **Confusing Hobbes and Locke.** Hobbes: absolutist (Leviathan, 1651). Locke: limited government (Two Treatises, 1690).
6. **Wrong Edict of Nantes context.** Issued 1598 by Henry IV; revoked 1685 by Louis XIV.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For absolutism essays:**
- Address ideology (divine right).
- Address methods of consolidation (Versailles, bureaucracy, army).
- Compare different absolutist rulers.

**For Glorious Revolution essays:**
- Address causes (Stuart conflict with Parliament, religious tensions).
- Address consequences (constitutional monarchy, Bill of Rights).
- Note Locke's influence.

**For DBQ-style sourcing:**
- HIPP for documents.
- Note perspective (monarchs, parliaments, philosophers, ordinary people).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Louis XIV (r. 1643-1715), the 'Sun King,' epitomized absolute monarchy. He centralized power, built Versailles to subordinate nobility through court life, and famously declared 'L'état, c'est moi' ('I am the state')."
2. "Peter the Great (r. 1682-1725) westernized Russia by modernizing the army and navy, founding St. Petersburg as 'window to the West,' and forcing nobles to adopt Western dress and customs — while paradoxically strengthening serfdom."

5. "France's absolutism (Louis XIV) and England's constitutionalism (post-Glorious Revolution) represented two paths to the modern state. France's absolutism eventually fell to the French Revolution (1789); England's constitutional monarchy evolved into modern parliamentary democracy."
