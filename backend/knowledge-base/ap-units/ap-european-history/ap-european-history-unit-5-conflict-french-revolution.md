## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP European History exam
- **Period:** c. 1648–1815 (focused on late 1700s-early 1800s)
- **Sub-topics covered:** French Revolution causes; phases of the Revolution (1789-1799); Reign of Terror; Napoleon's rise; Napoleonic Empire; Congress of Vienna (1815).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** French Revolution is THE central event of late 18th-century European history. Phases (constitutional → radical → Thermidorian → Napoleon) commonly tested. Congress of Vienna (1815) closes the unit and Period 2.

## Big Ideas

1. **French Revolution overthrew the Old Regime** in France and reshaped European politics.
2. **Causes were multiple:** financial crisis, Enlightenment ideas, social inequality (Estates), bourgeois ambition, Louis XVI's weakness.
3. **Revolution went through radical phases:** moderate (1789-1791) → radical (1792-1794, Reign of Terror) → Thermidorian (1794-1799) → Napoleon (1799-1815).
4. **Napoleon spread Revolutionary ideas** across Europe but also became authoritarian emperor.
5. **Congress of Vienna (1815)** restored conservative order after Napoleon's defeat. Closed the era opened by 1789.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Causes of French Revolution:**
  - **Financial crisis:** France bankrupt from wars (especially American Revolution support).
  - **Three Estates** (First: clergy, Second: nobility, Third: everyone else, ~98% of population).
  - **First and Second Estates exempt from most taxes**; burden on Third Estate.
  - **Bourgeoisie** (educated middle class) wanted political power matching economic role.
  - **Enlightenment ideas** undermined Old Regime legitimacy.
  - **Bad harvests** (1788-1789); bread prices spiked.
  - **Louis XVI** indecisive king; **Marie Antoinette** unpopular.
- **Phases of the French Revolution:**
  - **1789 — moderate phase begins:**
    - **Estates-General** convened (May 1789, first since 1614).
    - **National Assembly** formed (June); **Tennis Court Oath**.
    - **Storming of the Bastille** (July 14, 1789).
    - **Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen** (August 1789): natural rights, equality.
    - **Women's March on Versailles** (October 1789): forced royal family to Paris.
    - **Civil Constitution of the Clergy** (1790): Church property nationalized.
  - **1791-1792 — Constitutional Monarchy:**
    - Constitution of 1791: limited monarchy.
    - **Louis XVI's flight to Varennes** (June 1791): caught fleeing; trust shattered.
    - **War declared against Austria/Prussia** (April 1792).
  - **1792-1794 — Radical Phase (Republic and Terror):**
    - **September Massacres** (1792).
    - **Monarchy abolished**; First Republic declared (September 1792).
    - **Louis XVI executed** (January 1793); **Marie Antoinette executed** (October 1793).
    - **Reign of Terror** (1793-1794): **Jacobins** under **Robespierre** and **Committee of Public Safety**.
    - **Mass executions** by guillotine; ~17,000 official executions.
    - **Levée en masse** (1793): mass conscription; first modern citizen army.
    - **De-Christianization** campaign.
    - **Robespierre executed** (Thermidor, July 1794).
  - **1794-1799 — Thermidorian Reaction and Directory:**
    - Conservative reaction; ended Terror.
    - **The Directory** (1795-1799): weak government.
  - **1799-1815 — Napoleonic Era:**
    - **Coup of 18 Brumaire** (November 1799): Napoleon seized power.
- **Napoleon Bonaparte (1769-1821):**
  - Born in Corsica; rose through army during Revolution.
  - **Consul, then Emperor** (1804): crowned himself.
  - **Napoleonic Code** (1804): unified French law; influence across Europe; rationalized property law, civil law.
  - **Concordat of 1801** with Pope; resolved Church-state conflict.
  - **Created merit-based bureaucracy.**
  - **Conquered most of Europe** (Italy, Germany, Spain, etc.).
  - **Continental System** (1806): economic blockade of Britain.
  - **Disastrous invasion of Russia** (1812).
  - **Defeated at Leipzig** (1813).
  - **Abdicated; exiled to Elba** (1814).
  - **Hundred Days; Battle of Waterloo** (June 1815); defeated by **Wellington**.
  - **Exiled to St. Helena**; died there 1821.
- **Congress of Vienna (1814-1815):**
  - **Klemens von Metternich** (Austria) led.
  - **Conservative restoration:** restored Bourbon dynasty in France; redrew borders.
  - **Balance of power** principle.
  - **Concert of Europe:** great powers consult to prevent revolution and war.
  - Began era of post-Revolutionary conservative order.

### Adds for [4]

- **Long-term causes of French Revolution:**
  - Centuries-old privileges of First and Second Estates.
  - Financial crisis from royal extravagance + wars.
  - Enlightenment critiques of absolutism and inequality.
  - Bourgeoisie's growing wealth without proportional power.
- **Why Reign of Terror happened:**
  - **Foreign threats** (Austria, Prussia, Britain at war).
  - **Internal counter-revolution** (Vendée uprising).
  - **Economic crisis**, food shortages.
  - **Jacobin radicalization**: belief that "virtue" required suppressing "enemies."
  - **Robespierre's** ideology: "republic of virtue."
- **Levée en masse and modern war:**
  - Mass conscription created huge citizen army.
  - Nationalism mobilized populace.
  - Set template for modern total war.
- **Napoleonic reforms:**
  - **Napoleonic Code** (Civil Code of 1804): equality before law; property rights; rationalized civil law.
  - **Concordat with Catholic Church** (1801): restored Catholicism but state-controlled.
  - **Education reform**: state schools (lycées); promotion by merit.
  - **Bank of France** centralized finances.
  - **BUT:** restricted women's rights; reinstated slavery in colonies; created authoritarian rule.
- **Why Napoleon failed:**
  - **Russian invasion** (1812): "scorched earth"; brutal winter destroyed Grande Armée.
  - **Continental System** failed to break Britain.
  - **Nationalism** in conquered countries turned against him.
  - **Coalition** of European powers eventually overwhelmed France.
- **Congress of Vienna's principles:**
  - **Legitimacy:** restore old dynasties.
  - **Balance of power:** no one nation dominant.
  - **Compensation:** territories adjusted to compensate winners.
  - **Concert of Europe** for ongoing consultation.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why French Revolution mattered.**
  - Overthrew Old Regime in France.
  - Spread Enlightenment ideals (rights, equality, citizenship).
  - Created modern nationalism.
  - Set template for modern revolutions.
  - But also showed risks of revolutionary radicalism (Terror).
- **Napoleon's paradoxical legacy.**
  - Spread Revolutionary ideals across Europe (legal reform, secularism).
  - But became authoritarian emperor.
  - Inspired both liberal nationalism AND conservative reaction.
  - Napoleonic Code remained foundation of European civil law.
- **Congress of Vienna's significance.**
  - Restored conservative order.
  - Successfully prevented major European war for 99 years (until 1914).
  - But couldn't suppress liberal/national movements long-term.
  - Set up tension between conservative restoration and liberal/national revolution that defined 19th century.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Phases of the Revolution

For each, identify the phase or year:
(a) Storming of the Bastille.
(b) Reign of Terror.
(c) Napoleon seizes power (Coup of 18 Brumaire).
(d) Congress of Vienna.

- (a) **July 14, 1789** (moderate phase begins).
- (b) **1793-1794** (Jacobins; Robespierre).
- (c) **November 1799** (Brumaire).
- (d) **1814-1815**.

### Example 2 [3] — Three Estates

What were the three Estates and their grievances?
- **First Estate:** clergy (~0.5%); largely tax-exempt; held vast Church lands.
- **Second Estate:** nobility (~1.5%); largely tax-exempt; privileged.
- **Third Estate:** ~98% (bourgeoisie, urban workers, peasants); paid most taxes; little political power.
- **Grievances:** tax inequality; lack of political voice; food shortages; bourgeois ambitions for political role.

### Example 3 [4] — Reign of Terror

Why did the Revolution radicalize into the Reign of Terror?
- **Foreign war:** Austria, Prussia, Britain attacked France.
- **Internal counter-revolution** (Vendée).
- **Economic crisis** and food shortages.
- **Jacobin radicalization** under **Robespierre**.
- **Committee of Public Safety** ruled; mass executions.
- Terror ended when Robespierre himself executed (Thermidor, July 1794).

### Example 4 [4] — Napoleonic legacy

Assess Napoleon's domestic reforms.
- **Napoleonic Code (1804):** equality before law; rationalized civil law; influenced legal systems across Europe.
- **Concordat with Pope** (1801): resolved religious conflict; restored Catholic worship under state oversight.
- **Education:** lycées (state schools); merit-based bureaucracy.
- **Bank of France:** centralized finances.
- **Trade-offs:** rights of women restricted (Code subordinated women); slavery reinstated in colonies; authoritarian rule.

### Example 5 [5] — Congress of Vienna

What did the Congress of Vienna (1814-1815) accomplish?
- **Restored Bourbon dynasty** in France.
- **Redrew borders** to balance power.
- **Created Concert of Europe** for ongoing great-power consultation.
- **Significance:** prevented major European war until 1914 (99 years).
- **But:** liberal and national movements continued; conservative restoration could not suppress them indefinitely (1830, 1848 revolutions).

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing phases of Revolution.** Moderate (1789-1791); Constitutional Monarchy (1791-1792); Republic and Terror (1792-1794); Thermidor/Directory (1794-1799); Napoleon (1799-1815).
2. **Wrong dates for key events.** Bastille July 14, 1789. Louis XVI executed January 1793. Robespierre executed July 1794. Napoleon coup November 1799.
3. **Confusing the three Estates.** First (clergy), Second (nobility), Third (everyone else).
4. **Misnaming key documents.** **Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen** (1789, France). **Civil Constitution of the Clergy** (1790).
5. **Confusing Robespierre and Napoleon.** Robespierre: Jacobin Terror leader (1793-1794). Napoleon: emperor (1799-1815).
6. **Wrong Waterloo date.** **June 1815**, after Napoleon's Hundred Days.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Revolution causation essays:**
- Address multiple causes (financial, social, political, intellectual).
- Note short-term triggers AND long-term causes.
- Connect Enlightenment ideas to revolutionary ideology.

**For Reign of Terror essays:**
- Address context (foreign war, internal counter-revolution, economic crisis).
- Address Jacobin ideology.
- Address consequences and end.

**For Napoleon essays:**
- Distinguish reforms (Code, Concordat) from authoritarianism.
- Address spread of Revolutionary ideas across Europe.
- Address why he failed (Russia, nationalism, coalition).

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The French Revolution erupted from converging causes: financial bankruptcy from wars (including support for the American Revolution), inequitable tax burden falling on the Third Estate, Enlightenment critiques of absolutism, and a bourgeoisie demanding political power proportional to its economic role."
2. "The storming of the Bastille (July 14, 1789) and the Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen (August 1789) marked the moderate phase. The Revolution radicalized after Louis XVI's flight to Varennes (1791) and the outbreak of war (1792)."
3. "The Reign of Terror (1793-1794), led by Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety, executed roughly 17,000 amid foreign war, internal counter-revolution, and Jacobin demands for a 'republic of virtue.' Robespierre himself was executed in Thermidor (July 1794), ending the Terror."
4. "Napoleon's rule (1799-1815) was paradoxical: he spread Revolutionary ideals — the Napoleonic Code's equality before law, secular education, merit-based bureaucracy — while becoming an authoritarian emperor who restricted women's rights and reinstated colonial slavery."
5. "The Congress of Vienna (1814-1815), led by Metternich, restored conservative order through legitimacy, balance of power, and the Concert of Europe — preventing major European war for 99 years but unable to suppress the liberal and national movements that would erupt in 1830 and 1848."
