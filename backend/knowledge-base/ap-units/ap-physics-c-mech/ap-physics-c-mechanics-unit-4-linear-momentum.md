## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 10–20% of the AP Physics C: Mechanics exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** linear momentum; impulse-momentum theorem; conservation of momentum; elastic and inelastic collisions; center of mass; rocket propulsion (variable mass).
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Conservation of momentum in collisions. Center of mass calculations and motion. Impulse from F-t graphs. 2D collisions.

## Big Ideas

1. **Momentum:** p = mv. Vector.
2. **Impulse:** J = ∫F dt = Δp.
3. **Conservation of momentum:** if F_net = 0, total p constant.
4. **Elastic collisions:** KE conserved; perfectly inelastic: stick together.
5. **Center of mass:** moves as if all mass concentrated there.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Momentum:** p = mv. Units: kg·m/s.
- **Newton's 2nd (general form):** F = dp/dt.
- **Impulse:**
  - **J = ∫F dt = Δp.**
  - **Area under F-t graph.**
- **Conservation of momentum:** if F_ext = 0, ΣP_initial = ΣP_final.
- **Collision types:**
  - **Elastic:** KE conserved AND p conserved.
  - **Inelastic:** p conserved; some KE lost.
  - **Perfectly inelastic:** stick together; max KE loss.
- **Elastic collision (1D, m₁ vs m₂ at rest):**
  - **v₁' = (m₁ − m₂)v₁/(m₁ + m₂).**
  - **v₂' = 2m₁v₁/(m₁ + m₂).**
- **Perfectly inelastic (m₁ + m₂ stick):**
  - **(m₁ + m₂) v_f = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂.**
- **2D collisions:** conserve p in each component independently.
- **Center of mass:**
  - **r_cm = Σm_i r_i / Σm_i.**
  - **For continuous distribution:** r_cm = (1/M) ∫r dm.
  - **No external force:** v_cm constant.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why p conserved:** Newton's 3rd law (internal forces cancel).
- **2D collision:** (perpendicular components conserved separately).
- **Elastic collision properties:**
  - **Equal masses, head-on:** v₁ → 0; v₂ → v₁ (transfer velocities).
  - **Heavy hits light:** light goes forward fast; heavy slows slightly.
- **Variable mass (rocket equation):**
  - **m dv/dt = −v_exhaust dm/dt.**
  - **Δv = v_exhaust ln(m_initial/m_final).**

### Adds for [5]

- **Why CM useful:** complex motion decomposes into CM motion + relative motion.
- **Why elastic vs inelastic:** energy bookkeeping reveals nature of collision.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Impulse

3 kg ball changes velocity from 5 m/s to −5 m/s after wall hit. Impulse?
- **J = Δp = m(v_f − v_i) = 3(−5 − 5) = −30 kg·m/s.**
- **Magnitude:** 30 kg·m/s.

### Example 2 [3] — Inelastic collision

2 kg block at 4 m/s hits 3 kg stationary block; they stick. Final velocity?
- **(m₁+m₂)v_f = m₁v₁ → v_f = 2·4/5 = 1.6 m/s.**

### Example 3 [4] — Elastic collision

3 kg at 4 m/s elastically collides with 3 kg at rest. Find final velocities.
- **Equal masses, head-on:** velocities exchange.
- **v₁' = 0; v₂' = 4 m/s.**

### Example 4 [4] — Center of mass

Two masses: 2 kg at x=0; 3 kg at x=5. CM?
- **x_cm = (2·0 + 3·5)/(2+3) = 15/5 = 3 m.**

### Example 5 [5] — KE loss in inelastic

Above example (2 kg at 4 m/s + 3 kg at rest stick): KE lost?
- **Initial KE:** ½(2)(16) = 16 J.
- **Final KE:** ½(5)(1.6²) = ½·5·2.56 = 6.4 J.
- **Lost:** 9.6 J.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Forgetting momentum is vector.** Sign matters in 1D; components in 2D.
2. **KE NOT conserved** in inelastic collisions.
3. **Equal mass elastic collision** transfers velocity (head-on).
4. **CM with continuous distribution** requires integration.
5. **Internal forces don't change p** (action-reaction cancels).

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For collisions:** conserve p; use KE for elastic.
**For impulse:** integrate F over time, or use Δp.
**For CM:** Σm_i r_i / Σm_i.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Momentum p = mv is a vector. Newton's 2nd law in general form: F = dp/dt. Impulse J = ∫F dt = Δp."
2. "Conservation of momentum: when no external forces act, total momentum of a system is constant. ΣP_initial = ΣP_final."
3. "Elastic collisions conserve both momentum and kinetic energy. Inelastic collisions conserve only momentum (some KE is converted to other forms). Perfectly inelastic collisions involve maximum KE loss as objects stick together."
4. "In a perfectly inelastic collision in 1D: (m₁ + m₂)v_f = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂. In an elastic collision between equal masses (one at rest), velocities are exchanged."
5. "Center of mass r_cm = Σm_i r_i / Σm_i. The center of mass moves as if all mass were concentrated there with all external forces applied to it: F_ext = M·a_cm."
