## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** 5–10% of the AP Physics C: Mechanics exam
- **Sub-topics covered:** Newton's law of universal gravitation; gravitational field; gravitational potential energy; orbital motion; Kepler's laws; escape velocity.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Newton's law of gravitation. Orbital velocity. Gravitational PE U = −GMm/r. Kepler's three laws (especially T² ∝ r³).

## Big Ideas

1. **Universal gravitation:** F = Gm₁m₂/r².
2. **Gravitational field:** g = GM/r² (acceleration of test mass).
3. **Gravitational PE:** U = −GMm/r (zero at infinity).
4. **Orbital motion:** gravity provides centripetal force.
5. **Kepler's laws:** orbits are ellipses; equal areas in equal times; T² ∝ r³.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Newton's law of universal gravitation:**
  - **F = Gm₁m₂/r²** (G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²).
  - **Always attractive.**
- **Gravitational field:** g = F/m = GM/r² (vector, toward mass).
- **At Earth's surface:** g = GM_E/R_E² ≈ 9.8 m/s².
- **Gravitational potential energy (general):**
  - **U = −GMm/r** (zero at infinity).
  - **Negative because** bound system.
- **Total energy in orbit:**
  - **Circular orbit:** E = −GMm/(2r) = −½|U|.
  - **For elliptical orbit:** E = −GMm/(2a), where a is semi-major axis.
- **Orbital velocity (circular):**
  - **mv²/r = GMm/r².**
  - **v = √(GM/r).**
- **Orbital period:**
  - **T = 2πr/v = 2π√(r³/(GM)).**
- **Escape velocity:**
  - **From Earth:** ½mv_esc² = GMm/R_E.**
  - **v_esc = √(2GM/R_E) ≈ 11.2 km/s.**
- **Kepler's laws:**
  - **1st:** orbits are ellipses with the larger body at one focus.
  - **2nd:** equal areas swept in equal times (angular momentum conservation).
  - **3rd:** T² ∝ a³.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why E = −½U for circular orbit:** virial theorem.
- **Geosynchronous orbit:** T = 24 hours; r ≈ 4.2×10⁷ m from Earth's center.
- **Kepler's 3rd:** T² = (4π²/GM) · r³.
- **Kepler's 2nd is angular momentum conservation:** L = mvr (perpendicular), constant for central force.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why gravity has 1/r²:** flux through sphere argument.
- **Why orbits stable:** energy conserved; angular momentum conserved.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Gravity at surface

Earth mass 6×10²⁴ kg, radius 6.4×10⁶ m. Surface g?
- **g = GM/R² = (6.67×10⁻¹¹)(6×10²⁴)/(6.4×10⁶)² = 4×10¹⁴/4.1×10¹³ ≈ 9.8 m/s².** ✓

### Example 2 [3] — Orbital velocity

Satellite orbiting at altitude h above Earth's surface (r = R + h). v?
- **v = √(GM/r).**
- **For h ≈ 400 km (ISS):** r ≈ 6800 km. v ≈ √((6.67×10⁻¹¹)(6×10²⁴)/(6.8×10⁶)) ≈ √(5.88×10⁷) ≈ 7660 m/s.**

### Example 3 [4] — Period

Geosynchronous satellite (T = 86400 s). Find r.
- **T² = 4π²r³/(GM).**
- **r³ = GMT²/(4π²) = (6.67×10⁻¹¹)(6×10²⁴)(86400²)/(4π²).**
- **r ≈ 4.22×10⁷ m** (≈ 35,800 km altitude).

### Example 4 [4] — Escape velocity

From Earth?
- **v_esc = √(2GM/R) = √(2·9.8·6.4×10⁶) ≈ 11.2 km/s.**

### Example 5 [5] — Orbital energy

Total energy of mass m in circular orbit at radius r.
- **KE = ½mv² = ½m·GM/r.**
- **U = −GMm/r.**
- **E = KE + U = GMm/(2r) − GMm/r = −GMm/(2r).**
- **Negative:** bound orbit.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **U has negative sign** (bound system).
2. **r is from CENTER** of attractor (not surface).
3. **Orbital velocity decreases with altitude** (v ∝ 1/√r).
4. **Kepler's 3rd uses semi-major axis** for elliptical orbit.
5. **Escape velocity** assumes object goes to infinity with 0 KE.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For gravity:** F = Gm₁m₂/r².
**For orbit:** equate gravitational and centripetal forces.
**For Kepler:** T² ∝ r³.
