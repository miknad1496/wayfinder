## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam weight:** Poetry MCQ + Q1 (Poetry Analysis FRQ).
- **Sub-topics covered:** speaker, structure of poems, word choice (diction), comparison, voice and tone, line, stanza, basic literary devices.
- **Where this unit appears on the exam:** Q1 (Poetry Analysis FRQ) requires this. Multiple-choice on poetry passages tests speaker, tone, structure constantly.

## Big Ideas

1. **The speaker is NOT the poet** — speaker is a constructed voice within the poem.
2. **Poetry is compressed language** — every word matters; diction (word choice) carries weight.
3. **Structure shapes meaning** — line breaks, stanzas, white space all signify.
4. **Tone** is the speaker's attitude toward subject — distinct from mood (reader's feeling).
5. **Comparison (simile, metaphor, analogy)** is poetry's foundational technique.

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Speaker:**
  - **The voice of the poem** — distinct from the poet.
  - May be the poet's persona, a character, an object, an abstraction.
  - **Identify** speaker before analyzing.
- **Subject vs theme:**
  - **Subject:** what the poem is literally about.
  - **Theme:** the broader meaning/insight the poem offers.
- **Diction (word choice):**
  - **Connotation:** associations beyond literal meaning.
  - **Denotation:** dictionary definition.
  - **Formal vs colloquial.**
  - **Concrete vs abstract.**
  - **Diction creates tone.**
- **Tone:**
  - Speaker's attitude toward subject.
  - Identified through diction, imagery, rhythm.
  - **Always more specific than positive/negative.** (E.g., "wistful," "ironic," "indignant.")
- **Mood:**
  - Reader's emotional response.
  - Created by all elements working together.
- **Imagery:**
  - **Sensory details** (visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, gustatory).
  - Creates vivid mental picture.
- **Comparison devices:**
  - **Simile:** comparison using "like" or "as."
  - **Metaphor:** direct comparison.
  - **Extended metaphor:** sustained over multiple lines.
  - **Analogy:** more elaborate comparison.
  - **Personification:** giving human qualities to non-human.
- **Other key devices:**
  - **Symbol:** object that represents larger idea.
  - **Allusion:** reference to other text/event/person.
  - **Hyperbole:** exaggeration for effect.
  - **Understatement:** deliberate reduction.
  - **Irony:** discrepancy between expectation and reality.
  - **Paradox:** apparent contradiction containing truth.
  - **Oxymoron:** contradictory terms ("bittersweet").
  - **Apostrophe:** address to absent person/abstraction ("O Death!").
- **Structure:**
  - **Line:** unit of poem; line breaks create emphasis.
  - **Enjambment:** sentence runs past line break.
  - **End-stopped:** line ends with grammatical pause.
  - **Stanza:** grouped lines (couplet 2, tercet 3, quatrain 4, etc.).
  - **Form:** sonnet, ballad, ode, free verse, etc.
  - **Sonnet:** 14 lines; iambic pentameter; volta (turn).
  - **Volta:** turn in argument/feeling (often line 9 in sonnet).
- **Sound:**
  - **Rhyme:** end rhyme (line ends), internal rhyme (within line).
  - **Rhyme scheme:** ABAB, AABB, etc.
  - **Alliteration:** repeated initial consonants.
  - **Assonance:** repeated vowel sounds.
  - **Consonance:** repeated consonant sounds.
  - **Onomatopoeia:** word imitates sound ("buzz").
- **Title:**
  - **Often crucial** to interpretation.
  - **Don't ignore** — re-read after reading poem.

### Adds for [4]

- **Why diction matters:**
  - **Compressed language** — every word chosen.
  - **Connotations layer meaning** beyond denotation.
  - **Diction reveals speaker, tone, theme.**
- **Tonal complexity:**
  - **Tone often shifts** within a poem.
  - **Multiple tones** can coexist (nostalgic AND ironic).
  - **Identifying shift** is often key insight.
- **How metaphor creates meaning:**
  - **Tenor:** subject of comparison.
  - **Vehicle:** what it's compared to.
  - **Comparison reveals** quality of tenor through vehicle.
  - **Multiple metaphors** can create extended pattern.
- **Structure-meaning connection:**
  - **Line break creates emphasis** at end and beginning.
  - **Enjambment creates momentum or surprise.**
  - **Short stanzas** isolate; long stanzas accumulate.
  - **White space signifies** silence, separation, weight.
- **Sound and meaning:**
  - **Hard consonants** (k, p, t) create tension.
  - **Soft sounds** (s, l, m) create flow.
  - **Rhyme creates closure or pattern.**
  - **Unrhyme can create unease or freedom.**

### Adds for [5]

- **Why poetry rewards rereading.**
  - **Compressed; every element matters.**
  - **Multiple readings reveal layers.**
  - **Sound becomes audible** with reading aloud.
- **Why title matters.**
  - **Author's first communication** with reader.
  - **Often reframes** poem after reading.
  - **Sometimes ironic** in relation to content.
- **Why structure matters.**
  - **Form shapes meaning.**
  - **Sonnet's volta** structures argument.
  - **Free verse's choices** still create patterns.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Speaker identification

In a poem beginning "I wandered lonely as a cloud" (Wordsworth), who is the speaker?
- **The persona of the poet** — a contemplative, observing wanderer.
- **NOT necessarily Wordsworth himself** — though closely tied to poet's voice.
- **Always begin analysis with speaker.**

### Example 2 [3] — Identify devices

For each, identify device:
(a) "Her smile was sunshine."
(b) "She moved like a panther."
(c) "The wind whispered."
(d) "I died for beauty" (Dickinson).

- (a) **Metaphor.**
- (b) **Simile.**
- (c) **Personification.**
- (d) Could be **hyperbole** or paradox/figurative.

### Example 3 [4] — Diction analysis

In Robert Frost's "The Road Not Taken," what does the diction "yellow wood" suggest?
- **"Yellow"** suggests autumn — season of change, decline, beauty.
- **"Wood"** is wilder than "forest" or "garden" — suggests choice in untamed setting.
- **Together:** moment of decision in natural world undergoing change.
- **Sets up the choice the speaker faces.**

### Example 4 [4] — Tone shift

A poem opens nostalgic and ends bitter. How might you analyze?
- **Identify shift point:** specific line where tone changes.
- **Identify diction shift:** what words signal new tone?
- **Identify what causes shift:** new realization? Memory turn?
- **Connect to theme:** speaker's understanding evolves through poem.

### Example 5 [5] — Sonnet volta

In a Shakespearean sonnet (3 quatrains + couplet), where's the volta and what does it do?
- **Often between quatrain 3 and couplet** (line 12-13).
- **Sometimes between quatrains** (e.g., line 9).
- **Marks turn in argument** — from problem to resolution, claim to counterclaim, observation to insight.
- **Couplet often delivers** epigram or summary.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Confusing speaker and poet.** Speaker is constructed voice within poem. Don't assume = author.
2. **Identifying tone too vaguely.** "Sad" or "happy" inadequate. Use precise tone words: wistful, melancholy, indignant, jubilant, ironic, contemplative.
3. **Confusing simile and metaphor.** Simile uses "like" or "as." Metaphor doesn't.
4. **Ignoring structure.** Line breaks, stanzas, form all matter.
5. **Confusing tone and mood.** Tone: speaker's attitude. Mood: reader's feeling.
6. **Skipping title.** Title is part of poem; often crucial.

## Rubric-Aware Tactics

**For Q1 (Poetry Analysis FRQ):**
- **Identify speaker** before analyzing.
- **Identify tone with specific word.**
- **Cite specific lines/phrases** with quotation.
- **Connect technique to meaning.**
- **Address structure** as well as language.

**For MCQ:**
- **Read poem twice** before answering.
- **Read aloud** in your head for sound.
- **Identify shift points** in tone, perspective, image.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "The speaker [reveals/laments/celebrates] [subject] through diction that [connotes/evokes/establishes] a tone of [precise tone word]."
2. "Through the extended metaphor of [vehicle] for [tenor], the speaker reveals that [thematic claim], deepening as the poem progresses."
3. "The shift in tone from [tone 1] to [tone 2] at line [X], marked by [diction/imagery change], signals the speaker's evolving [understanding/attitude/insight]."
4. "The poem's structure — [enjambment/end-stop/stanza pattern] — reinforces meaning by [creating momentum/isolating image/marking turn]."
5. "The volta at line [X] turns the speaker from [position 1] to [position 2], a move underscored by the shift from [diction 1] to [diction 2]."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Unit

*Master speaker identification. Master precise tone vocabulary. Master common devices (simile, metaphor, personification, alliteration, irony). Master structural elements (line, stanza, enjambment, volta). Always cite specific lines with quoted phrases.*
