# AP Japanese Language and Culture — Overview — Wayfinder Teaching Brain

> Surfaces in chat when student mentions topics in this exam.

## Unit at a Glance

- **Exam structure:** Multiple-choice (Listening + Reading) + Free-response (Writing + Speaking).
- **Themes:** Six themes parallel other AP language exams — Family/Community, Identity, Beauty, Science/Tech, Global Challenges, Contemporary Life.

## Big Ideas

1. **Three writing systems:** hiragana (ひらがな), katakana (カタカナ), kanji (漢字).
2. **Politeness levels (keigo):** language varies by social hierarchy.
3. **Subject-Object-Verb (SOV)** word order; very different from English (SVO).
4. **Particles** (は wa, が ga, を o, に ni, で de) mark grammatical role.
5. **Japanese culture:** Shinto + Buddhism, hierarchy, group harmony (wa 和), aesthetics (wabi-sabi).

## Tier-Tagged Content

### Foundational [3] — needed for any score

- **Writing systems:**
  - **Hiragana (46 basic):** native Japanese words, grammatical particles.
  - **Katakana (46 basic):** foreign loanwords, emphasis, scientific names.
  - **Kanji:** Chinese-derived characters; ~2,000 in common use (jōyō kanji).
- **Word order:** SOV — Subject Object Verb. *Watashi wa hon o yomimasu* (I book read = "I read a book").
- **Particles:**
  - **は (wa):** topic marker.
  - **が (ga):** subject marker.
  - **を (o):** direct object.
  - **に (ni):** location/time.
  - **で (de):** location of action.
  - **へ (e):** direction.
- **Politeness (keigo):**
  - **Plain form** (with friends, family younger).
  - **Polite form (-masu, -desu):** standard polite.
  - **Honorific (sonkeigo):** elevating others.
  - **Humble (kenjōgo):** humbling self.
- **Family terms:**
  - **私 (watashi/watakushi):** I (formal).
  - **僕 (boku):** I (male, informal).
  - **お父さん (otōsan):** father.
  - **お母さん (okāsan):** mother.
  - **兄/弟 (ani/otōto):** older/younger brother.
  - **姉/妹 (ane/imōto):** older/younger sister.
- **Cultural concepts:**
  - **Wa (和):** harmony, group cohesion.
  - **Honne (本音)** vs **tatemae (建前):** true feelings vs public face.
  - **Wabi-sabi (侘寂):** beauty in imperfection, transience.
  - **Hierarchy** (sempai/kōhai = senior/junior; very important in school, work).
- **Religion:**
  - **Shinto (神道):** indigenous; kami (spirits), shrines.
  - **Buddhism (仏教):** introduced 6th century from Korea/China.
  - **Most Japanese practice both** (Shinto for births, weddings; Buddhism for funerals).
- **Holidays:**
  - **New Year (お正月 oshōgatsu):** most important holiday.
  - **Cherry blossom viewing (花見 hanami):** spring.
  - **Obon (お盆):** August; honor ancestors.
  - **Coming of Age Day (成人の日):** Jan, age 20.

### Adds for [4]

- **Three writing systems used together:** sentence might mix all three.
- **Honne vs tatemae:** Japanese society distinguishes inner truth from outer expression.
- **Group orientation:** company loyalty traditionally strong; "salary man" culture.

### Adds for [5]

- **Why politeness layers complex:** wrong level = social offense.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 [3] — Writing systems

How would *sushi* be written?
- **Hiragana:** すし.
- **Katakana:** スシ (sometimes for foreign use).
- **Kanji:** 寿司.

### Example 2 [4] — Particles

In *Watashi wa gakusei desu* ("I am a student"):
- **私 (watashi)** = I.
- **は (wa)** = topic marker.
- **学生 (gakusei)** = student.
- **です (desu)** = polite copula "am/is/are."

### Example 3 [5] — Honne and tatemae

What's the cultural significance of honne vs tatemae?
- **Honne (本音):** true inner feelings.
- **Tatemae (建前):** public façade, what's said for social harmony.
- **Maintains group harmony (wa).**
- **Western readers** often perceive as insincerity, but it's social skill.
- **Reading both** is high competence.

## Top Traps & Common Errors

1. **Three writing systems** — know which is used when.
2. **Word order SOV** — very different from English.
3. **Politeness levels** — must match social context.
4. **"Wa"** = harmony (cultural) AND topic particle (grammar) — different.

## "Phrases That Score" — verbatim language for FRQs

1. "Japanese uses three writing systems together: hiragana for native words and grammar, katakana for foreign loanwords and emphasis, and kanji (Chinese-derived characters) for content nouns and verb stems — often appearing in a single sentence."
2. "Politeness in Japanese (keigo) operates on multiple levels — plain, polite (-masu/-desu), honorific (sonkeigo), and humble (kenjōgo) — chosen based on relative social status of speaker and addressee."
3. "The distinction between honne (true inner feelings) and tatemae (public façade) reflects the Japanese cultural priority on group harmony (wa); reading both layers is essential for cross-cultural understanding."

## If You Do Nothing Else for This Exam

*Master three writing systems. Master particles. Master politeness levels. Master honne/tatemae and wa. Master family terms.*
